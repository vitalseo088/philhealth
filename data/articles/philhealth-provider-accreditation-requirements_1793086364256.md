# PhilHealth Provider Accreditation Requirements: Documents, Fees, and Process (2026)

To become a PhilHealth-accredited health facility, you generally need a valid license or certification from the Department of Health (DOH), a completed Provider Data Record, a notarized Performance Commitment, proof that you paid the accreditation fee, and the other documents listed for your facility type in PhilHealth's Annex A checklist. The rules come mainly from **PhilHealth Circular No. 2023-0012**, the "Omnibus Guidelines on the Accreditation of Health Facilities," as applied by PhilHealth's yearly renewal advisories.

This guide is for owners, administrators, and heads of hospitals, clinics, dialysis centers, maternity facilities, and other providers that want to join, or stay in, the National Health Insurance Program. It covers 2026 information. It explains the process in plain language, but it does not replace the circular and the official checklist, which you should read before filing.

> **Provider information can change. Check the official PhilHealth provider directory before visiting.** (If you are a patient trying to check a facility, see our guide on PhilHealth provider verification.)

## Quick Answer

| Item | What the official sources say |
|---|---|
| Main rule | PhilHealth Circular No. 2023-0012 (Omnibus Guidelines on Accreditation of Health Facilities) |
| Basic documents | Provider Data Record (PDR), notarized Performance Commitment (PC), DOH license/certification, proof of fee payment, plus the items for your facility type in Annex A |
| Where to file | Your PhilHealth Local Health Insurance Office (LHIO) or Regional Office (PRO) |
| Validity | Up to three calendar years from CY 2024, at the facility's choice and subject to requirements |
| Renewal filing period | October 1 to the last working day of December of the preceding year (extended in some years) |
| Fees | Set by facility type (for example, ₱10,000 for Level 3 hospitals); non-refundable |
| Where to download forms | PhilHealth's Downloads page, "Accreditation Documents" section |

## What Is PhilHealth Accreditation?

**Accreditation** is the privilege PhilHealth grants to a health facility to take part in the National Health Insurance Program. Without it, a facility generally cannot be reimbursed for PhilHealth benefits. Under the circular, PhilHealth sets the accreditation period and keeps the right to issue, deny, or withdraw accreditation after evaluating a facility's capability and integrity.

PhilHealth accredits several facility types under this circular, including Level 1, 2, and 3 hospitals, infirmaries, ambulatory surgical clinics, freestanding dialysis clinics, maternity care package providers, TB-DOTS providers, family planning clinics, animal bite package providers, drug abuse treatment and rehabilitation centers, cancer treatment facilities, and primary care (Konsulta, now YAKAP) providers. The circular also allows other facility types as PhilHealth deems necessary.

### Types of accreditation

- **Initial:** for a qualified facility applying for the first time.
- **Renewal (continuous):** for a facility applying before its current accreditation ends.
- **Re-accreditation:** for situations such as a lapse or denied application, filing after the deadline, transfer of location, change of ownership, change in classification, upgrading, adding a service that needs a new license or certificate, or resuming operations after closure.

## Who Can Apply?

The circular covers facilities that are currently accredited, have pending appeals or motions for reconsideration, or intend to join the program. Key conditions in the circular:

- The facility must be duly licensed or certified by the DOH, as applicable, during the whole accreditation period.
- Hospitals (Levels 1, 2, 3), infirmaries, ambulatory surgical clinics, and dialysis clinics that have operated for at least three years may apply for initial accreditation. Those that have operated for less than three years may still qualify if they meet the other requirements and at least one listed condition, such as having a managing professional with three years of experience in another accredited facility, being a tertiary-level facility or equivalent, or operating where existing accredited facilities cannot adequately serve the population.
- The facility must be a PhilHealth-registered employer and regularly remit its employees' premium contributions.
- The head of certain facility types (for example hospitals, infirmaries, dialysis clinics) must hold active PhilHealth accreditation for the entire accreditation period. For hospitals and infirmaries, the managing physician must be full-time and handle only one facility.

## Requirements Checklist

PhilHealth's official checklist is **Annex A: Documentary Requirements for Accreditation of Health Facilities**, available from the Downloads page. It lists different documents for each facility type and for initial, renewal, and re-accreditation. Download the current version and match your facility type, because the lists are not the same for everyone.

### Documents the circular describes for applications

| Document | Notes from the official sources |
|---|---|
| **Provider Data Record (PDR)** (Annex B) | Fully accomplished for initial, renewal, and re-accreditation, and when updating information such as head of facility or contact details |
| **Performance Commitment (PC)** (Annex C) | A notarized document signed by the facility's authorized representative, stating its commitments to PhilHealth. Its terms cannot be changed by the facility without PhilHealth's written approval |
| **DOH license/certification** (or a recognized agency's) | Needed, unless the facility appears in an official roster from the issuing agency, in which case inclusion in the roster suffices |
| **Proof of accreditation fee payment** | See the fee table below |
| **For corporations or partnerships** | Articles of Incorporation or Partnership (initial application or re-accreditation due to change in ownership) and the latest General Information Sheet (GIS) |
| **Contracts relevant to claims reimbursement** | Listed for hospitals in Annex A, for example agreements tied to services such as hemodialysis |
| **Accredited head of facility** | The facility's head must have valid accreditation, or apply together with the facility, where required |
| **Other items by facility type** | Examples in Annex A include training certificates for some packages and Mayor's Permits for certain clinics located in private corporations or schools |

**Required** items are those the circular states for all applications (PDR, PC, license or certification, fee). **May be required depending on your situation** are the facility-type and ownership-specific items in Annex A. The extracted text of Annex A that I could review was partly garbled, so confirm the exact list for your facility type from the official PDF before filing.

### Other conditions you agree to

The circular also says an accredited facility must have a quality improvement program, a referral arrangement or participation in a service delivery network, the capability to transmit claims electronically, and the capacity to receive reimbursements through PhilHealth's auto-credit payment scheme.

## Accreditation Fees

Under the circular's schedule, fees are non-refundable:

| Facility type | Fee |
|---|---|
| Level 3 hospital (teaching hospital) | ₱10,000 |
| Level 2 hospital | ₱8,000 |
| Level 1 hospital | ₱5,000 |
| Infirmary | ₱3,000 |
| Ambulatory surgical clinic | ₱5,000 |
| Freestanding dialysis clinic (hemodialysis and peritoneal) | ₱5,000 |
| Primary care package provider (Konsulta/YAKAP) | ₱2,000 |
| Maternity care package provider | ₱1,500 |
| Freestanding family planning clinic | ₱1,500 |
| TB-DOTS, animal bite, or outpatient HIV/AIDS treatment package provider | ₱1,000 each |

Notes:

- Hospital extension facilities, outpatient malaria package providers, community isolation units, COVID-19 home isolation package providers, SARS-CoV-2 testing laboratories, and drug abuse treatment and rehabilitation centers do not pay an accreditation fee under the circular.
- Facilities offering combined packages have separate fee rules (see the circular's Tables 4 and 5).
- A facility applying for multi-year accreditation pays the one-year fee multiplied by the number of years. If it is granted fewer years, the excess payment is credited as advance payment for later applications.
- The fee table above comes from the 2023 circular. The 2025 renewal advisory mentions fees for some newer categories (for example an oral health package), so check the current advisory for your category.

## Step-by-Step: How the Process Works

1. **Identify your application type.** Initial, renewal, or re-accreditation.
2. **Get the official forms and checklist.** Download Annex A, the PDR, and the PC from PhilHealth's Downloads page under "Accreditation Documents."
3. **Prepare your DOH license or certification** and any other documents for your facility type.
4. **Complete and sign the PDR and PC.** The PC must be notarized and signed by the authorized representative.
5. **Pay the accreditation fee** for your facility type.
6. **File with your LHIO or PRO.** The circular says to submit two sets of the complete application during the filing period. Applications may be submitted electronically or manually. For electronic submissions, hard copies must reach the LHIO or PRO within 30 days, or accreditation may be temporarily withdrawn.
7. **Pre-accreditation survey, if required.** See below.
8. **Wait for the decision.** PhilHealth emails an approval letter and Certificate of Accreditation upon approval. A denial letter is emailed within three days after the decision is approved.
9. **Appeal or file a motion for reconsideration within 30 days** if you disagree with a denial.

A "complete application" under the circular has an accomplished PDR and PC, updated licenses or certifications, and all documents on the checklist. Incomplete applications are returned with a list of deficiencies.

### Pre-accreditation survey (PAS)

A PAS is an on-site or virtual assessment. The circular says it applies to, among others, non-hospital primary care (Konsulta) providers that lack certain licenses or services and facilities with a gap in accreditation of six months or more. PhilHealth notifies the facility of the schedule within seven working days after receiving the application. Facilities generally get 60 calendar days to fix deficiencies, and primary care providers get 90 days.

## Timeline, Deadlines, and Validity

- **Renewal filing:** the circular sets October 1 to the last working day of December of the preceding accreditation year. Applications filed late are treated as re-accreditation and may cause a gap.
- **CY 2026 renewal:** PhilHealth Advisory No. 2025-0055 opened filing from October 1 to December 29, 2025. Advisory No. 2025-0070 later extended the deadline to January 14, 2026, and Advisory No. 2026-0006 extended it to January 30, 2026 for YAKAP clinics and GAMOT package providers.
- **CY 2027 renewal:** I did not find a published CY 2027 renewal advisory in the sources reviewed. Check PhilHealth's advisories page for the current filing period.
- **Validity:** beginning CY 2024, a facility may be granted continuous accreditation of up to three calendar years, subject to compliance. For facilities whose DOH license is valid for three years (dialysis clinics, ambulatory surgical clinics, and primary care facilities), accreditation follows the license's expiry. A facility that files on time stays accredited until PhilHealth denies the application.
- **Updated licenses:** facilities granted multi-year accreditation but with one-year permits must submit the updated license, certification, or permit by January of the applicable year (Advisory No. 2025-0055 set January 31, 2026 for CY 2026).

## What Can Go Wrong

The circular lists grounds for denial and temporary withdrawal. Some examples:

| Situation | Official treatment |
|---|---|
| Non-compliance with requirements | Basis for denial or temporary withdrawal |
| Lapse, revocation, or gap in the DOH license | A gap in the license causes a gap in accreditation; it can be reinstated if the gap is lifted within the accreditation period |
| Head of facility's accreditation expires | Facility must find a qualified replacement within 60 days and may name an officer-in-charge meanwhile; failure can lead to temporary withdrawal |
| License or certification expires during accreditation | Updated document due within 60 days of expiry or accreditation may be temporarily withdrawn |
| Change in ownership, management, or transfer without prior notice | Basis for denial or temporary withdrawal |
| Fraud-related conviction or cases | Basis for denial; some pending cases can lead to provisional accreditation for up to 24 months |

### Appeals

A facility can appeal or file a motion for reconsideration within 30 calendar days of receiving the decision; otherwise the decision becomes final. A denied facility may re-apply after resolving the reasons for denial. When an application, appeal, or motion is denied, the facility must put up a notice beside its PhilHealth signage saying it is no longer accredited.

## Example (Illustrative Only)

*This is an example to show the process, not a real case.*

A new clinic owner wants to offer PhilHealth primary care services. They confirm their DOH license, download Annex A and the PDR and PC, and check the fee for primary care package providers. They have the PC notarized, pay the fee, and submit the complete set to their LHIO. Because the clinic lacks a licensed laboratory, PhilHealth schedules a pre-accreditation survey and gives a period to fix the gaps. They keep their receiving copy and track the decision.

## Common Problems

- **Using the wrong checklist.** Requirements differ by facility type and application type. Use the current Annex A.
- **Late filing.** Filing after the period is treated as re-accreditation and may produce a gap.
- **License gaps.** An expired or lapsed DOH license affects accreditation.
- **Missing hard copies.** Electronic filings need hard copies within 30 days.
- **Head of facility not accredited.** Check the head's accreditation validity before filing.
- **Unpaid contributions.** Facilities must be registered employers and remit contributions.

## What To Do If Something Goes Wrong

- If your application is returned as incomplete, fix the listed deficiencies and resubmit.
- If it is denied, check the reason in the letter and the remedies in the circular, and act within 30 days.
- For questions, contact your PhilHealth Regional Office or LHIO, or the PhilHealth Action Center at actioncenter@philhealth.gov.ph.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is the main PhilHealth rule on provider accreditation?**
PhilHealth Circular No. 2023-0012, the Omnibus Guidelines on the Accreditation of Health Facilities. Yearly advisories add filing dates and other details.

**How long does accreditation last?**
Starting CY 2024, up to three calendar years, subject to requirements and unless withdrawn earlier.

**Can I file the application online?**
The circular allows electronic or manual filing with the LHIO or PRO, but hard copies of electronically filed documents are due within 30 days.

**Is the accreditation fee refundable?**
No, the circular says it is non-refundable.

**Do doctors and other professionals need accreditation too?**
PhilHealth accredits health care professionals separately, and the heads of some facility types must be accredited. The 2025 renewal advisory states a 120 to 20 days before expiry window for professionals' renewal, but the details are outside this guide. Check PhilHealth's professional accreditation materials.

**What if my application is denied?**
You may file an appeal or motion for reconsideration within 30 days, or re-apply once the reasons are resolved.
