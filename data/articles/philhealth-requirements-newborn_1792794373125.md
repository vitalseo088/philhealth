# 2. Article

# PhilHealth Requirements for Newborn 2026: Coverage, Registration and Documents

Your newborn is automatically covered by the National Health Insurance Program, according to PhilHealth's 2026 maternity circular. The baby gets a PhilHealth Identification Number (PIN) when the baby is registered or when the delivery claim is processed. To be safe, you can also declare the baby as your dependent using the PhilHealth Member Registration Form (PMRF) and the baby's birth record.

This guide is for new parents and family members. It covers automatic coverage, how to declare a newborn as a dependent, the documents listed by PhilHealth, and what the Newborn Care Package is. It reflects PhilHealth issuances reviewed in October 2026. One important limit: I could not verify the current peso amount of the Newborn Care Package, and I explain why below.

## Quick Answer: Newborn Requirements at a Glance

| Item | What PhilHealth's sources say | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Coverage | Newborns are automatically covered under the National Health Insurance Program | **Automatic** |
| PhilHealth number (PIN) | Issued to the newborn upon registration or claims processing | **Issued by PhilHealth** |
| Declaring the baby as your dependent | Use the PMRF with *Updating/Amendment* ticked, and the baby's birth record | **Recommended; document required** |
| Proof of birth | Birth or baptismal certificate with registry number, or alternatives listed below | **Required to declare a dependent** |

Sources: PhilHealth Circular No. 2026-0006; Circular No. 2020-0001 and annexes; Circular No. 2026-0005.

## What Is Covered and Who Qualifies?

Under Circular No. 2026-0006 (the maternity care circular), newborns are automatically covered under the National Health Insurance Program. PhilHealth says the newborn's PIN is issued upon registration or claims processing, and that PhilHealth benefits for newborn care will be issued in a separate circular.

For the **Newborn Care Package (NCP)**, which PhilHealth defines as the benefit for essential health services during the first few days of life, PhilHealth's guide to Circular 2018-0021 says a newborn is eligible if all of the following are met:

1. Either parent is eligible to avail of PhilHealth benefits (a newborn who is a qualified dependent is also eligible).
2. The baby was born in an accredited facility that performs deliveries, such as a hospital or birthing home.
3. The services were provided upon delivery.

That guide dates from 2018. The 2026 maternity circular says newborn benefits will be issued separately, and I did not find the replacement circular. Treat the 2018 eligibility rules as the latest I could verify, and ask your facility if anything has changed.

## Declaring Your Newborn as a Dependent

A dependent is a person who can use PhilHealth benefits through a member's record. PhilHealth's PMRF instructions say you can declare children below 21 who are unmarried and unemployed.

### Documents for a newborn or young child

According to PhilHealth Circular 2020-0001, Annex B, a child below 21 is documented with one of the following:

- a birth or baptismal certificate with registry number; **or**
- if the birth certificate is not available, a notarized affidavit of two disinterested persons attesting to the date of birth; **or**
- a duly accomplished Certificate of Live Birth, signed by the attendant at birth, the informant and the hospital staff who prepared it.

Other child types have their own documents, such as a court decree of adoption for a legally adopted child, or a stepchild's birth certificate plus the marriage certificate of the biological parent and stepparent.

The annexes I reviewed do not describe special rules for a baby whose surname differs from the member's, or for unmarried parents. If this applies to you, ask your PhilHealth office what they accept.

### Step-by-step: How to add your baby

1. **Get the PMRF.** Download it from the Downloads page of the PhilHealth website or get one from a PhilHealth office.
2. **Tick *Updating/Amendment*.** PhilHealth's own amendment page says to tick the "For Updating" box before filling out the form.
3. **Fill in the dependent section.** Write the baby's full name, birth date and relationship. The form says to write in capital letters and to put "N/A" for items that do not apply.
4. **Attach the baby's proof of birth.** Use one of the options above.
5. **Sign the form.** Your signature certifies that the information is true.
6. **Submit** at the nearest PhilHealth office. If you use a representative, they need your signed authorization letter plus one valid government photo ID of both you and the representative.
7. **Wait for your updated Member Data Record (MDR).** The amendment page says you await the printout of the updated MDR.

Self-paying members can also update records through the PhilHealth Member Portal or the eGovPH Super App under Circular 2026-0011.

## What Is the Newborn Care Package?

The Newborn Care Package (NCP) covers essential health services for the baby during the first few days of life. According to PhilHealth's guide to Circular 2018-0021, it includes:

- **Essential newborn care:** immediate drying, early skin-to-skin contact, timely cord clamping, non-separation of mother and baby for early breastfeeding, eye prophylaxis and vitamin K, weighing, and the first doses of the hepatitis B and BCG vaccines.
- **Newborn screening test:** a blood test done 24 to 72 hours after birth to detect congenital disorders. The 2018 circular moved to an expanded screening test that screens for 28 or more disorders.
- **Newborn hearing screening test.**

The facility, not the parent, files the claim. PhilHealth's 2018 guide lists these claim attachments: a Claim Signature Form with the filter card sticker, a properly accomplished PMRF, Claim Form 2 if applicable, a statement of account, the hearing screening result, and the Newborn Hearing Screening Registry Card (Blue Form) if applicable. The guide also says the facility must be accredited and the baby must be attended by an accredited health care professional.

### How much is the Newborn Care Package in 2026? (unclear)

- A May 2026 Philippine Information Agency report says PhilHealth "now provides" a **₱5,752.50** newborn care package under Circulars 2026-0005 and 2026-0006.

Because I could not find a current official circular that sets the amount, this guide does not give one. Ask the facility's PhilHealth desk what rate and package code applies, or check PhilHealth's circulars page.

## What Parents Need at the Hospital or Birthing Facility

- **Your PIN.** Bring it so the facility can verify your record. Circular 2026-0005 says claims submission for hospital maternal care does not require a printed copy of the MDR.
- **Claims are filed by the facility.** For hospital maternal cases, facilities file within 60 calendar days, and direct filing by members is not allowed except for confinement abroad and emergencies at non-accredited institutions.
- **No co-payment rules.** Circular 2026-0005 says all PhilHealth beneficiaries are entitled to no co-payment when admitted to basic or ward accommodation in accredited hospitals. Co-payment caps apply to semi-private beds. Services beyond coverage may be charged. PhilHealth coverage depends on the applicable benefit package and eligibility requirements.
- **Birthing homes and lying-in clinics.** Under Circular 2026-0006, the delivering provider has a care coordinator who facilitates newborn registration after delivery and linkage to a primary care provider. The mother's delivery benefit and the newborn's benefit are different benefits.

## If the Baby Is Sick or Born Early

PhilHealth's 2018 NCP guide says that when a newborn is sick, the appropriate case rate for the illness is reimbursed as the first case rate and the NCP is claimed as the second case rate. If a repeat newborn screening is needed because the baby was born preterm or small, the repeat screening is charged to the first case rate. PhilHealth also publishes Z Benefits for premature and small newborns, with their own self-assessment tools and annexes on the Downloads page. Ask the hospital about the benefit package that applies, since this guide does not cover those benefits.

## Examples

*These are illustrative examples, not official rulings.*

**Example 1: Mother is a member.** A member gives birth at an accredited hospital. The hospital treats the baby as covered, and claims the newborn services if the facility qualifies. After discharge, the mother submits a PMRF marked *Updating/Amendment* with the baby's birth certificate with registry number to declare the baby as a dependent.

**Example 2: Birth certificate not yet available.** A father wants to declare his baby but the registry number is not ready. Annex B of Circular 2020-0001 lists a duly accomplished Certificate of Live Birth, or a notarized affidavit of two disinterested persons attesting to the date of birth, as alternatives for children below 21. He asks the office whether they will accept one of these for now.

## Common Problems

- **Waiting for the birth certificate.** The annex lists alternatives. Ask your office which one they will accept.
- **Not declaring the baby on the member's record.** Automatic coverage applies, but a missing dependent entry can slow things down. Update the PMRF.
- **Facility not accredited for newborn care.** The NCP can be claimed only by accredited facilities that perform deliveries.
- **Incomplete claim documents.** Claims are filed by the facility. PhilHealth may return claims with incomplete requirements.
- **Confusing the mother's delivery benefit with the baby's benefit.** They are separate benefits.

## What To Do If Something Goes Wrong

- **If the facility asks you to pay for services that should be covered,** ask which benefit and rate applies. Circular 2026-0005 says patients can report non-compliance with no-copayment rules to the PhilHealth Corporate Action Center at (02) 8662-2588 or actioncenter@philhealth.gov.ph.
- **If your baby's record does not show,** check your MDR and submit the PMRF update again with the baby's proof of birth.
- **If you need urgent care for your baby,** seek care immediately. PhilHealth paperwork should not delay emergency care.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is my newborn automatically covered by PhilHealth?
Yes. Circular 2026-0006 says newborns are automatically covered under the National Health Insurance Program and issued a PIN upon registration or claims processing.

### Do I still need to add my baby as a dependent?
PhilHealth's PMRF is the way to declare dependents. Automatic coverage applies, but declaring your baby on your record keeps your record complete. Ask your PhilHealth office how it applies to you.

### What document do I need to declare my baby as a dependent?
A birth or baptismal certificate with registry number. If it is not available, PhilHealth's annex lists a notarized affidavit of two disinterested persons attesting to the date of birth, or a duly accomplished Certificate of Live Birth.

### Do I need a printed MDR at the hospital?
For hospital maternal care claims, Circular 2026-0005 says claims submission does not require a printed copy of the MDR.

### What does the Newborn Care Package cover?
Essential newborn care, newborn screening and newborn hearing screening, per PhilHealth's 2018 guide.

### How much is the Newborn Care Package now?
I could not confirm a current official amount. See the section above.

### Can I file the newborn claim myself?
The package is claimed by the accredited facility. For hospital maternal cases, PhilHealth says direct filing by members is not allowed except in limited situations.

### Is the baby covered if the mother has unpaid contributions?
Under Circular 2026-0011, self-paying members with missed contributions can still avail of benefits but must pay the missed contributions with interest. This is separate from newborn rules, so ask PhilHealth how it applies to you.

### Is there a fee to add a dependent?
The official pages I reviewed do not state a fee.
