# PhilHealth Hospital Bill Coverage 2026: How the Deduction Works and What You May Still Pay

PhilHealth does not pay a percentage of your hospital bill. For most admissions it pays a fixed amount called a **case rate**, which the accredited hospital deducts from your bill at discharge. Whether anything is left for you to pay depends on three things: the size of the bill compared with the case rate, the type of room you are in, and which policy applies to you and the hospital.

This guide is for patients and families preparing for, or going through, a hospital admission in 2026. It explains the rules in plain language. It is not medical or legal advice, and the details can differ by benefit package, so ask the hospital's PhilHealth desk about your specific case.

## Quick Answer

| Situation | What generally applies |
|---|---|
| Admitted to an accredited hospital for a covered condition | PhilHealth pays the case rate for the diagnosis, deducted from the bill at discharge |
| Bill is higher than the case rate | The difference may be charged to the patient unless a no-co-payment or No Balance Billing policy applies |
| Basic or ward accommodation | PhilHealth's recent package circulars say no co-payment applies for covered essential services |
| Private or semi-private room | A co-payment can apply, and extra charges for amenities, choice of doctor or upgrades are allowed under the circulars we reviewed |
| Patient has an HMO or private insurance | PhilHealth and legally required discounts are deducted first; the HMO applies afterward |

Two cautions: the case rate is not the same for every illness, and PhilHealth rules change by circular. Confirm the rules for your admission before you are discharged.

## What Is a Case Rate?

A **case rate** is a fixed peso amount PhilHealth pays for a specific diagnosis or procedure. The system is called **All Case Rates**. PhilHealth's own plain-language guide describes it as a fixed amount for a particular illness that covers professional fees and facility charges such as room and board, diagnostic tests and laboratories, medicines and medical supplies.

Because the amount is fixed, the actual bill can be higher or lower than the case rate. In several recent PhilHealth circulars, payment is based on the actual charges in the hospital's Statement of Account, up to the applicable case rate. Ask the hospital whether this applies to your package.

## How the PhilHealth Deduction Works at Discharge

1. The hospital codes your **primary diagnosis**, which determines the case rate that applies.
2. The hospital prepares a **Statement of Account (SOA)**, the itemized bill.
3. Legally required discounts, such as senior citizen and PWD discounts, and the PhilHealth benefit are deducted first from the total bill.
4. Any benefits from an HMO, private health insurance or employer are applied after those deductions.
5. You pay whatever balance remains, if any.
6. The hospital files the claim with PhilHealth within 60 calendar days.

The hospital files the claim, not the patient. PhilHealth's 2026 circulars say direct filing by members is not allowed, except for confinements abroad under separate rules.

One of PhilHealth's 2026 circulars also says accredited hospitals should deduct PhilHealth benefits at discharge on any day of the week and at any time, and that a printed Member Data Record (MDR) is not required for claims submission under that benefit.

## Basic or Ward Accommodation vs. Private Rooms

This is the biggest factor in whether you pay anything on top of the case rate.

PhilHealth's 2026 circulars define **basic or ward accommodation** as regular meals, a bed in a shared room with three or more occupants, at least fan ventilation, and a shared toilet and bath.

Under PhilHealth Circular No. 2026-0008 (the leptospirosis benefit package), PhilHealth states that all beneficiaries are entitled to **no co-payment** when admitted to basic or ward accommodation in public and private hospitals. Accredited hospitals are not to balance bill or charge patients in basic or ward accommodation for the essential services covered by that package.

Outside basic or ward accommodation, the same circular allows a **co-payment**, which it defines as a predetermined amount charged for amenities, specialist fees for non-basic accommodation, choice of physician, or additional or upgraded services. For that package, the co-payment is capped at 20% of the package rate. That 20% figure is stated for the leptospirosis package only. We did not find the same figure stated as a general rule, so ask the hospital what applies to your package.

## Zero Balance Billing and No Balance Billing

These two terms are related but not identical.

- **No Balance Billing (NBB)** has been a PhilHealth policy since 2011. Under it, qualified patients are not charged anything above PhilHealth's package rate when admitted to ward accommodation. News reports describe the qualified groups as indigent and sponsored members, domestic workers, senior citizens and lifetime members.
- **Zero Balance Billing (ZBB)** was announced in 2025 for hospitals run by the Department of Health. The Philippine Information Agency, citing PhilHealth, reported that it covers all PhilHealth members admitted to basic or ward accommodation in DOH-retained hospitals. A private room means regular PhilHealth benefits with co-payment apply.

The policies have different scopes and the sources we reviewed do not fully spell out how they interact. Ask the hospital whether your admission falls under either, and whether the hospital is a DOH-retained facility.

## Rules That Changed Recently

| Rule | What changed | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 45-day benefit limit | Lifted for all members and qualified beneficiaries, effective April 4, 2025. Hospitals must review cases that go past 45 days | PhilHealth Circular No. 2025-0007 |
| Admissions of less than 24 hours | Expands payment under existing case rates when a patient dies or is transferred before 24 hours. It is not a new benefit, and improved, recovered, HAMA and absconded dispositions are not covered under it | PhilHealth Circular No. 2026-0013 (published September 3, 2026) |
| Single Period of Confinement rule | PhilHealth has issued circulars lifting this rule for All Case Rate claims. We did not review the full text, so we do not describe its details here | PhilHealth Circulars No. 2024-0021 and 2025-0015 |

## Who Is Covered?

PhilHealth's 2026 circular on leptospirosis cites the Universal Health Care Act, which says every Filipino citizen is automatically included in the National Health Insurance Program. The sources we reviewed do not clearly explain how current contribution status affects an inpatient claim, so we are not stating a required number of paid months. Check your membership and contribution status with PhilHealth.

The hospital must also be **PhilHealth-accredited**. Provider information can change. Check the official PhilHealth provider directory before visiting.

## What To Do Before and During Admission

1. **Confirm the hospital is PhilHealth-accredited.**
2. **Tell the admitting desk you are a member.** Bring your PhilHealth Identification Number (PIN) or any ID the hospital asks for.
3. **Ask which accommodation type you are in** and whether it is basic or ward.
4. **Ask how the diagnosis will be coded** and which case rate applies.
5. **Ask what is outside the package** such as amenities, choice of doctor or upgrades, and what they would cost. PhilHealth's recent circulars say the attending physician should explain the covered services as part of informed consent.
6. **Request an itemized Statement of Account** before you pay.
7. **Check the PhilHealth deduction** and any legal discounts on the bill.
8. **Keep copies** of your admission papers, bill and receipts.

## Documents Hospitals Submit

Hospitals, not patients, prepare the claim. PhilHealth's guide for Circular No. 2026-0013 lists the main documents for admissions of at least 24 hours: the Claim Signature Form, Claim Form 2, Claim Form 4, the Statement of Account, the admitting order or admitting form with the nurse-signed admission slip, and other documents needed to show medical necessity. This list is for hospitals. Members should not assume they must prepare these forms themselves.

## Common Problems

- **The deduction is smaller than expected.** Case rates differ by diagnosis, and older rates still appear on many websites. Ask the hospital which rate was applied.
- **You were charged for medicines bought outside.** PhilHealth's 2026 leptospirosis circular says hospitals should keep minimum stocks of essential medicines and supplies to avoid unwarranted out-of-pocket purchases. Ask whether the items were covered.
- **Extra charges in a ward.** Under the recent circulars, covered essential services for basic or ward patients should not carry extra charges. Ask for an explanation in writing.
- **Disagreement about room type.** Ask which accommodation you were assigned and why.
- **A short stay.** See the rules on admissions of less than 24 hours above.

## What To Do If Something Goes Wrong

- Ask the hospital's billing office for a written explanation of the charges and the PhilHealth deduction.
- Report concerns to the PhilHealth Corporate Action Center at **(02) 8662-2588** or **actioncenter@philhealth.gov.ph**. PhilHealth's 2026 circular says beneficiaries are encouraged to report concerns this way. You can also contact your nearest Local Health Insurance Office. Contact details can change, so check philhealth.gov.ph.
- Keep your bill, receipts and admission papers.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Does PhilHealth pay the whole hospital bill?
Not necessarily. PhilHealth pays a case rate. If the bill is higher, the difference may be charged to the patient unless a no-co-payment, No Balance Billing or Zero Balance Billing policy applies.

### How much does PhilHealth cover?
It depends on the diagnosis or procedure. See PhilHealth's Case Rate Search on philhealth.gov.ph or Circular No. 2024-0037 and its annexes for the published rates.

### Can I pay nothing in a ward?
PhilHealth's recent circulars say no co-payment applies to covered essential services in basic or ward accommodation. ZBB in DOH-retained hospitals is reported to cover all members in basic or ward accommodation. Confirm with the hospital.

### Can the hospital charge me for a private room?
Yes. The circulars we reviewed allow co-payment for non-basic accommodation, amenities, choice of physician and upgrades.

### Which is deducted first, PhilHealth or my HMO?
PhilHealth and legally required discounts such as senior citizen and PWD discounts come first. HMO and private insurance benefits are applied afterward.

### Is there still a 45-day limit?
No. PhilHealth lifted the 45-day benefit limit rule effective April 4, 2025.

### Can I file the claim myself?
For hospital admissions, the hospital files the claim. Direct filing by members is not allowed except for confinements abroad.

### Is a printed MDR required?
PhilHealth's 2026 leptospirosis circular says a printed MDR is not required for claims submission under that benefit. Other benefits may have their own rules.
