# PhilHealth Requirements for Pregnant Woman 2026: What You Need and What Is Covered

If you are pregnant and already enrolled in PhilHealth, PhilHealth's 2026 maternity rules say you are **immediately eligible** to use your benefits. You do not need to wait out a contribution period, according to the circular I reviewed. What you do need is to be registered with a PhilHealth Identification Number (PIN), use a PhilHealth-accredited provider, and keep your antenatal (prenatal) records complete.

This guide is for pregnant women, and for family members helping them, who want to know what to prepare. It covers the rules for outpatient prenatal care, routine tests, delivery in a birthing facility, and postpartum care under PhilHealth's 2026 maternity circular. It does not give medical advice. Hospital-based delivery rules are covered by separate issuances, and I explain below what I could and could not verify.

## Quick Answer: What a Pregnant Woman Needs

| Requirement | What it means | Status |
|---|---|---|
| PhilHealth membership (PIN) | You should be enrolled in the National Health Insurance Program (NHIP). Newborns are automatically covered. | **Required to use benefits** |
| Accredited provider | Antenatal care, tests and delivery must be done by PhilHealth-accredited facilities for the benefit to be claimed | **Required** |
| Antenatal Care Record (ANCR) or equivalent DOH mother-and-baby book | Bring to every prenatal visit so the health worker can fill it in | **Required for the facility's claim** |
| Laboratory request form (LRF) | Issued by your prenatal provider so you can get the routine tests at an accredited laboratory | **Needed for the routine tests** |
| Signature on claim and record forms | You sign the Claim Signature Form and care records | **Required for the claim** |
| YAKAP clinic registration | You are expected to "endeavor" to register with a YAKAP clinic | **Encouraged by the circular** |

Source: PhilHealth Circular No. 2026-0006, *Expansion of Maternity Care Benefits for Non-Hospital and Outpatient Facilities (Revision)*.

## What Changed in 2026?

PhilHealth has reorganized its maternity benefits. Under Circular No. 2026-0006, the Expanded Maternity Care Package has been "unbundled" into four parts:

1. antenatal care (prenatal visits and routine tests),
2. intrapartum care (labor and normal delivery),
3. postpartum care, and
4. emergency stabilization and step-up referral (when complications arise during labor and the mother must be transferred to a hospital).

The circular also adds a **care coordinator** at each accredited provider, a designated staff member who helps you move through the full course of care: enrollment, scheduling your visits, referrals for tests, your birth plan and post-delivery follow-up.

**Conflict to note:** an earlier version of this policy, Circular No. 2026-0004, lists slightly different gestation-week ranges for the trimester visits than the revision (2026-0006). The subject line of 2026-0006 marks it as a revision. This guide follows the revision. The copy I read does not clearly show a separate publication date, so check PhilHealth's circulars page if the exact date matters to you.

PhilHealth states that relevant provisions of Circular No. 025, s. 2015 are repealed. Older articles that quote the 2015-era Maternity Care Package or NSD package amounts may therefore be outdated.

## Who Is Eligible?

- **Pregnant persons enrolled in the NHIP** are immediately eligible to use PhilHealth benefits under the Universal Health Care Act, per Circular 2026-0006. Eligibility is governed by Circular No. 2022-0013 (Granting of Immediate Eligibility to Filipino Citizens).
- **Pregnant persons below 21 and their infants** are eligible under existing PhilHealth membership and eligibility policies. The care coordinator is responsible for facilitating membership verification and, where applicable, enrollment of pregnant minors.
- **Self-paying members with unpaid contributions** can still use benefits, but under Circular 2026-0011 they are required to pay all missed contributions, including interest. This is separate from the maternity circular, so ask PhilHealth how it applies to you.

Many older articles say you need a certain number of paid months before delivery. The current maternity circular I reviewed does not impose such a requirement, so I have not included one.

## Step-by-Step: How to Prepare

1. **Make sure you have a PIN.** If you are not registered, see our guide on [PhilHealth registration documents]. You need a PMRF and valid proof of identity.
2. **Register with a YAKAP clinic.** The circular says pregnant persons "shall endeavor" to register and be empaneled to a YAKAP clinic. If your clinic is not accredited for maternity care, it refers you to an accredited prenatal care provider.
3. **Choose accredited providers.** Ask any clinic, birthing home, laboratory or hospital whether it is PhilHealth-accredited for the service before you book it. Provider information can change. Check the official PhilHealth provider directory before visiting.
4. **Start your Antenatal Care Record.** Your provider fills in your Antenatal Care Record (ANCR), including a **written birth plan**. The DOH Mother-Baby Book, Mother and Child Book, Rekord ni Nanay or a similar DOH record is treated as equivalent. Bring it to every visit. It is filled out in duplicate so that both you and the facility keep a copy.
5. **Give your last menstrual period (LMP) accurately.** The ANCR must have the LMP completely filled in because it is used to compute your age of gestation.
6. **Complete your prenatal visits.** The circular sets at least **eight** antenatal visits, following DOH guidelines: at least 1 in the first trimester, 2 in the second, and 5 in the third.
7. **Take your lab request form to an accredited laboratory.** Your prenatal provider prepares it. It must show the requesting facility, its accreditation number, your name and age, your PIN, the date, the list of tests and the signature and PRC license number of the attending health worker.
8. **Plan where you will deliver.** Your care coordinator helps you prepare the birth plan, including arrangements with an accredited birthing facility or hospital.
9. **Sign the forms.** You sign the Claim Signature Form and, where required, your care records, so the facility can claim.
10. **Attend postpartum visits and register your baby.** See below.

## What Is Covered Under the 2026 Outpatient and Birthing Facility Rules?

PhilHealth pays accredited facilities through benefit rates. You should not be charged for the essential services listed in the circular.

### Prenatal visits (clinical antenatal care)

Each visit includes consultation, history and examination, supplements (iron and folic acid or multiple micronutrient supplements, plus calcium in later trimesters), health education, and a written birth plan. Vital signs, fetal growth monitoring, deworming and tetanus vaccination are included according to the trimester and what is indicated.

Extra visits beyond the covered number may be charged by private prenatal providers, but the most a patient can be charged in co-payment is **₱900 per pregnancy**, according to the circular. This co-payment limit applies to private prenatal providers.

### Routine prenatal tests (diagnostic antenatal care)

These tests are bundled and, per the circular, should be at no cost to you. The laboratory must perform each set completely; otherwise the claim is not reimbursable.

| Test set | Contents | Benefit rate |
|---|---|---|
| First routine set | Pregnancy test, blood typing, complete blood count, urinalysis, hepatitis B screening, HIV screening, syphilis screening | ₱2,000 |
| Second routine set | Oral glucose tolerance test (once, at one of the two second-trimester visits) | ₱900 |
| Third routine set | Complete blood count and urinalysis (once, at one of the five third-trimester visits) | ₱500 |
| First-trimester ultrasound | Transvaginal ultrasound | ₱1,000 |
| Pelvic ultrasound | Claimed once, in the second or third trimester | ₱1,000 |

If your first checkup happens later in pregnancy, the circular allows several of these sets to be requested at the same visit, depending on your gestational age.

### Delivery in a birthing facility

A normal spontaneous delivery in a non-hospital birthing facility (for example, a DOH-licensed birthing home or lying-in clinic) has a benefit rate of **₱14,000** under the circular, covering labor, delivery and immediate postpartum care before discharge. Only **low-risk** normal deliveries are reimbursable at these facilities. First-time mothers can qualify as long as they have no other characteristics that make the pregnancy high-risk. A partograph, the chart that tracks labor, is required for the facility's claim.

If a complication arises during labor, the facility stabilizes you and arranges a transfer to a hospital. Emergency stabilization and step-up referral benefits apply only to emergencies detected during labor and delivery, not to conditions that existed before labor began.

### Postpartum care

Three postpartum visits are reimbursable, claimed at the end of the sixth week after delivery. Each visit includes supplements, family planning, lactation and nutrition guidance, and mental health screening and support. A Postpartum Care Record is filled in for the claim.

### Medicines

Prenatal and postpartum supplements are supposed to be dispensed by the facility. Alternatively, you can be referred to a YAKAP clinic for a prescription under PhilHealth GAMOT.

## Delivery in a Hospital

Hospital delivery, including cesarean section, is governed by other PhilHealth issuances. PhilHealth Circular No. 2026-0016 (published October 9, 2026) is titled *Quality Standards on Maternal Health Care: Antenatal Routine Laboratory Tests for Low Risk Pregnancy, Obstetric Sonography, Cesarean Section (CS) Delivery and Two-way Referral System*. I could not open the full text, so I cannot confirm its rules or amounts here. Ask your hospital's PhilHealth desk about the case rate that applies to you, and remember that PhilHealth coverage depends on the applicable benefit package and eligibility requirements. Inpatient benefits are generally paid to accredited facilities and deducted from the bill, not paid to you directly.

## After the Baby Is Born

- Newborns are automatically covered by the National Health Insurance Program. They receive their own PIN upon registration or claims processing.
- The provider should help assign your baby to a primary care or YAKAP provider.
- PhilHealth's newborn care benefits are to be issued in a separate circular, so this guide does not cover them.

## Examples

*These are illustrative examples, not official rulings.*

**Example 1: Early prenatal care.** A member is eight weeks pregnant. She visits her YAKAP clinic, which is not a maternity provider, so it refers her to an accredited prenatal clinic. The clinic starts her ANCR with a birth plan and gives her a lab request form. She takes the form to an accredited laboratory for the first routine set and a transvaginal ultrasound.

**Example 2: Late first visit.** A member first seeks prenatal care at 16 weeks. Under the circular's own example, the first routine tests, the second routine set and the pelvic ultrasound can be requested at that same visit.

## Common Problems

- **Incomplete records.** The facility's claim depends on your ANCR, so bring it every time and make sure each visit is filled in. A third-trimester claim with fewer than five visit dates needs a justification letter from the facility (for example, for preterm labor or an emergency).
- **Tests not done as a complete set.** The bundled lab set must be completed, or the claim is not reimbursable.
- **Provider not accredited.** Check accreditation before you go.
- **Charged for covered services.** The circular says the facility should not charge you for the essential listed services. Repeat or additional diagnostics may be charged from the second repeat onwards, and extra visits may be charged within the ₱900 co-payment limit at private prenatal providers.
- **Benefit claimed for a high-risk delivery at a birthing home.** Non-hospital birthing facilities are limited to low-risk normal deliveries.

## What To Do If Something Goes Wrong

- **If a facility says a covered service has a fee,** ask them to show the PhilHealth circular and the benefit code. If it is unresolved, contact PhilHealth.
- **If you are not sure your membership is active,** check your record or ask your LHIO.
- **If you experience warning signs in pregnancy or labor,** go to the nearest health facility or emergency service right away. PhilHealth benefit rules are not a reason to delay care.
- **To contact PhilHealth,** the Corporate Action Center is listed as 02 8662 2588 in PhilHealth's 2026 circular guides. Numbers can change, so confirm on philhealth.gov.ph.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What are the PhilHealth requirements for a pregnant woman?
Be enrolled in PhilHealth with a PIN, use accredited providers, and keep your antenatal care record complete. Under the 2026 maternity circular, enrolled pregnant persons are immediately eligible.

### Do I need to have paid contributions for a certain number of months?
The 2026 maternity circular I reviewed says enrolled pregnant persons are immediately eligible and does not state a contribution waiting period. Self-paying members with missed contributions can still use benefits but must pay the missed amounts with interest under Circular 2026-0011.

### Are prenatal checkups covered?
Yes, at accredited facilities. The circular sets at least eight visits and lists the services included in each visit.

### Are prenatal lab tests and ultrasound covered?
Yes. The circular covers routine bundled tests and two ultrasounds, and says they should be at no cost to you when done at accredited facilities.

### Can a first-time mother deliver in a birthing home?
Under the circular, a first-time mother's delivery is reimbursable at a non-hospital facility if she has no other characteristic that makes the pregnancy high-risk. DOH guidelines on who may deliver in birthing facilities apply.

### Does PhilHealth pay my whole hospital bill?
The sources I reviewed do not support such a claim. Benefits are paid through benefit rates or case rates and applied according to the rules for each benefit.

### Is my baby covered automatically?
Yes. The circular says newborns are automatically covered by the National Health Insurance Program and receive their own PIN.

### Is a pregnant minor covered?
Pregnant persons below 21 and their infants are eligible under existing PhilHealth policies, and the care coordinator helps with enrollment of pregnant minors where applicable.

### Does this article replace medical advice?
No. It explains PhilHealth benefit rules only. Ask your doctor, midwife or nurse about your care.
