# PhilHealth Surgery Coverage 2026: How the Case Rate Works and What To Check

In 2026, PhilHealth pays for surgery mostly through **procedure case rates**: a fixed amount per operation that is split into a hospital (facility) fee and a doctor's professional fee, paid to an accredited facility and deducted from your bill. A smaller group of major operations is covered by **Z benefits**, which are larger packages for specific conditions. Neither is a promise that PhilHealth pays your entire bill, so the most useful thing you can do is find out the exact amount for your procedure before admission and confirm the deduction before you pay.

This guide is for PhilHealth members and dependents who are planning surgery, or checking a bill after one. It explains PhilHealth's rules, not medical advice. Whether you need an operation, and which one, is a decision for your doctor.

## Quick Answer

| Situation | Benefit that usually applies | How it is paid |
|---|---|---|
| Surgery during a hospital admission | Procedure case rate under All Case Rates (ACR) | Fixed amount to the accredited hospital, deducted from your bill |
| Elective operation where you go home the same day | Day surgery case rate | Same as above, at accredited hospitals or ambulatory surgical clinics |
| Certain major operations (for example selected heart surgeries, kidney transplant, some cancer treatment, some orthopedic implants) | Z benefit package | Larger package at a contracted facility, with eligibility criteria and a signed Member Empowerment form |
| Surgery after an ER visit where admission orders were issued | Inpatient case rate | Admission order within 24 hours decides, not where you wait |
| Procedure done in the ER that already has a case rate | Procedure case rate | Applies regardless of entry point |
| Maternal and gynecologic surgery in a hospital | Rules in PhilHealth's 2026 maternal case rate circular | See our maternity guide |

## What Is a PhilHealth Procedure Case Rate?

PhilHealth defines a **case rate** as a fixed amount it reimburses for a specific illness or case. For inpatient and day-surgery care it is paid to accredited health facilities through **All Case Rates (ACR)**, and the amount is deducted from the member's total bill, which includes the professional fees of attending physicians, before discharge. PhilHealth's benefits page says the case rate is inclusive of hospital charges and the attending physician's professional fees.

A few terms help here:

- **Procedure case rate:** the case rate category for surgical or procedural interventions, listed by **RVS code** (relative value scale code). Medical conditions such as pneumonia have a separate list of *medical* case rates.
- **Health facility fee and professional fee:** each procedure case rate in PhilHealth's Annex B is split into these two parts. The facility fee goes to the hospital for charges such as room and board, the operating room, supplies and medicines. The professional fee goes to the doctors.
- **First case rate and second case rate:** a patient's main condition or procedure is the first case rate. PhilHealth allows a second case rate only for a listed set of conditions and procedures (Annex D of Circular 2024-0037).
- **Accredited facility:** a hospital or clinic accredited by PhilHealth. PhilHealth publishes the accredited list on its website.

Use PhilHealth's **Case Rates Search** (announced by PhilHealth at philhealth.gov.ph/services/acr/) or ask the hospital's PhilHealth coordinator for the case rate, facility fee and professional fee for your procedure code.

## What Changed: The 50% Increase

PhilHealth Circular 2024-0037, "Fifty Percent (50%) Adjustment of Select Case Rates", raised the case rates of most medical and procedure packages by 50 percent. It applies to hospital admissions starting **January 1, 2025**, and the circular says the Annexes list the medical and procedure case rates, a list of excluded benefits, and rates for primary care facilities such as infirmaries and dispensaries. Reports at the time put the number of affected packages at about 9,000 and noted that Z benefit packages that were being re-costed, newly approved packages starting in 2023, and other specific cases were excluded from the increase.

So if you see an older figure for the same operation, check whether it predates January 2025. Also check whether a newer circular has since replaced the rate for your procedure. For example, a 2026 circular revised case rates for selected maternal and gynecologic services in the hospital setting.

## Rules That Changed Around Surgical Admissions

Two older limits were removed, which matters if you need a long stay or a repeat admission:

- **The 45-day benefit limit** (a maximum of 45 days of confinement per year) was lifted by PhilHealth Circular 2025-0007. A June 2025 advisory says it took effect on April 4, 2025. It does not cover hemodialysis, which stays at 156 sessions a year.
- **The Single Period of Confinement rule**, under which readmissions for the same illness or procedure within 90 days were paid only once, was lifted. PhilHealth Circular 2025-0015 (Revision 1) extends the lifting to certain denied claims with readmissions since February 20, 2019.

Some pages on PhilHealth's own benefits page still mention the 45-day limit and the Single Period of Confinement rule for outpatient radiotherapy and similar benefits. Those mentions appear to predate the 2025 circulars. If a hospital cites either rule to you, ask which current circular it relies on.

## Day Surgery

PhilHealth defines **day surgery** (ambulatory or outpatient surgery) as elective, non-emergency operations, from minor to major, where the patient goes home the same day. Payment is made to the accredited facility through All Case Rates, the amount is deducted from the total bill including professional fees, and the case rate is inclusive of hospital charges and the attending physician's fees. PhilHealth lists accredited **ambulatory surgical clinics** as where this is available. The documents it names are a copy of the Member Data Record and a duly accomplished PhilHealth Claim Form 1.

## Z Benefits for Major Operations

**Z benefits** are separate packages for catastrophic or high-cost conditions. They apply only to patients who meet the selection criteria and who are treated at facilities PhilHealth has contracted for the package. Most require a signed Member Empowerment (ME) form. The criteria are decided by your treating doctors and PhilHealth, not by this guide.

## Who Is Eligible?

PhilHealth's inpatient benefits page names two documents for inpatient claims: a copy of the Member Data Record or the PhilHealth Benefit Eligibility Form (PBEF), and PhilHealth Claim Form 1. PhilHealth's 2025 emergency care circulars say all Filipino citizens registered with PhilHealth are eligible with immediate eligibility under Circular 2022-0013. We did not find an official statement in the documents we reviewed that spells out contribution conditions for scheduled surgery, so confirm your status through the Member Portal or your Local Health Insurance Office (LHIO) before admission.

PhilHealth only pays accredited facilities. Do not assume a hospital, clinic or surgeon is accredited. Check PhilHealth's accredited facility list, which the benefits page links to.

## No Balance Billing: When You Should Pay Nothing Extra

**No Balance Billing (NBB)** means qualified patients are not charged anything above the case rate. PhilHealth's NBB circular (2017, Revision 2) covers members and dependents who are indigent, sponsored, domestic workers (kasambahay), senior citizens aged 60 and above, or lifetime members, for benefits including All Case Rates and Z benefits. Under that circular:

- NBB applies to **ward-type accommodation** in the covered facilities. It does not apply if the patient chooses a private room or transfers to one, and facilities cannot make patients sign a waiver of NBB.
- If no ward bed is available, the provider must make the next higher accommodation available at no added cost.
- Once a patient is in a ward, NBB applies whether the patient is attended by a private consultant or a salaried physician.
- Drugs, supplies (the circular names implants, plates, screws and pins), diagnostics, blood products and professional fees are charged against the case rate, and patients should not be asked to buy them or to replace consumed items.
- ER, outpatient and observation-room services immediately before admission in the same facility are covered by NBB and charged against the case rate.

This is a 2017 circular. PhilHealth may have issued newer rules, so confirm with PhilHealth or the hospital's PhilHealth coordinator which NBB rules apply now. Members who do not qualify for NBB, or who choose a private room, may be billed amounts above the case rate. The documents we reviewed do not set what a surgeon may charge above the professional fee portion in those cases.

## Surgery That Starts in the ER

PhilHealth's July 2026 advisory on ER cases says that if admission orders are issued within 24 hours, the applicable inpatient case rate applies, and the controlling factor is the admission order, not where the patient is physically waiting. A procedure done at the ER that has an existing case rate is paid under the procedure case rate regardless of entry point. See our emergency room coverage guide for the full 24-hour rule.

## Step-by-Step: Before, During and After Your Operation

1. **Confirm membership.** Check your PhilHealth record through the Member Portal or an LHIO. Have your PIN or Member Data Record ready.
2. **Confirm the facility and surgeon.** Check that the hospital or clinic is on PhilHealth's accredited list. For a Z benefit, check that the facility is a contracted Z benefit facility.
3. **Ask for the procedure code and the case rate.** Ask the hospital's PhilHealth coordinator which RVS code will be used and what the case rate, facility fee and professional fee are, or look it up in the Case Rates Search.
4. **Ask whether a Z benefit or second case rate may apply.** Both depend on listed criteria and lists, and your doctors and the hospital determine them.
5. **Ask about the room.** Ask how your room choice affects what you pay, and whether you qualify for No Balance Billing.
6. **Submit the documents the hospital asks for.** PhilHealth's inpatient benefits page names the Member Data Record or PBEF and Claim Form 1. Day surgery needs the Member Data Record and Claim Form 1.
7. **Check the statement of account before paying.** The case rate should appear as a deduction before discharge. If the hospital says it will "reimburse you later", ask for the rule it is relying on. PhilHealth's NBB circular says patients should not be asked to pay with the promise of reimbursement.
8. **Keep your documents:** itemized statement of account, receipts, discharge papers and claim forms.

## What Will It Cost You?

- **Within the case rate:** PhilHealth's payment to the facility covers the facility and professional fee components. Whether you pay anything more depends on your NBB status, your accommodation, the services you used and the facility's charges.
- **Above the case rate:** the case rate is a fixed amount, not a percentage of your bill. Websites that say PhilHealth covers "50 to 80 percent" or "20 to 40 percent" of a bill are not citing a PhilHealth rule.
- **Mandatory discounts:** hospitals must follow laws on senior citizen and PWD discounts.
- **Private insurance or HMO:** PhilHealth's July 2026 advisory encourages members with private cover to ask the hospital to coordinate both.

## Examples

*These are illustrations, not real cases or medical advice.*

**Example 1: scheduled inpatient surgery.** A member plans an operation at an accredited hospital. Before admission, the member asks the PhilHealth coordinator for the procedure code and the case rate. At discharge, the statement of account shows the case rate deducted, and the member pays the balance for anything not covered, such as private room charges.

**Example 2: day surgery.** A member has an elective procedure at an accredited ambulatory surgical clinic and goes home the same day. The clinic deducts the day surgery case rate from the bill, using the Member Data Record and Claim Form 1.

**Example 3: a Z benefit.** A patient's doctors determine that a Z benefit package may apply. The patient signs the Member Empowerment form, is treated at a contracted facility, and the package amount is applied if the criteria are met.

## Common Problems

- **The hospital quotes a different case rate than the one you found.** Rates changed in January 2025, and some were revised again by later circulars. Ask for the circular and RVS code the hospital used.
- **The case rate is not deducted from your bill.** Raise it with the hospital's PhilHealth coordinator or P-CARES before paying. PhilHealth's 2026 advisory for ER cases says failure to deduct at the point of care does not remove the right to the benefit, and describes filing for reimbursement at an LHIO within the prescribed period. That advisory is about the ER benefit. Ask PhilHealth what period applies to your claim.
- **You were told you have used your 45 days.** The 45-day limit was lifted in 2025.
- **You were told a readmission will not be paid because it was within 90 days.** The Single Period of Confinement rule was lifted.
- **The facility is not accredited.** PhilHealth pays accredited facilities. Check before admission, not after.
- **You are asked to buy implants, medicines or blood.** For NBB-eligible patients, PhilHealth's NBB circular says these are charged against the case rate. If you are not NBB-eligible, ask the hospital to explain in writing which items are included in the case rate.

## What To Do If Something Goes Wrong

1. Ask the hospital's PhilHealth coordinator or **P-CARES** representative to explain the deduction and the rule used.
2. Keep every document, including the statement of account.
3. Report to your LHIO or the **PhilHealth Action Center**. PhilHealth's July 2026 advisory lists the hotline (02) 8662-2588, mobile hotlines Smart 0998-857-2957 and 0968-865-4670, Globe 0917-127-5987 and 0917-110-9812, and email actioncenter@philhealth.gov.ph. Confirm current contact details on philhealth.gov.ph.
4. For NBB violations, PhilHealth's NBB circular names P-CARES in the facility and the Action Center as the places to report.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How much does PhilHealth cover for surgery?**
It depends on the procedure. PhilHealth pays a fixed case rate for the procedure code, split into a facility fee and a professional fee, or a Z benefit amount for eligible major operations. It is not a percentage of your bill.

**Is the surgeon's fee included?**
Yes, the case rate includes a professional fee component, and the amount deducted from your bill includes the professional fees of attending physicians, per PhilHealth's benefits page. What a doctor may charge beyond that for patients who are not under No Balance Billing is not set out in the documents we reviewed.

**Did the 50% increase apply to all surgeries?**
It applied to select case rates, including most procedure case rates, for admissions starting January 1, 2025. Certain packages were excluded, and later circulars have revised some rates.

**Does PhilHealth cover day surgery?**
Yes, through All Case Rates at accredited hospitals and ambulatory surgical clinics, for elective operations where you go home the same day.

**Does PhilHealth cover implants?**
Selected orthopedic implants are listed as Z benefit packages with specific criteria. For other operations, implants are part of the case rate's facility charges and not a separate payment, according to the NBB circular for NBB-eligible patients.

**Can I use PhilHealth in a private hospital?**
PhilHealth pays accredited facilities, public or private. NBB in private hospitals applies only where the hospital has an agreement with PhilHealth to implement it.

**Is there still a 45-day limit?**
No. PhilHealth lifted it effective April 4, 2025, except for hemodialysis.

**What if I am readmitted for the same condition?**
The Single Period of Confinement rule has been lifted, so readmissions within 90 days are no longer paid only once, according to PhilHealth's circulars.

**What are Z benefits?**
Packages for specific catastrophic conditions and procedures, with selection criteria, at contracted facilities.

**Does PhilHealth cover cosmetic surgery?**
The documents we reviewed do not address cosmetic surgery as a category. Ask your hospital whether your procedure is an admissible case. PhilHealth says only admissible cases are reimbursed.
