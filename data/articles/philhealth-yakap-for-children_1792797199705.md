# PhilHealth YAKAP for Children: How Parents Register a Child and What to Expect (2026)

A child can use PhilHealth YAKAP if the child is a PhilHealth beneficiary, meaning a member or a qualified dependent, and has been registered with a YAKAP Clinic. For a minor (under 18), a parent or legal guardian must do all the official steps: registration, clinic selection, and signing the clinic agreement.

YAKAP (Yaman ng Kalusugan Program) is PhilHealth's outpatient primary care benefit. It brings together check-ups, laboratory tests, referral for select cancer screening, and outpatient medicines through a benefit called GAMOT. This guide is for parents, guardians, and caregivers, and reflects PhilHealth issuances available as of October 24, 2026. It is not medical advice: your child's doctor decides what care or medicines your child needs.

## Quick Answer

| Question | Short answer |
|---|---|
| Can children use YAKAP? | Yes, as PhilHealth beneficiaries (members or qualified dependents). PhilHealth's selection rules cover all Filipinos. |
| Who counts as a qualified child dependent? | Children below 21 who are unmarried and unemployed, plus certain other categories (see below). The parent must declare them in the Member Data Record (MDR). |
| Who registers a minor? | A parent or legal guardian does all official transactions for a child under 18. |
| Can I choose a different clinic for my child? | Yes. Clinic selection is done per beneficiary, including each dependent. |
| What does it cost? | PhilHealth's advisory says nothing in public YAKAP clinics and a ₱900 yearly cap in private clinics, worded "per member." See the cost section for what is unclear. |
| What about medicines? | GAMOT has a ₱20,000 annual benefit limit, used with a prescription from an authorized physician. |

## What Is PhilHealth YAKAP?

YAKAP is the evolution of the older PhilHealth Konsulta program. It aims to keep people well through regular primary care, instead of waiting for illness to reach the hospital. PhilHealth's 2026 circular says YAKAP integrates:

- primary care services (health assessment, consultations, follow-ups),
- the PhilHealth GAMOT outpatient medicines benefit, and
- select cancer screening tests.

You may still see "Konsulta" on older forms. The current program name is YAKAP.

As of May 15, 2026, PhilHealth reported 4,287 accredited YAKAP Clinics nationwide, reaching 33.2 million Filipinos.

YAKAP is outpatient care. It is separate from PhilHealth's hospital benefits, which are generally paid to accredited health facilities and deducted from the bill under applicable rules.

## Who Is Eligible? Child Dependents Explained

PhilHealth defines a YAKAP beneficiary as a member or a qualified dependent. According to PhilHealth's qualified dependents page, these children can be dependents without extra premiums:

| Child | Rule |
|---|---|
| Legitimate, legitimated, acknowledged or illegitimate (as shown on the birth certificate), adopted children, and stepchildren | Below 21 years old, unmarried and unemployed |
| Children 21 or older | Only if they have a congenital or acquired disability that makes them totally dependent on the member, as determined by PhilHealth |
| Foster children | As defined under the Foster Care Act of 2012 (RA 10165) |

One condition matters a lot: **dependents must be declared by the principal member and listed in the member's MDR** for hassle-free availment. If your child is not on your MDR, update your records first. PhilHealth identifies parent-child relationships by the birth certificate. The official page I reviewed does not list the exact papers an office will ask for when adding a dependent, so ask your PhilHealth Local Health Insurance Office (LHIO) what to bring.

PhilHealth has also issued Circular 2026-0015 on coverage of solo parents and their children or dependents, and Circular 2026-0017 on persons with disability. If either applies to your family, read the circular or ask your LHIO, because they may affect how your child is covered.

## Who Does What: Parents, Guardians and Older Dependents

PhilHealth Circular 2025-0017 sets the rule:

- **Beneficiaries who are minors (under 18):** all official transactions with PhilHealth, including registration, clinic selection and signing the clinic agreement (the Mutual Care Agreement), must be done by a parent or legal guardian.
- **Beneficiaries 18 and above:** they may register, choose a clinic, sign agreements and deal with PhilHealth directly, without parent or guardian consent. This can apply to a dependent aged 18 to 20.

## Step-by-Step: Register Your Child for YAKAP

PhilHealth's process has four steps: registration, selection, First Patient Encounter, and empanelment.

### Step 1: Make sure your child is a declared dependent

Check that your child is listed in your MDR. If not, update your membership record through your LHIO.

### Step 2: Choose a YAKAP Clinic for your child

You can choose through any of these channels:

- **eGovPH app:** Go to PhilHealth services, open the YAKAP tab, and choose "Add YAKAP Clinic" for a member or dependent.
- **PhilHealth Member Portal:** Log in with your PIN, scroll to your list of dependents, find the YAKAP Clinic column next to each child's date of birth, and select a clinic. PhilHealth's steps say to repeat this for each dependent.
- **Any PhilHealth office:** Staff can help through assisted selection, which includes an identity check and a Primary Care Selection Form.
- **At a YAKAP Clinic:** The clinic can help you register and choose it through the eGovPH app or Member Portal.

Because selection is done per dependent in the Member Portal, your child's clinic does not have to be the same clinic as yours. Check that a clinic is accredited before choosing. PhilHealth publishes its list of accredited YAKAP Clinics on its website. Provider information can change, so check the official PhilHealth provider directory before visiting.

**Schools and community groups:** PhilHealth's rules also allow settings-based selection for learning institutions. A school coordinates with PhilHealth, and the school head certifies that proper orientation was held and that consent was obtained, including from parents or guardians of minor learners.

### Step 3: Attend the First Patient Encounter (FPE)

Clinics must check daily for new beneficiaries who selected them and schedule an FPE. The FPE is the first contact of the year where the clinic records or updates basic health data to identify health risks. PhilHealth states it is **not yet a consultation**.

### Step 4: The parent or guardian signs the YAKAP Empanelment Slip

After the FPE, the Mutual Care Agreement (also called the YAKAP Empanelment Slip) is signed. For a minor, this is the parent or guardian. Your child is then "empaneled," meaning the clinic becomes your child's regular primary care provider until December 31 of that year. The slip is signed again each year.

Clinics verify identity before the FPE and before each consultation using PhilHealth's identity tool. That tool only processes people registered in PhilSys (the national ID system). If a beneficiary is not PhilSys-registered, PhilHealth's rules say a valid government-issued ID or alternative documents are needed for manual validation. The official text does not say which documents are accepted for a young child, so ask the clinic or LHIO before the visit.

## What YAKAP Provides

PhilHealth describes YAKAP as covering free consultations, essential laboratory and diagnostic services, GAMOT medicines, and outpatient cancer screening. PhilHealth coverage depends on the applicable benefit package and eligibility requirements.

At minimum, YAKAP Clinics must provide the FPE, empanelment, initial assessment and risk profiling, primary care consultations, laboratory tests and medicines, referrals (for example to oral health care, maternity care or TB treatment), and record keeping. The clinic acts as your child's navigator: other facilities are expected to refer the patient back to the clinic.

The official sources I reviewed do not describe child-specific packages such as vaccinations or growth monitoring under YAKAP, or a list of laboratory tests for children, so this guide does not make claims about them. Ask your clinic what it offers.

## Costs

PhilHealth's member advisory (Advisory 2026-0003, signed January 8, 2026) says:

| Clinic type | What you may be charged |
|---|---|
| Public YAKAP clinic | Nothing. Covered consultations, basic laboratory services and other included primary care services must be free. |
| Private YAKAP clinic | Co-payment of up to ₱900 per member per year. After reaching ₱900, remaining covered and necessary YAKAP services for the year should be provided at no extra cost. |

**What is unclear:** the advisory says "per member." The official sources I reviewed do not spell out whether each dependent child has a separate ₱900 limit. Ask the private clinic how it counts this for your child, and keep your receipts.

The advisory also says you should not be denied primary care services at your empaneled clinic after completing the FPE and signing. Clinics also may not reject a beneficiary who chose them unless they have reached the number of beneficiaries they declared they can accommodate.

## GAMOT Medicines for Children

GAMOT (Guaranteed and Accessible Medications for Outpatient Treatment) is the medicines part of YAKAP. Rules from PhilHealth Circular 2025-0013:

- **Annual benefit limit:** ₱20,000, with no out-of-pocket payment until it is used up. PhilHealth tracks consumption using the beneficiary's PIN. Each GAMOT Availment Slip shows the remaining balance, so ask for it and check your child's balance. News reports describe the limit as applying to each member and dependent; check with your clinic.
- **Resets every January 1**, with no carryover.
- **Prescription required:** An authorized physician issues it through the GAMOT App, with a Unique Prescription Security Code (UPSC). The physician confirms identity using the PIN or a valid government-issued ID.
- **Where to claim:** Any accredited GAMOT facility, with the prescription and a government-issued ID.
- **Claiming windows:** Anti-infectives within 2 days of the prescription date, and other medicines within 2 weeks. After that you pay out of pocket, and the doctor should update the prescription if the medicine is still needed.
- **Prescription validity:** Cancelled automatically after 90 days.
- **Anti-infective medicines** are always dispensed in full within the claiming period.

The GAMOT List includes liquid forms such as syrups, suspensions and drops for some medicines, alongside tablets and capsules. Whether any medicine suits your child is a medical decision for the doctor.

**If a representative picks up the medicines:** the circular allows this with an authorization letter, the UPSC, and valid government-issued IDs of the representative and the beneficiary. The official text does not address minors separately, so ask the pharmacy what ID it will accept for a child.

**What GAMOT does not cover:** medicines for hospital confinement or outpatient emergency care; branded medicines priced above the fixed fee (unless dispensed at the fixed fee); medicines covered by other outpatient benefits; and medicines not on the GAMOT List.

Under Circular 2026-0007, prescriptions must be for the patient's active or current condition. Standby or "just in case" prescriptions are not eligible for GAMOT claims, and oral antibiotics cannot be prescribed for more than 14 days per prescription.

The original GAMOT List had 75 medicines. PhilHealth said revisions are issued through supplemental circulars (for example, Circular 2026-0018 on anti-TB drugs under GAMOT), and PhilHealth confirmed in a January 2026 advisory that 21 essential medicines remain available at YAKAP-accredited clinics throughout 2026. Check the current list.

## Examples

*These are illustrative examples, not real cases.*

**Example 1: A 6-year-old.** A parent already has a PIN. She checks the Member Portal dependents list, finds her son's name, and selects a clinic near his school in the YAKAP Clinic column. The clinic schedules the FPE, and she signs the empanelment slip for him.

**Example 2: A 19-year-old dependent.** A college student who is still an unmarried, unemployed dependent is 18 or older, so he can choose his own clinic and sign for himself, without a parent's consent.

**Example 3: Two clinics.** A parent works in one city and selects a clinic near her office. Her daughter is selected to a clinic near home, since clinic choice is done per dependent.

## Common Problems

**"My child isn't in the dependents list."** The member must declare dependents in the MDR. Update your record at the LHIO.

**"My child has no ID or PhilSys record."** The identity tool needs PhilSys; otherwise a valid government ID or alternative documents are needed. PhilHealth's text does not list which documents for young children, so ask the clinic or LHIO.

**"We chose a clinic but never went."** Beneficiaries who selected a clinic but did not complete an FPE are not retained for the next year and must select again.

**"I want to change clinics."** Empaneled beneficiaries can only transfer in the fourth quarter (October to December), effective January. Exceptions apply if the clinic closes, has its accreditation revoked, suspended or withdrawn, or gets a DOH cease and desist order. If you selected a clinic but your child has not had the FPE, you can transfer any time through the nearest LHIO.

**"The clinic can't give the medicines."** News reports in 2026 said some clinics needed time to finish administrative requirements before issuing prescriptions. GAMOT prescriptions can be claimed at any GAMOT facility. A facility's refusal to serve a beneficiary is grounds for investigation.

## What to Do If Something Goes Wrong

1. **Charged in a public clinic, or above ₱900 in a private clinic?** Politely cite Advisory 2026-0003, then report it.
2. **Denied service after the FPE and signing?** Report it.
3. **A pharmacy refused to serve you?** Report it through PhilHealth's official channels.

PhilHealth's 24/7 Corporate Action Center: (02) 8662-2588 (also listed as (02) 866-225-88). Text lines: Smart 0998-857-2957 or 0968-865-4670, Globe 0917-127-5987 or 0917-110-9812. Email: actioncenter@philhealth.gov.ph or yakaptugon@philhealth.gov.ph.

When reporting, give your PIN, your child's name, the clinic name, dates, amount charged, and any receipts or GAMOT Availment Slip.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is PhilHealth YAKAP free for children?

In public YAKAP clinics, covered primary care services must be free. In private clinics, PhilHealth says co-payment is capped at ₱900 per member per year, and the official sources don't make clear how this applies to each dependent. GAMOT medicines are free up to the annual limit.

### Can a child be registered without a parent?

Under PhilHealth's rules, minors cannot transact on their own. A parent or legal guardian must do the registration, clinic selection and signing. Dependents 18 and above can do these themselves.

### Until what age can my child be my dependent?

Unmarried, unemployed children below 21 qualify. Children 21 or older qualify only with a disability that makes them totally dependent, as determined by PhilHealth.

### Does my child need to use my clinic?

No. In PhilHealth's Member Portal steps, clinic selection is done separately for each dependent.

### Is YAKAP the same as Konsulta?

YAKAP is an evolution of Konsulta, redesigned with expanded outpatient diagnostics and medicines.

### Does YAKAP cover hospital stays?

No. YAKAP is outpatient primary care. Hospital benefits are a separate part of PhilHealth coverage.
