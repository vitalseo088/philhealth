# PhilHealth Pneumonia Coverage 2026: What It Pays and What You Still May Owe

PhilHealth pays for pneumonia admissions through a fixed amount called a **case rate**. Based on PhilHealth's own announcements, the rate is **₱29,250 for moderate-risk pneumonia** and **₱90,100 for high-risk pneumonia**. Both apply to hospital admissions that started on or after the dates explained below.

This guide is for patients and families who are, or may soon be, dealing with a hospital admission for pneumonia in 2026. It explains what the rates mean, how they are applied to a hospital bill, and what to ask the hospital. It is not medical advice, and it cannot tell you whether a particular patient needs admission or which risk level applies. Your doctor decides that.

## Quick Answer

| Pneumonia type | Case rate | Previous rate | Applies to admissions starting |
|---|---|---|---|
| Moderate-risk pneumonia | ₱29,250 | ₱19,500 | January 1, 2025 |
| High-risk pneumonia | ₱90,100 | ₱32,000 | December 16, 2023 |

A few things to keep in mind:

- The case rate is a **fixed payment**, not a promise that the whole bill is paid.
- The risk level (moderate or high) is set by the attending doctor's diagnosis and the hospital's coding, not by the patient.
- We did not find a later official PhilHealth issuance that changes these two pneumonia rates, but PhilHealth rules change by circular. Confirm with the hospital's PhilHealth desk at admission.

## What Is a PhilHealth Case Rate?

A case rate is a pre-set peso amount PhilHealth pays for a specific diagnosis or procedure. PhilHealth moved to this system, called **All Case Rates**, so that hospitals are paid a fixed amount per treated case instead of being reimbursed per service.

For an admission, the case rate is generally paid to the accredited hospital and deducted from the patient's bill. You do not normally pay the full amount first and wait for a refund, though how this works for your admission depends on the hospital and your membership status.

The case rate is split into two parts:

- **Health facility fee:** covers the hospital's costs such as room, medicines and laboratory work.
- **Professional fee:** covers the doctors' fees.

PhilHealth's published case rate annex for the ₱29,250 pneumonia entries shows a facility fee of ₱20,475 and a professional fee of ₱8,775. The exact split for your claim depends on the diagnosis code the hospital uses.

## PhilHealth Pneumonia Coverage by Risk Level

### Moderate-risk pneumonia: ₱29,250

PhilHealth raised most case rates by 50% for admissions starting January 1, 2025, under PhilHealth Circular No. 2024-0037. PhilHealth's own January 2025 announcement lists moderate-risk pneumonia as moving from ₱19,500 to ₱29,250.

News reports have described moderate-risk pneumonia as the most claimed PhilHealth benefit package. The rate is a fixed amount tied to the diagnosis, not to the number of days stayed, subject to the claim rules below.

### High-risk pneumonia: ₱90,100

High-risk pneumonia was handled separately. It was not part of the 50% increase because it had already been "rationalized" (re-costed) earlier. PhilHealth Circular No. 2023-0027, the implementing guidelines for high-risk pneumonia, applies to members and dependents admitted starting December 16, 2023. PhilHealth's own 2025 benefits material lists the change as ₱32,000 to ₱90,100.

High-risk pneumonia has an extra claim rule on length of stay, covered next.

## Rules That Can Affect Whether a Claim Is Paid

### Length of stay for high-risk pneumonia

PhilHealth Advisory No. 2025-0005 (signed January 23, 2025) says PhilHealth will not reimburse high-risk pneumonia admissions of **two days or less** when the patient's disposition on Claim Form 2 is "improved" at discharge. Those claims will be denied.

The same advisory says that for other medical conditions outside the scope of the circulars it names, length of stay is no longer the basis for reimbursement, as provided in PhilHealth Circular No. 2017-0028. That circular's policy applies to pneumonia claims generally. This is why older articles that mention a minimum of four days of confinement for pneumonia are out of date.

### Treatment duration is not a billing requirement

The same advisory explains that the recommended five-day treatment duration for low to moderate adult community-acquired pneumonia (from PhilHealth Circular No. 2024-0027) is a quality-of-care statement meant to prevent under-treatment. It is **not** a requirement for reimbursement.

### Admissions that end in under 24 hours

A news report from September 2026 describes PhilHealth Circular No. 2026-0013, which expands payment under existing inpatient case rates for qualified admissions that end before 24 hours because of death or transfer. Per the report, it creates no new benefit or case rate, and patients discharged as improved, recovered, or against medical advice are not covered by that circular. We could not confirm the circular's full text on philhealth.gov.ph, so ask PhilHealth or the hospital if this applies to your situation.

## Will PhilHealth Pay the Whole Hospital Bill?

Not necessarily. The case rate is a fixed amount. If the actual bill is higher, the difference is the patient's responsibility unless another rule applies.
Two policies can reduce what a patient pays:

- **No co-payment policy.** PhilHealth has called on partner hospitals to strictly follow the no co-payment policy based on Section 9 of the Universal Health Care Act and related PhilHealth circulars. In PhilHealth's words, members should not be charged additional fees beyond what PhilHealth covers for the standard of care and services included in the benefit packages.
- **No Balance Billing (NBB).** PhilHealth's 2025 benefits material describes NBB as meaning no other fees or expenses are charged to the patient beyond PhilHealth's package rate, and says it covers only confinements in basic or ward accommodations. The available sources we reviewed do not fully list which member groups qualify, so confirm your eligibility with PhilHealth.

If you choose a private room or request services beyond the standard of care in the package, expect charges outside the case rate.

## Who Is Eligible?

Case rates apply to PhilHealth members and their qualified dependents admitted to PhilHealth-accredited facilities. The sources we reviewed do not clearly state the current contribution requirements for inpatient claims, so we are not listing a specific number of paid months. Ask PhilHealth or check your Member Data Record and contribution status before admission when possible.

The hospital must also be **PhilHealth-accredited**. Confirm this with the hospital's PhilHealth desk or PhilHealth's official provider information.

## What To Do at Admission and Discharge

1. **Tell the admitting desk you are a PhilHealth member** and bring your PhilHealth Identification Number (PIN) or Member Data Record (MDR).
2. **Ask which case rate applies.** Ask the hospital's PhilHealth officer how the diagnosis will be coded and whether it is moderate-risk or high-risk pneumonia.
3. **Ask about room type.** Ask whether your accommodation affects the No Balance Billing policy.
4. **Request an itemized statement of account** at discharge showing the PhilHealth deduction.
5. **Check the deduction** against the case rate that applies to your admission date.
6. **Keep copies** of your admission and discharge papers and the billing statement.

## Common Problems

- **The deducted amount is lower than expected.** Rates differ by admission date and diagnosis code. The older figures (₱15,000, ₱19,500 and ₱32,000) still appear on some websites but are out of date for admissions starting on the dates above.
- **The hospital charges extra even though you are a PhilHealth member.** Ask whether the services were part of the standard package and whether the room type affects the policy. PhilHealth has publicly asked hospitals to follow the no co-payment policy.
- **A high-risk pneumonia claim is denied after a short stay.** Under Advisory No. 2025-0005, a stay of two days or less with an "improved" disposition is not reimbursed for high-risk pneumonia.
- **Your diagnosis is coded differently.** The case rate is set by the diagnosis recorded in the medical record and claim form, so the amount may differ if the final diagnosis is something other than pneumonia.

## What To Do If Something Goes Wrong

- Ask the hospital's billing office for a written explanation of the charges and the PhilHealth deduction.
- Contact PhilHealth through its 24/7 touch points. A January 2025 PhilHealth announcement listed **(02) 866-225-88** and the email **actioncenter@philhealth.gov.ph**. Contact details can change, so check philhealth.gov.ph for the current ones.
- Keep your records in case you need to file a complaint or request a review.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How much does PhilHealth cover for pneumonia in 2026?
Moderate-risk pneumonia has a case rate of ₱29,250 for admissions starting January 1, 2025. High-risk pneumonia has a case rate of ₱90,100 for admissions starting December 16, 2023. We found no later official change to either rate, but verify with PhilHealth.

### Does PhilHealth pay the entire hospital bill for pneumonia?
No. PhilHealth pays a fixed case rate. Any difference between the bill and the case rate may be the patient's responsibility, unless a policy such as No Balance Billing applies.

### What is the difference between moderate-risk and high-risk pneumonia for PhilHealth?
PhilHealth treats them as separate benefit packages with different case rates and different claim rules. The risk classification is made by the attending doctor, so ask the hospital how your admission is classified.

### Is there a minimum number of days in the hospital?
For high-risk pneumonia, PhilHealth will not reimburse admissions of two days or less with an "improved" disposition (Advisory No. 2025-0005). For other pneumonia admissions, length of stay is no longer a basis for reimbursement under Circular No. 2017-0028, per the same advisory.

### Why do some websites show ₱15,000 or ₱32,000?
Those were earlier rates. Moderate-risk pneumonia moved from ₱15,000 to ₱19,500 and then to ₱29,250, and high-risk pneumonia moved from ₱32,000 to ₱90,100. Websites that list older numbers are outdated.

### Does PhilHealth cover pneumonia vaccines?
The pneumonia case rate is for hospital treatment. The sources reviewed for this article do not describe vaccination as part of that benefit, so ask PhilHealth or your doctor about preventive services.

### Can I use PhilHealth at any hospital?
Use it at a PhilHealth-accredited facility. Provider information can change. Check the official PhilHealth provider directory before visiting.
