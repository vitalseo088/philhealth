# PhilHealth YAKAP Clinic Requirements 2026: What Members and Clinics Need

If you want to use YAKAP, you need two things: a **PhilHealth Identification Number (PIN)** and a **YAKAP clinic of your choice**. Picking the clinic is not the last step, though. Your registration is only complete after your First Patient Encounter and your signature on the YAKAP Empanelment Slip.

This guide covers both sides of the phrase "YAKAP clinic requirements" as of **October 2026**:

- **For members:** what you need to register with and use a YAKAP clinic.
- **For clinics:** what a facility must have to qualify as a YAKAP provider.

It reflects PhilHealth Circular No. 2025-0017 and later advisories. It does not cover benefit amounts or the medicine list, and it is not medical advice. Whether a service is covered depends on the applicable benefit package and your eligibility.

## What Is a YAKAP Clinic?

YAKAP stands for *Yaman ng Kalusugan Program*. PhilHealth describes it as an evolution of the Konsulta program. Its primary care clinics were formerly called Primary Care (PC) Clinics, and the circular says a PC Clinic is "currently referred to as a YAKAP Clinic."

A YAKAP clinic is an accredited public or private provider that acts as your first contact for primary care. The relationship is called **empanelment**: a formal care relationship between you and your chosen clinic, created after your First Patient Encounter.

Under PhilHealth rules, once you are empaneled you are considered empaneled to that clinic for at least a year, unless you transfer.

## Who Can Register With a YAKAP Clinic?

PhilHealth's circular defines a beneficiary as any member or qualified dependent who is eligible to avail of PhilHealth benefits, subject to registration, coverage and entitlement rules.

- **Adults (18 and above)** can register, select a clinic and sign agreements on their own, without parental or guardian consent.
- **Minors** cannot transact on their own. A parent or legal guardian handles registration, clinic selection and the agreement.

## Requirements for Members

### What you definitely need

1. **A PhilHealth Identification Number (PIN).** If you do not have one, PhilHealth's circular says to register online or through assisted registration at a Local Health Insurance Office (LHIO), the Corporate Action Center or P-CARES, using a PhilHealth Member Registration Form (PMRF).
2. **Identity verification.** PhilHealth uses the **PhilHealth Check Utility (PCU)**, an online tool that checks identity with a selfie-based liveness check against PhilSys and PhilHealth records.
3. **A valid government-issued ID if you are not PhilSys-registered.** The PCU can only process PhilSys-registered individuals. If you are not registered, PhilHealth requires a valid ID (or alternative documents for manual validation).
4. **A chosen, accredited YAKAP clinic.**

### What may be required, depending on how you register

| Situation | What PhilHealth's circular says may be needed |
|---|---|
| Registering through the eGov PH Super App or Member Portal | Follow the on-screen steps. The Member Portal requires your PIN. |
| Walk-in, assisted selection at an LHIO | PCU verification, then a duly accomplished **Primary Care Selection Form** (formerly the Konsulta Registration Form) naming your chosen clinic. |
| Selection through the Corporate Action Center | A completed Primary Care Selection Form plus a government-issued ID, sent by email or other digital messaging channel. |
| Group or settings-based selection (workplace, school, community group) | Handled by the organization's head or delegated authority, who submits certifications and a consent list. Individual forms are no longer required in this channel. |

PhilHealth's official documents do not list other personal documents for individual clinic selection. If a clinic or office asks for something more, ask them to cite the rule.

## How to Choose a YAKAP Clinic: Step by Step

**Step 1: Get your PIN.** Skip this if you already have one.

**Step 2: Select your clinic.** Use one of these channels:

- **eGov PH Super App:** open the app, go to NGAs, search for PhilHealth, select PhilHealth Member Portal, click the YAKAP tab, then choose *Add YAKAP Clinic* (for yourself or a dependent). You can search by province and city or municipality.
- **PhilHealth Member Portal:** log in with your PIN, select *Click here to Select YAKAP Clinic*, search by province or city/municipality, and choose a clinic. For a dependent, scroll to the dependents list and use the YAKAP Clinic column beside their date of birth. Repeat for each dependent.
- **Any PhilHealth office (LHIO):** you can use a dedicated terminal or ask for assisted selection (PCU check, then the Primary Care Selection Form).
- **The YAKAP clinic itself:** per the circular, the clinic assists you in registering and selecting a clinic using the eGov PH Super App or the Member Portal.
- **Other channels:** the PhilHealth Corporate Action Center, or P-CARES staff stationed in hospitals.

**Step 3: Wait for confirmation and the FPE schedule.** PhilHealth processes your selection, and the clinic or PhilHealth notifies you. The clinic then schedules your First Patient Encounter.

**Step 4: Complete your First Patient Encounter (FPE).** The circular describes the FPE as the first patient contact for the year, where the clinic takes or updates your basic health data to identify your health risks. It states plainly that the **FPE is not a medical consultation.** The clinic verifies your identity through the PCU before the FPE.

**Step 5: Sign the YAKAP Empanelment Slip (YES), also called the Mutual Care Agreement (MCA).** You and the clinic sign it after the FPE. This confirms your empanelment. The template says it runs until December 31 of the current calendar year, which is why you sign a new one every year.

> **Note on a changed process:** Several secondary websites say the older clinic-assisted selection module in the My PhilHealth Portal was discontinued on March 1, 2026. We could not confirm that date in the official documents we reviewed. Use the eGov PH Super App, the Member Portal, an LHIO, or the other channels listed in the circular.

## Rules Your Chosen Clinic Must Follow

PhilHealth Advisory No. 2026-0023 (signed March 9, 2026) clarifies several rules that affect which clinic you can pick.

| Rule | What it means for you |
|---|---|
| **Fixed and permanent physical clinic** | Your clinic must be a fixed, permanent, accredited clinic. Mobile health units, telemedicine-only providers and transient outreach teams cannot be your YAKAP clinic. |
| **Accredited only** | Empanelment to an unaccredited primary care facility is not valid YAKAP registration. It is not covered for benefit entitlement, capitation payment or claim reimbursement. |
| **30-km radius** | The clinic should be within 30 km of your declared residence or office address, so you can reach it for follow-ups and laboratory services. |
| **Farther clinic** | Allowed if it is your clear and informed choice, shown by selecting it and signing the YES/MCA. PhilHealth "strongly discourages" this. |
| **Mobile or telemedicine partners** | These may only act as supplemental providers of an accredited YAKAP clinic. Their services are credited to the clinic you are empaneled with. |
| **Outreach events** | If you were registered during a mobile outreach, you must still be guided to complete empanelment with an accredited YAKAP clinic. |

PhilHealth also says a clinic cannot reject you if you selected it, unless it has reached its declared beneficiary limit.

For areas with no clinic within 30 km, the advisory says a supplemental circular will set guidelines for out-of-facility service delivery. We did not find that circular in the sources we reviewed.

## Requirements for Clinics That Want to Become YAKAP Providers

This section is for clinic owners and administrators. PhilHealth publishes a **Self-Assessment / Accreditation Survey Tool for YAKAP Providers**, which PhilHealth also uses to validate an applicant. It is undated, so confirm with your PhilHealth Regional Office that you are using the current version.

First, the basic eligibility rule: under Advisory 2026-0023, accreditation is limited to primary care providers with permanent physical clinics.

The tool's minimum requirements include the following.

| Area | What the tool lists |
|---|---|
| **Licenses** | DOH license (for hospitals, infirmaries, ambulatory surgical clinics and similar facilities); Mayor's/Business Permit for private facilities. |
| **Infrastructure** | Signage; clean, smoke-free environment; adequate lighting, electricity and clean water; seating in a ventilated area; consultation area with privacy and a separate examination area; functional toilet; entrance/exit signage; fire safety; non-slippery floors; PWD access; safe storage of laboratory reagents, if applicable. |
| **Infection control** | Hygiene supplies, puncture-proof sharps containers, gloves and masks, surface disinfection, instrument-cleaning area, segregated waste bins, pandemic control measures. |
| **Basic equipment** | Non-mercurial BP apparatus and thermometer, stethoscope, adult and infant weighing scales, tape measure, nebulizer, sterilizer or equivalent, speculums, glucometer and ECG machine (may be outsourced with evidence of compliance), and other listed supplies. |
| **Human resources** | A physician with updated PhilHealth accreditation and a valid PRC license; at least two staff (2 nurses, or 1 nurse and 1 midwife) who are active PhilHealth members with valid PRC licenses. |
| **Health information system** | PhilHealth-certified EMR; steady 25–100 Mbps internet compatible with the EMR; compatible computer set-up; updated Google Chrome; printer; face-capturing device (webcam or phone); power back-up or offline-compatible solution; PCU system. |
| **Policies and records** | Patient identification verification; FPE and comprehensive health screening protocols; emergency plans; drug storage and dispensing; records for FPE, empanelment, clinical charts and referrals. |
| **Care coordination** | Referral arrangements for oral health, optometry and diagnostics, unless the clinic provides them itself. |
| **Optional service lines** | If the clinic provides them: laboratory and cancer screening (DOH laboratory license), optometry, preventive oral health, and a pharmacy with FDA license stocking the 21 essential and 54 GAMOT drugs listed in the tool. |

Some items, such as the PCU system and certain protocols, are marked in the tool as applying after approval of initial accreditation.

### The EMR (electronic medical record) requirement

Advisory No. 2025-0077 says PhilHealth's eKonsulta system would be decommissioned by July 1, 2026, and that newly accredited YAKAP clinics effective 2026 must engage an EMR service provider as part of accreditation. The advisory points clinics to PhilHealth's list of certified service providers.

A July 3, 2026 news report says PhilHealth later deferred the shutdown so eKonsulta would keep working until the end of 2026. We could not read the underlying advisory directly, so confirm the current cut-off with your Regional Office before relying on a date.

### Other clinic duties that affect members

- **GAMOT physician accounts:** PhilHealth limits GAMOT application access to four authorized physicians per clinic, with exceptions for clinics that exceed their declared beneficiary limit.
- **PCU liveness check:** providers must run it for every patient encounter, per Advisory 2025-0072.
- **Digital YES:** Advisory 2026-0029 says the digital YES, accessed through the Health Care Institution (HCI) Portal, is the primary and required platform for empanelment.
- **Sanctions:** under the circular, repeated non-compliance with service commitments can lead to warnings, then suspension of accreditation and/or a fine.

### What we could not verify for clinic accreditation

- **The accreditation fee.** Secondary websites disagree (one lists ₱1,000, another says no fee). The official documents we read do not settle it for YAKAP clinics.
- **The complete document checklist and application procedure.** The self-assessment tool is a facility checklist, not a full filing guide. Ask your PhilHealth Regional Office what to submit and where.

## Transferring to Another YAKAP Clinic

| Your situation | Rule |
|---|---|
| Selected a clinic but **have not had your FPE** | You can transfer at any time of the year by visiting your nearest LHIO. It takes effect right after processing. |
| **Already empaneled** (FPE done) | You can transfer only in the **fourth quarter** of the year. The transfer takes effect in January of the following year. |
| Empaneled, but the clinic **closed**, had its **accreditation revoked, suspended or withdrawn**, or got a **DOH cease-and-desist order** | You may transfer before the fourth quarter. |

PhilHealth also states that if you selected a clinic but did **not** complete an FPE, you will **not** be retained for the next calendar year. You can reselect the same clinic or choose another. Retained members sign a new YES each year.

## Example (Illustrative Only)

*This is an example, not a real case.* Maria has a PIN and registers through the Member Portal. She chooses a clinic about 8 km from her home, which is within the 30-km standard. The clinic schedules her FPE. At the visit, staff verify her identity through the PCU, record her basic health data, and she signs the YES. She is now empaneled for the year. If she is unhappy with the clinic in June, she generally has to wait for the fourth quarter to transfer, unless the clinic closes or loses its accreditation.

## Common Problems

- **You chose a clinic but nothing happened.** Selection alone does not complete registration. You still need the FPE and the signed YES. Contact the clinic to confirm your FPE schedule.
- **The PCU cannot verify you.** The PCU only processes PhilSys-registered individuals. If you are not registered, bring a valid government-issued ID for manual validation.
- **Your PIN is missing, duplicated or tagged with an error.** The PCU is designed to flag duplicate or erroneous records so staff can help correct them. An LHIO can assist with registration or record updates.
- **A mobile team "registered" you.** If the registration came from an outreach event, you still need to complete empanelment with an accredited fixed YAKAP clinic.
- **The clinic you picked is too far.** The 30-km standard exists so you can reach follow-up care. If you pick a farther clinic on purpose, you can, but PhilHealth discourages it.
- **You want to switch clinics mid-year.** See the transfer rules above.

## What to Do If Something Goes Wrong

1. **Ask the clinic** which step is missing: selection, FPE or YES.
2. **Visit your nearest LHIO** for record corrections, selection problems or transfers.
3. **Contact PhilHealth.** Official documents list the 24/7 Corporate Action Center hotline at (02) 8662-2588 and actioncenter@philhealth.gov.ph.
4. **Keep your records:** your PIN, a screenshot of your clinic selection, and your signed YES.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do I need a PIN before choosing a YAKAP clinic?
Yes. PhilHealth's circular treats registration with PhilHealth and getting a PIN as the first step of the process.

### Is the First Patient Encounter a check-up?
No. The circular states the FPE is not a medical consultation. It is the clinic's first contact to take or update your basic health data and identify health risks.

### Can I pick any clinic in the Philippines?
You can select an accredited fixed clinic of your choice. PhilHealth's standard is within 30 km of your declared address, and a farther clinic is discouraged but allowed if it is your informed choice.

### Can a mobile clinic or telemedicine provider be my YAKAP clinic?
No. Under Advisory 2026-0023, they can only serve as supplemental providers partnered with an accredited YAKAP clinic.

### Do my dependents need to pick a clinic too?
Yes. In the Member Portal you select a clinic for each dependent separately.

### Do I need to sign the YAKAP Empanelment Slip every year?
Yes. Beneficiaries are asked to sign a new Mutual Care Agreement annually to confirm continued empanelment.

### What happens if I never complete the FPE?
You will not be retained for the next calendar year, and you would need to select a clinic again.

### Can a minor register on their own?
No. A parent or legal guardian handles transactions for minors.

### What does a clinic need to become a YAKAP provider?
A permanent physical clinic that meets PhilHealth's facility, staffing, equipment and EMR requirements, as listed in the self-assessment tool above. Contact your PhilHealth Regional Office for the current application steps.

### How much does YAKAP registration cost?
The official documents we reviewed do not state a member registration fee, so we do not give a figure here. Ask PhilHealth or check the official YAKAP page.
