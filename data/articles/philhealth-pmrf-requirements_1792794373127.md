# PhilHealth PMRF Requirements 2026: What to Bring and How to File

The PhilHealth Member Registration Form (PMRF) is the one form you need to register as a new member or to update an existing PhilHealth record. For first-time registration, PhilHealth requires a properly filled-out PMRF plus at least one valid proof of identity. If you are updating your record or adding dependents, you also need documents that prove the change.

This guide is for first-time registrants, members who need to correct or update their records, and members who want to declare dependents. It reflects PhilHealth's rules as reviewed in October 2026, including recent 2026 circulars for self-paying members, persons with disability (PWDs) and solo parents. Requirements can differ by member type, so check the section that matches you.

## Quick Answer: PMRF Requirements at a Glance

| Your situation | What to submit |
|---|---|
| First-time registration | Accomplished PMRF (tick *Registration*) + at least one valid proof of identity |
| Updating or correcting your record | PMRF (tick *Updating/Amendment*) + supporting documents for the change |
| Adding or changing dependents | PMRF + document proving each dependent's relationship to you |
| Self-earning, kasambahay or family driver | PMRF with your declared monthly income, signed (a duly attested PMRF with self-declared income is considered sufficient) |
| Professional or other direct contributor | PMRF; PhilHealth may ask for additional documents to validate the income you declare |
| Person with disability (walk-in) | PMRF + photocopy of your PWD ID, submitted at the nearest Local Health Insurance Office (LHIO) |
| Solo parent | PMRF + copy of the front and back of your valid Solo Parent ID (SPIC) |
| Someone filing for you | Authorization letter signed by you + one valid government-issued photo ID of both you and your representative |

Source: PhilHealth Circular No. 2020-0001 and its annexes; Circular Nos. 2026-0015 and 2026-0017 (Tamang Sagot guides).

## What Is the PMRF?

The PMRF is PhilHealth's form for registering new members and for updating or amending the records of existing members. Registration gives you a PhilHealth Identification Number (PIN), which the form describes as unique and permanent. You should use the same PIN in all transactions with PhilHealth.

The PMRF copy currently listed on PhilHealth's downloads page is the "UHC v.1 January 2020" version, which follows the Universal Health Care Act. PhilHealth also publishes a separate PMRF for foreign nationals (PMRF-FN) on the same page. This article does not cover the foreign-national form's requirements, because the available official pages do not spell them out.

## Who Needs to File a PMRF?

- **New registrants.** Anyone not yet registered who needs a PIN.
- **Existing members updating records.** This includes changes of name, date of birth, sex, civil status, address, or contact details, and changes to your dependents.
- **Members whose category has changed.** The form has a Member Type section where you tick the category that best describes your current status.

PhilHealth's own explainer for the 2020 circular says members should update their information, especially when adding dependents or changing details such as address and contact number.

## Documents You May Need

### Proof of identity (first-time registrants)

A PMRF must be accompanied by at least one valid proof of identity if you are registering for the first time. PhilHealth's list accepts either:

- a birth or baptismal certificate with registry number, **or**
- any valid government-issued ID that shows your date of birth.

The official list of accepted IDs includes (among others) a passport, driver's license, PRC ID, postal ID, voter's ID, GSIS e-Card or SSS UMID card, Senior Citizen ID, OFW ID, TIN ID, and PhilHealth ID. It also lists company IDs issued by private entities registered with or regulated by the BSP, SEC or Insurance Commission. The list is "not limited to" these, so ask your LHIO if your ID is not on it.

### Proof of relationship (adding dependents or amending records)

When you declare or update dependents, PhilHealth's annex lists these documents:

| Dependent | Document listed |
|---|---|
| Legal spouse who is not an active member | Marriage certificate/contract with registry number (for a Muslim spouse, a specific affidavit of marriage that is registered or authenticated with the PSA) |
| Unmarried, unemployed child below 21 (legitimate or illegitimate) | Birth or baptismal certificate with registry number; if unavailable, a notarized affidavit of two disinterested persons attesting to the date of birth, or a duly accomplished Certificate of Live Birth |
| Legally adopted child below 21 | Court decree of adoption |
| Stepchild below 21 | Marriage certificate of the biological parent and stepparent + the stepchild's birth certificate |
| Child 21 or older with mental or physical disability | Birth certificate with registry number + medical certificate from the attending physician describing the extent of disability |
| Foster child | Birth/foundling certificate or child profile, plus the Foster Family Care License and Foster Placement Authority |
| Minor registrant | Birth certificate with registry number |

The form's instructions say qualified dependents are your living spouse, children below 21, and parents who are 60 or older and totally dependent on you. The annex table I reviewed does not list a specific document for dependent parents, so confirm with your LHIO what they will accept.

Dependents with disability are registered as principal members under the PMRF instructions (citing RA 11228).

### Proof of income (direct contributors)

A direct contributor is a member who has the capacity to pay premiums, such as an employee, self-earning individual, professional practitioner or OFW. Proof of income is required for all direct contributors **except** employees in formal employment with no other income source, sea-based migrant workers and lifetime members. You declare gross income, meaning income before taxes and other deductions.

- **Kasambahays, family drivers and self-earning individuals:** a duly attested PMRF with your self-declared income is sufficient.
- **Practicing professionals, sole proprietors, and certain service, sales, trade and elementary-occupation workers:** PhilHealth may request additional documents to validate the income you declared.
- **Mixed income** (for example, a professional who is also employed): you report income from all sources, and extra documents may be requested.

Documents PhilHealth lists as acceptable proof of income include a duly signed PMRF, the latest BIR-received income tax return or certificate of no income, the latest payslip, the latest financial statement, an overseas employment contract, a certificate of financial assessment, a certificate of income from an LGU or authorized organization, and a certificate of membership with monthly income from an association of vendors or drivers. PhilHealth may also determine other documents.

### Special cases

- **Persons with disability (PWDs).** For walk-in registration, submit a filled-out PMRF and a photocopy of your PWD ID at the nearest LHIO. PhilHealth checks your record against the PWD Registry. If you are not yet in the registry, you are tagged "For Verification," keep your current category and usual premium, and can still use PhilHealth benefits. Employed PWDs have additional options through their employer. (Circular No. 2026-0017.)
- **Solo parents.** Bring a PMRF and a copy of the front and back of your valid Solo Parent ID. PhilHealth says the PMRF is being revised to include solo-parent fields. Until then, write "Solo Parent" and your SPIC number at the bottom of the Indirect Contributor membership type in Part IV of the current PMRF. (Circular No. 2026-0015.)
- **Self-paying members.** You can register or update by submitting a PMRF with supporting documents at any LHIO, PhilHealth Express or accredited registration partner, or through the PhilHealth Member Portal or the eGovPH Super App. If you also want a YAKAP clinic assignment, the circular mentions a Primary Care Selection Form (PCSF). (Circular No. 2026-0011.)
- **Someone filing on your behalf.** The representative must present an authorization letter signed by you, plus one valid government-issued photo ID of both you and the representative.

### What the official sources do not clearly specify

Some blogs say you must bring two 1×1 photos. The official circular and annexes I reviewed list the PMRF, proof of identity and supporting documents, and do not list ID photos. If your LHIO asks for photos, follow their instruction.

## Step-by-Step: How to File the PMRF

1. **Get the PMRF.** Download it from the Downloads page of the PhilHealth website or get one from a PhilHealth office. Avoid copies from random sites or social media.
2. **Tick your purpose.** Choose *Registration* for a new PIN or *Updating/Amendment* to change your record. For updates, also tick the specific item you are changing and write the correct data.
3. **Fill in the form.** Write in **capital letters**, and write "N/A" for items that do not apply. All fields are mandatory unless marked optional. Enter your name and your mother's full maiden name exactly as they appear in your birth certificate. Your mobile number is required.
4. **Declare your member type and dependents.** Tick the Member Type that best describes your current status. For self-earning individuals, kasambahays and family drivers, write your actual monthly income. List only qualified dependents.
5. **Sign and date.** Sign over your printed name. If you cannot write, affix your right thumbmark. By signing, you certify that everything you wrote is true and accurate.
6. **Attach your documents.** Bring the proof of identity, dependent documents or proof of income that apply to you (see above).
7. **Submit.** Give the PMRF and documents to the nearest PhilHealth office or LHIO, or use the channel that applies to your member type (such as the Member Portal or eGovPH Super App for self-paying members).
8. **Wait for your record.** For updates, PhilHealth's amendment page says you await the printout of your updated Member Data Record (MDR). PWD registrants who are verified receive an MDR and PhilHealth Card.

Leave the "For PhilHealth Use Only" areas of the form blank.

## Examples

*These are illustrative examples, not official case rulings.*

**Example 1: First-time self-earning registrant.** A freelance designer with no PIN ticks *Registration*, ticks *Self-Earning Individual* under Member Type, writes her actual monthly income, signs the form, and attaches one valid government ID showing her date of birth. Under Circular 2020-0001, a signed PMRF with self-declared income is considered sufficient proof for a self-earning individual, though PhilHealth could still ask for more if needed.

**Example 2: Adding a newborn.** A member ticks *Updating/Amendment*, lists his child under Declaration of Dependents, and attaches the child's birth certificate with registry number.

**Example 3: Name correction.** A member whose PhilHealth record misspells her surname ticks *Updating/Amendment* and *Change/Correction of Name*, and writes the FROM and TO versions. Because the form instructs you to enter names as they appear in your birth certificate, a birth certificate is the natural supporting document to bring. Check with your LHIO if they want something additional.

## Common Problems

- **Name or birth details don't match your birth certificate.** The form asks for your name and your mother's maiden name as they appear in your birth certificate. Mismatches are a common reason records need correcting later.
- **Missing signature or thumbmark.** The form is only certified true when signed.
- **Declaring someone who doesn't qualify as a dependent.** Only the living spouse, children below 21, and parents 60 or older who are totally dependent on you qualify (with the exceptions in the table above).
- **Wrong purpose box.** If you already have a PIN, tick *Updating/Amendment*, not *Registration*.
- **Missing proof of income.** Direct contributors who are not formally employed generally need to show income. PhilHealth may ask for extra documents if your declared income needs validation.
- **Representative without authorization.** A representative must bring a signed authorization letter and valid photo IDs for both of you.
- **Using a false document.** Circulars 2026-0015 and 2026-0017 warn that false IDs or false information can lead to record correction, recovery of wrongly received benefits or subsidies, and possible administrative, civil or criminal liability.

## What To Do If Something Goes Wrong

- **Your PMRF was not accepted or something is missing.** Ask the LHIO exactly which document is missing and resubmit. The documents differ by member type and situation.
- **Your record still shows old details.** Submit a new PMRF with *Updating/Amendment* ticked and the supporting documents for each change.
- **You can't visit an office.** Self-paying members can register or update through the PhilHealth Member Portal or the eGovPH Super App, and PWD circular guidance says records can be updated online through those channels too. Online ID verification may rely on PhilSys registration; without it, you may need to present a valid proof of identity, which can cause delays, according to the PWD circular guidance.
- **You need to ask PhilHealth directly.** The PhilHealth Corporate Action Center is listed as 02 8662 2588, with mobile lines 0998 857 2957 and 0968 865 4670 (Smart) and 0917 127 5987 and 0917 110 9812 (Globe), in PhilHealth's 2026 circular guides. Numbers can change, so confirm on philhealth.gov.ph.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What are the basic PhilHealth PMRF requirements?
A properly filled-out PMRF and at least one valid proof of identity for first-time registration. Updates and dependent changes need supporting documents showing the change or relationship.

### Do I need a PMRF if I already have a PhilHealth number?
Yes, if you want to update or amend your record, such as adding a dependent, changing your name or civil status, or updating contact details. Tick *Updating/Amendment* and write your PIN.

### Is a birth certificate required for first-time registration?
Not necessarily. PhilHealth accepts either a birth/baptismal certificate with registry number or a valid government-issued ID that shows your date of birth.

### Can someone else submit my PMRF for me?
Yes. The representative needs your signed authorization letter and one valid government-issued photo ID of both of you.

### Who can I declare as a dependent?
Your living spouse, your children below 21 (unmarried and unemployed), and parents 60 or older who are totally dependent on you. Children with disability are covered by special rules, and solo parents have an additional provision for dependents listed on their Solo Parent ID under Circular 2026-0015.

### Do I need to declare income on the PMRF?
Direct contributors generally do, except employees in formal employment with no other income, sea-based migrant workers and lifetime members. Declare gross (before-tax) income.

### Where can I get the PMRF?
From the Downloads page of PhilHealth's website, or from a PhilHealth office.

### Is there a different PMRF for foreign nationals?
Yes. PhilHealth lists a separate PMRF for foreign nationals (PMRF-FN) on its downloads page.

### Can PWDs and solo parents register using the PMRF?
Yes. PWDs submit a PMRF and a photocopy of their PWD ID. Solo parents submit a PMRF with the Solo Parent subtype and SPIC number written in, plus copies of the front and back of their SPIC.
