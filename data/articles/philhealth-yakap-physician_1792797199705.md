# PhilHealth YAKAP Physician: What They Do, Who Can Prescribe, and How Doctors Join (2026)

A PhilHealth YAKAP physician is a PhilHealth-accredited doctor who delivers care under YAKAP (Yaman ng Kalusugan Program), PhilHealth's outpatient primary care benefit. As a patient, you do not sign up with a named doctor: you choose a YAKAP Clinic, and your clinic's physicians care for you. To get GAMOT medicines (PhilHealth's outpatient drug benefit), your prescription must come from an "authorized physician," a clinic-nominated doctor with access to PhilHealth's GAMOT App.

This guide is for patients and for doctors and clinic staff. It reflects PhilHealth issuances available as of October 24, 2026. It explains PhilHealth's rules only and is not medical advice.

## What Is a YAKAP Physician?

PhilHealth Circular 2026-0007 defines "Primary Care Physicians (YAKAP Physicians)" as PhilHealth-accredited physicians under YAKAP who are responsible for delivering medical services under established clinical, prescribing and quality assurance standards. They work within YAKAP Clinics (primary care clinics accredited by PhilHealth), which PhilHealth calls YAKAP Providers together with GAMOT facilities (pharmacies) and cancer screening facilities.

Under PhilHealth's rules, a YAKAP Clinic must at minimum deliver:

- a First Patient Encounter (FPE) and empanelment,
- an initial assessment, basic health data collection and risk profiling,
- primary care consultations, laboratory tests and medicines,
- referrals (for example to higher-level facilities, TB treatment, maternity care, oral health or cancer screening), and
- creation and upkeep of patient health records.

The clinic also acts as your first point of contact and navigator, coordinating with pharmacies and screening centers. These providers are expected to refer you back to your clinic after care.

## For Patients

### How you are linked to a YAKAP physician

You register with PhilHealth (you need a PhilHealth Identification Number, or PIN), choose a YAKAP Clinic, attend a First Patient Encounter, and sign the YAKAP Empanelment Slip. The FPE records your basic health data to identify risks, and PhilHealth states it is **not yet a consultation**. After signing, you are "empaneled," meaning the clinic is your regular primary care provider until December 31 of that year. You sign again each year.

You can choose your clinic through the eGovPH app, the PhilHealth Member Portal, any PhilHealth office, or with help at the clinic. Check the official PhilHealth list of accredited YAKAP Clinics before choosing. Provider information can change, so check the official PhilHealth provider directory before visiting.

Before consultations, clinics verify your identity using PhilHealth's identity tool. Bring a valid ID.

### Why your prescription depends on an authorized physician

For GAMOT medicines, PhilHealth's circular says only medications prescribed by authorized physicians **through the GAMOT App** are covered. When a prescription is made, it carries a Unique Prescription Security Code (UPSC). You take it, with a government-issued ID, to a GAMOT facility. The physician must confirm your identity by PIN or a valid government ID before prescribing.

A prescription from a doctor who is not an authorized YAKAP physician, or one written outside the GAMOT App, is not covered under the rules above. If you have already collected a prescription by other means, ask your clinic whether it is a GAMOT prescription.

Other prescribing rules that affect you:

- Prescriptions must be for your active or current condition. "Standby" prescriptions for non-active conditions, or ones issued on request, are not eligible for GAMOT claims.
- Oral antibiotics cannot be prescribed for more than 14 days per prescription.
- Maintenance medicines can be prescribed up to three months, but a pharmacy dispenses up to a one-month supply at a time.
- Before issuing a new prescription, the physician must check for active prescriptions and review them.
- You can claim anti-infective prescriptions within 2 days and other medicines within 2 weeks of issuance, and prescriptions are cancelled after 90 days.

### Costs

PhilHealth Advisory 2026-0003 says nothing may be charged for covered YAKAP services in a public clinic. In a private clinic, your co-payment is capped at ₱900 per member per year. You should not be denied primary care services at your empaneled clinic after completing your FPE and signing.

### If prescriptions fail or a clinic cannot prescribe

In February 2026, PhilHealth acknowledged reports of problems accessing GAMOT medicines, and traced system-related issues mainly to clinics that had not completed physician nomination and readiness requirements (Advisory 2026-0017). If your clinic says it cannot generate a GAMOT prescription, ask whether its physician nomination is complete. You can also report the problem to PhilHealth (see below).

## For Doctors and Clinics

### Step 1: Get PhilHealth accreditation as a professional

PhilHealth's accreditation page says physicians, dentists, midwives and nurses may apply. Initial accreditation and re-accreditation can be applied for anytime. Renewal is filed 120 to 20 days before the expiration of the PRC license or accreditation validity.

PhilHealth's documentary requirements annex (Circular 2023-0024) lists general requirements for all professionals:

1. Provider Data Record (PDR)
2. Performance Commitment
3. Updated PRC license, or a PRC document proving renewal
4. Two passport-size photos (35 mm x 45 mm)

Physician-specific requirements for initial accreditation include a Certificate of Good Standing from the Philippine Medical Association or its local component society. General practitioners with training also need a Certificate of Completed Residency Training. Medical specialists need a specialty board certificate and a Certificate of Good Standing from the specialty society. That annex also lists "Primary Care Physician as Konsulta Package Provider" with a DOH "Certification as Primary Care Worker for UHC." The annex is dated "as of CY 2023" and still uses the Konsulta name, so confirm with your PhilHealth Regional Office whether this applies under YAKAP.

### Step 2: Be affiliated with an accredited YAKAP Clinic that is "ready"

PhilHealth Advisory 2026-0009 (January 20, 2026) sets the access rule: a physician can prescribe in the GAMOT App only if (1) the physician's YAKAP Clinic has met all mandatory components of the YAKAP Clinic Assessment Readiness Tool as validated by PhilHealth Regional Offices, and (2) the clinic nominated that physician as an Authorized Physician.

Advisory 2026-0017 (February 18, 2026) lists the minimum clinic readiness requirements:

- a 720p camera
- a laptop, computer or desktop
- a stable internet connection (minimum 100 Mbps)
- a functional electronic medical record (EMR) system
- PhilHealth Check Utility (PCU) liveness check capability
- staff training

PhilHealth also requires onsite validation by the Local Health Insurance Office (LHIO) before prescribing access is granted.

### Step 3: The clinic nominates its physicians

Under Advisory 2026-0009:

- Nominees must be PhilHealth-accredited physicians affiliated with the nominating YAKAP Clinic.
- The default limit is **four authorized physicians per clinic**.
- A clinic that exceeds its declared beneficiary limit may request more accounts, supported by beneficiary volume data and subject to final approval of the Regional Vice President.
- The clinic submits a signed nomination form (hard copy or email) to its LHIO. The form asks for each physician's name, PhilHealth Accreditation Number and PRC license number.
- Replacing a physician is done through a section of the same form, which authorizes deactivating the current account.
- The clinic is responsible for the security of the accounts and must tell PhilHealth about personnel changes.
- Until the nomination list is submitted, prescriptions cannot be generated through the GAMOT App.

### Step 4: Prescribe according to PhilHealth's standards

Circular 2026-0007 expects YAKAP physicians to:

- follow current clinical practice guidelines and PhilHealth quality policies;
- prescribe by generic name and align medicines with the approved indications in the GAMOT table;
- prescribe only when clinically indicated, in appropriate doses and duration, at the lowest possible cost;
- check drug interactions and avoid inappropriate polypharmacy (routine use of five or more medicines at once, when unnecessary);
- encode diagnoses with ICD-10 codes (PhilHealth asks YAKAP facilities to go to the 4th character where available) so they appear on prescriptions and screening requests; and
- keep complete patient records.

Prescribing beyond what is medically warranted, or engaging in inappropriate polypharmacy to take advantage of the benefit, can lead to monitoring and other actions. PhilHealth may also apply pre-payment review, post-audit and payment recovery.

For cancer screening, a physician's referral should follow the patient's risk profile and PhilHealth's screening quality standards for breast, colorectal, liver and lung cancer.

### New in 2026: anti-TB drugs through GAMOT

News reports of a PhilHealth statement say Circular 2026-0018 lets eligible patients with confirmed drug-susceptible tuberculosis get first-line anti-TB medicines from GAMOT facilities, from November 1, 2026, with prescriptions issued by YAKAP physicians through the GAMOT App. Read the circular itself before applying it in practice.

## Examples

*These are illustrative examples, not real cases.*

**Example 1 (patient):** A patient is empaneled at a public YAKAP Clinic. The clinic's authorized physician sees her, issues a GAMOT prescription through the GAMOT App, and gives her the UPSC. She takes it with a government ID to an accredited GAMOT pharmacy within the claiming period.

**Example 2 (clinic):** A private clinic with six physicians nominates four for GAMOT App access, as the default rule requires. Later its patient numbers pass its declared limit, so it requests extra accounts with beneficiary volume data for the Regional Vice President's approval.

## Common Problems

**"My doctor can't generate a prescription."** The clinic may not have completed physician nomination or readiness validation. Ask the clinic, and report persistent problems to PhilHealth.

**"I want a particular doctor."** PhilHealth's rules describe choosing a clinic. Ask the clinic about physician scheduling, because the official sources I reviewed do not say.

**"My prescription expired."** Prescriptions are cancelled after 90 days, and claiming windows are 2 days for anti-infectives and 2 weeks for other medicines. The physician updates the prescription if the medicine is still medically necessary.

**"I'm a doctor and I'm not sure my accreditation is current."** Check PhilHealth's list of accredited health care professionals and your PRC license validity. PhilHealth ties renewal timing to the PRC license or accreditation validity.

## What to Do If Something Goes Wrong

- **Charged in a public clinic, or above ₱900 in a private clinic:** cite Advisory 2026-0003 and report it.
- **Denied care after the FPE and signing:** report it.
- **GAMOT prescription cannot be generated:** ask the clinic about nomination and readiness status, then contact PhilHealth.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is a YAKAP physician different from a regular PhilHealth-accredited doctor?

A YAKAP physician is a PhilHealth-accredited physician working under YAKAP, held to its clinical, prescribing and quality standards. A GAMOT prescription additionally requires that the physician be an authorized physician with GAMOT App access.

### Can any accredited doctor prescribe GAMOT medicines?

No. Only authorized physicians, nominated by a YAKAP Clinic that has met its readiness requirements, can prescribe in the GAMOT App.

### How many doctors can a clinic nominate?

Four by default. More may be requested with beneficiary volume data, subject to Regional Vice President approval.

### Do I pay the YAKAP physician?

Not in a public YAKAP Clinic. In a private clinic, co-payment is capped at ₱900 per member per year.

### Does the First Patient Encounter count as a consultation?

No. PhilHealth states the FPE is the initial contact for the year to record basic health data, and is not yet a consultation.

### Where can I find an accredited clinic or doctor?

PhilHealth publishes lists of accredited YAKAP Clinics and accredited health care professionals on its website. Check the current list before visiting.
