# PhilHealth Provider Verification: How to Check If a Facility Is Accredited (2026)

Before you are admitted, book a test, or register at a clinic, you can check whether the facility is recognized by PhilHealth. The reliable way is to look it up in PhilHealth's own published lists, not to rely on a sign, an advertisement, or what someone tells you.

This guide is for PhilHealth members, dependents, and family members helping a patient. It explains which official list to use for each type of provider, how to read the dates in those lists, and what to do if you cannot confirm a facility. It covers 2026 information and does not give medical advice.

> **Provider information can change. Check the official PhilHealth provider directory before visiting.**

## Quick Answer

1. Go to PhilHealth's official [Accredited Health Facilities page](https://www.philhealth.gov.ph/partners/providers/facilities/accredited/). At the time of this review (October 26, 2026) it showed "Updated as of September 30, 2026."
2. Open the list that matches the type of provider you need.
3. Search the PDF for the facility's name and city.
4. Check the listed expiry date and, where the list has them, the services or tests shown.
5. Confirm with the facility and, if unsure, with PhilHealth.

| What you want to verify | Where to look on the official site |

## What "Accredited" Means

**Accreditation** is PhilHealth's recognition that a health facility may provide services to members under the National Health Insurance Program. It is not a permanent status. PhilHealth reviews accreditation in cycles, and its Circular No. 2023-0012 (the omnibus guidelines on accreditation of health facilities) provides that, starting in 2024, a facility may be granted up to three years of accreditation, though some facilities must still submit updated license documents each January.

A facility can also lose its status. The circular's official Q&A describes situations such as denied applications, suspension, and temporary withdrawal (for example, when a facility's accredited medical director's own accreditation lapses and is not renewed).

**Contracted** is a separate term used for Z Benefit packages, which are special packages for certain serious illnesses. These have their own list on PhilHealth's [Contracted Health Facilities page](https://www.philhealth.gov.ph/partners/providers/facilities/contracted/), also updated as of September 30, 2026.

## Step-by-Step: How to Verify a Provider

### Step 1: Use only the official website

The only official PhilHealth website is www.philhealth.gov.ph. In a January 29, 2026 notice, PhilHealth warned about fake websites that imitate its pages and said official government sites typically end in .gov.ph. Type the address yourself rather than following a link from a message.

### Step 2: Open the right list

From the Accredited Health Facilities page, choose the list for the provider type. Lists are separate, so a hospital may be found in the Hospitals list but not in, say, the Cancer Screening list.

### Step 3: Search for the facility

Use your device's find function (Ctrl+F on a computer) and type part of the name. Try alternate spellings and the city. Facilities with several branches can appear more than once.

### Step 4: Read the entry

For what the lists show, take cancer screening as an example. The official list contains the facility name, contact details, street, municipality, an expiry date, and tick marks showing which tests the facility is listed for. Other lists may be organized differently, so read the column headings of the list you open.

### Step 5: Check the date

Note the "Updated as of" date at the top and the entry's expiry date. For instance, many entries in the 2026 cancer lists show an expiry of 12/31/2026, which means renewal for the following cycle is something to confirm directly.

### Step 6: Confirm with the facility and PhilHealth

Call or message the facility using the contact details in the official list. Ask whether it is currently accredited for the service you need, how PhilHealth benefits are applied, and what to bring. If you still have doubts, contact PhilHealth through its Action Center at actioncenter@philhealth.gov.ph or visit a PhilHealth office.

## Verifying Specific Provider Types

### Hospitals and other facilities
Use the Hospitals list or the list for the specific service (dialysis, maternity, and so on). The facility must be on the list for that service.

### YAKAP clinics
YAKAP (Yaman ng Kalusugan Program) is PhilHealth's primary care program, which replaced the Konsulta name. Check the **Yakap Clinics** list. PhilHealth's advisories require YAKAP providers to put up official YAKAP signage by April 1, 2026. Hospitals at Levels 1 to 3 that run a YAKAP facility display both the PhilHealth signage and the YAKAP signage, while stand-alone YAKAP and GAMOT facilities display the YAKAP signage only (Advisory Nos. 2025-0061 and 2026-0020).

Since March 1, 2026, PhilHealth stopped using the My PhilHealth Portal for primary care clinic selection. Members can select a clinic through the eGov Super App or the PhilHealth Member Portal, or by visiting the nearest Local Health Insurance Office (Advisory No. 2026-0019).

### Cancer screening and GAMOT providers
Both have their own lists on the accredited facilities page. The cancer screening list shows which tests each facility is accredited for.

### Z Benefit packages
Check the contracted list for the specific package. Being an accredited hospital does not automatically mean being contracted for a Z Benefit package.

### Doctors and other health professionals
PhilHealth also accredits health care professionals, and YAKAP clinics must nominate PhilHealth-accredited physicians (Advisory No. 2026-0009). The official pages I reviewed did not point to a public search tool for checking an individual doctor. Ask the facility for the doctor's PhilHealth accreditation details and, if needed, confirm with PhilHealth.

## Is the PhilHealth Signage Enough?

No. Signage is a sign that a facility says it participates, but the lists are the record to check. PhilHealth's own Q&A on Circular 2023-0012 notes that when a facility's application, appeal, or motion for reconsideration is denied, it must put up a notice beside its PhilHealth signage stating it is no longer accredited, and that the facility, not PhilHealth, owns and handles the signage. That means a sign can still be on display when status has changed. Use the sign as a prompt to check the list.

## Example (Illustrative Only)

*This is an example to show the process, not a real case.*

A member wants a mammogram near her home. She opens the Cancer Screening Test Providers PDF, searches for a nearby hospital, and finds an entry with ticks under "Mammo" and an expiry date. She also notes she is not sure about her primary care clinic, so she checks the Yakap Clinics list too. Before going, she calls the hospital to confirm that the test is available and what documents to bring.

## Common Problems

- **"I can't find the facility on the list."** Try other spellings, the branch name, or the parent company name. Check the other lists. The facility may not be accredited for that particular service.
- **"The list I found is old."** Use the version linked from the official page and check the "Updated as of" date.
- **"The facility's expiry date has passed or is coming up."** Ask the facility and PhilHealth whether renewal is in process.
- **"A website says a facility is accredited."** Third-party sites can be outdated or wrong. Check the official list.
- **"A facility is on the list but doesn't offer the service."** Accreditation lists do not guarantee that a service is available on a given day. Call first.

## What To Do If Something Goes Wrong

- If you cannot confirm a facility's status, ask it for its PhilHealth accreditation details in writing and contact PhilHealth before proceeding with non-urgent care.
- If a claim is refused or a benefit is not applied, ask the facility's PhilHealth desk for the reason in writing, keep your documents, and contact the PhilHealth Action Center.
- Report suspicious websites or accounts to actioncenter@philhealth.gov.ph. PhilHealth also reminds members not to share personal details such as their PIN on social media pages, and to use official hotlines and email for queries about membership and claims (PhilHealth news, October 17, 2025).
- In an emergency, get care first and sort out the paperwork afterwards. Your medical needs come before verification.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Where is PhilHealth's official list of accredited facilities?**
On the Accredited Health Facilities page of www.philhealth.gov.ph, under Partners → Providers.

**How often is the list updated?**
The page shows an "Updated as of" date. At review, it was September 30, 2026. The pages do not state a fixed update schedule in the sections reviewed.

**Does accreditation expire?**
Yes. Entries show expiry dates, and the omnibus accreditation guidelines describe renewal cycles of up to three years from 2024. Some entries on current lists expire on 12/31/2026.

**Is a PhilHealth sign enough to prove accreditation?**
No. Check the list. A facility that loses accreditation must display a notice beside its PhilHealth signage, but that depends on the facility doing so.

**Can I verify a doctor's PhilHealth accreditation online?**
I did not find an official public lookup for individual doctors in the pages reviewed. Ask the facility and PhilHealth.

**Who do I contact if I'm unsure?**
The PhilHealth Action Center (actioncenter@philhealth.gov.ph), your PhilHealth Regional Office, or a Local Health Insurance Office.
