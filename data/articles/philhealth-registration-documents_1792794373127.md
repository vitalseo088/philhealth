# PhilHealth Registration Documents 2026: What to Prepare Before You Apply

To register with PhilHealth, you need a properly filled-out Member Registration Form (PMRF) and at least one valid proof of identity. Depending on who you are, you may also need proof of income, papers that prove your dependents' relationship to you, or a special ID such as a PWD ID or Solo Parent ID.

This guide is for anyone who has no PhilHealth Identification Number (PIN) yet and wants to know which PhilHealth registration documents to bring. It reflects what PhilHealth's official issuances say as reviewed in October 2026. Requirements differ by member category, so read the section that matches your situation.

## Quick Answer: Documents Checklist

| Document | Needed by | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Accomplished PMRF (tick *Registration*) | Everyone registering | **Required** |
| At least one valid proof of identity | First-time registrants | **Required** |
| Proof of income | Most direct contributors (see below) | **Depends on category** |
| Proof of relationship for each dependent | Members declaring dependents | **Only if you declare dependents** |
| PWD ID photocopy | PWDs registering as walk-ins | **Special case** |
| Solo Parent ID (front and back) | Solo parents | **Special case** |
| Authorization letter + valid photo IDs of you and your representative | If someone files for you | **Only if using a representative** |

Source: PhilHealth Circular No. 2020-0001 and annexes; Circular Nos. 2026-0015 and 2026-0017 (Tamang Sagot guides).

## What Is PhilHealth Registration?

Registering means getting a PhilHealth Identification Number (PIN), a unique and permanent number you use in all PhilHealth transactions. Under the Universal Health Care Act, PhilHealth's own explainer says all Filipinos are automatically members of the National Health Insurance Program, and the PMRF is the form used to put a person's record into the PhilHealth database or to update it.

After registering, you receive a Member Data Record (MDR), the printout of your membership record. Verified PWD registrants, for example, receive an MDR and a PhilHealth Card.

## Who Needs to Register?

Anyone without a PIN. PhilHealth's rules divide members into two groups:

- **Direct contributors** are people who can pay premiums, such as employees, self-earning individuals, professional practitioners, kasambahays and family drivers, overseas Filipino workers (OFWs), Filipinos living abroad, those with dual citizenship, lifetime members, and Filipinos aged 21 and above with capacity to pay.
- **Indirect contributors** are people whose premiums are subsidized by the national government, such as DSWD-identified indigents, 4Ps/MCCT beneficiaries, senior citizens not otherwise covered, persons with disability, and Filipinos 21 and above without capacity to pay.

The group you belong to affects which documents apply to you.

## The Core Documents

### 1. The PMRF

Download the PMRF from the Downloads page of PhilHealth's website or get one from a PhilHealth office. The version PhilHealth currently posts is dated "UHC v.1 January 2020." Some guides say to bring two copies. The official circular and form I reviewed do not say how many copies are required, so ask your office or bring one extra copy to be safe.

### 2. Valid proof of identity

At least one is required for first-time registrants. PhilHealth accepts either:

- a birth or baptismal certificate with registry number, **or**
- a valid government-issued ID that shows your date of birth.

The official list includes a passport, driver's license, PRC ID, postal ID, voter's ID, GSIS e-Card or SSS UMID card, Senior Citizen ID, OFW ID, TIN ID and PhilHealth ID, and it is "not limited to" these. It also includes company IDs from private entities regulated by the BSP, SEC or Insurance Commission. If your ID isn't listed, ask the office whether it is acceptable.

### 3. Proof of income (for direct contributors)

PhilHealth requires proof of income from all direct contributors **except** formally employed workers with no other income source, sea-based migrant workers and lifetime members. You declare your gross income, which is income before taxes and deductions.

- **Kasambahays, family drivers and self-earning individuals:** a duly attested PMRF with your self-declared income is sufficient.
- **Practicing professionals, sole proprietors and certain service, sales and trade workers:** PhilHealth may request more documents to validate the income you declared.
- **Mixed income** (for example, a professional who is also employed): report income from all sources. Extra documents may be requested.

Accepted proof of income listed by PhilHealth includes a signed PMRF, the latest BIR-received income tax return or certificate of no income, the latest payslip, the latest financial statement, an overseas employment contract, a certificate of financial assessment, a certificate of income from an LGU or authorized organization, and a certificate of membership with monthly income from an association of vendors or drivers.

### 4. Documents for dependents (only if you declare any)

| Dependent | Document PhilHealth lists |
|---|---|
| Legal spouse who is not an active member | Marriage certificate/contract with registry number |
| Unmarried, unemployed child below 21 | Birth or baptismal certificate with registry number (alternatives exist if unavailable, such as a notarized affidavit of two disinterested persons or a duly accomplished Certificate of Live Birth) |
| Legally adopted child below 21 | Court decree of adoption |
| Stepchild below 21 | Marriage certificate of biological parent and stepparent + stepchild's birth certificate |
| Child 21 or older with disability | Birth certificate with registry number + medical certificate from the attending physician describing the disability |
| Foster child | Birth/foundling certificate or child profile + Foster Family Care License + Foster Placement Authority |

Qualified dependents are your living spouse, children below 21, and parents aged 60 or older who are totally dependent on you. The annex I reviewed does not list a document for dependent parents, so confirm with your office. Dependents with disability are registered as principal members under the PMRF instructions.

## Documents by Member Type

*Requirements below follow the official sources I could verify. Where they are silent, I say so.*

### Employed members

Your employer normally handles registration for you. Newly hired employees without a PIN accomplish a PMRF, and employers submit registration through PhilHealth's employer channels. The available official sources I reviewed do not give a detailed employee-side document list beyond the PMRF and proof of identity. Ask your HR department what they need from you.

### Self-earning individuals and other self-paying members

A self-paying member is someone without a formal employer who can pay their own premiums, such as a professional practitioner or business owner. Under Circular No. 2026-0011, self-paying members can register or update by:

- submitting a PMRF and supporting documents at any Local Health Insurance Office (LHIO), PhilHealth Express or an accredited registration partner;
- using the PhilHealth Member Portal; or
- using the eGovPH Super App.

If you also want to be assigned to a YAKAP clinic, the same circular mentions a Primary Care Selection Form (PCSF). PhilHealth's member pages also say that someone leaving formal employment for self-employment can request an amendment to become a Self-Earning Individual, declare monthly income and pay the premium at any PhilHealth office, accredited payment center or the Member Portal.

### Persons with disability (PWDs)

Under Circular No. 2026-0017, walk-in registration requires a filled-out PMRF and a photocopy of the PWD ID, submitted at the nearest LHIO. PhilHealth checks your record against the PWD Registry. Employed PWDs may also have their employer submit a PMRF, a photocopy of the PWD ID and an Employer Certification on the Employment of Persons with Disability. A PWD who is not yet in the registry is tagged "For Verification," keeps their current category and premium, and can still use PhilHealth benefits.

### Solo parents

Under Circular No. 2026-0015, bring a PMRF and a copy of the front and back of your valid Solo Parent ID (SPIC). PhilHealth says the PMRF is being revised to include solo-parent fields. Until then, write "Solo Parent" and your SPIC number at the bottom of the Indirect Contributor membership type in Part IV of the current PMRF. Without a valid SPIC, you cannot be tagged as a solo-parent member, though you can still enroll under another applicable category.

### OFWs and other categories

The PMRF's Member Type section includes land-based and sea-based migrant workers, Filipinos living abroad, those with dual citizenship, and foreign nationals. A separate PMRF for foreign nationals (PMRF-FN) is posted on PhilHealth's downloads page. The available official pages I reviewed do not spell out category-specific document lists for these groups, so check with PhilHealth before applying.

## Step-by-Step: How to Prepare and Submit

1. **Identify your member type.** Employed, self-earning, PWD, solo parent, OFW or another category.
2. **Get the PMRF** from philhealth.gov.ph or a PhilHealth office. Avoid copies from unofficial sites.
3. **Fill in the form.** The instructions say to write in capital letters, write "N/A" for items that do not apply, and enter your name and your mother's full maiden name exactly as in your birth certificate. Mobile number is required.
4. **Tick *Registration* and your Member Type.** Self-earning individuals, kasambahays and family drivers write their actual monthly income.
5. **Declare dependents** (if any) and prepare their documents.
6. **Sign the form** over your printed name, or use your right thumbmark if you cannot write.
7. **Gather your proof of identity** and any other documents for your category.
8. **Submit** at the nearest LHIO or through the channel for your category, such as the Member Portal or eGovPH Super App for self-paying members.
9. **Wait for your MDR** (and PhilHealth Card, where issued).

Leave the "For PhilHealth Use Only" areas blank.

## Examples

*These are illustrative examples, not official rulings.*

**Example 1: Freelancer registering for the first time.** A freelancer with no PIN ticks *Registration* and *Self-Earning Individual*, writes her monthly income, signs, and brings one valid government ID showing her date of birth. Under Circular 2020-0001, a signed PMRF with self-declared income is sufficient for a self-earning individual.

**Example 2: Registering with a spouse and two children.** A member declares his wife and two children below 21. He brings his own valid ID, the marriage certificate, and each child's birth certificate with registry number.

**Example 3: PWD walk-in.** A PWD with a PWD ID from the local PDAO brings a filled-out PMRF and a photocopy of the ID to the LHIO.

## Common Problems

- **Name mismatch.** The form says to enter names as on your birth certificate. Mismatches with your ID can cause trouble later.
- **Wrong purpose.** If you already have a PIN, tick *Updating/Amendment* instead of *Registration*.
- **Missing signature or thumbmark.** Your signature certifies the information is true.
- **Declaring people who don't qualify as dependents.**
- **No proof of income when required.** Check the proof-of-income section above.
- **Representative without authorization.** Representatives need a signed authorization letter and one valid government photo ID of both of you.
- **False documents.** The 2026 PWD and solo-parent circulars warn that false IDs or information can lead to correction of records, recovery of wrongly received benefits or subsidies, and possible liability.

## What To Do If Something Goes Wrong

- **Documents rejected:** ask which document is missing and for which category rule.
- **Cannot go in person:** self-paying members can use the PhilHealth Member Portal or eGovPH Super App. PhilHealth's PWD circular guidance says identity verification online may rely on PhilSys registration; without it you may need to present a valid proof of identity, which can cause delays.
- **Need help:** PhilHealth's 2026 circular guides list the Corporate Action Center at 02 8662 2588, with mobile lines 0998 857 2957 and 0968 865 4670 (Smart) and 0917 127 5987 and 0917 110 9812 (Globe). Numbers can change, so confirm on philhealth.gov.ph.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What documents do I need to register in PhilHealth?
A filled-out PMRF and at least one valid proof of identity. Other documents depend on your category, income and dependents.

### Do I need a birth certificate?
Not necessarily. PhilHealth accepts a birth or baptismal certificate with registry number or a valid government ID showing your date of birth.

### Do I need proof of income?
Most direct contributors do, except formally employed workers with no other income, sea-based migrant workers and lifetime members. For self-earning individuals, kasambahays and family drivers, a duly attested PMRF with self-declared income is sufficient.

### Can someone register for me?
Yes, with your signed authorization letter and one valid government photo ID of both you and your representative.

### Can I register online?
Self-paying members can use the PhilHealth Member Portal or the eGovPH Super App under Circular 2026-0011. Other categories should confirm available channels with PhilHealth.

### Do I need ID photos?
Some blogs say two 1×1 photos are required. The official circular and annexes I reviewed do not list photos. Follow your office's instruction.

### Is there a fee to register?
The official sources I reviewed do not state a fee, so I can't confirm one way or the other. Ask the office.

### Where do I get the PMRF?
From the Downloads page of PhilHealth's website or from a PhilHealth office.
