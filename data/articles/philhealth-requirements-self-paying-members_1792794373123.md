# PhilHealth Requirements for Self-Paying Members 2026: Registration, Income Proof and the SPA

A self-paying member is someone who personally pays their own PhilHealth premiums, such as a self-earning individual, a professional practitioner, or an overseas Filipino (other than seafarers and sea-based workers). Under PhilHealth Circular No. 2026-0011, you need to (1) register with a PIN and keep your records updated, (2) show proof of income, and (3) pay using a **Statement of Premium Account (SPA)**, the billing statement PhilHealth generates for you.

This guide is for freelancers, business owners, licensed professionals and overseas Filipinos who pay their own premiums. It reflects PhilHealth issuances reviewed in October 2026, including Circular 2026-0011 (self-paying members) and Circular 2026-0010 (one-time interest waiver for missed contributions). I do not give premium amounts, because the circular says your SPA is the basis for what you pay.

## Quick Answer: What Self-Paying Members Need

| Requirement | What PhilHealth's circular says | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Registration and PIN | Register, get a PhilHealth Identification Number (PIN), and choose a Primary Care Provider | **Required** |
| PMRF and valid ID | The PMRF is the registration form; first-time registrants need a valid proof of identity (Circular 2020-0001) | **Required for registration** |
| Updated records | Keep your monthly income, contact information and declared dependents current | **Required** |
| Proof of income | Any of the documents listed below, including a signed declaration on the PMRF | **Required** |
| Statement of Premium Account (SPA) | Needed for payment transactions; payments must match the SPA amount | **Required (transition in 2026; strict from January 1, 2027)** |
| Payment by December 31 | All premiums for the calendar year are due by December 31 of that year, with a grace period to January 31 | **Deadline** |

Source: PhilHealth Circular No. 2026-0011 and Circular No. 2020-0001.

## Who Counts as a Self-Paying Member?

Circular 2026-0011 defines self-paying members as those who personally pay their own premiums, such as:

- **Self-earning individuals:** people who render services or sell goods as a livelihood outside an employer-employee relationship.
- **Professional practitioners:** doctors, lawyers, certified public accountants and others who must pass a government licensure exam to practice.
- **Overseas Filipinos, except seafarers and other sea-based workers.** Circular 2026-0010 lists land-based migrant workers, Filipinos living abroad and dual citizens among those covered by the interest waiver.

PhilHealth says self-paying members get the same benefits as other members, including inpatient, outpatient, Z benefits and primary care benefits under YAKAP. Coverage depends on the applicable benefit package and eligibility requirements.

## Step-by-Step: How to Register or Update

1. **Prepare the PMRF** (and the Primary Care Selection Form, or PCSF, if you want to select a YAKAP clinic). Download from the Downloads page of PhilHealth's website or get one at a PhilHealth office.
2. **Prepare proof of identity.** A first-time registrant needs at least one valid proof of identity: a birth or baptismal certificate with registry number, or a valid government-issued ID showing date of birth.
3. **Declare your monthly income** and dependents on the PMRF.
4. **Choose how to submit.** Circular 2026-0011 allows:
   - submission of the PMRF and/or PCSF with supporting documents at any Local Health Insurance Office (LHIO), PhilHealth Express or accredited registration partner;
   - the PhilHealth Member Portal at memberinquiry.philhealth.gov.ph/member; or
   - the eGovPH App.
5. **Get your PIN and start using benefits.** Upon registration, self-paying members are granted immediate eligibility to program benefits.
6. **Update your records whenever your income, contact information or dependents change.** PhilHealth says this is essential for generating an accurate SPA.

## Proof of Income

### Self-earning individuals and professional practitioners

Any of these may serve as acceptable proof of income:

- BIR-received Income Tax Return (ITR);
- income declared, attested and duly signed in the PMRF (or its electronic form); or
- any documentary submission that the PhilHealth Regional Office will examine and validate.

### Overseas Filipinos (except seafarers and sea-based workers)

- Employment contract or certificate of employment with salary;
- payslips or remittance receipts;
- BIR-received ITR;
- income declared, attested and duly signed in the PMRF; or
- any other documentary submission to be validated by the PhilHealth Regional Office.

PhilHealth says that where the income you declare cannot be verified, or does not match your last premium payment, it will set up a verification and reconciliation process requiring you to provide updated income documents within a specified period.

## The Statement of Premium Account (SPA)

The SPA is a system-generated billing statement showing the reference number and total premium due for the covered period, including any interest and discounts.

- **When it is generated:** automatically on the first day of each applicable month, for all unpaid applicable months from January 2026 onward (or from your registration date, whichever is later).
- **Where you get it:** through your PhilHealth Member Portal account, and by notification to your declared email and/or contact number.
- **What it shows:** the premium due, due date, payment instructions, how interest accrues on late payments, and unpaid periods.
- **How the amount is set:** the circular says the premium on your SPA is primarily based on the last premium payment posted in PhilHealth's Contributions Database.
- **If you have no SPA or the amount is wrong:** generate a new one through the Member Portal or the SPA generator, or request an adjustment from any PhilHealth office.
- **Payment:** present the SPA when you pay. Premium payments must match the SPA amount. You can pay at an LHIO or an accredited collecting agent (ACA), nationwide or abroad, or online through the Member Portal or other available platforms.
- **Representatives:** a representative at a PhilHealth office needs your signed authorization letter, one valid government photo ID of you, and one valid government photo ID of the representative.

### When the SPA becomes strictly required

| Period | Rule |
|---|---|
| January 1 to December 31, 2026 (transition) | The SPA is the standard and preferred way to bill and pay. PhilHealth may accept payment without an SPA for first-time SPA users with system or record problems, members with pending income or record verification, or when SPA systems are unavailable. |
| From January 1, 2027 | A valid SPA becomes mandatory for all payment transactions across all channels, except authorized system downtime and exempted cases. |

Payments at ACAs in communities classified as Geographically Isolated and Disadvantaged Areas (GIDAs) by the DOH are exempt from the SPA requirement.

## Deadlines, Grace Period and Interest

- You can pay for any number of applicable months, but all premiums for the calendar year must be settled by **December 31** of that year.
- Self-paying members may settle unpaid premiums for the preceding year until **January 31** of the next year without interest. PhilHealth calls this a one-month grace period.
- After that, unpaid balances accrue interest, compounded monthly, **not exceeding 1.5%**, counted from the actual due date of December 31 of the applicable year.
- **Missing payments does not remove your benefits.** The circular says failure to pay premiums does not prevent the enjoyment of program benefits, but you are required to pay all missed contributions including interest. Intentional fraud or collusion to defraud the program can lead to suspension of benefit entitlement after due process.

## If You Missed Contributions Before 2026

Circular 2026-0011 left pre-2026 arrears to a separate policy. That policy is Circular 2026-0010, which provides a **one-time waiver of accrued interest** for self-paying members' late or missed contributions from **November 2019 to December 2025**.

**What the waiver does and does not do:**
- It waives interest only. You still owe the principal premium arrears, which you can pay in one payment or through an approved installment arrangement.
- Interest previously paid is not refunded.

**Conditions** (you must comply with all):
1. Pay all premium contributions due for 2026 and succeeding periods.
2. Empanel yourself and any qualified dependents under YAKAP, with proof of assignment to a Primary Care Provider (completing the First Patient Encounter and empanelment later within the period PhilHealth sets).
3. Update your member records, including declared monthly income and dependents.

**Documents to submit** to any PhilHealth Regional, Branch, LHIO or Express Office:
- Letter of request for interest waiver;
- valid government-issued ID;
- proof of premium payments for the current and succeeding years;
- proof of YAKAP assignment or selection for you and your dependents; and
- an updated PMRF with supporting documents, if applicable (revised income, corrected or new dependents, updated address and contact details).

**Maximum installment terms (no new interest while you stay compliant):**

| Months with arrears | Maximum term |
|---|---|
| 1 to 12 | 12 months |
| 13 to 24 | 24 months |
| 25 to 36 | 36 months |
| 37 to 48 | 48 months |
| More than 48 | 60 months |

**Deadline:** apply or submit your proposed installment arrangement until **December 31, 2027**, or within any later period PhilHealth sets. Waivers can be applied for starting in 2026, and PhilHealth says full and strict implementation begins January 1, 2027. If you fail to comply, the waiver can be revoked after notice, and the remaining arrears become immediately due with interest.

You can check your contribution record and any arrears through the Member Portal or the eGovPH app, or ask any LHIO. You may also request an adjustment of assessed arrears for specific months on grounds such as non-earning or category misclassification, with supporting documents.

## If You Cannot Afford to Pay

Circular 2026-0011 says members who cannot pay should be referred to a DSWD or LGU social worker, a medical social worker or another licensed social worker for a financial-capacity assessment. If assessed as having no capacity to pay, you submit a case study, a certificate of indigency or financial incapacity, or a similar document issued by the social worker to PhilHealth, to be classified as Point of Service – Financially Incapable (POS-FI) or Indirect Contributor – Financially Incapable (FI), as applicable.

**Note a difference in wording:** Circular 2026-0011 says a member with an outstanding balance from November 2019 onward who provides these documents "will be granted automatic exemption" from paying the current month and all earlier outstanding contributions, while Circular 2026-0010 says relief from assessed arrears "may be granted" upon PhilHealth's evaluation. Ask your LHIO how this applies to you.

## Examples

*These are illustrative examples, not official rulings.*

**Example 1: New freelancer.** A freelance illustrator registers through the eGovPH app, declares her monthly income on the electronic PMRF, and receives her PIN. A signed income declaration on the PMRF is among the accepted proofs of income. Each month, her SPA is generated and shows what she owes.

**Example 2: Overseas Filipino worker.** An OFW wants to register and pay for her premiums. She uses her employment contract or payslips as proof of income and pays using the SPA through the Member Portal.

**Example 3: Catching up on old arrears.** A member missed 20 months between 2021 and 2023. Under Circular 2026-0010, if he pays his 2026 premiums, updates his records and has himself and dependents assigned to a YAKAP clinic, he can request the interest waiver and an installment arrangement of up to 24 months for the principal.

## Common Problems

- **SPA amount looks wrong.** Generate a new SPA through the Member Portal or SPA generator, or request an adjustment at a PhilHealth office. The SPA is primarily based on your last posted premium payment, so outdated income can cause errors.
- **Payment refused without an SPA.** Beginning in 2027, payments without an SPA will not be accepted except authorized system downtime and exempted cases.
- **Paying an amount that does not match the SPA.** Payments must match the amount on the SPA.
- **Missing the December 31 deadline.** You have until January 31 of the next year before interest accrues from December 31.
- **Outdated records.** Update your income, contact details and dependents when they change.
- **Using false documents.** PhilHealth warns that misrepresentation can lead to denial or revocation of waivers and other penalties.

## What To Do If Something Goes Wrong

- **If you can't see your SPA,** use the Member Portal, the SPA generator at accredited collecting agents or LHIOs, or ask a PhilHealth office.
- **If your payment doesn't appear,** keep your payment receipt and SPA reference, and ask your LHIO to validate it.
- **If you can't pay,** ask a licensed social worker for an assessment (see above).
- **To contact PhilHealth,** the Corporate Action Center is listed as 02 8662 2588 in PhilHealth's 2026 circular guides. Numbers can change, so confirm on philhealth.gov.ph.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Who is a self-paying member?
Someone who personally pays their own PhilHealth premiums, such as a self-earning individual, professional practitioner, or overseas Filipino other than seafarers and sea-based workers.

### What do I need to register as a self-paying member?
A PMRF, valid proof of identity if you are registering for the first time, and your declared income. You can submit at an LHIO, PhilHealth Express or accredited registration partner, or use the Member Portal or eGovPH App.

### Is the SPA mandatory?
It is the standard and preferred mechanism in 2026 with limited exceptions, and becomes strictly required for all payments from January 1, 2027.

### When do I need to pay my premiums?
You can pay for any number of months, but the whole calendar year must be paid by December 31, with a grace period until January 31 of the next year for the previous year's unpaid premiums.

### Can I use PhilHealth if I have unpaid contributions?
Yes. The circular says failure to pay premiums does not prevent enjoyment of benefits, but you must pay the missed contributions with interest.

### Is there a waiver for my old unpaid contributions?
For November 2019 to December 2025, Circular 2026-0010 offers a one-time waiver of accrued interest (not the principal), with installment options, if you meet its conditions and apply by December 31, 2027 or any later period PhilHealth sets.

### How much is my premium?
Your SPA shows the amount due. The circular says it is primarily based on your last posted premium payment, and PhilHealth may ask for updated income documents if your declared income is not verifiable.

### Can someone pay or file for me?
A representative at a PhilHealth office needs your signed authorization letter, your valid photo ID and their own valid photo ID.
