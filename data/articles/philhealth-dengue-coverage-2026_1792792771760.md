# PhilHealth Dengue Coverage 2026: What It Pays and What You May Still Owe

PhilHealth pays for a dengue hospital admission through a fixed amount called a **case rate**. According to PhilHealth's own announcement, the rate is **₱19,500 for mild dengue** and **₱47,000 for severe dengue**. The severe dengue rate has applied since November 1, 2024.

This guide is for patients and families dealing with a dengue admission in 2026. It explains what the rates mean, how they are applied to a hospital bill, what PhilHealth's 2026 dengue circular does and does not say, and what to ask the hospital. It is not medical advice. Your doctor decides whether admission is needed and how the illness is classified.

## Quick Answer

| Dengue type (as PhilHealth describes it) | Case rate | Previous rate |
|---|---|---|
| Mild dengue | ₱19,500 | ₱10,000 |
| Severe dengue | ₱47,000 | ₱16,000 |

Keep these points in mind:

- The case rate is a **fixed payment**, not a promise that the whole bill is paid.
- The category (mild or severe) comes from the doctor's diagnosis as coded by the hospital, not from the patient.
- PhilHealth's announcements use the terms "mild" and "severe." The sources we reviewed do not clearly say how dengue **with warning signs** is classified for payment, so ask the hospital's PhilHealth desk which rate applies to the diagnosis on your chart.
- PhilHealth rules change by circular. Confirm the rate at admission.

## What Is a PhilHealth Case Rate?

A case rate is a pre-set peso amount PhilHealth pays for a specific diagnosis. Under the **All Case Rates** system, the hospital is paid a fixed amount per treated case instead of being paid for each service.

For an inpatient admission, the claim is filed by the hospital, not by the patient. PhilHealth's 2026 guidance on inpatient claims says the facility applies the benefit to the patient's bill and files the claim for the member or dependent. A patient cannot file the claim directly for these admissions.

## PhilHealth Dengue Coverage by Severity

### Mild dengue: ₱19,500

PhilHealth's February 18, 2025 announcement lists mild dengue coverage at ₱19,500, up from ₱10,000. PhilHealth said the new dengue package rates were among those enhanced and rationalized in 2024. PhilHealth also raised most case rates by 50% for admissions starting January 1, 2025 (Circular No. 2024-0037). We could not open the annex text to confirm the individual dengue row, so check the Case Rate Search on philhealth.gov.ph or ask the hospital for the code and rate that applies.

### Severe dengue: ₱47,000

PhilHealth Circular No. 2024-0025, "Implementing Guidelines on the Adjusted Rates for Severe Dengue," is on PhilHealth's official 2024 circular list with a publication date of November 1, 2024. A news report on the circular says the increase from ₱16,000 to ₱47,000 took effect that day. PhilHealth's own February 2025 announcement describes the severe dengue increase as 193%.

The same news report notes that attending physicians are required to inform patients of the minimum standards of care for severe dengue and of any out-of-pocket or co-payment charges that come from amenities.

## What PhilHealth's 2026 Dengue Circular Says

PhilHealth Circular No. 2026-0009, "Quality Standards on the Diagnosis and Management of Dengue (Adult and Children) as a Reference of the Corporation," is listed on PhilHealth's website with a publication date of July 10, 2026. It takes effect 15 days after publication.

Important points for readers:

- **It is a quality-of-care policy.** It sets minimum standards for diagnosing and managing dengue in hospitalized patients. The circular text we reviewed does not state these standards as a condition for payment, but it does carry a penalty clause for violations under the Universal Health Care Act and the National Health Insurance Act.
- **It describes who may be managed at home and who is expected to be admitted.** Patients without warning signs may be observed at home, but must be taken to a hospital if there is no improvement and any listed warning sign appears. Patients with certain coexisting conditions, such as pregnancy, infancy, old age, or comorbidities, and those living alone or far from medical facilities are expected to be admitted. Patients with severe dengue need emergency treatment at a healthcare facility. This is a description of the policy, not advice for any specific patient.
- **It lists discharge criteria.** Among them: fever-free for at least 48 hours without fever-reducing medicine, no longer needing intravenous fluids, stable vital signs, and a platelet count above 50,000 per cubic millimeter. Your doctor decides when discharge is appropriate.

## Admissions Shorter Than 24 Hours

PhilHealth Circular No. 2026-0013 (published September 3, 2026) expands payment under **existing** medical case rates for qualified admissions that end in less than 24 hours because the patient died or was transferred to another facility. PhilHealth says it is not a new benefit or case rate.

Under that circular:

- Payment is based on actual charges, and does not exceed the applicable case rate. If actual charges are lower, only the actual charges are paid.
- Patients discharged as improved or recovered, those who leave against medical advice, and those who abscond are not covered under it.
- Admissions longer than 24 hours stay under the existing All Case Rates system.

## Will PhilHealth Pay the Whole Hospital Bill?

Not necessarily. The case rate is a fixed amount. If the bill is higher, the difference may be the patient's responsibility unless another policy applies.

Two policies can reduce what a patient pays:

- **No co-payment policy.** In January 2025, PhilHealth asked partner hospitals to strictly follow the no co-payment policy under Section 9 of the Universal Health Care Act. PhilHealth said members should not be charged additional fees beyond what PhilHealth covers for the standard of care included in the benefit packages.
- **No Balance Billing (NBB).** PhilHealth's 2025 benefits material describes NBB as no other fees being charged to the patient beyond the package rate, and says it covers only confinements in basic or ward accommodations. The sources we reviewed do not fully list which members qualify, so confirm with PhilHealth.

If you choose a private room or request amenities beyond the standard of care, expect charges outside the case rate.

## Who Is Eligible?

Case rates apply to PhilHealth members and their qualified dependents admitted to PhilHealth-accredited facilities. The sources we reviewed do not clearly state the current contribution requirements for inpatient claims, so we are not listing a specific number of paid months. Check your membership and contribution status with PhilHealth before admission when possible.

The hospital must be **PhilHealth-accredited**. Provider information can change. Check the official PhilHealth provider directory before visiting.

## What To Do at Admission and Discharge

1. **Tell the admitting desk you are a PhilHealth member** and bring your PhilHealth Identification Number (PIN) or Member Data Record (MDR).
2. **Ask which case rate applies** and how the diagnosis will be coded.
3. **Ask about room type** and whether it affects the No Balance Billing policy.
4. **Ask the hospital to explain any charges** for amenities before you accept them.
5. **Request an itemized Statement of Account** at discharge showing the PhilHealth deduction.
6. **Keep copies** of your admission and discharge papers and your billing statement.

## Common Problems

- **The deduction is lower than expected.** Older figures (₱10,000 and ₱16,000) still appear on many websites. They are out of date.
- **Extra charges despite being a member.** Ask whether the items are part of the standard package or optional amenities, and whether your room type affects the policy.
- **Confusion over mild vs. severe.** The category depends on the diagnosis recorded in the medical record. Ask the PhilHealth desk to explain how your admission was coded.
- **A transfer or very short stay.** See Circular No. 2026-0013 above, and ask the hospital which rules apply.

## What To Do If Something Goes Wrong

- Ask the billing office for a written explanation of the charges and the PhilHealth deduction.
- Contact the PhilHealth Corporate Action Center at **(02) 8662-2588** or **actioncenter@philhealth.gov.ph**, the contact details listed in PhilHealth's 2026 circular guidance. Contact details can change, so check philhealth.gov.ph.
- Keep your records in case you need to ask for a review.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How much does PhilHealth cover for dengue in 2026?
PhilHealth's announcements list ₱19,500 for mild dengue and ₱47,000 for severe dengue. We found no later official change to those amounts, but verify with PhilHealth.

### Does PhilHealth pay the entire hospital bill for dengue?
No. PhilHealth pays a fixed case rate. Any difference may be the patient's responsibility unless a policy such as No Balance Billing applies.

### What is the difference between mild and severe dengue for PhilHealth?
They are separate benefit amounts. The doctor's diagnosis and the hospital's coding decide which applies. The sources we reviewed do not say how dengue with warning signs is classified for payment, so ask the hospital.

### Why do some websites show ₱10,000 or ₱16,000?
Those were the earlier rates. PhilHealth's announcement describes mild dengue moving from ₱10,000 to ₱19,500 and severe dengue from ₱16,000 to ₱47,000.

### Does the 2026 dengue circular change how much PhilHealth pays?
Circular No. 2026-0009 sets quality standards for dengue care. The text we reviewed does not set or change a case rate.

### Can I file the claim myself?
For inpatient admissions, the hospital files the claim and applies the benefit to your bill.

### Does PhilHealth cover outpatient care for dengue symptoms?
In February 2025, PhilHealth told members to visit their chosen Konsulta Package Provider if they have dengue symptoms, saying the package is free for members and dependents. PhilHealth's 2026 circular list also includes a YAKAP program. The sources we reviewed do not explain how the two relate now, so check with PhilHealth.

### Can I use PhilHealth at any hospital?
Use it at a PhilHealth-accredited facility. Provider information can change. Check the official PhilHealth provider directory before visiting.
