# PhilHealth Breast Cancer Benefit 2026: What the Z Package Covers and How to Avail It

The PhilHealth breast cancer benefit is a **Z benefit package** of **up to ₱1.4 million** per patient for one cycle of care. It covers patients in **stages 0 to 4**. The amount is paid to a **contracted health facility**, not to the patient, and it is a maximum, not a guaranteed amount. How much is used depends on the doctor's treatment plan.

This guide is for PhilHealth members, qualified dependents and their families. It explains PhilHealth's benefit rules only. It is not medical advice, and PhilHealth does not decide which treatment a patient needs. The treating doctor does. Coverage depends on the applicable benefit package and eligibility requirements.

## Quick Answer

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Maximum amount | Up to ₱1.4 million for one cycle of care |
| Previous amount | ₱100,000 |
| Stages covered | Stages 0 to 4 (the previous package covered stages 0 to IIIA) |
| Who can avail | PhilHealth members or their qualified dependents |
| Where | Contracted health facilities with a Z Benefits Coordinator |
| Who submits the papers | The contracted hospital, not the patient or family |
| Effective date reported | March 30, 2024 (PhilHealth Circular No. 2024-0007, as reported by the Philippine Information Agency) |

A **Z benefit package** is a special PhilHealth package for serious and costly conditions. It is separate from the regular **case rates**, which are fixed amounts PhilHealth pays an accredited hospital for a particular condition or procedure.

## What Is the PhilHealth Breast Cancer Z Benefit?

It is a package that pays for the main parts of breast cancer care: diagnosis, surgery, chemotherapy, hormonal therapy, targeted therapy and follow-up monitoring. PhilHealth raised it from ₱100,000 to up to ₱1.4 million, an increase PhilHealth described as 1,300%.

PhilHealth's October 2025 announcement says it covers patients in stages 0 to 4, **including those already undergoing treatment**, and that ₱72.03 million had been paid under the package as of September 2025.

## What Does the Package Cover?

The package amount is built from components. The ranges below were published by the Philippine Information Agency when the package took effect. They vary with the doctor's recommendation and the patient's condition.

| Component | Amount range |
|---|---|
| Diagnostic tests and prognostication (assessing likely outcome) | ₱1,000 to ₱10,000 |
| Surgery (partial mastectomy up to modified radical mastectomy) | ₱30,000 to ₱140,000 |
| Hormonal therapy (hormonotherapy) | ₱2,700 or ₱18,000 |
| Cytotoxic chemotherapy | ₱43,000 to ₱185,010 |
| Targeted therapy | More than ₱1 million in total; ₱333,336 for a maximum of 18 cycles |
| Follow-up monitoring (surveillance), basic | ₱5,000 |
| Follow-up monitoring (surveillance), specific | ₱9,000 |

**Important:** These are component ranges from the 2024 rollout. The sources reviewed do not say whether any figure was changed later, and they do not explain how the components add up to the ₱1.4 million ceiling for a given patient. Ask the hospital's Z Benefits Coordinator for the current schedule that applies to your case.

## Who Is Eligible?

- **Members and qualified dependents.** PhilHealth's announcement says the package is for members or their qualified dependents. A **qualified dependent** is a family member who is recognized as a dependent in the member's PhilHealth record.
- **Stage 0 to 4 breast cancer**, as diagnosed and staged by the treating doctor.
- **Treated at a contracted facility.** The package is delivered through hospitals that have an agreement with PhilHealth for this package.

The sources reviewed do not clearly specify other conditions, such as contribution requirements or exact clinical criteria. Older PhilHealth benefit listings mention a signed **Member Empowerment (ME) form**, a consent form signed by the patient. Ask the coordinator whether it applies to you.

## Step-by-Step: How to Avail the Breast Cancer Z Benefit

1. **Get a diagnosis and stage from your doctor.** Package use depends on the diagnosis.
2. **Go to a contracted health facility.** PhilHealth's list of contracted facilities is published on its website. The Philippine Information Agency article named East Avenue Medical Center in Quezon City and the Philippine General Hospital in Manila as two Metro Manila examples. Check the current list, since it can change.
3. **Look for the Z Benefits Coordinator.** This staff member explains the process and coordinates with PhilHealth.
4. **Let the hospital submit the documents.** According to the 2024 PIA report, the contracted hospital, not the patient or family, submits the documentary requirements to PhilHealth.
5. **Wait for PhilHealth's approval.** After approval, the doctor may provide the mandatory services under the package.
6. **Keep copies** of forms, results and statements of account.

The sources reviewed do not give a processing time, so do not assume one.

## Breast Cancer Screening: Covered Separately Under YAKAP

Screening is not part of the Z package. It falls under **YAKAP** (Yaman ng Kalusugan Program), PhilHealth's primary care program. PhilHealth's October 2025 announcement lists these outpatient screening rates:

| Screening test | PhilHealth rate |
|---|---|
| Mammogram | ₱2,610 |
| Breast ultrasound | ₱1,350 |

A July 2025 PhilHealth announcement said members could get cancer screening free of charge at select hospitals with a valid prescription from their accredited primary care provider. A June 2026 news report on Cebu provincial hospitals said new mammography machines were awaiting operating licenses and that reimbursement under YAKAP was expected, but it gave no amounts or start date. Confirm with your YAKAP clinic whether a given facility already offers the service.

## Costs: What You May Still Pay

The Z package pays a set amount to the facility. The sources reviewed do not clearly state what out-of-pocket charges may remain for the breast cancer package. A PNA report on the October 2025 increase said no co-payment applies to patients admitted in basic or ward-type accommodation. Charges for upgraded rooms, services outside the package or items the package does not cover may still apply.

**Example (illustration only, not real billing):** A patient with an early-stage diagnosis is approved for the package. The doctor's plan uses only part of the ₱1.4 million ceiling. PhilHealth pays the facility the package amounts for the services actually provided, and the patient is billed only for items outside the package. Ask for a written breakdown.

## Common Problems

- **The hospital is not a contracted facility.** The Z package is delivered through contracted facilities, so confirm before treatment starts.
- **You were told the amount is ₱100,000.** That is the old package. The current package is up to ₱1.4 million.
- **You were told only stages 0 to IIIA qualify.** That was the old rule. PhilHealth now says stages 0 to 4.
- **You are already in treatment.** PhilHealth says patients already undergoing treatment are covered. Ask the coordinator how your case is handled.
- **You expect ₱1.4 million to be available to every patient.** It is a maximum. The amount depends on the treatment plan and package components.
- **Screening is not available nearby.** YAKAP screening depends on the facility, so confirm with your YAKAP clinic.

## What To Do If Something Goes Wrong

1. Ask the Z Benefits Coordinator or billing desk to explain in writing how the package was applied.
2. Contact PhilHealth. Its October 2025 announcement lists these 24/7 numbers: (02) 866-225-88, 0998-857-2957, 0968-865-4670, 0917-1275987 and 0917-1109812. Check PhilHealth's official channels for current contact details before calling.
3. Keep your forms, receipts and statements of account.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How much does PhilHealth pay for breast cancer?
Up to ₱1.4 million for one cycle of care under the Z benefit, for stages 0 to 4.

### Does PhilHealth cover breast cancer stage 4?
Yes. PhilHealth says the package covers stages 0 to 4. The earlier package covered stages 0 to IIIA only.

### Does PhilHealth cover chemotherapy for breast cancer?
Yes, chemotherapy is a component of the package. The 2024 PIA report listed ₱43,000 to ₱185,010 for cytotoxic chemotherapy.

### Does PhilHealth cover mastectomy?
Surgery is part of the package, ranging from partial mastectomy to modified radical mastectomy, with a reported range of ₱30,000 to ₱140,000.

### Does PhilHealth cover targeted therapy?
Yes. The 2024 report listed targeted therapy at more than ₱1 million in total, or ₱333,336 for a maximum of 18 cycles.

### Does PhilHealth cover mammograms?
Under YAKAP, PhilHealth lists ₱2,610 for a mammogram and ₱1,350 for a breast ultrasound. Availability depends on the facility.

### Is the money paid to me?
No. It is paid to the contracted health facility.

### Who submits the requirements?
According to the PIA report, the contracted hospital does.

### Can dependents use it?
Yes. PhilHealth says members or qualified dependents can avail.

### Do I still pay anything?
It depends. PNA reported no co-payment for basic or ward accommodation. Other charges may apply, so ask the hospital for a written estimate.
