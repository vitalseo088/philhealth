## PhilHealth Normal Delivery 2026: How Much It Covers and Who Qualifies

Here's the direct answer: as of the update that took effect around April 30, 2026, **PhilHealth covers a normal (vaginal) delivery at ₱29,000 when it happens in an accredited Level 1 to Level 3 hospital, or ₱14,000 when it happens in an accredited non-hospital birthing facility** such as a birthing home, lying-in clinic, or rural health unit/primary care facility. The amount is deducted directly from your bill — it is not paid to you in cash.

This guide is for PhilHealth members and their dependents planning a vaginal delivery in **2026**, and explains the current rates, eligibility, what's included, and how the benefit is actually applied at the facility. It's based on two official PhilHealth Circulars: **No. 2026-0005** (covering hospital-based delivery) and **No. 2026-0006** (covering non-hospital and outpatient maternity care), both published in 2026.

> **Important limitation:** The hospital-based rate (₱29,000) and the non-hospital rate (₱14,000) are **not interchangeable** — they come from two different circulars covering two different facility types. Some online sources blend these into a single "₱29,000 for any delivery" statement, which is inaccurate. Which rate applies to you depends entirely on where you deliver.

### Quick Answer

| Delivery Setting | Benefit Code | Case Rate (₱) | Governing Circular |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hospital (Level 1–3), Normal Spontaneous Delivery | NSD01 | 29,000 | PhilHealth Circular No. 2026-0005 |
| Non-hospital birthing facility (birthing home, lying-in clinic, infirmary, PCF) | NSDBF | 14,000 | PhilHealth Circular No. 2026-0006 |

Both amounts cover the intrapartum period (labor and delivery) and immediate postpartum care before discharge. They do not automatically include everything you might be billed for — see "What's Included" below.

## What Is the PhilHealth Normal Delivery Benefit?

The **Normal Spontaneous Delivery (NSD) Benefit** is a PhilHealth case rate — a fixed, pre-set amount PhilHealth pays an accredited facility for managing a normal delivery. Per the official definition in both circulars, it "covers only health services in the intrapartum period and immediate post-partum period for normal delivery, regardless of the type of health care institution," and includes monitoring and management of labor, assistance in delivery, and immediate post-partum care before discharge.

The current amounts were set through two separate 2026 issuances:

- **PhilHealth Circular No. 2026-0005** — *Rationalization of Case Rates for Select Maternal and Gynecologic Services in the Hospital Setting* — sets the ₱29,000 rate for hospitals.
- **PhilHealth Circular No. 2026-0006** — *Expansion of Maternity Care Benefits for Non-Hospital and Outpatient Facilities (Revision 1)* — sets the ₱14,000 rate (benefit code NSDBF) for birthing homes, lying-in clinics, infirmaries, and other non-hospital birthing facilities, and also unbundled the wider Maternity Care Package into separate antenatal, intrapartum, postpartum, and emergency-referral components.

Both circulars took effect for admissions/availment upon their publication, and repeal the relevant provisions of the older PhilHealth Circular No. 025, s. 2015.

## Who Is Eligible?

Per PhilHealth Circular No. 2026-0006, **"All pregnant persons enrolled in the National Health Insurance Program (NHIP) shall be immediately eligible to avail of PhilHealth benefits, in accordance with the Universal Health Care Act (UHC Act),"** referencing PhilHealth Circular No. 2022-0013 (Granting of Immediate Eligibility to Filipino Citizens). The same circular also notes that pregnant persons below 21 years old and their infants remain eligible under existing PhilHealth policies on membership and eligibility.

PhilHealth Circular No. 2026-0005 (the hospital-based circular) states more generally that **"All Filipinos are automatically entitled to avail of this benefits package," and that "PhilHealth beneficiaries shall comply with the existing membership eligibility rules."**

In practical terms:

- You must be an active PhilHealth member, or a qualified dependent of one.
- Delivery must take place in a **PhilHealth-accredited facility** — direct filing is not allowed for delivery at a non-accredited facility except in specific exceptions (see below).
- A printed Member Data Record (MDR) is not required for the hospital-based benefit; hospitals are expected to verify eligibility electronically.

**On a specific contribution-count requirement** (e.g., a fixed number of monthly premiums paid within a set period before delivery): neither PC No. 2026-0005 nor PC No. 2026-0006 restates a specific contribution-month rule for the NSD benefit itself. Other online sources cite varying figures for maternity benefits generally (some cite three months of contributions within six months, others cite nine months within twelve, and others describe the UHC Act as removing contribution-count conditions). Because these figures conflict and are not confirmed in the two governing circulars for this specific benefit, **verify your own contribution and eligibility status directly through the PhilHealth Member Portal or a PhilHealth office** before your due date rather than relying on a specific number from an unofficial source.

## Normal Delivery Case Rates and What They Cover

### 1. Hospital-Based Normal Delivery (PC No. 2026-0005)

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Benefit code | NSD01 |
| Case rate | ₱29,000 |
| Applies to | Accredited Level 1 to Level 3 hospitals |
| Co-payment, basic/ward accommodation | None — no balance billing for covered items |
| Co-payment cap, semi-private accommodation | ₱23,000 |
| Private/suite accommodation | Not capped — costs beyond the package are out-of-pocket |

Under the "no co-payment" policy for basic/ward accommodation, an accredited hospital **cannot bill you** in excess of the ₱29,000 case rate for medicines, procedures, supplies, professional fees, or salaried labor already covered by the package, if you stay in a shared ward-type bed.

### 2. Non-Hospital Normal Delivery (PC No. 2026-0006)

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Benefit code | NSDBF |
| Case rate | ₱14,000 |
| Applies to | Accredited birthing homes/lying-in clinics, infirmaries, DOH-licensed Primary Care Facilities with a birthing "One Stop Shop" |
| Restriction | **Only low-risk NSD is reimbursable** in non-hospital birthing facilities — high-risk pregnancies must be managed in a hospital |
| Documentation required | A properly completed partograph (labor progress chart) is a mandatory claims requirement |

If complications arise during labor and a referral to a hospital becomes necessary, the birthing facility can separately claim an **Emergency Stabilization and Step-Up Referral** benefit:

| Stage of Complication | Benefit Code | Rate (₱) |
|---|---|---|
| Referral due to complication during 1st stage of labor | NSD1L | 1,600 |
| Referral due to complication during 2nd stage of labor | NSD2L | 8,500 |
| Referral due to complication during 3rd stage of labor / immediate postpartum | NSD3L | 14,700 |

These referral benefits are only claimable for genuine labor/delivery emergencies — not for pre-existing medical conditions identified before labor began.

## Prenatal (Antenatal) and Postpartum Benefits That Go With Delivery

Under PC No. 2026-0006, antenatal care (ANC) is unbundled into per-trimester packages, claimable when the mandatory visits for that trimester are completed:

| Visit Package | Timing | Benefit Code | Number of Visits Covered | Rate (₱) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1st Trimester | 1–13 weeks | ANV01 | 1 | 1,100 |
| 2nd Trimester | 14–27 weeks | ANV02 | 2 | 1,500 |
| 3rd Trimester | 28–40 weeks | ANV03 | 5 | 3,750 |
| Tetanus vaccine, 1st dose | 20–36 weeks | TDAP1 | 1 | 150 |
| Tetanus vaccine, 2nd dose | 27–36 weeks | TDAP2 | 1 | 150 |

Diagnostic antenatal tests (blood typing, CBC, urinalysis, HBsAg, HIV and syphilis screening, OGTT, and up to two ultrasounds) are covered separately under fixed diagnostic packages (ANC1–ANC3 and ultrasound codes ANS1/ANS2), bundled as a complete set per visit window.

After delivery, **three postpartum visits within six weeks of delivery** are reimbursable under benefit code PPC01, at a combined rate of ₱1,350, covering supplement provision, family planning counseling, lactation and nutrition guidance, and mental health screening and support.

## Step-by-Step: How the Benefit Is Applied

1. **Choose an accredited facility** — confirm during prenatal visits whether you plan to deliver at a hospital or a non-hospital birthing facility, and confirm its PhilHealth accreditation for maternity case rates.
2. **Attend your antenatal visits and keep your Antenatal Care Record (or Mother-Baby Book).** This documentation is part of the claims requirement for both antenatal and delivery benefits.
3. **Deliver at the accredited facility.** For non-hospital facilities, only low-risk deliveries can be managed there; higher-risk cases should be referred to a hospital in advance.
4. **The facility verifies your PhilHealth eligibility and prepares the claim**, including the partograph (for non-hospital NSD claims) or the operative/labor record (for hospital NSD claims).
5. **The applicable case rate — ₱29,000 or ₱14,000 — is deducted from your Statement of Account (SOA)** before you're billed for the remainder.
6. **You pay only the balance, if any.** In basic/ward accommodation, no balance billing applies for services already covered by the case rate.
7. **The facility files the claim with PhilHealth electronically**, generally within the standard filing period. In the normal scenario, you do not file the claim yourself.

## Exceptions: When You File the Claim Yourself

Direct filing by the member is generally not required — the accredited facility files on your behalf. The main exceptions carried over into these circulars are **confinement abroad** and **emergency cases handled at a non-accredited facility**. PhilHealth will not cover delivery at a non-accredited facility under ordinary circumstances, so confirming accreditation before your due date matters.

## Common Problems and What to Do

**You were billed for something the case rate should have covered, in a basic/ward bed.**
This is a no-balance-billing violation. Report it to PhilHealth's Corporate Action Center via hotline (02) 8662-2588 or email actioncenter@philhealth.gov.ph.

**You were asked to buy supplements, medicines, or supplies the package should include.**
You are entitled to a full refund from the facility for essential items you paid for out-of-pocket that were supposed to be part of the covered package. Keep your official receipts.

**Your birthing home says your pregnancy is now high-risk and you need to transfer.**
This is expected: only low-risk deliveries are reimbursable in non-hospital birthing facilities. A step-up referral to a hospital, with the appropriate Emergency Stabilization and Step-Up Referral benefit claimed by the referring facility, is the normal process — it is not a denial of your PhilHealth coverage.

**You're not sure whether you meet a contribution requirement.**
As noted above, the governing circulars do not spell out a specific contribution-count rule for this benefit, and other sources disagree. Confirm your record directly with PhilHealth before your due date.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Does PhilHealth cover the full cost of a normal delivery?**
Not automatically. PhilHealth coverage depends on the applicable benefit package and eligibility requirements. The case rate (₱29,000 in hospitals, ₱14,000 in birthing facilities) is deducted from your bill; whether this fully offsets your bill depends on your facility's actual charges and your room/accommodation type.

**Can I choose a birthing home instead of a hospital to save money?**
That decision should be based on medical guidance, not cost alone — only low-risk pregnancies are eligible for delivery (and PhilHealth reimbursement) in a non-hospital birthing facility. High-risk pregnancies require hospital-level care.

**Are prenatal check-ups covered separately from the delivery itself?**
Yes. Antenatal care is unbundled into its own per-trimester benefit packages under PC No. 2026-0006, separate from the delivery (NSD) benefit itself.

**Do I need to file a claim form myself?**
Generally no — the accredited facility files the claim and deducts the benefit automatically. You would only file directly in specific exceptions, such as confinement abroad or an emergency at a non-accredited facility.

**What if my delivery happened before the 2026 rate increase took effect?**
The circulars apply to admissions/availment upon their effectivity date. Deliveries before that date would fall under the previously applicable rates, not the current ones.

**Is a Cesarean section covered under the same rate as normal delivery?**
No — Cesarean delivery is covered under a separate, higher case rate structure under PC No. 2026-0005, distinct from the Normal Spontaneous Delivery benefit described here.
