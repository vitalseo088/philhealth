# PhilHealth Laboratory Benefits 2026: What's Covered and How to Use Them

In 2026, PhilHealth pays for laboratory tests in three main ways. Outpatient, it covers a list of 13 lab tests through your **YAKAP Clinic**, plus six cancer screening tests at accredited screening facilities. In the hospital, lab work is bundled into the fixed **case rate** for your admission. In every outpatient case, the doctor's order and your registration with a clinic matter as much as the test itself.

This guide is for PhilHealth members and their dependents who want to know which tests are covered, what they need to do first, and what to check before paying anything out of pocket. It explains PhilHealth's rules, not medical advice. Whether you need a particular test is a decision for your doctor.

> **Before you rely on the numbers below:** media reports disagree on how many YAKAP lab tests there are (13, 19 and 20 all appear). We used the 13-test list published by PhilHealth's regional office and cross-checked it against PhilHealth's earlier Konsulta benefit table. See [Where sources disagree](#where-sources-disagree). Confirm the current list with your YAKAP Clinic.

## Quick Answer

| Where you get the test | What PhilHealth covers | What you generally need |
|---|---|---|
| **YAKAP Clinic (outpatient)** | 13 lab and diagnostic tests (listed below) | Registered clinic, completed First Patient Encounter, and a YAKAP physician's order |
| **Accredited cancer screening facility (outpatient)** | Mammogram, breast ultrasound, low-dose chest CT, alpha-fetoprotein (AFP), liver ultrasound, colonoscopy | A screening request form from your YAKAP physician and meeting the eligibility criteria |
| **Hospital admission** | Diagnostics and laboratories are included in the case rate | An accredited hospital; what you pay depends on the type of room and the rules for your case |
| **Pregnancy (hospital setting)** | Extra antenatal diagnostics claimable under a fee schedule on top of the basic package | An accredited hospital and the attending doctor's determination that the tests are needed |

## What Are PhilHealth Laboratory Benefits?

"PhilHealth laboratory benefits" is not one single benefit. It is a set of benefits that happen to include lab work. A few terms help here:

- **YAKAP (Yaman ng Kalusugan Program)** is PhilHealth's primary care benefit package. PhilHealth describes it as an evolution of the Konsulta program. Earlier issuances called it by other names, including Primary Care Benefit 1, Expanded Primary Care Benefit, PhilHealth Tsekap and PhilHealth Konsulta. If you see "Konsulta" on an older page, YAKAP is its successor.
- **YAKAP Clinic** is the PhilHealth-accredited public or private primary care provider you choose and register with. PhilHealth's circulars also call it a Primary Care Clinic.
- **First Patient Encounter (FPE)** is your first contact with the clinic for the year, where the clinic collects or updates basic health data to identify your health risks. PhilHealth says the FPE is not yet a consultation.
- **Empanelment** is the formal care relationship between you and your clinic. It starts after your FPE and the signing of a **Mutual Care Agreement**, also called the **YAKAP Empanelment Slip (YES)**.
- **Case rate** is a fixed amount PhilHealth pays a hospital for a specific illness or procedure. PhilHealth's case rate policy says it covers professional fees and facility charges, including room and board, diagnostics and laboratories, and drugs and supplies.

## Who Is Eligible?

PhilHealth's circular on outpatient cancer screening applies to all Filipinos and other qualified PhilHealth beneficiaries who are registered with a PhilHealth primary care provider. The YAKAP selection rules apply to members and qualified dependents. Dependents choose a clinic too, and a parent or legal guardian handles the transactions of a minor. Beneficiaries aged 18 and above can transact on their own.

The YAKAP circulars we reviewed do not spell out contribution-payment conditions for these outpatient benefits. A Philippine Information Agency (PIA) feature advises members to check that their contributions are up to date before registering. If you are unsure of your status, check your record through the PhilHealth Member Portal or your Local Health Insurance Office (LHIO).

## YAKAP Laboratory Tests Covered in 2026

PhilHealth's Region 10 office, as reported by PIA, listed the following 13 tests under YAKAP. The short descriptions are general explanations of what each test is, not guidance on whether you need it.

| # | Test | What it is (plain language) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Complete Blood Count (CBC) with platelet count | Counts blood cells and platelets |
| 2 | Lipid profile | Total cholesterol, HDL and LDL cholesterol, and triglycerides |
| 3 | Fasting Blood Sugar (FBS) | Blood sugar level after fasting |
| 4 | Oral Glucose Tolerance Test (OGTT) | Blood sugar measured before and after a sugar drink |
| 5 | Glycosylated Hemoglobin (HbA1c) | Average blood sugar over recent weeks |
| 6 | Creatinine | Blood test often used to look at kidney function |
| 7 | Chest X-ray | Imaging of the chest |
| 8 | Sputum microscopy | Examination of a sputum (phlegm) sample |
| 9 | Electrocardiogram (ECG) | Recording of the heart's electrical activity |
| 10 | Urinalysis | Urine test |
| 11 | Pap smear | Cervical cell sample |
| 12 | Fecalysis | Stool examination |
| 13 | Fecal Occult Blood Test (FOBT) | Stool test for hidden blood |

**These tests are not an automatic yearly panel.** PhilHealth officials have said a doctor's referral or prescription is required for laboratory services, cancer screening tests and medicines, and the tests are made available based on the patient's needs. PhilHealth's YAKAP clinical standards describe clinics as delivering "primary care consultations, laboratory tests and medicines" as part of their minimum services. In practice, your YAKAP physician decides which tests to order after your assessment.

If your clinic has no laboratory of its own, PhilHealth's earlier Konsulta benefit table says clinics assist beneficiaries in accessing services at subcontracted or partner facilities. Ask your clinic where your tests will be done and confirm that it is covered before you go.

## PhilHealth Cancer Screening Tests: Rates and Eligibility

PhilHealth added six outpatient cancer screening tests under YAKAP. PhilHealth Circular 2025-0014, published July 30, 2025 and effective 15 days after publication, sets the package rates. The rates below are what PhilHealth pays the screening facility.

| Test | Screens for | PhilHealth package rate |
|---|---|---|
| Mammogram | Breast cancer | ₱2,610 |
| Breast ultrasound | Breast cancer | ₱1,350 |
| Low-dose chest CT scan | Lung cancer | ₱7,220 |
| Alpha-fetoprotein (AFP) blood test | Liver cancer | ₱1,230 |
| Liver ultrasound | Liver cancer | ₱960 |
| Colonoscopy (as a screening test) | Colorectal cancer | ₱23,640 |

PhilHealth's circular states there is **no co-payment and no balance billing** for all PhilHealth beneficiaries in public and private facilities. Facilities are paid a fixed rate whether their charges are higher or lower. Patients with breast symptoms, such as a lump, pain or nipple discharge, are also covered under this package.

### Who qualifies for each screening?

In November 2025, PhilHealth published separate quality standards for each cancer. They amend the original eligibility table in Circular 2025-0014 where the two conflict. Your YAKAP physician makes the actual eligibility assessment, which must be documented.

**Breast (Circular 2025-0024)**
- Target groups: asymptomatic average-risk women aged 50 to 69; asymptomatic women aged 30 to 49 at high risk (a first-degree relative diagnosed at 49 or younger); women aged 40 to 49 with identified risk factors; or anyone with breast symptoms.
- Mammogram: every two years for women aged 40 to 69.
- Breast ultrasound: may be done yearly for high-risk women aged 40 to 69, together with a mammogram.

**Lung (Circular 2025-0027)**
- Target group: asymptomatic adults aged 50 to 80 who have either at least 30 pack-years of smoking and currently smoke or quit within the past 15 years, or a first-degree family history of lung cancer with a confirmed smoking history.
- Low-dose chest CT: every 12 months.

**Colorectal (Circular 2025-0025)**
- Target group: adults aged 45 to 75, or people with risk factors such as a first-degree relative with colorectal cancer or polyps, or certain underlying conditions.
- PhilHealth's standard names the fecal immunochemical test (FIT) as the preferred first-line stool test, repeated every 12 months. Colonoscopy may be done at the latest within nine months after a positive FIT or FOBT. If a colonoscopy is negative and you remain asymptomatic, screening may resume with a stool test 10 years later.
- *Unclear:* the 13-test YAKAP list names "fecal occult blood test", while the colorectal standard prefers FIT. The sources we reviewed do not say how a clinic handles FIT under the lab component. Ask your clinic.

**Liver (Circular 2025-0026)**
- Target group: people with identified risk factors, namely chronic hepatitis B or C in men aged 40 and above or women aged 50 and above, cirrhosis at any age, or a first-degree family history of liver cancer.
- AFP and liver ultrasound: every six months, done together. PhilHealth's standard notes AFP is not recommended as a standalone test.

## Step-by-Step: How to Get Lab Tests Covered Through YAKAP

PhilHealth's selection and empanelment circular (2025-0017, published October 17-18, 2025) sets four stages: registration, selection, First Patient Encounter and empanelment.

1. **Get or update your PhilHealth Identification Number (PIN).** Register or update your member record first. If you have PhilSys, the eGovPH app can issue a PIN on verification.
2. **Choose your YAKAP Clinic.** Use the eGovPH app or the PhilHealth Member Portal (YAKAP tab, then add a clinic for yourself or a dependent), or visit an LHIO. PhilHealth also accepts selections through its Corporate Action Center and through P-CARES staff stationed in hospitals.
3. **Go for your First Patient Encounter.** The clinic verifies your identity with the **PhilHealth Check Utility (PCU)**, which includes a face-verification liveness check. If you are not registered in PhilSys, bring a valid government ID. After the FPE you sign the Mutual Care Agreement (YES). You sign a new one each year to stay empaneled.
4. **See the doctor.** The clinic's physician assesses you and decides which tests, if any, to order. PhilHealth reminds clinics to run the PCU liveness check at consultations too.
5. **Get the test.** Go to your clinic's lab or the partner laboratory it names. For cancer screening, take the **Screening Test Request Form** from your YAKAP physician to an accredited cancer screening facility. The facility validates the request with your clinic, performs the test, and you sign an acknowledgement that it was done. Results are shared with you, your clinic and PhilHealth.
6. **Follow up with your clinic.** Your clinic is meant to receive the results, advise you on follow-up and the timing of the next screening, and refer you to a higher-level facility if a result is positive.

**Good to know about the clinic you choose:**
- You are empaneled until December 31 of the year. Empaneled members can transfer only in the fourth quarter (effective January), unless the clinic closes or loses accreditation, or the Department of Health issues a cease and desist order. If you have selected a clinic but have not yet had your FPE, you can transfer at any time.
- A clinic cannot reject you if you selected it, unless it has reached its declared beneficiary limit.
- If you get your **first-ever** cancer screening request from a private, non-primary-care physician, PhilHealth's circular says you stay eligible. You must then be referred to your designated primary care provider, and the screening facility and PhilHealth help you register with one.

## Hospital Stays: Where Lab Tests Fit

When you are admitted to an accredited hospital, PhilHealth pays under the **All Case Rates** system. The case rate policy defines a case rate as a fixed amount covering professional fees and all facility charges, including room and board, diagnostics and laboratories, drugs, medicines and supplies. Individual lab tests are not usually billed on top of the package for covered cases.

Two cautions:

- **A case rate is not a promise that PhilHealth pays your whole bill.** The amount is fixed per case. Whether you pay anything extra depends on the type of accommodation and the no-co-payment and no-balance-billing rules that apply.
- **Rates change.** PhilHealth raised about 9,000 case rates by 50 percent on January 1, 2025. Use PhilHealth's Case Rates Search portal for the current amount for your condition.

**Example of the rules in practice (maternal and gynecologic care):** a 2026 PhilHealth circular on maternal and gynecologic case rates states that the package covers surgical procedures, laboratory tests, medicines and supplies. It entitles all beneficiaries admitted to basic or ward accommodation to no co-payment, and sets a co-payment cap for semi-private beds. Hospitals may also claim **additional antenatal diagnostics** under a fee schedule when those tests are essential to managing the pregnancy under clinical practice guidelines. The fees are what PhilHealth pays the facility:

| Test | PhilHealth fee (₱) | Test | PhilHealth fee (₱) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Blood typing | 576 | HbA1c | 870 |
| BUN | 377 | OGTT | 900 |
| CBC | 310 | HIV testing | 658 |
| Creatinine | 733 | RPR or VDRL | 300 |
| FBS | 156 | Urinalysis | 263 |
| HBsAg | 345 | Urine culture and sensitivity | 1,600 |
| LDH | 137 | SGOT/AST | 249 |
| SGPT/ALT | 309 | Pelvic or transvaginal ultrasound | 1,000 |

The signing date on our copy of that circular was not legible. Check the circular on PhilHealth's website for its effectivity before relying on it.

## What Will It Cost You?

- **YAKAP lab tests and cancer screening:** the Presidential Communications Office said at the YAKAP launch that laboratory tests and cancer screenings are free of charge, and PhilHealth's cancer screening circular states no co-payment and no balance billing. Both assume you follow the process above.
- **Tests not on the covered list:** a PhilHealth Region 7 official told PIA that additional payment may apply if a requested service is not in the covered package.
- **Hospital admissions:** depends on the case rate, your accommodation and the rules above.
- **Medicines are separate.** Medicines fall under the PhilHealth GAMOT benefit, which PhilHealth and the government have described as up to ₱20,000 per year per patient. It is outside the scope of this lab guide.

## Examples

*These are illustrations, not real cases or medical advice.*

**Example 1: routine YAKAP lab work.** A member registers a PIN through the eGovPH app, selects a nearby YAKAP Clinic, completes the FPE and signs the YES. During a consultation the clinic physician orders tests from the 13-test list. The clinic's lab, or a partner lab the clinic names, performs them under the benefit.

**Example 2: a screening referral.** During a consultation, a YAKAP physician assesses a member against PhilHealth's published eligibility criteria and, if the member qualifies, fills in a Screening Test Request Form for an accredited facility. The member brings the form, has the test, and signs the acknowledgement. The facility files the claim with PhilHealth. If the member is told to pay for a covered screening, that is a reason to contact the clinic or PhilHealth (see below).

## Common Problems

- **You cannot find your clinic or it has stopped participating.** PhilHealth required YAKAP providers to move to a certified electronic medical records system, and its advisory said the old eKonsulta system would be decommissioned by July 1, 2026. We did not verify how clinics were affected. Check the current clinic list in the eGovPH app or Member Portal before visiting.
- **The clinic asks you to sign nothing, or says your PCU check failed.** The Mutual Care Agreement and PCU liveness checks are required parts of the process. If you are not PhilSys-registered, bring a valid ID.
- **The clinic is full.** Clinics must accept members who select them unless they have reached their declared limit. If a clinic is full, choose another.
- **You want to switch clinics mid-year.** Transfer rules limit this for empaneled members (see above).
- **The test you were told to get is not on the list.** Ask your doctor whether another PhilHealth benefit applies. Do not assume the test is covered or excluded.
- **The screening facility is not accredited.** Cancer screening is covered only at PhilHealth-accredited screening facilities with the right Department of Health license.

## What To Do If Something Goes Wrong

1. Keep your documents: the request form, the acknowledgement you signed, receipts and any claim details.
2. Ask the clinic or facility to explain in writing which benefit applies and why you are being charged.
3. Contact your LHIO or the **PhilHealth Corporate Action Center**. The PhilHealth circulars list the hotline as (02) 8662-2588 on the letterhead (written (02) 866-2588 in the circular text) and the email as actioncenter@philhealth.gov.ph. Confirm the current contact details on philhealth.gov.ph.
4. For a hospital that fails to follow no-co-payment rules in maternal and gynecologic cases, PhilHealth's circular encourages patients to report the facility to the Action Center.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Does PhilHealth cover blood tests?**
Under YAKAP, several blood tests are on the 13-test list (CBC, lipid profile, FBS, OGTT, HbA1c and creatinine), ordered by your YAKAP physician. In a hospital admission, lab tests are included in the case rate.

**Are lab tests free under YAKAP?**
The government has described the YAKAP lab tests and cancer screenings as free of charge for beneficiaries who follow the process. Tests outside the covered list may involve additional payment.

**Do I need a doctor's referral?**
Yes for YAKAP lab tests and cancer screenings, according to PhilHealth officials. The order comes from your YAKAP physician after assessment.

**Is HbA1c covered?**
HbA1c is on the 13-test list reported for YAKAP, if your YAKAP physician orders it.

**Is a thyroid test covered?**
It is not on the 13-test list we found. Ask your clinic and your doctor.

**Does PhilHealth cover a mammogram?**
Yes, as a cancer screening package (₱2,610) for those who meet the criteria, with a YAKAP physician's request. The breast standard covers women aged 40 to 69 (every two years), and people with breast symptoms.

**Does PhilHealth cover colonoscopy?**
PhilHealth pays ₱23,640 for colonoscopy performed as a screening test, subject to the colorectal screening standard. Colonoscopy that is part of a hospital admission falls under the case rate rules.

**Can I get my lab test at any laboratory?**
The circulars we reviewed describe coverage through accredited YAKAP Clinics and their partner facilities, and through accredited screening facilities. Check accreditation before you go.

**Are my dependents covered?**
Dependents can select a YAKAP Clinic too. A parent or legal guardian handles transactions for a minor.

**Is Konsulta the same as YAKAP?**
YAKAP is described by PhilHealth as an evolution of Konsulta.

**How often can I have tests?**
For cancer screening, PhilHealth sets intervals per cancer (for example, mammogram every two years for ages 40 to 69, and low-dose CT every 12 months for eligible people). For the 13 YAKAP lab tests, we did not find a stated annual limit; they are ordered as needed by your physician.
