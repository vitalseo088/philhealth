## PhilHealth Accredited Mental Health Facility: Where to Go and What's Covered in 2026

Here's the direct answer: PhilHealth accredits two separate tiers of mental health provider — facilities for **general mental health services** and facilities for **specialty mental health services** — and publishes each as its own list on its official Accredited Health Facilities page. The outpatient benefit is **₱9,000 per person per year for general mental health services** and **₱16,000 per person per year for specialty services**, under PhilHealth Circular No. 2023-0018.

This guide is for PhilHealth members and families in **2026** trying to work out where mental health care is actually covered, which tier applies to their situation, and how to confirm a facility is currently accredited.

> **Provider information can change. Check the official PhilHealth provider directory before visiting.** Accredited facility lists are published with an "updated as of" date and individual facilities can gain or lose accreditation. This guide does not name individual facilities — the official directory is the only version current enough to act on.

> **If you or someone you know is in crisis right now**, PhilHealth's own announcement of this benefit points people to 24/7 crisis support, including the National Center for Mental Health hotline at **0917-899-8727** or **989-8727**. That's a faster route than working through coverage questions.

## What Is the PhilHealth Outpatient Mental Health Benefit?

PhilHealth's **Outpatient Benefits Package for Mental Health** is an outpatient benefit package — meaning it covers care received without being admitted — established under **PhilHealth Circular No. 2023-0018**. According to PhilHealth's own description, members and dependents can access services including initial assessments, follow-up consultations, diagnostic tests, and psychosocial support.

The package covers conditions including **depression, psychosis, epilepsy, child and adolescent mental and behavioral disorders, dementia, and self-harm or suicide risks**, among others. Those requiring specialized care are provided access to interventions from psychiatrists, neurologists, and psychologists.

PhilHealth coverage depends on the applicable benefit package and eligibility requirements. This article explains the benefit rules — which facility types are accredited for what, and what each package pays — not which form of care is clinically appropriate for any individual. That determination belongs to a qualified health professional.

## Who Is Eligible?

Based on PhilHealth's published information on this package:

- The benefit is available to **PhilHealth members and their dependents**.
- For **psychiatric services**, individuals must be **10 years old and above**.
- For **neurological services**, PhilHealth's Region 1 benefit administration has described **no age restriction** as applying.

## The Two Tiers of Accredited Facility

This is the distinction that determines where you go, and it's the main thing to get right before traveling to a facility.

### General Mental Health Services (₱9,000/year)

Accredited facilities for general mental health services include:

- Rural health units (RHUs)
- City or municipal health offices
- **Level 1 and Level 2 hospitals**
- DOH Medicine Access Program–Mental Health (MAP-MH) sites
- Standalone mental health clinics

**What this tier covers:** screening, assessment, diagnostics, up to 12 follow-up visits, psychoeducation, psychosocial support, and access to medicines under the **Medicine Access Program for Mental Health (MAP-MH)**.

### Specialty Mental Health Services (₱16,000/year)

Accredited facilities for specialty mental health services include:

- **Accredited Level 2 and Level 3 hospitals** with specialized staff
- Custodial care facilities
- **National specialty centers identified by the DOH**

**What this tier adds:** everything in the general package, **plus psychotherapy**.

Note that **Level 2 hospitals appear in both tiers** — a Level 2 hospital may be accredited for general services, specialty services, or both, depending on whether it has the required specialized staff. This is precisely why checking the specific list matters rather than assuming based on hospital level alone.

## How to Find an Accredited Mental Health Facility

1. **Go to PhilHealth's Accredited Health Facilities page** (philhealth.gov.ph → Partners → Providers → Facilities → Accredited).
2. **Choose the right list** — the page carries separate entries for **"General Mental Health Services"** and **"Specialty Mental Health Services."** Open the one matching the tier of care you need.
3. **Check the "updated as of" date** on the page before relying on the list.
4. **Find your city or municipality** in the listing.
5. **Call ahead** to confirm the facility is currently providing the service and to ask about scheduling — accreditation status on a list is not the same as same-day availability.

If you're unsure which tier applies to your situation, an accredited general mental health provider — an RHU or city/municipal health office is often the most accessible starting point — can assess and refer onward to specialty services where clinically indicated.

## Why Some Areas Have Few Accredited Providers

Provider availability for this package is still expanding, and the reason is worth understanding if your search turns up little nearby.

PhilHealth has publicly described a provider-side bottleneck: doctors and general practitioners need to undergo **mental health Gap Action Programme (mhGAP) training through the DOH** in order to become accredited with PhilHealth for these services. PhilHealth has also said it is calling for more providers to participate, and as of March 2026 was conducting online orientations for accredited providers on claim preparation processes to strengthen filing in regions where the package had already been fully rolled out.

Practical implication: the absence of an accredited facility in your immediate area reflects rollout stage rather than exclusion from the benefit. Your local PhilHealth office can advise on the nearest accredited access point.

## Claims and Filing

Claims for the Outpatient Benefits Package for Mental Health are submitted **through the eClaims System**, per PhilHealth Advisory No. 2025-0036. As with other PhilHealth outpatient benefits, claims filing is generally handled by the accredited facility rather than by the member directly.

If a facility tells you it isn't yet filing claims for this package, that's a provider-side capability matter — the same gap PhilHealth's 2026 provider orientations were aimed at addressing — not a statement about your entitlement.

## Common Problems and What to Do

**You can't find an accredited mental health facility near you.**
Check both official lists (general and specialty), since a facility may appear on one and not the other. If nothing is nearby, contact your local PhilHealth office about the nearest accredited access point. Rollout is still expanding, and gaps reflect provider accreditation stage.

**A facility says it doesn't offer the PhilHealth mental health package.**
Accreditation is service-specific. A hospital accredited for other PhilHealth packages isn't automatically accredited for mental health services, and a Level 2 hospital accredited for general services may not be accredited for specialty services.

**You were told the facility can't file the claim.**
PhilHealth Advisory No. 2025-0036 directs claims for this package through the eClaims System, and PhilHealth has been running provider orientations on claim preparation. Ask the facility's PhilHealth desk directly, and raise unresolved issues with PhilHealth's Corporate Action Center at (02) 8662-2588 or actioncenter@philhealth.gov.ph.

**You need psychotherapy but your nearby facility only offers general services.**
Psychotherapy sits in the specialty package (₱16,000/year), accessed at accredited Level 2 and 3 hospitals with specialized staff, custodial care facilities, or DOH-identified national specialty centers. An accredited general provider can assess and refer.

**You're not sure whether your medicines are covered.**
The general package includes access to medicines under the **Medicine Access Program for Mental Health (MAP-MH)**. DOH MAP-MH sites are among the facility types accredited for general mental health services.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How much does PhilHealth cover for mental health care?**
₱9,000 per person per year for general mental health services and ₱16,000 per person per year for specialty mental health services, under the outpatient package established by PhilHealth Circular No. 2023-0018.

**Where can I find the official list of accredited mental health facilities?**
On PhilHealth's Accredited Health Facilities page, which carries separate listings for "General Mental Health Services" and "Specialty Mental Health Services."

**Is psychotherapy covered?**
Psychotherapy is described as part of the specialty mental health services package, which also includes everything in the general package.

**Is there an age requirement?**
For psychiatric services, individuals must be 10 years old and above. PhilHealth Region 1's benefit administration has described no age restriction for those needing neurological services.

**Can I use both the ₱9,000 and ₱16,000 packages?**
The available official sources describe these as two distinct annual packages tied to two tiers of accredited facility, but do not clearly spell out the rules for combining or sequencing them within the same year for one person. Confirm this with PhilHealth or your accredited facility rather than assuming.

**Does this cover inpatient psychiatric admission?**
This particular package is an **outpatient** benefit. Inpatient psychiatric confinement falls under separate PhilHealth rules not detailed in the sources reviewed for this article — ask your facility or PhilHealth directly about inpatient coverage.

**Are dependents covered?**
PhilHealth's description of the package refers to members and their dependents accessing the services.
