# PhilHealth Contribution for Employed Members: The 2026 Guide

If you're an employed member, your PhilHealth contribution for 2026 is 5% of your monthly basic salary, split equally between you and your employer — 2.5% deducted from your pay, 2.5% shouldered by your employer. This guide explains how that's computed, what your employer is legally required to do with it, and what to check if a contribution seems missing or wrong.

This guide is for private-sector and government employees, HR and payroll staff, and business owners handling PhilHealth remittance in 2026. It does not cover self-employed, voluntary, OFW, or indirect (government-subsidized) contributors, whose rules differ in some respects.

## Quick Answer

| Item | 2026 Rule |
|---|---|
| Contribution rate | 5% of monthly basic salary |
| Income floor | ₱10,000 (minimum premium: ₱500/month) |
| Income ceiling | ₱100,000 (maximum premium: ₱5,000/month) |
| Employee share | 2.5% of basic salary |
| Employer share | 2.5% of basic salary |
| Who remits | The employer, on behalf of both shares |
| Remittance system | Electronic Premium Remittance System (EPRS) |

This 5% rate, ₱10,000 floor, and ₱100,000 ceiling were confirmed for 2026 in a PhilHealth advisory reported by the Philippine Information Agency in May 2026, and match the official PhilHealth contribution table's figures for the 2024-onward schedule.

## What Does "PhilHealth Contribution for Employed" Mean?

An **employed member** is anyone working under an employer — private company or government agency — whose PhilHealth premium is a shared responsibility. Unlike self-employed or voluntary members who pay their own premium in full, an employed member's contribution is split: part comes out of the employee's salary, and part is added by the employer. The employer is legally responsible for deducting the correct amount, adding its own share, and remitting the combined total to PhilHealth on time.

## Who Is Covered as an Employed Member?

You're generally covered as an employed Direct Contributor if you are:

- A private-sector employee under an employer-employee relationship
- A government employee (national or local government units)
- A Kasambahay (household worker) — with a specific rule: if you earn below ₱5,000/month, your household employer generally shoulders the full premium; at ₱5,000/month or more, a proportionate worker share may apply

If you're unsure whether your work arrangement qualifies you as an employed member (for example, in contract-based or agency-hired setups), your HR department or a PhilHealth Local Health Insurance Office (LHIO) can confirm your classification.

## The Contribution Formula and Rate

**Formula:**

Monthly Basic Salary × 5% = Total Monthly Premium, split 2.5% employee / 2.5% employer

This 5% rate is the final step of a phased schedule under PhilHealth Circular No. 2019-0009, issued pursuant to Republic Act No. 11223 (the Universal Health Care Act). The circular originally scheduled rate increases from 2.75% in 2019 to 5% by 2025, with the income floor fixed at ₱10,000 and the ceiling eventually reaching ₱100,000. Some scheduled increases in earlier years (2021–2023) were suspended by government directive at the time, which is why the historical numbers differ year to year — this history doesn't affect the 2026 figure. PhilHealth's official contribution table shows 5%, a ₱10,000 floor, and a ₱100,000 ceiling for "2024 to 2025," and a PhilHealth advisory reported in May 2026 confirmed the same rate continues for 2026.

**Monthly basic salary**, for this computation, is fixed base pay for regular working hours. Based on PhilHealth's own guidance for the CY 2025 advisory, it excludes items such as sales commission, overtime pay, allowances, 13th month pay, bonuses, gratuity payments, undertime, tardiness, leave without pay, and absences. If you're unsure what figure your payroll department is using, ask them directly — this is the single most common source of confusion for employees checking their own deduction.

## Employer Responsibilities

For an employed member's contribution to count, the employer must:

1. **Deduct the correct employee share** from the employee's basic salary each pay period.
2. **Add the employer's own equal share** — this cannot be charged to the employee.
3. **Remit both shares together** through the Electronic Premium Remittance System (EPRS), PhilHealth's mandatory online channel for employer payment and reporting.
4. **Submit the required remittance report** so the payment is posted correctly to each employee's individual record.
5. **Register new hires** (generally through the applicable employer/employee report forms) and report separations within the required period.

### Remittance Deadlines

Employer remittance deadlines are tied to the last digit of the employer's PhilHealth Employer Number (PEN):

| Last Digit of PEN | Payment Window (Following Month) |
|---|---|
| 0 to 4 | 11th to 15th |
| 5 to 9 | 16th to 20th |

**A note on this deadline:** available sources consistently describe this PEN-based window, but PhilHealth's own published materials on remittance deadlines are not fully consistent with each other across different pages, and at least one detailed guide could not confirm which version currently supersedes the other. Employers should confirm their exact due date directly through their EPRS account notices or with PhilHealth rather than relying on any single secondary source, including this one.

## What Happens If Contributions Are Late or Unremitted

Non-remittance or late remittance by an employer can trigger:

- **Interest on unpaid amounts.** Republic Act No. 11223 (UHC Act) states that missed employer contributions accrue interest compounding monthly, with a minimum rate described for employers that is higher than the rate applied to self-earning individuals, professionals, and migrant workers. Because different PhilHealth issuances over the years describe interest computations differently (including an older circular's "2% or ₱200, whichever is higher" formula), the exact rate an individual employer will be charged should be confirmed directly with PhilHealth or through the employer's EPRS account, rather than assumed from any single source.
- **Fines.** Separate PhilHealth issuances describe a fine for non-remittance, under-remittance, selective remittance, or non-submission of remittance reports, computed per employee — the exact figures and conditions should be verified against the current implementing rules rather than assumed.
- **Employer liability for denied benefits.** If an employee's PhilHealth claim is denied specifically because the employer failed to remit or report contributions that were legally due, the law places liability on the employer for the resulting medical expense, separate from any interest or fines.

**A temporary 2026 program:** PhilHealth Circular No. 2026-0001 introduced a one-time waiver of interest allowing employers to settle unpaid contributions covering July 2013 to December 2024 within a one-year window, at reduced interest (as low as 0% for the shortest settlement period, up from there depending on the chosen payment term) instead of the standard rate. This is a temporary settlement program with applications accepted only until December 31, 2026 — it does not change the standard, ongoing interest rules for contributions outside that covered period, and it does not waive the unpaid principal contributions themselves.

## How to Verify Your Contribution as an Employee

1. Go to the PhilHealth Member Portal on the official PhilHealth website.
2. Log in using your PhilHealth Identification Number (PIN) and password.
3. Open your contribution or premium payment history.
4. Compare the posted amount and period against your payslip deduction.
5. If a month is missing or the amount looks wrong, raise it with your HR/payroll department first, since remittance is their legal responsibility.

## Worked Examples

*The following are illustrative examples only, based on the formula above.*

**Example 1 — Employee earning ₱18,000/month basic salary:**
₱18,000 × 5% = ₱900 total → ₱450 employee share (payslip deduction), ₱450 employer share.

**Example 2 — Employee earning ₱9,500/month basic salary (below the floor):**
Computed on the ₱10,000 floor instead: ₱10,000 × 5% = ₱500 total → ₱250 employee share, ₱250 employer share.

**Example 3 — Employee earning ₱120,000/month basic salary (above the ceiling):**
Computed on the ₱100,000 ceiling: ₱100,000 × 5% = ₱5,000 total → ₱2,500 employee share, ₱2,500 employer share.

## Common Problems

- **Payslip deduction doesn't match your own computation.** Usually a basic-salary definition mismatch (e.g., allowances included by mistake). Confirm with HR what figure they use.
- **Contribution missing from the Member Portal.** Postings can lag; keep your payslip as a record while you wait, and follow up if it's still missing after a reasonable period.
- **Employer appears to be under-remitting or not remitting at all.** This is a compliance issue on the employer's part, not something you can fix from the Member Portal alone.
- **Confusion between employee share and employer share on payslips.** Only the employee's 2.5% should appear as a payroll deduction — the employer's 2.5% is a separate cost to the company, not a second deduction from your pay.

## What to Do If Something Goes Wrong

If you believe your employer has not remitted your PhilHealth contributions correctly:

1. Gather your payslips showing the PhilHealth deduction for the affected months.
2. Raise the discrepancy with your HR or payroll department first — under the law, the employer is responsible for remittance, not the employee.
3. If the issue is unresolved, or if the employer is unresponsive, report the matter to PhilHealth directly through a Local Health Insurance Office (LHIO) or PhilHealth's official reporting channels, bringing your payslips and any other supporting documents.
4. Keep records of your correspondence with both your employer and PhilHealth in case a claim is affected later.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can my employer deduct more than 2.5% of my salary for PhilHealth?**
No. Based on the official formula, the employee share is fixed at half of the total 5% premium — 2.5% of your monthly basic salary, subject to the ₱10,000 floor and ₱100,000 ceiling. A deduction significantly different from that should be raised with HR.

**Can my employer pass its share of the premium on to me?**
Available official sources describe the employer's share as a separate obligation the employer must add on top of the employee deduction, not something the employer may recover from the employee's pay. If you believe this is happening, raise it with HR or PhilHealth directly.

**What if I have more than one employer at the same time?**
The available official sources reviewed for this guide do not clearly detail how multiple concurrent employers should coordinate a single employee's contribution. If this applies to you, confirm directly with PhilHealth or an LHIO rather than assuming a rule.

**Is the 5% rate going to increase again after 2026?**
Based on available official reporting, the 5% rate is the final step of the UHC Act's phased schedule, and PhilHealth's May 2026 advisory described it as the final adjustment. Any further change would require a new circular or legislation.

**What if my employer never registered me with PhilHealth at all?**
This is a registration issue separate from remittance and should be raised with PhilHealth directly, since it affects whether any contributions can be posted to your record at all. The available sources for this guide did not include the specific step-by-step process for that scenario, so it isn't detailed here — confirm the correct procedure with PhilHealth.
