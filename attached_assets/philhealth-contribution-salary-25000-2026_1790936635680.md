# 2. Article

## PhilHealth Contribution for a ₱25,000 Salary in 2026

Here's the direct answer: if your monthly basic salary is **₱25,000**, your total PhilHealth contribution for 2026 is **₱1,250 per month**. How that amount is paid depends on your membership category — if you're an **employed member**, it's split evenly between you and your employer (**₱625 each**); if you're a **self-paying member** (self-earning, a professional practitioner, or most Overseas Filipinos), you pay the full **₱1,250** yourself, since there's no employer to share the cost.

This guide walks through exactly how that number is calculated, based on PhilHealth's current 2026 contribution rate, and shows the computation for different membership categories at this same salary level.

## Quick Answer

| Membership Category | Monthly Premium | Who Pays It |
|---|---|---|
| Employed member | ₱1,250 total | ₱625 (you) + ₱625 (employer) |
| Self-paying member (self-earning, professional practitioner, most OFWs) | ₱1,250 total | You, in full |

## The Formula

PhilHealth's contribution rate for **2026** is **5% of monthly basic salary or declared monthly income**, applied within an income floor of ₱10,000 and a ceiling of ₱100,000. This rate has been confirmed as the **final scheduled rate adjustment** under the Universal Health Care Act.

**Formula:**

> Monthly Basic Salary (or Declared Monthly Income) × 5% = Total Monthly Premium

For a ₱25,000 salary:

> ₱25,000 × 5% (0.05) = **₱1,250**

Since ₱25,000 falls between the ₱10,000 floor and the ₱100,000 ceiling, the straightforward 5% calculation applies directly — no adjustment toward either the minimum or maximum premium is needed at this income level.

## How the ₱1,250 Is Split: Employed Members

For an **employed member** — someone with an employer-employee relationship — the total monthly premium is **shared equally between the employee and the employer**. At a ₱25,000 salary:

| Share | Amount |
|---|---|
| Employee's share (deducted from your payslip) | ₱625.00 |
| Employer's share (paid on top, by your employer) | ₱625.00 |
| **Total remitted to PhilHealth** | **₱1,250.00** |

In practice, you'll typically see only the **₱625.00 employee share** reflected as a deduction on your payslip — the employer's matching ₱625.00 doesn't come out of your salary, but your employer is responsible for adding it and remitting the combined ₱1,250.00 total to PhilHealth.

## If You're a Self-Paying Member Instead

If you earn the equivalent of ₱25,000 a month but **don't have an employer** — for example, as a self-earning individual, a professional practitioner, or an Overseas Filipino (excluding sea-based migrant workers) — you're classified as a **self-paying member** under PhilHealth Circular No. 2026-0011. In this category, there's no employer counterpart to split the cost with, so:

| Share | Amount |
|---|---|
| Your share | ₱1,250.00 |
| Employer's share | None — not applicable |
| **Total you pay** | **₱1,250.00** |

Self-paying members can choose to pay this monthly, quarterly, semi-annually, or annually through PhilHealth's accredited payment channels, as long as the full year's contributions are settled by December 31 of that year (with a one-month grace period into January of the following year).

## What If Your Salary Changes During the Year?

Your PhilHealth contribution is computed based on your **current monthly basic salary or declared income** at the time it's reported — if your salary increases or decreases, your premium is expected to be recalculated accordingly going forward under the same 5% formula, subject to the ₱10,000 floor and ₱100,000 ceiling. If you're self-paying, this generally means updating your declared income with PhilHealth so it's reflected in your Statement of Premium Account (SPA).

## Common Problems and What to Do

**Your payslip shows a different deduction than ₱625.**
Check whether your "monthly basic salary" for PhilHealth purposes matches what you expect — some employers compute based on a specific definition of basic pay that may differ slightly from your gross pay. If the deduction still looks wrong after checking that, ask your HR/payroll department to confirm how they computed it.

**You're self-paying and unsure what income to declare.**
Your contribution is based on your actual monthly income from self-employment or practice. If your income varies month to month, PhilHealth's acceptable proof-of-income options (an ITR, or income declared and signed directly on the PMRF) are there precisely because self-paying income isn't always a fixed, predictable number like a payslip.

**You think you're being charged more than ₱1,250 for a ₱25,000 salary.**
Double-check that your reported salary for PhilHealth purposes is actually ₱25,000 and not a higher gross figure that includes allowances or other pay components not meant to be included in the base used for computation. If the amount still seems off, raise it with your employer (if employed) or a PhilHealth office directly.

**You're not sure if ₱1,250 is really the current 2026 rate.**
Some older or inaccurate sources online cite different rates (such as 5.5%) for recent years — the confirmed current rate for 2026 is **5%**, described by PhilHealth as the final scheduled increase under the Universal Health Care Act's rate schedule.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How much is the PhilHealth contribution for a ₱25,000 monthly salary?**
₱1,250 total per month, based on the current 2026 rate of 5% of monthly salary or income.

**How much will be deducted from my payslip if I earn ₱25,000 and I'm employed?**
₱625.00 — your employer separately adds a matching ₱625.00, for a combined ₱1,250.00 remitted to PhilHealth.

**Do self-employed or freelance workers pay the same amount?**
At the same ₱25,000 income level, yes, the total premium computed is the same ₱1,250 — but a self-paying member pays the full amount themselves, since there's no employer share.

**Is 5% the final PhilHealth contribution rate, or will it increase again?**
PhilHealth has described 5% as the final scheduled rate adjustment under the Universal Health Care Act's rate schedule for this cycle.

**What if my salary is exactly at the ₱10,000 floor or ₱100,000 ceiling?**
At ₱10,000 or below, the premium is fixed at the minimum of ₱500/month. At ₱100,000 or above, it's fixed at the maximum of ₱5,000/month. A ₱25,000 salary falls well within the range where the straightforward 5% calculation applies directly.

**Does this computation apply to OFWs too?**
Yes — the same 5% rate, floor, and ceiling apply to OFWs classified as self-paying members (excluding sea-based migrant workers, who generally follow a separate arrangement), and they pay the full computed amount themselves.
