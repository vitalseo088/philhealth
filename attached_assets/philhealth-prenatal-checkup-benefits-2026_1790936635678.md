## PhilHealth Prenatal Checkup Benefits 2026: What's Covered and How Much

Here's the direct answer: in 2026, PhilHealth covers **at least eight prenatal (antenatal) visits** across pregnancy, paid out as three bundled amounts per trimester — **₱1,100 for the 1st trimester, ₱1,500 for the 2nd, and ₱3,750 for the 3rd** — plus separate coverage for tetanus vaccination and a defined set of laboratory tests and ultrasounds. These amounts come from **PhilHealth Circular No. 2026-0006**, *Expansion of Maternity Care Benefits for Non-Hospital and Outpatient Facilities (Revision 1)*, published May 12, 2026.

This guide is for pregnant PhilHealth members or dependents who want to know exactly what a prenatal checkup benefit includes, how the visit schedule works, and how the benefit is claimed — separate from the delivery benefit itself, which this guide does not detail.

> **Important limitation:** Prenatal (antenatal) care is now a **separate, unbundled benefit** from delivery under the current rules — it is not part of the same case rate as your Cesarean or normal delivery payment. If you're looking for delivery coverage amounts, that's covered under a different circular (PhilHealth Circular No. 2026-0005 for hospital-based delivery), not detailed here.

### Quick Answer

| Prenatal Benefit | Timing | Benefit Code | Rate (₱) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1st Trimester visit(s) | 1–13 weeks, at least 1 visit | ANV01 | 1,100 |
| 2nd Trimester visits | 14–27 weeks, at least 2 visits | ANV02 | 1,500 |
| 3rd Trimester visits | 28–40 weeks, at least 5 visits | ANV03 | 3,750 |
| Tetanus vaccine, 1st dose | 20–36 weeks | TDAP1 | 150 |
| Tetanus vaccine, 2nd dose (if warranted) | 27–36 weeks | TDAP2 | 150 |

Diagnostic tests (blood work and ultrasounds) are covered under a separate set of benefit codes described below.

## What Is the PhilHealth Prenatal Checkup Benefit?

Antenatal care (ANC) — commonly called prenatal care — is preventive healthcare given to a pregnant person through regular checkups meant to detect and manage pregnancy complications early, monitor fetal growth, and support a healthy pregnancy. PhilHealth's ANC benefit covers the **clinical visits themselves** (Clinical Antenatal Care, or CANC) and the **required laboratory and ultrasound work-up** (Diagnostic Antenatal Care, or DANC) as two related but separately billed components.

This structure was set by PhilHealth Circular No. 2026-0006, which unbundled the older, single Maternity Care Package into separate antenatal care, intrapartum (delivery), postpartum care, and emergency referral components — meaning your prenatal visits are now claimed and paid independently of your eventual delivery benefit.

## Who Is Eligible?

Per the circular, **"All pregnant persons enrolled in the National Health Insurance Program (NHIP) shall be immediately eligible to avail of PhilHealth benefits, in accordance with the Universal Health Care Act (UHC Act),"** citing PhilHealth Circular No. 2022-0013 (Granting of Immediate Eligibility to Filipino Citizens). Pregnant persons below 21 years old and their infants remain eligible under existing PhilHealth membership and eligibility policies.

In practice:

- You need an active PhilHealth membership, either as a member or a qualified dependent.
- Visits must be with a **PhilHealth-accredited antenatal care provider** — this can include YAKAP primary care clinics, accredited infirmaries, hospital outpatient departments, or other accredited maternal care providers.
- The circular encourages all pregnant persons to register and be empaneled with a **PhilHealth YAKAP clinic**; if that clinic isn't itself accredited for maternal care, it is expected to refer you to one that is.

**On a specific contribution-count requirement:** the governing circular does not restate a fixed number of monthly contributions required for this specific benefit. If you're unsure of your eligibility status, confirm directly through the PhilHealth Member Portal or a PhilHealth office rather than relying on an unconfirmed figure from another source.

## The Required Prenatal Visit Schedule

PhilHealth requires and covers a minimum of **eight antenatal visits**, distributed across pregnancy as follows:

- **1st trimester (1–13 weeks):** at least 1 visit
- **2nd trimester (14–27 weeks):** at least 2 visits
- **3rd trimester (28–40 weeks):** at least 5 visits

Each set of visits is claimed as a bundle **at the end of the corresponding trimester**, rather than paid per individual visit.

### Mandatory Services at Each Visit

| Visit Type | Mandatory Services |
|---|---|
| 1st Trimester Visit | Consultation; medical history and physical exam; supplement provision (iron and folic acid, or multiple micronutrient supplementation); patient health education; written birth plan attached to the Antenatal Care Record |
| 2nd Trimester Visits | Consultation; vital signs; fetal growth monitoring; supplement provision (IFA/MMS, calcium); deworming; tetanus vaccination if indicated |
| 3rd Trimester Visits | Consultation; vital signs; fetal growth monitoring; supplement provision (IFA/MMS, calcium); tetanus vaccination if indicated |

The facility is expected to procure and dispense supplements in quantities sufficient to last until your next visit or through the trimester, or refer you to a YAKAP facility for these under the Guaranteed and Accessible Medications for Outpatient Treatment (GAMOT) Package.

### Additional Visits Beyond the Required Eight

If you need more than the required eight visits, additional visits at a **private** ANC provider may be chargeable to you, but the circular caps this at a **maximum co-payment of ₱900 per pregnancy**, regardless of how many additional visits are needed.

## Tetanus Vaccination Coverage

At least one tetanus vaccination is indicated in every pregnancy, reimbursable at the end of the third trimester as a first dose (TDAP1, ₱150). Where a second dose is warranted, the first dose is given in the second trimester and the second in the third trimester (TDAP2, ₱150). **Tetanus vaccination is only reimbursable when given by private facilities** providing antenatal services, per the circular's claims rules.

## Diagnostic Antenatal Care: Lab Tests and Ultrasounds

Routine laboratory work-up and ultrasounds are billed separately from your clinical visits, by the diagnostic facility or laboratory that performs them:

| Diagnostic Package | Timing | Benefit Code | Rate (₱) | Includes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| First routine diagnostics | With 1st or 2nd prenatal visit, per schedule | AND1 | 2,000 | Pregnancy test, blood typing, complete blood count (CBC), urinalysis, Hepatitis B screening (HBsAg), HIV screening, syphilis screening |
| Second routine follow-up diagnostics | Once during either 2nd-trimester visit | AND2 | 900 | Oral Glucose Tolerance Test (OGTT) |
| Third routine follow-up diagnostics | Once during any of the five 3rd-trimester visits | AND3 | 500 | Complete blood count (CBC), urinalysis |
| Transvaginal ultrasound | 1st trimester | ANS1 | 1,000 | Transvaginal ultrasound |
| Pelvic ultrasound | 2nd or 3rd trimester (once only) | ANS2 | 1,000 | Pelvic ultrasound |

Two important rules apply here:

- **The bundled tests within each diagnostic package must be performed as a complete set.** If even one test in a bundle is skipped, the claim for that bundle becomes non-reimbursable.
- **Only two ultrasounds are reimbursable** across the pregnancy — one transvaginal (1st trimester) and one pelvic (claimed once, either 2nd or 3rd trimester).

All routine antenatal diagnostic services in this bundled set are meant to be provided **at no cost to the patient**, per the circular's instruction to facilities.

## Documentation You'll Need

- **Antenatal Care Record (ANCR)** — or an equivalent record such as the Mother-Baby Book, Mother and Child Book, or Rekord ni Nanay. This should be brought to every visit and filled out by your attending health professional. It documents your Last Menstrual Period (LMP), which is used to calculate your age of gestation for all benefit computations.
- **Laboratory Request Form (LRF)** — prepared by your antenatal care provider and used to route you to an accredited diagnostic facility for your required tests.

## Step-by-Step: How to Use Your Prenatal Checkup Benefit

1. **Register with a PhilHealth-accredited antenatal care provider**, ideally a YAKAP clinic, as early in pregnancy as possible.
2. **Attend your first-trimester visit**, where your ANCR is opened, your LMP is recorded, and a birth plan is started.
3. **Complete the required lab work-up** at a PhilHealth-accredited diagnostic laboratory, using the Laboratory Request Form from your ANC provider.
4. **Attend your second-trimester visits** (at least two), including fetal growth monitoring, supplement provision, and tetanus vaccination if indicated.
5. **Complete your third-trimester visits** (at least five), along with your final routine diagnostics and second ultrasound if not yet done.
6. **Keep your ANCR updated and with you at every visit** — both the facility and you should retain a filled-out copy.
7. **Your ANC provider claims each trimester's visit bundle** and your diagnostic facility separately claims the lab/ultrasound bundles — you generally don't file these claims yourself.

## Common Problems and What to Do

**You missed some of the required five third-trimester visits.**
A claim for third-trimester care (ANV03) with fewer than five visit dates requires a justification letter, based on circumstances such as premature rupture of membranes, preterm labor and delivery, another obstetrical condition or emergency, or a fortuitous event. Discuss this with your provider if your visit count falls short for a valid medical reason.

**You were charged for a routine antenatal test that should have been free.**
The circular specifies these bundled tests should be at no cost to the patient. Raise this with the facility first; if unresolved, escalate to PhilHealth's Corporate Action Center at (02) 8662-2588 or actioncenter@philhealth.gov.ph.

**You needed more than eight visits and were charged extra.**
This is expected only at private ANC providers, and the total co-payment for all additional visits combined is capped at ₱900 per pregnancy. If you were charged more than this cap, that may be worth raising with the facility or PhilHealth directly.

**You're not sure if your ultrasound is covered.**
Only one transvaginal ultrasound (1st trimester) and one pelvic ultrasound (2nd or 3rd trimester, claimed once) are reimbursable. Additional ultrasounds beyond these two would not fall under this specific benefit based on the available official source.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How many prenatal checkups does PhilHealth cover?**
At least eight visits across the three trimesters: one in the first trimester, two in the second, and five in the third.

**How much is the PhilHealth prenatal checkup benefit worth in total?**
The three trimester bundles combined total ₱6,350 (₱1,100 + ₱1,500 + ₱3,750), plus up to ₱300 for two tetanus doses, plus the diagnostic packages (AND1, AND2, AND3, and two ultrasounds) claimed separately by the diagnostic facility.

**Are lab tests and ultrasounds included in the visit fee?**
No — they're billed as separate diagnostic packages (AND1/AND2/AND3 and ANS1/ANS2), typically claimed directly by the diagnostic laboratory or ultrasound facility that performs them.

**Do I have to pay for my prenatal checkups?**
The mandatory clinical visits and the bundled routine diagnostic tests are meant to be provided at no cost to the patient under the circular's essential-services and no-co-payment provisions, with a capped exception of up to ₱900 per pregnancy for additional visits beyond the required eight at private providers.

**What if I don't complete all eight required visits?**
Falling short of the required visit count in a given trimester, particularly the third, may require a justification letter for the claim to be reimbursable, depending on the circumstances.

**Is this the same benefit as my delivery coverage?**
No. Prenatal (antenatal) care is a separate, unbundled benefit from delivery (intrapartum) and postpartum care under the current PhilHealth structure — each is claimed and paid independently.
