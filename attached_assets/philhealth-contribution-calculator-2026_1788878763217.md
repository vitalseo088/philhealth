# PhilHealth Contribution Calculator 2026: How to Compute Your Monthly Premium

If you want to know how much you should be paying PhilHealth each month in 2026, the calculation comes down to one formula: your monthly basic salary multiplied by 5%, applied within a fixed income floor and ceiling. This guide walks through that formula, shows worked examples for employed, self-employed, and voluntary members, and explains where to check the actual amount PhilHealth has posted to your account — since no calculator, including this guide, can substitute for your official record.

This guide is for employees, self-employed individuals, voluntary members, and employers who want to compute or verify a 2026 PhilHealth premium. It does not cover indirect contributors (senior citizens, indigents, and other subsidized groups), whose premiums are government-paid rather than self-computed.

## Quick Answer

For 2026, the PhilHealth premium contribution rate is 5% of a member's monthly basic salary, subject to an income floor of ₱10,000 and an income ceiling of ₱100,000. This rate carries over unchanged from 2024–2025 and was confirmed for 2026 in a PhilHealth advisory issued in May 2026.

| Monthly Basic Salary | Rate Applied | Total Monthly Premium |
|---|---|---|
| ₱10,000 and below | Fixed at the floor | ₱500.00 |
| ₱10,000.01 to ₱99,999.99 | 5% of actual salary | ₱500.01 to ₱4,999.99 |
| ₱100,000 and above | Fixed at the ceiling | ₱5,000.00 |

For employed members, this total is split equally: 2.5% is deducted from the employee's salary, and 2.5% is shouldered by the employer. Self-employed, voluntary, and land-based OFW members pay the full 5% themselves.

## What Is the PhilHealth Contribution Calculator?

"PhilHealth contribution calculator" generally refers to any tool — official or independent — used to estimate a member's monthly premium based on their income. PhilHealth itself does not appear to publish a dedicated public-facing calculator webpage; instead, it publishes the official contribution formula and rate table through its circulars, and members verify their actual posted contributions through the PhilHealth Member Portal. Third-party calculators (including payroll platforms and HR sites) apply the same official formula described below, but only the Member Portal or your employer's payroll record reflects what has actually been remitted to your account.

## Who Needs to Compute Their Own Contribution?

You generally need to calculate your own premium if you fall under one of these categories, known as **Direct Contributors**:

- **Employed members** — private-sector and government employees whose employer shares the premium
- **Self-employed members** — individuals who earn income without an employer
- **Voluntary members** — including those not currently working who choose to continue paying
- **Land-based Overseas Filipino Workers (OFWs)** — who pay the full premium themselves
- **Kasambahay (household workers)** — with special rules on employer versus worker share

If you are a **senior citizen, an identified indigent, a person with disability (PWD) covered under the relevant law, or another government-subsidized member**, you are classified as an Indirect Contributor and your premium is government-paid. If you're unsure which category applies to you, PhilHealth's Member Portal or a PhilHealth Local Health Insurance Office (LHIO) can confirm your classification.

## The Official Contribution Formula

**Formula:**

Monthly Basic Salary × 5% = Total Monthly Premium

The 5% rate is the final step of a phased schedule set out in PhilHealth Circular No. 2019-0009, issued under Republic Act No. 11223 (the Universal Health Care Act). That circular originally scheduled the rate to climb from 2.75% in 2019 to 5% by 2025, with the income floor fixed at ₱10,000 throughout and the ceiling rising in stages to ₱100,000. PhilHealth's official contribution table confirms the 5% rate, ₱10,000 floor, and ₱100,000 ceiling applied for "2024 to 2025," and a PhilHealth advisory reported by the Philippine Information Agency in May 2026 confirmed that the rate remains at 5% for 2026, describing it as the final scheduled adjustment under the law. Several rate increases originally scheduled for earlier years (2021–2023) were also suspended by the national government at the time, which is why some older sources may show inconsistent figures for those specific years — that history does not affect the 2026 rate.

### How the Floor and Ceiling Work

- **Income floor (₱10,000):** If your monthly basic salary is ₱10,000 or below, your premium is computed as if you earned exactly ₱10,000. You will not pay less than ₱500 per month.
- **Income ceiling (₱100,000):** If your monthly basic salary is ₱100,000 or more, your premium is capped at ₱5,000 per month, regardless of how much more you earn.
- **In between:** Your premium is a straight 5% of your actual monthly basic salary.

"Monthly basic salary" refers to your fixed base pay for regular working hours. Based on how PhilHealth and related payroll guidance describe it, this generally excludes overtime pay, allowances, bonuses, and commissions, and the 13th month pay is a separate statutory benefit that is not used to compute a monthly premium. If you're unsure what figure your employer uses as your basic salary for PhilHealth purposes, your HR or payroll department can confirm it.

## How Contribution Rules Differ by Member Type

| Member Type | Who Pays | Rate Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Employed (private/government) | Split 50/50 between employee and employer | 5% of monthly basic salary |
| Self-employed | Member pays in full | 5% of declared monthly income |
| Voluntary member | Member pays in full | 5% of declared monthly income |
| Land-based OFW | Member pays in full | 5% of declared monthly income |
| Kasambahay | Generally the household employer, unless the Kasambahay earns ₱5,000/month or more, in which case a proportionate share may apply | 5% of monthly basic salary |
| Indirect contributor (senior citizen, indigent, qualified PWD, etc.) | Government-subsidized | Not self-computed |

Self-employed and voluntary members declare their own monthly income to PhilHealth, and that declared amount — not a third party's estimate — is what the 5% formula is applied to.

## Step-by-Step: How to Compute Your Contribution

1. **Identify your monthly basic salary or declared income.** For employees, this is the fixed base salary stated in your payroll record, not your total take-home pay. For self-employed and voluntary members, this is the income you declare to PhilHealth.
2. **Check where your amount falls against the floor and ceiling.** If it's ₱10,000 or below, use ₱10,000. If it's ₱100,000 or above, use ₱100,000. Otherwise, use your actual figure.
3. **Multiply by 5%.** This gives you the total monthly premium.
4. **Split the total if you're an employed member.** Divide the total by two: one half is your payroll deduction, the other half is your employer's share.
5. **Verify against your official record.** Log in to the PhilHealth Member Portal to confirm the amount actually posted to your account matches your computation.

## Worked Examples

*The following are illustrative examples only, using the formula above.*

**Example 1 — Employed member earning ₱20,000/month:**
₱20,000 × 5% = ₱1,000 total monthly premium → ₱500 employee share, ₱500 employer share.

**Example 2 — Employed member earning ₱8,000/month (below the floor):**
Because ₱8,000 is below the ₱10,000 floor, the premium is computed on ₱10,000 instead: ₱10,000 × 5% = ₱500 total → ₱250 employee share, ₱250 employer share.

**Example 3 — Self-employed member declaring ₱35,000/month income:**
₱35,000 × 5% = ₱1,750 total, paid in full by the member (no employer share).

**Example 4 — Employed executive earning ₱150,000/month (above the ceiling):**
Because ₱150,000 exceeds the ₱100,000 ceiling, the premium is capped: ₱100,000 × 5% = ₱5,000 total → ₱2,500 employee share, ₱2,500 employer share.

## How to Verify Your Actual Contribution

A calculation using the formula above gives you an *estimate* of what should be deducted or paid. To confirm what PhilHealth has actually recorded:

1. Go to the PhilHealth Member Portal through the official PhilHealth website.
2. Log in using your PhilHealth Identification Number (PIN) and password.
3. Open your contribution or premium payment history.
4. Compare the posted amounts and periods against your own computation or payslip.
5. If you're employed, cross-check the figures with your payroll department if anything looks off.

## Common Problems

- **Computed amount doesn't match payslip deduction.** This is usually a basic-salary mismatch — confirm with HR what base figure they use for PhilHealth purposes.
- **Contribution not showing in the Member Portal.** Postings can take time to reflect, especially for recent payments; keep your receipt or reference number while you wait.
- **Uncertainty over member category.** If you're not sure whether you're an employed, self-employed, voluntary, or indirect contributor, a PhilHealth Local Health Insurance Office can confirm your classification.
- **Declared income for self-employed/voluntary members feels unclear.** PhilHealth's own materials on declared-income rules for these categories should be checked directly, since specific documentation requirements can vary by case.

## What to Do If Something Goes Wrong

If your posted contributions are missing, incorrect, or inconsistent with your payslip:

- Keep all payment receipts, reference numbers, and payslips as supporting records.
- Report the discrepancy to your employer's HR/payroll team first if you're employed, since remittance is their responsibility.
- If unresolved, raise the issue directly with PhilHealth through the Member Portal's support channel or a PhilHealth branch/LHIO, bringing your supporting documents.
- Do not assume a missing contribution will resolve itself before you need to claim a benefit — unresolved gaps can affect eligibility.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is the PhilHealth contribution rate increasing again after 2026?**
Based on available official reporting, the 5% rate reached its final scheduled level under the Universal Health Care Act's phased schedule, and PhilHealth's May 2026 advisory described it as the final adjustment. Any further change would require a new circular or legislation, so members should watch for official PhilHealth announcements rather than assume the rate is permanently fixed.

**Does PhilHealth have its own online calculator?**
The available official PhilHealth materials describe the contribution formula and table rather than an interactive calculator tool. Independent calculators found online apply the same official 5% formula, floor, and ceiling, but only PhilHealth's own Member Portal reflects your actual posted contributions.

**Do self-employed and voluntary members pay the same rate as employees?**
Yes, the 5% rate, floor, and ceiling apply the same way — the difference is that self-employed, voluntary, and land-based OFW members pay the full amount themselves instead of splitting it with an employer.

**What counts as "monthly basic salary" for the computation?**
It refers to fixed base pay for regular working hours. Based on available guidance, allowances, overtime, bonuses, and 13th month pay are generally not included, but your employer's payroll or PhilHealth's own definitions should be checked if your situation is unclear.

**Where can I find the exact PhilHealth premium schedule history?**
PhilHealth Circular No. 2019-0009 sets out the year-by-year phased schedule from 2019 through the 5% rate, and PhilHealth's official contribution table PDF confirms the figures that apply from 2024 onward.
