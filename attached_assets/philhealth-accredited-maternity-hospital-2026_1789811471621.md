## PhilHealth Accredited Maternity Hospital: How to Confirm One Before Your Due Date

Here's the direct answer: whether a hospital is "PhilHealth accredited for maternity" depends on **which two official PhilHealth listings it appears on** — the general **"Hospitals and Infirmaries/Dispensaries"** accreditation list (which shows the facility's DOH hospital level, relevant to delivery coverage) and, separately, the **"Maternity Care Package Providers"** list for non-hospital birthing facilities and antenatal care providers. Both are published on PhilHealth's Accredited Health Facilities page, and checking the right one before your due date is the only reliable way to confirm coverage.

This guide is for expecting parents in **2026** who want to confirm a specific hospital or maternity facility is currently PhilHealth-accredited, understand what "accredited" actually determines for delivery coverage, and know what to do if there's no accredited option nearby.

> **Provider information can change. Check the official PhilHealth provider directory before visiting.** Accreditation status, hospital levels, and even a facility's specific service accreditations can change. This guide does not name individual hospitals — the official PhilHealth listings are the only current, verifiable reference.

## What Does "PhilHealth Accredited" Actually Mean for a Maternity Hospital?

**Accreditation** is PhilHealth's formal process of verifying a health facility's qualifications and capabilities against PhilHealth's guidelines and standards, so the facility can participate in the National Health Insurance Program and receive PhilHealth payment for services rendered. A hospital that isn't accredited for a given service **cannot have that service reimbursed by PhilHealth**, regardless of your own membership standing.

For maternity care specifically, accreditation isn't a single yes/no status — it depends on **facility type and hospital level**:

- **Hospital-based delivery** (normal delivery and Cesarean section) is covered under **PhilHealth Circular No. 2026-0005**, which applies to **accredited Level 1 to Level 3 hospitals**.
- **Non-hospital delivery and outpatient maternity care** (birthing homes, lying-in clinics, infirmaries, rural health units with a birthing "One Stop Shop") is covered under **PhilHealth Circular No. 2026-0006**, and these facilities appear on a separate accreditation list.

This is why the *same* hospital could, in principle, be accredited for general PhilHealth services but not specifically verified for maternity-related case rates — checking the specific listing matters more than assuming based on the hospital's reputation or size.

## The Two Official Lists to Check

### 1. "Hospitals and Infirmaries/Dispensaries" (for hospital-based delivery)

This is PhilHealth's general hospital accreditation list, found on PhilHealth's Accredited Health Facilities page. A hospital's presence here confirms its accreditation status and typically indicates its **DOH-assigned level** (Level 1, 2, or 3), which determines the complexity of services it's licensed and accredited to provide — including whether it can manage normal delivery and Cesarean sections under PhilHealth's current maternity case rates.

### 2. "Maternity Care Package Providers" (for non-hospital and outpatient maternity care)

PhilHealth separately publishes a **"List of Accredited Maternity Care Package Providers"** — a dedicated directory (updated periodically, with the current version showing a March 31, 2026 update date) listing facility names and addresses specifically accredited for maternity care package services. Based on the structure of this list and PhilHealth Circular No. 2026-0006, this generally covers **birthing homes, lying-in clinics, infirmaries, and primary care facilities** providing antenatal, delivery, and postpartum care outside the hospital setting — distinct from the general hospital list above.

**If you're planning your prenatal care rather than delivery itself:** PhilHealth Circular No. 2026-0006 specifies that if your chosen **YAKAP clinic is not itself accredited as a maternal care provider, it is expected to refer you to an accredited provider of antenatal care.** This means your entry point doesn't have to be the final answer — ask your YAKAP clinic directly if you're unsure.

## Understanding DOH Hospital Levels

PhilHealth's hospital-based maternity case rates (₱29,000 for normal delivery, ₱58,000–₱62,000 for Cesarean section, per PhilHealth Circular No. 2026-0005) apply specifically to **Level 1 to Level 3 hospitals** — reflecting the DOH's hospital licensing classification, which broadly indicates a hospital's capacity and the range of services it's equipped and licensed to provide. A hospital's DOH level is a separate designation from PhilHealth accreditation itself, though the two are closely related since PhilHealth accreditation requirements reference a facility's DOH licensing status.

**Practical takeaway:** confirming a hospital's DOH level and its PhilHealth accreditation are two different checks. If you can't find level information from PhilHealth's own listing, the hospital's administrative or PhilHealth desk can confirm both directly.

## How to Check Accreditation Before Your Due Date

1. **Go to PhilHealth's Accredited Health Facilities page** (philhealth.gov.ph → Partners → Providers → Facilities → Accredited).
2. **For a hospital delivery**, open the **"Hospitals and Infirmaries/Dispensaries"** listing and search for your chosen hospital.
3. **For a birthing home, lying-in clinic, or non-hospital option**, open the **"Maternity Care Package Providers"** listing instead.
4. **Check the "updated as of" date** on the page — accreditation lists are periodically refreshed, and the version you're viewing should be reasonably current.
5. **Call the facility directly** to confirm current accreditation status and, for hospitals, ask about their DOH level and whether they currently accept PhilHealth maternity case rate claims — a listing is a snapshot, and direct confirmation closer to your due date is the most reliable check.
6. **If your area has limited accredited options**, ask your local PhilHealth office or your prenatal care provider about the nearest accredited alternative, including non-hospital options if your pregnancy is assessed as low-risk.

## What Accreditation Does and Doesn't Guarantee

**What it means:** an accredited facility can have PhilHealth case rates deducted directly from your bill at discharge, and — for basic/ward accommodation — is subject to PhilHealth's no-balance-billing policy for services already covered by the case rate.

**What it doesn't mean:** accreditation is not a guarantee of bed availability, physician availability on a given date, or that every possible service you might need during delivery is included in the case rate. It also doesn't override clinical judgment — whether a non-hospital facility is appropriate for your delivery depends on whether your pregnancy is assessed as low-risk, a decision for your care team, not something this article can determine for you.

PhilHealth coverage depends on the applicable benefit package and eligibility requirements — accreditation is a prerequisite for coverage at a given facility, not a guarantee of the full range of care you might need there.

## Common Problems and What to Do

**The hospital you planned to use isn't on the accredited list.**
Don't assume — call the hospital's PhilHealth or admitting desk directly, since listings are periodic snapshots and a hospital's status can have changed. If it's genuinely not accredited, ask your OB-GYN or the hospital for a referral to an accredited alternative before your due date, not during labor.

**You can't tell what DOH level your hospital is.**
This information may not always be obvious from the accreditation listing alone. Ask the hospital's admin or PhilHealth desk directly, since this affects which maternity case rate applies.

**Your area has no accredited hospital nearby, only a birthing home.**
If your pregnancy is assessed as low-risk, a PhilHealth-accredited non-hospital birthing facility is a legitimate, separately-covered option under PhilHealth Circular No. 2026-0006 — check the "Maternity Care Package Providers" list. If your pregnancy is high-risk, hospital-level care is what your physician would advise, and your local PhilHealth office can help identify the nearest accredited hospital option.

**Your prenatal care clinic said it isn't accredited for maternal care.**
Ask them for a referral to an accredited antenatal care provider — PhilHealth Circular No. 2026-0006 specifically expects a non-accredited YAKAP clinic to make this referral rather than leave you without a covered option.

**You were billed for something in a ward bed you thought was covered.**
This may be a no-balance-billing violation. Raise it with the facility first; if unresolved, contact PhilHealth's Corporate Action Center at (02) 8662-2588 or actioncenter@philhealth.gov.ph.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How do I know if a hospital is PhilHealth-accredited for maternity?**
Check PhilHealth's "Hospitals and Infirmaries/Dispensaries" accreditation listing for the hospital itself, and confirm its DOH level, since hospital-based maternity case rates apply specifically to Level 1–3 hospitals. Then confirm directly with the hospital closer to your due date.

**Is a birthing home the same as an accredited maternity hospital?**
No — birthing homes, lying-in clinics, and similar non-hospital facilities are accredited and listed separately, under "Maternity Care Package Providers," and are covered under a different set of rules than hospital-based delivery.

**Does PhilHealth accreditation guarantee a bed will be available when I deliver?**
No — accreditation confirms the facility can process PhilHealth benefits for the relevant service; it says nothing about bed or staff availability on any given date.

**Can I switch hospitals if I find out mine isn't accredited?**
Yes, and doing so before your due date — rather than during labor — is the more manageable path. Ask your OB-GYN or the hospital itself for an accredited alternative.

**Does a Level 1 hospital cover the same maternity case rates as a Level 3 hospital?**
Both fall within the Level 1–3 range that PhilHealth's current hospital-based maternity case rates apply to; the case rate amounts themselves are tied to the type of procedure (normal delivery vs. Cesarean section), not the specific hospital level, based on the official circular reviewed for this article.

**Where can I find an accredited antenatal care provider if my YAKAP clinic isn't one?**
Your YAKAP clinic is expected to refer you to an accredited antenatal care provider if it isn't accredited itself — ask them directly, or check the "Maternity Care Package Providers" listing.
