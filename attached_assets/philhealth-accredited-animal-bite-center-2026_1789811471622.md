## PhilHealth Accredited Animal Bite Center: Where to Go and What's Covered in 2026

Here's the direct answer: if you've been bitten or scratched by an animal, go to the nearest **Animal Bite Treatment Center (ABTC)** or PhilHealth-accredited health facility as soon as possible — PhilHealth's **Animal Bite Treatment (ABT) package** currently covers **₱5,850 per case** for rabies post-exposure treatment, available at **over 700 accredited providers nationwide**. Don't wait to "confirm eligibility" first; rabies is close to universally fatal once symptoms appear, and prompt treatment is the actual protection.

This guide is for PhilHealth members and their families in **2026** who need to know where to get treated, what the package actually pays for, and how accreditation works — based on official PhilHealth sources.

> **This is not medical advice.** Whether you need post-exposure treatment, and what specific treatment protocol applies, is a clinical determination made by the treating health professional based on the type of exposure and the animal involved — not something to self-diagnose from this article. If you've been bitten or scratched, seek care promptly rather than researching first.

> **Provider information can change. Check the official PhilHealth provider directory before visiting.** This guide does not name specific centers — PhilHealth's own accredited facilities listing and the DOH's ABTC network are the current, verifiable references.

## What Is the PhilHealth Animal Bite Treatment Package?

The ABT package is PhilHealth's benefit for **Post-Exposure Prophylaxis (PEP)** — the anti-rabies treatment given after a person has been exposed to a potentially rabid animal, through a bite, scratch, lick, or similar contact. The package framework originates from **PhilHealth Circular No. 015, s. 2012**, issued in response to **Republic Act No. 9482** (the Anti-Rabies Act of 2007).

**On the current rate:** PhilHealth's own official announcement, published March 18, 2025, states the ABT package **"has been increased to P5,850 from the previous P3,000."** This is the most current, PhilHealth-sourced figure available for this article, and it supersedes the ₱3,000 fixed rate set in the original 2012 circular.

> **A note on conflicting figures found online:** some regional PhilHealth reports and third-party sources describe different or intermediate amounts — for example, a late-2024 regional report describing a total benefit of ₱3,900, or blog posts describing separate tiered amounts by exposure category (such as a lower amount for one exposure category and a higher amount for another). PhilHealth's own March 2025 nationwide announcement states a **single fixed amount of ₱5,850 per case**, without a category-based split. Because this is the most recent, most authoritative, and most specific official statement found, this article treats ₱5,850 as the current figure and does not present the other numbers as current.

Rabies is a serious public health concern in the Philippines: the Department of Health recorded **426 rabies cases in 2024, all of which resulted in death** — a fatality rate DOH and PhilHealth both describe as effectively 100%. This is the context for why PhilHealth frames prompt treatment-seeking, rather than hesitation, as the priority message of this benefit.

## Who Is Eligible?

Based on PhilHealth's own announcements of this package, **PhilHealth members and their qualified dependents** who have been exposed to rabies through an animal bite or scratch are entitled to the ABT package.

The original 2012 circular states that the **existing standards for the management of animal bite patients, as prescribed by the DOH**, are used as the reference for classifying exposures and determining the appropriate treatment. In practice, this means the treating facility — not the patient — determines, based on DOH-recognized exposure categories and clinical judgment, whether post-exposure treatment is indicated for your specific situation.

## What the ABT Package Covers

Per PhilHealth's official description, the package covers:

- **Rabies vaccine**
- **Rabies immune globulin** (for immediate protection in higher-risk exposures)
- **Local wound care**
- **Tetanus toxoid and anti-tetanus serum (ATS)**
- **Antibiotics**
- **Treatment supplies** — syringes, alcohol, antiseptics

The 2012 circular's general framework also notes that **pre-exposure prophylaxis** (vaccination given before any bite occurs, as a preventive measure) is excluded from this package — the ABT package is specifically a **post-exposure** benefit.

PhilHealth coverage depends on the applicable benefit package and eligibility requirements. This article explains the ABT package's benefit rules; it does not tell you whether your specific bite or scratch requires treatment — that's a clinical decision for the treating health professional to make.

## What Kind of Facility Qualifies as an "Animal Bite Center"?

There are two related but distinct things worth knowing here:

1. **Animal Bite Treatment Centers (ABTCs)** are health facilities — a mix of hospital-based, city/rural health unit-based, and LGU-operated centers — set up and recognized under the national rabies prevention framework to provide anti-rabies treatment.
2. **PhilHealth-accredited providers for the ABT Package** are the specific set of facilities PhilHealth has accredited to receive reimbursement under this benefit. The 2012 circular specifies that providers (government and private) must be **recognized by the National Rabies Prevention and Control Program** to participate.

In practice, an ABTC that is also PhilHealth-accredited is where you'd get both the treatment and the PhilHealth-covered cost deduction. PhilHealth's official Accredited Health Facilities page maintains a specific listing titled **"Animal Bite Package Providers"** — this is the authoritative reference for which facilities are currently accredited for PhilHealth reimbursement under this package.

## How to Find an Accredited Animal Bite Center

1. **If you've just been bitten or scratched, don't wait to look this up — seek care immediately** at the nearest hospital emergency room or health facility, and ask specifically about anti-rabies treatment. Time matters more than finding the "perfect" facility.
2. **To plan ahead or confirm PhilHealth accreditation**, go to PhilHealth's Accredited Health Facilities page (philhealth.gov.ph → Partners → Providers → Facilities → Accredited) and open the **"Animal Bite Package Providers"** listing.
3. **Check the "updated as of" date** on the page before relying on it.
4. **Look for a facility in your city or municipality.**
5. **Bring your PhilHealth Identification Number (PIN) or Member Data Record (MDR)** if you have it readily available, along with a valid ID — though seeking treatment promptly should not be delayed while gathering documents.

## Step-by-Step: What Happens When You Go for Treatment

1. **You present at an ABTC or PhilHealth-accredited facility** after a bite, scratch, or other potential rabies exposure.
2. **The facility assesses the exposure** using DOH-prescribed standards to classify the case and determine the appropriate treatment protocol.
3. **Post-exposure treatment is administered** as clinically indicated — this may include the rabies vaccine series, rabies immune globulin, wound care, tetanus prophylaxis, and antibiotics.
4. **If you're a PhilHealth member or qualified dependent at an accredited facility**, the applicable portion of the cost is covered under the ₱5,850 ABT package, generally deducted from what you'd otherwise be billed.
5. **The facility files the claim with PhilHealth** — under the standard framework for this kind of outpatient package, this is generally handled by the accredited provider rather than the patient directly.

## Common Problems and What to Do

**You're not sure if the nearest facility is PhilHealth-accredited for this package.**
Check PhilHealth's official "Animal Bite Package Providers" listing — but if you've just been exposed, don't delay treatment while checking; seek care first and address the coverage question with the facility directly.

**You were charged out-of-pocket for something described as covered.**
Raise it with the facility's PhilHealth desk first. If unresolved, contact PhilHealth's Corporate Action Center at (02) 866-225-88, or the mobile touchpoints PhilHealth has published: (Smart) 0998-857-2957 or 0968-865-4670, (Globe) 0917-1275987 or 0917-1109812.

**You don't have your PhilHealth ID or MDR with you.**
Seek treatment first regardless — this is a time-sensitive medical situation. Sort out documentation and coverage confirmation with the facility afterward.

**The animal that bit you seemed healthy or is a vaccinated pet.**
Whether treatment is needed is a clinical determination based on DOH-prescribed exposure standards, not something to decide based on how the animal appeared. PhilHealth's own public messaging explicitly encourages people not to hesitate to seek medical attention "whether it is a pet or not."

**You're confused by different peso amounts you've seen for this benefit online.**
As explained above, PhilHealth's own most recent official announcement states a single fixed rate of ₱5,850 per case. Older or regional figures you may encounter (₱3,000, ₱3,900, or category-based splits) are either outdated or not confirmed at the national level in the sources reviewed for this article.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How much does PhilHealth cover for animal bite treatment?**
₱5,850 per case, per PhilHealth's official 2025 announcement — increased from a previous ₱3,000 fixed rate.

**Where can I find a PhilHealth-accredited animal bite center?**
On PhilHealth's Accredited Health Facilities page, under the "Animal Bite Package Providers" listing. PhilHealth has also cited more than 700 accredited providers nationwide as of its March 2025 announcement, with continued expansion.

**Do I need to be a PhilHealth member to get treated?**
The ABT package benefit applies to PhilHealth members and their qualified dependents. Regardless of membership status, if you've been bitten or scratched, seeking prompt medical attention is the priority — coverage questions can be resolved at the facility.

**What does the package actually pay for?**
Rabies vaccine, rabies immune globulin, local wound care, tetanus toxoid and anti-tetanus serum, antibiotics, and related treatment supplies.

**Is pre-exposure rabies vaccination covered under this package?**
No — the original circular describes pre-exposure prophylaxis as excluded from this specific post-exposure benefit package.

**Do I need to file a claim myself?**
Under the standard framework for outpatient packages like this, the accredited facility generally files the claim, rather than the patient.

**What if I was bitten by a vaccinated pet — do I still need treatment?**
That's a clinical decision made by the treating health professional based on DOH exposure classification standards, not something to determine on your own. When in doubt, seek care.
