# PhilHealth Mental Health Benefits 2026: What's Covered and How to Use Them

PhilHealth's main mental health benefit is the **Outpatient Mental Health Package**. It gives each patient **₱9,000 a year for general mental health services** or **₱16,000 a year for specialty services**, paid to the accredited provider, not to you. It covers outpatient care only, so it does not pay for hospital stays.

This guide is for PhilHealth members, their dependents and families who want to understand the 2026 coverage and how to start using it. It explains the benefit rules only. It is not medical advice, and it cannot tell you whether you or someone you know needs treatment. A health professional can.

**If you or someone you know is in immediate danger or thinking about self-harm,** call local emergency services. PhilHealth's own announcement lists the National Center for Mental Health 24/7 crisis hotline at 0917-899-8727 or 989-8727.

## Quick Answer

| Package | Yearly amount per patient | Paid in two parts | Typical places to get it |
|---|---|---|---|
| General Mental Health Services | ₱9,000 | ₱5,400 + ₱3,600 | Rural health units, city or municipal health offices, Level 1 and 2 hospitals, DOH medicine access program sites, stand-alone mental health clinics |
| Specialty Mental Health Services | ₱16,000 | ₱9,600 + ₱6,400 | Accredited Level 2 and 3 hospitals with specialists, custodial care facilities, DOH-identified national specialty centers |

The two parts are called **tranches**: the provider claims the first part after the initial services and the second part after the follow-up visits are completed. As a patient, you do not claim these amounts yourself.

## What Is the Outpatient Mental Health Package?

PhilHealth launched the package in October 2023 under PhilHealth Circular No. 2023-0018. It pays accredited providers a fixed yearly amount for each patient who receives mental health services without being admitted.

The benefit is built around the Department of Health's mhGAP program (the Mental Health Gap Action Programme), a framework that trains health workers to screen and assess common mental, neurological and substance use conditions. Under the circular, patients are screened by an mhGAP-trained health worker and registered in a mental health registry.

PhilHealth's 2025 announcement says the package covers conditions such as depression, psychosis, epilepsy, child and adolescent mental and behavioral disorders, dementia, and risk of self-harm or suicide, among others.

## What Does It Cover?

**Both packages include:**
- Screening or assessment based on mhGAP
- Diagnostic tests
- Up to 12 follow-up visits
- Psychoeducation (explaining the condition and its management) and psychosocial support
- Medicines available through the DOH medicine access program for mental health

**The specialty package also includes psychotherapy** and access to psychiatrists, neurologists and psychologists.

**What it does not do:** The package does not finance the DOH's purchase of essential medicines. Medicines are provided through the DOH program rather than bought with PhilHealth's money.

## Who Can Use It?

PhilHealth's announcement says members and dependents aged 10 and above can avail of the package. The circular describes the rule slightly differently: psychiatric cases must be at least 10 years old, while neurologic cases such as epilepsy can be covered at any age. The circular's wording also refers to all Filipinos who meet the age criteria, so the available official sources are not fully consistent on whether non-members can use it. Ask the provider to confirm your status.

You must also be screened by an mhGAP-trained health worker and registered, and the provider must be accredited for the package.

## Who Provides the Service?

Only PhilHealth-accredited providers can bill the package. The circular requires them to meet facility standards, including trained staff, emergency supplies and medicines, and a referral arrangement with a referral center.

Availability can be limited. A November 2025 news report quoted a PhilHealth official saying about 3,000 physicians had completed mhGAP training but only about 100 were accredited at that time. A March 2026 report from the Cordillera region said claims for the general package remained very low and PhilHealth planned an orientation for accredited providers. The number of accredited providers may be higher by now, so check before you go.

**Provider information can change. Check the official PhilHealth provider directory before visiting.**

## Is This Part of YAKAP?

No. According to a March 2026 report, the mental health package is separate from the YAKAP program. You can use it at any provider accredited for the package, even if your YAKAP clinic is not accredited for it.

## Step-by-Step: How to Use the Package

1. **Find an accredited provider.** Start with your rural health unit, city or municipal health office, or a hospital, and ask whether it is accredited for the PhilHealth mental health package. Confirm using the official provider directory.
2. **Ask for a screening or assessment.** The mhGAP-trained health worker or doctor assesses you and decides whether the general or specialty package fits your situation.
3. **Get registered.** The provider enters you in the mental health registry. Ask whether your PhilHealth membership details are needed.
4. **Attend the follow-up visits.** The package allows up to 12. Completing the required visits matters because the second payment tranche depends on them.
5. **Let the provider file the claims.** Providers prepare and submit the claims. You may be asked for signatures or to complete a satisfaction questionnaire, and you may receive a record booklet (the circular mentions a "mental health passport"). Keep these.
6. **Ask about referrals before you transfer.** If you need specialty care elsewhere, the circular allows transfers between accredited providers up to twice per fiscal year. Transferring to a non-accredited provider waives the benefit.

## Costs: What You May Still Pay

Under the circular, patients cannot be charged for the minimum standards of care covered by the package. Co-payments are limited to amenities, service upgrades or non-mental-health services, and they are capped.

**Example (illustration only):** A patient registered under the general package has a yearly benefit of ₱9,000. The provider claims ₱5,400 after the first stage and ₱3,600 after completing the follow-up visits. The patient is not billed for the covered services, but could be charged for an optional upgrade or a service outside the package.

PhilHealth's payment goes to the provider, and the exact charges for anything outside the package depend on the facility. Ask for a written breakdown.

## What Changed Since 2023

| Topic | Earlier information | Current position |
|---|---|---|
| Claims filing | Providers filed claims manually with the PhilHealth Regional Office | PhilHealth Advisory No. 2025-0036 (June 18, 2025) says providers file through eClaims, and claims for patients enrolled on or after July 1, 2025 must be filed electronically |
| Claim codes | Original package codes (MHG1, MHG2, MHS1, MHS2) | Adjusted codes (MHG01, MHG02, MHS01, MHS02) |
| 45-day limit | The 2023 circular tied registration to the yearly 45-day benefit allowance | PhilHealth Circular No. 2025-0007 lifted the 45-day benefit limit rule, effective April 4, 2025. The available sources do not explain how this changes the registration step |
| Package amounts | ₱9,000 and ₱16,000 | PhilHealth's March 2025 announcement still lists these amounts; no change was found |

## Inpatient Mental Health Care

The 2023 circular states that the outpatient package is for outpatient care only. Hospital admission for mental and behavioral disorders falls under PhilHealth's inpatient case rates instead. The circular listed a figure for that case rate, but PhilHealth adjusted case rates in 2024 and 2025, and this guide could not confirm the current 2026 amount. Ask the hospital which case rate applies and check it against PhilHealth's current schedule. A case rate is a fixed amount paid to the facility, not a guarantee that the whole bill is covered.

## Common Problems

- **The nearest facility is not accredited.** Ask your health office or PhilHealth office which nearby providers are accredited.
- **You are told the service is not available.** Some accredited facilities have filed few or no claims, so ask directly whether they offer the package.
- **Your age or condition does not seem to fit.** The package has age rules, and psychiatric and neurologic cases are treated differently. Ask the provider to explain.
- **Medicines are not covered by PhilHealth's money.** Ask whether the facility is a DOH medicine access program site.
- **You were transferred or referred.** Transferring to a non-accredited provider waives the benefit, so confirm accreditation first.

## What To Do If Something Goes Wrong

1. Ask the provider to explain in writing what is covered and what you are being charged for.
2. Contact your PhilHealth Regional or Local Health Insurance Office. PhilHealth's Action Center number printed in its 2025 advisory is (02) 8662-2588. Check PhilHealth's official channels for current contact details before calling.
3. Keep your records, receipts and any registry or passport booklet.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Does PhilHealth cover mental health treatment?
Yes, for outpatient care through the Outpatient Mental Health Package. Inpatient care is covered separately through case rates.

### How much does PhilHealth pay for mental health?
₱9,000 per patient per year for general services and ₱16,000 for specialty services.

### Does PhilHealth cover therapy or a psychiatrist?
The specialty package includes psychotherapy and access to psychiatrists, neurologists and psychologists at accredited facilities.

### Does PhilHealth cover depression?
PhilHealth's announcement lists depression among the conditions the package addresses. A health professional decides whether you need the general or specialty package.

### Can children and teenagers use it?
PhilHealth's announcement says patients aged 10 and above, and the circular includes child and adolescent mental and behavioral disorders. Neurologic cases can be covered at any age under the circular.

### Does PhilHealth pay for medicines?
Medicines are provided through the DOH medicine access program. The package does not finance the DOH's purchase of essential medicines.

### Do I need to be a member?
The available official sources are not fully consistent. PhilHealth says members and dependents can avail, while the circular's wording refers to Filipinos who meet the age criteria. Ask the provider.

### Do I need a referral or prior approval?
The summary of the circular reviewed for this article mentions screening and registration, but no pre-authorization step. Referral and transfer rules apply if you move between providers.

### Can I use it if my YAKAP clinic is not accredited for it?
Yes, according to a March 2026 report, because the package is separate from YAKAP.
