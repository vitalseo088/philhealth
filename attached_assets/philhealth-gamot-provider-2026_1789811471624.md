# 2. Article

## PhilHealth GAMOT Provider: How to Find One and What to Expect in 2026

Here's the direct answer: a **PhilHealth GAMOT provider** — officially called a **GAMOT Facility** — is a drug dispensing facility (a pharmacy, drugstore, or health facility with an FDA or DOH license to retail drugs) that PhilHealth has engaged to dispense medicines under the GAMOT outpatient drug benefit. At an active GAMOT facility, covered medicines are dispensed to eligible members **with no out-of-pocket expense**, within an annual benefit of up to **₱20,000 per beneficiary** covering **75 essential medications**.

This guide is for two audiences: members who want to know where and how to claim GAMOT medicines in **2026**, and pharmacies or health facilities considering what participation actually commits them to. It's based on PhilHealth Circular No. 2025-0013 (the nationwide GAMOT implementation) and PhilHealth Circular No. 2026-0002 (supplemental guidelines for participating facilities).

> **Provider information can change. Check the official PhilHealth provider directory before visiting.** GAMOT facilities can be temporarily deactivated or can voluntarily withdraw from the program, and PhilHealth maintains and updates the list of active GAMOT facilities in its own systems. This guide deliberately does not publish a list of named pharmacies, since accreditation status changes and any static list would go stale.

## What Is a GAMOT Facility?

PhilHealth Circular No. 2026-0002 defines the key terms plainly:

- A **Drug Dispensing Facility** is any establishment carrying on the retail business of selling drugs and medicines to customers, holding a License to Operate (LTO) as a drug retailer from the **Food and Drug Administration (FDA)** or the **Department of Health (DOH)**.
- A **GAMOT Facility** is a drug dispensing facility that has been **engaged by PhilHealth** to provide the contents of the outpatient drug benefit package.

**PhilHealth GAMOT** itself — Guaranteed and Accessible Medications for Outpatient Treatment — is described in the circular as PhilHealth's outpatient drug benefit package, functioning as "the pharmaceutical service delivery arm of PhilHealth's outpatient benefits." It's a component of the broader **YAKAP (Yaman ng Kalusugan Program)**, and covers medications for conditions including infections, asthma, COPD, diabetes, high cholesterol, high blood pressure, heart conditions, and nervous system disorders.

Facilities use a **GAMOT Application (GAMOT App)** — software used to prescribe medications, validate prescriptions, record dispensing, and monitor facility performance.

## How to Use a GAMOT Provider as a Member

### What You Need First

1. **Register as a YAKAP beneficiary.** This can be done through the eGovPH app, the PhilHealth member online portal, at PhilHealth offices, or through other available channels. GAMOT sits inside YAKAP, so YAKAP registration is the entry point.
2. **Get a prescription from a YAKAP or PhilHealth-certified physician.** GAMOT medicines are dispensed against a valid prescription, not on request.
3. **Bring one government-issued ID and your prescription** to a GAMOT facility.

### What Happens at the Facility

The medicine is dispensed and charged directly to PhilHealth. Under the Performance Commitment that every GAMOT facility signs, facilities undertake to **"provide and dispense the necessary drugs and medicines in strict accordance with the rules and guidelines of the 'PhilHealth GAMOT' Program with no out-of-pocket expenses on the part of the qualified PhilHealth Member and their dependents."**

Two documentation details worth knowing: facilities are required to ensure that the **true signatures of both the pharmacist and the PhilHealth member** appear on the **GAMOT Availment Slip** for every transaction, and to maintain an accurate registry of member availments. Signing that slip is a normal part of the process.

PhilHealth coverage depends on the applicable benefit package and eligibility requirements — GAMOT covers medicines on the official GAMOT List, within the annual limit, not every medicine a member might be prescribed.

## What a Facility Must Do to Be a GAMOT Provider

For pharmacies and health facilities considering participation, PhilHealth Circular No. 2026-0002's Performance Commitment template sets out the core representations and undertakings:

### Eligibility Representations

- Be a duly registered, licensed, or certified pharmaceutical or health facility capable of providing drugs and medicines for the program, holding a License/FDA Certificate.
- Be a member in good standing of the relevant industry association (the Philippine Hospital Association for hospitals and infirmaries; the corresponding body for pharmaceutical companies, drugstores, and retailers).
- Warrant that health care professionals and personnel are PhilHealth-accredited where applicable, properly registered, and hold appropriate credentials and privileges.

### Ongoing Undertakings

- Maintain active NHIP membership as an employer for the duration of participation.
- Promptly inform PhilHealth **before** any change in location, ownership, or management.
- Dispense covered medicines with no out-of-pocket cost to qualified members.
- Accept payment through PhilHealth's **Auto-Credit Payment Scheme (ACPS)**, under which claim settlements are credited directly to the facility's designated deposit account.
- Maintain a **PhilHealth Bulletin Board** posting updated program information in a conspicuous, patient-accessible place, and keep required forms available.
- Maintain a registry of member availments and submit monitoring reports as required.
- Allow PhilHealth surveys, facility visits, and administrative assessments at reasonable times.

All accredited GAMOT facilities were required to submit an **updated Performance Commitment within 30 days from the issuance of PhilHealth Circular No. 2026-0002**.

## When a GAMOT Provider Stops Dispensing: Temporary Deactivation

This is the part most directly relevant to members, and it's the main subject of PhilHealth Circular No. 2026-0002. A GAMOT facility can **temporarily deactivate** its participation — pausing GAMOT dispensing — but only under tightly defined conditions.

### When Deactivation Is Allowed

A facility may temporarily deactivate, **on a per-branch basis**, only when:

- Payments for **valid and compliant claims** are delayed beyond **60 calendar days** from the date of claim submission; **and**
- The delay is **not** attributable to the facility — meaning not caused by incomplete or deficient documentation, claims under fraud/abuse investigation, claims returned for correction, administrative holds from the facility's non-compliance, or any other cause originating from the facility's own actions or omissions.

The circular is explicit that deactivation is **"a last resort, with due consideration primarily for member welfare."** Before deactivating, a facility must exhaust coordination with PhilHealth — including at least **three documented coordination attempts over at least 30 calendar days**, with at least one written communication to the Regional Office — and prepare a mitigation plan for continuity of care.

### Notice Requirements

| Requirement | Timeline |
|---|---|
| Prior written notice to the PhilHealth Regional Office | 15 calendar days before intended deactivation |
| PhilHealth Regional Office written response to the facility | Within 7 calendar days of receiving the notice |
| Conspicuous notices posted at facility entrances and service areas | At least 7 calendar days before the effective date |
| Member Advisory issued by the Regional Vice President | Within 24 hours of the deactivation effective date |

### What Protects Members During a Deactivation

Under the circular, a deactivating facility must:

- Coordinate with the PhilHealth Regional Office to identify alternative access points;
- Refer affected members and give them **clear written information** on available alternative GAMOT facilities — including facility name, address, contact information, operating hours, and distance/transport options;
- **Continue to accept and fill prescriptions for medicines not covered under the GAMOT List**; and
- Maintain a registry of members who sought GAMOT services during the deactivation period.

And PhilHealth, for its part, must maintain a dedicated hotline or member support help desk, navigate affected members to alternative facilities, provide updates through SMS/email/other channels, track affected members, and coordinate with the relevant LGU on alternative access arrangements.

### Reactivation

Once payment — **whether partial or full** — is received on the outstanding claims that triggered the deactivation, the facility must **reactivate and resume dispensing within 24 hours**, remove all deactivation notices, notify the Regional Office, restore GAMOT App connectivity, and post reactivation notices. PhilHealth then updates its **list of active GAMOT facilities** in its systems within the prescribed timelines.

### Deactivation vs. Withdrawal

These are not the same thing, and the circular draws the line clearly:

| | Temporary Deactivation | Voluntary Withdrawal |
|---|---|---|
| Nature | Temporary pause in program participation | Permanent termination of participation |
| Trigger | Qualifying payment delays beyond 60 days | The facility's own decision |
| Notice required | 15 calendar days | 15 calendar days |
| To resume later | Automatic reactivation on payment receipt | Requires a new application and full compliance with accreditation requirements |

## Common Problems and What to Do

**You went to a pharmacy and were told it's no longer dispensing GAMOT medicines.**
The facility should be posting conspicuous notices and giving you written referral information to alternative GAMOT facilities, including addresses, contact details, operating hours, and distance. PhilHealth is also required to post advisories on its website and social media and to help navigate members to alternatives.

**You were asked to pay out-of-pocket for a medicine that should be covered.**
Facilities commit, under their signed Performance Commitment, to dispense covered GAMOT medicines with no out-of-pocket expense to qualified members within the program rules. If you were charged for a covered medicine within your annual limit, that's worth raising — first with the facility, then with PhilHealth's Corporate Action Center at (02) 8662-2588 or actioncenter@philhealth.gov.ph.

**Your prescription was refused at a GAMOT facility.**
Check two things: whether the prescribing physician is YAKAP or PhilHealth-certified, and whether the specific medicine is on the official GAMOT List. Medicines outside the list aren't covered by this benefit even at a participating facility.

**You're not sure whether a nearby pharmacy is currently an active GAMOT provider.**
Verify through PhilHealth's own channels rather than a third-party list — PhilHealth maintains the list of active GAMOT facilities in its systems and updates it when facilities deactivate or reactivate.

**You've used up part of your ₱20,000 for the year and want to check the balance.**
Facilities are required to maintain a registry of member availments and regularly update the actual amount deducted from each member's benefit, so the facility's record is one point of reference; PhilHealth's own member channels are the other.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is a PhilHealth GAMOT provider?**
A drug dispensing facility — licensed by the FDA or DOH as a drug retailer — that PhilHealth has engaged to dispense medicines under the GAMOT outpatient drug benefit. The official term in PhilHealth's circulars is "GAMOT Facility."

**How much are GAMOT medicines worth per year?**
Up to ₱20,000 annually per beneficiary, covering 75 essential medications.

**Do I pay anything at a GAMOT provider?**
Facilities commit to dispensing covered GAMOT medicines with no out-of-pocket expense to qualified members, within the program's rules and the annual limit.

**Do I need to register for anything before using a GAMOT provider?**
Yes — you need to be registered as a YAKAP beneficiary first, since GAMOT is a component of the YAKAP program, and you need a prescription from a YAKAP or PhilHealth-certified physician.

**What do I bring to the pharmacy?**
One government-issued ID and your prescription.

**Can a GAMOT provider stop dispensing free medicines?**
Yes, but only under specific conditions — principally when PhilHealth payment for valid and compliant claims is delayed beyond 60 calendar days through no fault of the facility, and only after exhausting coordination efforts, with 15 days' prior notice and a member protection plan in place.

**If a provider deactivates, do they still serve me at all?**
A deactivating facility is required to continue accepting and filling prescriptions for medicines **not** on the GAMOT List — the suspension applies to GAMOT Benefit Package dispensing specifically.

**How quickly does a deactivated provider resume dispensing?**
Within 24 hours of receiving payment — whether partial or full — on the outstanding claims that formed the basis of the deactivation.
