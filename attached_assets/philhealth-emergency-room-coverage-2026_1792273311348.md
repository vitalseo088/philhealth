# PhilHealth Emergency Room Coverage 2026: What It Pays and What To Check

In 2026, PhilHealth emergency room coverage works in two ways. If you are treated in an accredited hospital's emergency department and the case is resolved within 24 hours, the **Outpatient Emergency Care Benefit (OECB)** pays for listed services at fixed fees. If the doctor orders admission, the **All Case Rates (ACR)** inpatient case rate applies instead. Neither one is a promise that PhilHealth pays your entire bill, so the most important step is to confirm that the benefit was deducted before you settle the balance.

This guide is for PhilHealth members, dependents and families facing an ER visit or an ER bill. It explains PhilHealth's rules, not medical advice. In a life-threatening situation, get care first. Coverage questions can be sorted out afterward.

## Quick Answer

| Situation | Which PhilHealth benefit applies | Key condition (per PhilHealth) |
|---|---|---|
| ER visit, treated and discharged | OECB, Facility-based Emergency | Case managed and resolved within 24 hours, in an accredited hospital |
| ER patient who dies in the ER before admission | OECB, Facility-based Emergency | Circular says benefit applies if the patient expired in the ED prior to admission |
| ER patient with admission orders | Inpatient ACR case rate | Admission order issued within 24 hours; where the patient physically waits is immaterial |
| Procedure done in the ER that already has a case rate | ACR procedure case rate | Applies regardless of entry point |
| Ambulance to the hospital | OECB, Prehospital Emergency | Accredited ambulance provider; fixed package plus a distance package |
| Services not on the fixed fee list | Not covered by the OECB | Subject to out-of-pocket payment |

## What Is the PhilHealth Outpatient Emergency Care Benefit?

The OECB is PhilHealth's benefit for emergency care that does not lead to a hospital admission. Before it existed, PhilHealth's ACR paid for emergency department care mainly as part of an admitted patient's hospital course, and for some surgical emergencies. PhilHealth's own circular says there was a gap for acute care that did not need admission. The OECB was created to fill it.

It has two parts:

- **Facility-based Emergency (FBE) benefit:** care in the emergency department of an accredited hospital and its extension facilities. It is governed by PhilHealth Circular 2025-0020 (Revision 1).
- **Prehospital Emergency (PHE) benefit:** ambulance transport and on-board emergency care. It is governed by Circular 2025-0022.

Some terms help here:

- **Emergency care** means care for conditions that need immediate attention to prevent death, serious harm or deterioration, and PhilHealth says this includes both **emergent** care (life-threatening or with potential for permanent disability) and **urgent** care (the patient should be seen as early as possible but is not in immediate risk to life or permanent disability). A 2025 PhilHealth advisory confirmed that the benefit covers both.
- **Essential Emergency Care List (EECL)** is the list of diagnostic and laboratory services, medicines and supplies in an emergency facility that PhilHealth covers, each with a **fixed fee**. PhilHealth pays that fixed fee per item whether the hospital's actual charge is higher or lower.
- **Case rate** is a fixed amount for a specific illness or procedure that covers professional fees and facility charges such as room and board, diagnostics and laboratories, and drugs and supplies.

## Who Is Eligible?

Under the circulars:

- **Filipino citizens** who are registered with PhilHealth are eligible, with immediate eligibility under PhilHealth Circular 2022-0013.
- **Not yet registered?** PhilHealth's circular lets a patient register at the point of service through the eGovPH app and the PhilHealth Member Portal, which issues a PhilHealth Identification Number (PIN). If that is not possible online, the patient fills out a PhilHealth Member Registration Form (PMRF) through the accredited hospital. The hospital verifies citizenship with at least one Philippine government-issued ID after managing the emergency, and PhilHealth validates it after discharge. PhilHealth says it will pay for covered services even if the citizenship check turns out differently, as long as registration was done in good faith.
- **Foreign nationals** are eligible if they are registered and have met the required qualifying contributions.
- **Filipinos working abroad** may avail of the package subject to the circular's availment rules.

The circulars we reviewed do not require YAKAP clinic registration to use the ER benefit.

## What the Facility-Based ER Benefit Covers

For a case to be covered under the FBE benefit, PhilHealth's circular sets four conditions:

1. The services or commodities are on the Essential Emergency Care List.
2. They were provided in the emergency department or its extension facility.
3. The patient was discharged within 24 hours after entering the ED, or died in the ED before admission.
4. The case does not include services with an existing surgical or medical case rate that the hospital can claim.

The benefit covers diagnostic and imaging services, laboratory work-up (routine and blood chemistry), and therapeutics including drugs and medicines, plus associated ED fees.

### The 27 core presentations, and why they are not the only basis

PhilHealth's circular lists 27 "core presentations", which are symptoms or signs a patient arrives with. They include change in vision, generalized weakness, dizziness, palpitation, persistent vomiting, diarrhea, fever or chills, burn, seizure, severe headache, chest pain, difficulty of breathing, abdominal pain, ingestion or substance abuse, obstetric and gynecologic conditions and sexual assault. A July 2026 PhilHealth advisory says the basis for coverage **is not limited to this list**. It is meant for data gathering and should not be read as the only basis for eligibility. Hospitals record the nearest symptom on the list when they file the claim.

### What the fixed fees look like

Annex B of the circular lists 343 items numbered through 0343. Examples, as issued in 2025 (PhilHealth says fees can be adjusted and the latest published schedule takes precedence):

| Item | Fixed fee |
|---|---|
| Emergent ER consultation (outpatient) | ₱707 |
| Urgent ER consultation (outpatient) | ₱260 |
| Emergency bed (same-day discharge) | ₱650 |
| Electrocardiogram (ECG) | ₱423 |
| Chest X-ray | ₱426 |
| Ultrasound (any organ or body region) | ₱698 |
| CT scan, cranial, plain | ₱4,949 |
| Complete blood count (CBC) | ₱310 |
| Urinalysis | ₱263 |
| Nebulization | ₱714 |
| Tetanus toxoid | ₱224 |

**These are what PhilHealth reimburses the hospital per item. They are not the price of an ER visit and not a ceiling on your bill.** PhilHealth's circular says a hospital's statement of account that is higher or lower than a fixed fee is not treated as an overpayment or underpayment. One item in the list, a ₱300 "referral to a YAKAP provider" incentive, is paid to the ER physician and is not a charge or credit to you.

### What the hospital may not charge separately

PhilHealth's circular says overhead costs, including **ER fees, special compounding fees, reader's fees and utility fees**, and the supplies needed to provide ED services, are bundled into the fixed fee. Accredited hospitals "shall not charge patients these costs separately from the fixed fee." If you see them as separate lines on your bill, ask the hospital to explain.

### What falls outside the benefit

Services and commodities not on the Annex B list are not covered by the OECB and can be subject to out-of-pocket payment. The circular gives outpatient consultations with specialists other than the ED attending physician as an example.

The circular's scope also excludes:

- Emergency services in an accredited hospital but outside the ED or its extension facilities
- Services already included in existing packages
- Prehospital emergency services provided at home
- Emergency services for patients who are eventually admitted and successfully transferred to a hospital accommodation

## The 24-Hour Rule: ER Visit or Admission?

A July 6, 2026 PhilHealth advisory (No. 2026-0041) explains how PhilHealth decides which benefit applies. It describes the OECB as a "tiding-over" benefit that covers care that stabilizes the patient until discharge or admission, and says it is not a holding benefit.

| Scenario | Applicable benefit | Key condition |
|---|---|---|
| Emergent or urgent case with any outcome within 24 hours | OECB | Case managed and resolved within 24 hours in the ER |
| Outpatient procedure done at the ER that has an existing case rate | Procedure case rate (ACR) | Procedure has an existing ACR case rate, regardless of entry point |
| ER patient for whom admission orders were issued | Applicable inpatient ACR case rate | Admission orders issued within 24 hours; physical location in the ER is immaterial |

The advisory says a clear disposition (discharge or admission) must be reached and documented within 24 hours of ER admission. For admitted cases, the controlling factor is **the admission order, not where the patient is physically waiting**. A patient who lingers in the ER without clear orders falls outside the intent of both the OECB and the ACR.

## Ambulance Coverage: The Prehospital Emergency Benefit

Circular 2025-0022 covers emergency transport and on-board services by PhilHealth-accredited ambulance service providers, from home, the place of incident or a facility to a Level 1, 2 or 3 hospital. It covers land ambulances, including those ferried by boat to continue across water. Sea and air ambulances are left to later revisions.

Eligibility follows the same registration rules as the ER benefit, and an unregistered patient may register at the point of service when the ambulance hands the patient over. Only patients assessed by an emergency medical technician (EMT) as having derangements in the primary survey, or triaged as Expectant, Immediate or Delayed, can avail of it.

The benefit pays the ambulance provider a service package plus a distance package:

| Service package (first case rate) | Rate |
|---|---|
| Basic Life Support ambulance | ₱4,100 |
| Advanced Life Support ambulance | ₱4,600 |
| Physician-managed Advanced Life Support ambulance | ₱6,100 |

| Distance travelled (second case rate) | Rate |
|---|---|
| 0 to 5 km | ₱250 |
| More than 5 to 10 km | ₱500 |
| More than 10 to 15 km | ₱750 |
| More than 15 to 20 km | ₱1,000 |
| More than 20 km | ₱1,250 |

The circular states there is **no co-payment** for covered ambulance services listed in the package inclusions. Each patient is covered for a maximum of two prehospital claims per day. It does not cover patient transport vehicles, the transfer of an already admitted patient to another hospital, or transfers to a non-hospital facility. Check that your ambulance provider is accredited. PhilHealth said it would publish the list of accredited providers on its website, and a March 2026 advisory invited more ambulance providers to apply.

## What To Do During and After an ER Visit

1. **Get care first.** The advisory reminds hospitals of the Anti-Hospital Deposit Law (Republic Act 10932), which prohibits requiring deposits or advance payments as a condition for emergency care.
2. **Give your PIN or ID.** If you are not registered, ask the hospital's PhilHealth coordinator how to register at the point of service.
3. **Ask for help.** Hospital coordinators and PhilHealth Customer Assistance, Relations and Empowerment Staff (**P-CARES**) must be accessible to explain coverage, help file benefits and escalate unclear cases to the PhilHealth Regional Office.
4. **Check the bill before paying.** Eligible members must have the OECB or the applicable case rate deducted directly from the hospital bill. Verify that the deduction is there before you settle the balance.
5. **Ask for your benefit information.** You can request, at any time, the applicable PhilHealth benefit for your case, including the OECB amount and ACR case rates.
6. **Keep your documents:** the itemized statement of account, receipts and any waiver or claim forms you are given.
7. **If the benefit was not deducted,** see below.

## Costs: What PhilHealth Pays and What You May Pay

- **Services on the fixed fee list:** PhilHealth pays the fixed fee to the hospital. The hospital applies it as a deduction from your bill. The circular does not state that PhilHealth pays the entire ER bill, and the fixed fees are not a total cost.
- **Services not on the list:** out-of-pocket payment may apply.
- **Mandatory discounts:** hospitals must follow laws on senior citizen and PWD discounts and the order of charging in the joint DOH-DSWD-PCSO-PhilHealth guidelines.
- **Private insurance or HMO:** the advisory encourages members with HMO or private cover to ask the hospital to coordinate both so that coverage is maximized.
- **Stockouts:** PhilHealth's circular says accredited hospitals must ensure patients do not incur out-of-pocket costs because of stockouts or unavailable services, and that such costs are eligible for direct reimbursement from the hospital.

## Examples

*These are illustrations, not real cases or medical advice.*

**Example 1: treated and discharged.** A member goes to an accredited hospital's ER, is seen, has an ECG and an X-ray ordered, and is sent home within 24 hours. The hospital files under the OECB. PhilHealth pays the fixed fees for the listed services, and the bill should show the deduction. Anything not on the list, such as a specialist consultation, may be charged separately.

**Example 2: admitted from the ER.** A patient in the ER is judged to need admission, and the order is issued within 24 hours. The inpatient case rate applies, and the patient's physical location while waiting for a bed does not change that.

**Example 3: the ambulance.** A patient is carried by a Basic Life Support ambulance of an accredited provider over 12 km to a hospital. The provider files the BLS package and the 10-to-15 km distance package. The patient is not charged a co-payment for services included in the package.

## Common Problems

- **The benefit was not deducted.** PhilHealth's advisory says a failure to deduct at the point of care does not take away your right to the benefit.
- **The patient stayed in the ER more than 24 hours.** Whether the OECB or the ACR applies depends on when a disposition or admission order was issued. Ask the hospital for the time of the order.
- **The hospital is not accredited.** The circular covers accredited hospitals (Levels 1 to 3). We did not find a general rule for ER visits to non-accredited hospitals in the documents we reviewed. A 2026 maternal-care circular mentions direct filing for emergency cases at non-accredited institutions in its own context. Ask PhilHealth before assuming either way.
- **You are told a benefit cannot be applied because the case is "complicated" or the pathway is unclear.** The advisory says benefits shall not be denied or withheld on that ground alone, and that such cases should be escalated.
- **A separate "ER fee" appears on the bill.** PhilHealth says ER fees are bundled into the fixed fee.
- **You are asked for a deposit before emergency treatment.** The advisory reminds hospitals of the prohibition under Republic Act 10932.

## What To Do If Something Goes Wrong

1. **Ask for the PhilHealth coordinator or P-CARES** at the hospital and request that the correct benefit be applied.
2. **If it was not deducted, you may file for reimbursement.** PhilHealth's advisory says you or your authorized representative can file at any Local Health Insurance Office (LHIO) within the prescribed filing period. We did not find the period stated in the advisory, and secondary sources give different numbers, so ask the LHIO for the current deadline and documents. A news report of PhilHealth's 2025 advisories listed Claim Form 1 or a PhilHealth Benefit Eligibility Form, an itemized statement of account, and the hospital waiver and official receipt among the documents, so keep those.
3. **Escalate.** You can take the issue to your PhilHealth Regional Office or file a complaint with the PhilHealth Action Center. PhilHealth's July 2026 advisory lists:
   - Hotline: (02) 8662-2588
   - Mobile hotlines: Smart 0998-857-2957 and 0968-865-4670; Globe 0917-127-5987 and 0917-110-9812
   - Email: actioncenter@philhealth.gov.ph
   - A "Click-to-Call" icon on philhealth.gov.ph

   Confirm current contact details on philhealth.gov.ph before you call.

## After the ER: Follow-Up Care

PhilHealth's circular tells hospital EDs to try to refer patients triaged at the least urgent level (Level 5) to the outpatient department or to their YAKAP clinic, and says non-urgent follow-up consultations after ED management should happen at the patient's YAKAP clinic. If you do not have a YAKAP clinic, the hospital is meant to help you select one. See our YAKAP clinic guide for how to choose one.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Does PhilHealth cover ER visits?**
Yes, for accredited hospitals. If you are treated and the case is resolved within 24 hours, the OECB pays listed services at fixed fees. If you are admitted, the inpatient case rate applies.

**Is the ER bill free under PhilHealth?**
Not automatically. The OECB pays fixed fees per listed service, and the fees are not the total cost of a visit. Services not on the list may be charged to you.

**What is the 24-hour rule?**
The OECB applies to emergency cases managed and resolved within 24 hours. If admission orders are issued within 24 hours, the inpatient case rate applies, regardless of where the patient waits.

**Do I need to be registered with PhilHealth first?**
Registered Filipinos are eligible, and unregistered Filipinos can register at the point of service. Foreign nationals need to be registered and to have met qualifying contributions.

**Do I need a YAKAP clinic for the ER benefit?**
The circulars we reviewed do not require it. The hospital is meant to refer you to a YAKAP clinic for follow-up.

**Does PhilHealth pay for an ambulance?**
Yes, through the Prehospital Emergency benefit, when the ambulance provider is PhilHealth-accredited and the patient meets the triage criteria. See the rates above.

**Can the hospital ask for a deposit before treating an emergency?**
PhilHealth's advisory reminds hospitals of the Anti-Hospital Deposit Law, which prohibits requiring deposits or advance payments as a condition for emergency care.

**What if I am admitted after being in the ER?**
The applicable inpatient case rate applies, based on the issuance of the admission order. A case rate is a fixed amount per case, not a guarantee that PhilHealth pays the whole bill.

**Is the list of 27 symptoms the only way to qualify?**
No. PhilHealth's July 2026 advisory says the list is for data gathering and is not the sole basis for eligibility.

**What if the hospital did not deduct the benefit?**
You can file for reimbursement at an LHIO within the prescribed filing period and escalate through P-CARES, the Regional Office or the Action Center.
