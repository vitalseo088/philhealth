# PhilHealth Spouse Dependent Requirements: Who Qualifies and What to Prepare (2026)

If you are a PhilHealth member and want your spouse covered under your membership, you generally need to **declare your spouse as a dependent** in your PhilHealth records and be ready to show proof of marriage. A spouse can only be a dependent if they are not already a PhilHealth member in their own right. This guide explains the PhilHealth spouse dependent requirements in plain language, what documents you may need, and what to do if your records are wrong.

This guide is for employed, self-paying, and other contributing members. It is written for 2026, but PhilHealth forms and procedures can change, so confirm the latest steps with PhilHealth before you file.

## What Is a PhilHealth Dependent?

A **dependent** is a person who can receive PhilHealth benefits through a member's contributions, without paying their own premium. The member pays (or the employer pays, in part, for employed members), and the declared dependents are covered under that membership.

Under the Universal Health Care Act (Republic Act No. 11223), Filipinos are generally considered PhilHealth members, and the law and its rules also define who counts as a member's dependents. Being listed as a dependent in the member's record is what lets hospitals and PhilHealth confirm that coverage applies.

## Who Counts as a Dependent?

The usual categories of dependents recognized under PhilHealth rules are:

- The member's **legitimate spouse** who is not a member
- The member's **children** who are below 21, unmarried, and unemployed
- Children aged 21 or above who have a permanent disability and cannot support themselves
- **Parents aged 60 or above** who are not otherwise covered, subject to the income condition set by the rules **[VERIFY exact wording and threshold]**

This article focuses on the spouse. Always check the current dependent definitions on the official PhilHealth website, since they are tied to the law and its implementing rules.

### When Your Spouse Cannot Be Your Dependent

Your spouse generally cannot be listed as your dependent if they are **already a PhilHealth member** under another category, for example, if they are employed and their employer pays contributions for them, or if they are a self-paying member. In that case, they are covered through their own membership.

If both spouses are members, each one is covered by their own membership. Whether children can be declared under one or both parents is a separate question you should confirm with PhilHealth.

### Details That Must Match

A common reason for delays is a mismatch. Check that these are consistent across your PMRF, marriage certificate, and IDs:

- Spelling of full names (including middle names)
- Birthdates
- Civil status

## How to Declare Your Spouse as a PhilHealth Dependent

The steps below describe the general process. Your exact steps may differ depending on your membership category.

1. **Get your current PMRF.** Obtain the latest form from PhilHealth. **[VERIFY form version and whether it can be downloaded]**
2. **Fill in your member details** and the section for dependents, listing your spouse's full name, date of birth, and relationship.
3. **Prepare your proof of marriage.** Bring a PSA-issued marriage certificate and a valid ID.
4. **Submit your documents.**
   - *Employed members:* usually through the employer's HR or PhilHealth officer.
   - *Self-paying and other members:* at a PhilHealth office or Local Health Insurance Office.
   - Ask PhilHealth whether online submission through the Member Portal is available for dependents. **[VERIFY]**
5. **Confirm the update.** After processing, check that your Member Data Record (MDR) shows your spouse as a dependent. Keep a copy of your submission.

## Costs and Contributions

Declaring a spouse as a dependent does **not** require a separate premium. Dependents are covered under the member's contribution, as long as the member's membership is active and eligible. **[VERIFY current wording from PhilHealth]**

Coverage for a given hospital stay or service still depends on the applicable benefit package, eligibility rules, and facility accreditation. PhilHealth benefits are not a promise that the entire bill is paid. Inpatient benefits are generally paid to accredited health facilities based on case rates and deducted from the bill under the applicable rules.

## Examples

*These are illustrations, not real cases.*

**Example 1: Employed member, non-working spouse.** Maria is an employed member. Her husband has no job and is not a PhilHealth member. Maria can ask her HR to declare him as her dependent using the PMRF and her marriage certificate.

**Example 2: Both spouses are members.** Ben is employed and his wife Ana is a self-paying member. Each is covered by their own membership. Ana does not need to be declared as Ben's dependent.

**Example 3: Name mismatch.** A wife's middle name is spelled differently on the PMRF and the marriage certificate. The records office may ask for a correction before updating the dependent entry. Fix the mismatch first to avoid delays.

## Common Problems

- **Spouse not appearing in the MDR.** The update may still be processing, or the form may have been incomplete.
- **Name or birthdate mismatch.** Correct the record using the supporting document that shows the right details.
- **Spouse is already a member.** Duplicate coverage is generally not how dependents work. Ask PhilHealth which record applies.
- **Marriage certificate issues.** If you cannot get a PSA copy yet, ask PhilHealth what alternatives, if any, they accept **[VERIFY]**.
- **Hospital says the dependent is not on file.** Present your submitted PMRF copy and ask the hospital's PhilHealth desk to verify with PhilHealth.

## What To Do If Something Goes Wrong

1. Keep copies of everything you submitted and any acknowledgment slip.
2. Check your MDR through the official PhilHealth channels.
3. Visit the PhilHealth office or Local Health Insurance Office that handles your record and ask for the status.
4. Use PhilHealth's official hotline or email channels listed on the PhilHealth website. Do not rely on numbers from unofficial pages.
5. If the issue affects an active hospital admission, ask the hospital's PhilHealth coordinator to help with real-time verification.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can my husband or wife use my PhilHealth?
Yes, if they are your legitimate spouse, are not a PhilHealth member themselves, and are properly declared as your dependent in your records.

### Does my spouse need to pay separately?
Not for being your dependent. A dependent is covered under the member's contribution. Confirm the latest rules with PhilHealth.

### Is a PSA marriage certificate required?
It is commonly required as proof of marriage. Confirm whether other copies are accepted before you submit.

### What if my spouse is employed?
If your spouse is already a member through their job, they are covered under their own membership and are not declared as your dependent.

### Can I add a common-law partner?
The rules center on the legitimate spouse. For your specific situation, ask PhilHealth directly rather than assuming.

### How long does updating dependents take?
The available sources do not clearly specify a standard processing time, so this guide does not state one.

### Can I update dependents online?
Check the PhilHealth Member Portal or ask PhilHealth. This guide does not confirm an online dependent-update process. **[VERIFY]**

### Do I need to update my dependents every year?
The sources available for this draft do not specify a yearly requirement. Update your record whenever your family situation changes, such as marriage or a change in your spouse's membership status.
