## PhilHealth Contribution for a ₱30,000 Salary in 2026

Here's the direct answer: if your monthly basic salary is **₱30,000**, your total PhilHealth contribution for 2026 is **₱1,500 per month**. How that amount is paid depends on your membership category:

- If you're an **employed member**, the contribution is shared equally: **₱750 is deducted from your salary** and **₱750 is paid by your employer**.
- If you're a **self-paying member** (freelancer, self-earning individual, professional practitioner, or land-based OFW), you pay the full **₱1,500 monthly premium** yourself, since there is no employer to split the cost.

This guide walks through exactly how that number is calculated, based on PhilHealth's confirmed 2026 contribution rate, and compares the numbers across different employment setups and adjacent salary levels.

## Quick Answer

## The Formula

Under the Universal Health Care Act (RA 11223) and PhilHealth circulars, the contribution rate for **2026** is **5.0% of monthly basic salary or declared income**, applied within an income floor of ₱10,000 (minimum premium: ₱500/month) and an income ceiling of ₱100,000 (maximum premium: ₱5,000/month).

**Formula:**

> Monthly Basic Salary × 5% = Total Monthly Premium

For a ₱30,000 salary:

> ₱30,000 × 5% (0.05) = **₱1,500.00**

Because ₱30,000 falls cleanly within the ₱10,000 floor and the ₱100,000 ceiling, the straight 5% calculation applies directly without any floor or ceiling adjustments.

## How the ₱1,500 Is Split: Employed Members

For an **employed member** working under an employer-employee relationship, the monthly premium is shared equally between the employee and the employer:

| Share | Percentage | Monthly Amount | Per Cut-Off (Semi-Monthly) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Employee's share (deducted from your payslip) | 2.5% | ₱750.00 | ₱375.00 |
| Employer's share (paid by your employer) | 2.5% | ₱750.00 | ₱375.00 |
| **Total remitted to PhilHealth** | **5.0%** | **₱1,500.00** | **₱750.00** |

If your company follows a twice-monthly pay schedule (e.g. 15th and 30th), you will see **₱375.00** deducted per cut-off on your payslip. Your employer adds their matching ₱375.00 counterpart, remitting ₱750.00 per pay period (₱1,500.00 total monthly) to PhilHealth through the Electronic Premium Remittance System (EPRS).

## If You're a Self-Paying Member Instead

If you earn ₱30,000 a month but **do not have an employer** — such as an independent freelancer, direct contractor, sole proprietor, or land-based OFW — you are classified as a **self-paying direct contributor** under PhilHealth Circular No. 2026-0011.

Because there is no employer to share the cost, you pay the full 5%:

| Payment Frequency | Computation | Total Amount Due |
|---|---|---|
| Monthly | ₱30,000 × 5% | ₱1,500.00 |
| Quarterly (every 3 months) | ₱1,500 × 3 | ₱4,500.00 |
| Semi-Annually (every 6 months) | ₱1,500 × 6 | ₱9,000.00 |
| Annually (full year) | ₱1,500 × 12 | ₱18,000.00 |

### Official SPA Requirement for 2026

Under PhilHealth's "No SPA, No Payment" policy, self-paying members must present an official **Statement of Premium Account (SPA)** when paying at banks, Bayad Centers, or digital wallets (GCash, Maya, Link.BizPortal). You can generate an official voucher using our free [PhilHealth SPA Generator](/tools/spa-generator.html).

## What If Your Salary Changes During the Year?

Your PhilHealth contribution is computed based on your **current monthly basic salary or declared income** at the time it's reported:

- **For Employees:** If your basic salary changes, your employer's payroll office updates the figure in the EPRS, and future deductions adjust accordingly.
- **For Self-Paying Members:** If your freelance income changes, you can file an updated PhilHealth Member Registration Form (PMRF) with supporting income proof to update your declared income with PhilHealth.

## Common Problems and What to Do

**Your payslip shows a deduction different from ₱750.**
Confirm whether your employer computed the contribution based on your basic pay or gross earnings. PhilHealth contributions must be calculated strictly on basic salary, excluding overtime, holiday pay, and allowances.

**You are paying as a voluntary member and the payment app rejects your transaction.**
Starting in 2026, payment channels require an active SPA transaction code before accepting contributions. Generate an SPA first via the [PhilHealth SPA Generator](/tools/spa-generator.html).

**You suspect your employer deducted ₱750 but did not remit it.**
Log into your PhilHealth Member Portal at `memberinquiry.philhealth.gov.ph` or check via the official eGovPH mobile app to review your posted contribution history.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How much is the PhilHealth contribution for a ₱30,000 monthly salary?**
₱1,500 total per month. If employed, ₱750 is deducted from your salary and ₱750 is paid by your employer. If self-employed, you pay ₱1,500 in full.

**How much will be deducted from my payslip each cut-off for a ₱30,000 salary?**
For semi-monthly payroll schedules, ₱375.00 is deducted from each paycheck.

**Is ₱30,000 subject to any salary floor or ceiling?**
No. ₱30,000 is between the ₱10,000 floor and the ₱100,000 ceiling, so the standard 5% rate applies directly.

**Can self-paying members pay in advance?**
Yes. Self-paying members can pay semi-annually (₱9,000) or annually (₱18,000) in advance for the calendar year.
