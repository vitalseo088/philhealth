## 2. Article

# PhilHealth Cataract Surgery Coverage (2026): Package Rates by Lens Type and What to Know Before Surgery

PhilHealth pays a **fixed package amount for cataract surgery with an intraocular lens (IOL) implant**. For adults, the amount depends on the type of lens and runs from **₱20,200 to ₱80,900 per eye**. Pediatric packages are much higher. These rates took effect on **January 30, 2025** under PhilHealth Circular 2025-0001, and I found no later official change as of this review in October 2026.

This guide is for adult patients, parents of children with cataract, and family members comparing costs. It explains the PhilHealth rules in plain language. It does not tell you whether you need surgery or which lens is right for you. That is a decision for you and your eye doctor.

> **Important:** The package amount is not the same as "PhilHealth pays the whole bill." It is a fixed amount deducted from your total hospital or clinic bill. What you pay beyond it depends on your lens, your facility and the rules that apply to your membership category.

## What Is the PhilHealth Cataract Package?

A cataract is a clouding of the eye's natural lens. Surgery removes the cloudy lens and replaces it with an artificial one, called an **intraocular lens (IOL)**. PhilHealth pays for this through a **case rate**, which is a fixed amount for a specific procedure. The facility deducts the case rate from your bill. The package is not a cash payout to you.

PhilHealth describes cataract as one of its priority conditions, and says the package was raised after a directive from the President. According to PhilHealth, it paid out ₱3.58 billion for 224,209 cataract removal procedures in 2024.

## How Much Does PhilHealth Pay for Cataract Surgery?

### Adult package rates (per eye)

| What is done | PhilHealth amount |
|---|---|
| Cataract extraction only (no lens) | ₱20,200 |
| With monofocal (standard) IOL | ₱28,300 |
| With monofocal toric IOL | ₱43,800 |
| With multifocal IOL | ₱66,900 |
| With multifocal toric IOL | ₱80,900 |

*Plain-language notes:* A **monofocal** lens gives clear vision at one distance, usually far. A **toric** lens also corrects astigmatism. A **multifocal** lens is designed to help with both near and far vision. PhilHealth treats extended depth of focus (EDOF) lenses as a type of multifocal lens.

> **A figure that differs between sources.** PhilHealth's own announcement and a SunStar report give ₱43,800 for the monofocal toric lens. One Inquirer report printed ₱48,300. This guide uses PhilHealth's figure. Also, PhilHealth's announcement describes the ₱80,900 multifocal toric amount in a way that mentions the right eye only, and the sources reviewed do not explain this. Ask your facility how the amount applies to each eye.

### Pediatric package rates

| What is done | PhilHealth amount |
|---|---|
| Without IOL, one eye | ₱135,000 |
| Without IOL, both eyes | ₱139,050 |
| With IOL, one eye | ₱179,000 |
| With IOL, both eyes | ₱187,100 |

These amounts are for children, but the sources reviewed do not state the age cut-off for the pediatric package. Ask the facility which category applies.

### What changed from the old rate

Before Circular 2025-0001, PhilHealth paid ₱16,000 per eye, with no separate adult and pediatric rates. The adult package now starts at ₱20,200 and the highest adult amount is ₱80,900.

## Who Can Use the Cataract Package?

The sources reviewed describe the package by **procedure and patient group** (adult or pediatric). They do not set out separate medical eligibility criteria in a way I could verify.

What the official documents do say:

- **Facility:** Surgery must be done in a DOH-licensed and PhilHealth-accredited facility, either a **hospital** or an **ambulatory surgical clinic (ASC)**.
- **Medical record:** PhilHealth's plain-language explainer for Circular 2025-0010 says the record should clearly contain the pre-operative assessment, the biometry result (the eye measurement used to choose the lens power), the IOL power calculation, the operative procedure, post-operative medicines and findings from follow-up visits.
- **Indications for surgery:** PhilHealth's explainer says surgery is indicated for blurred vision that cannot be corrected by medical means, and for cataract with other accompanying eye or vision diseases. Whether *you* qualify medically is your doctor's judgment.
- **Membership status:** Whether your PhilHealth membership is active and which documents are needed for the claim should be confirmed with the facility. The sources reviewed do not list them for this package.

## The Pre-Surgery Authorization (CPSA)

PhilHealth uses a **Cataract Pre-Surgery Authorization (CPSA)** for adult cataract surgery. The facility files it, and PhilHealth revised the adult CPSA checklist in Advisory 2025-0012.

Key points from the advisories:

- **The facility handles it.** Advisory 2025-0068 describes CPSA requests as something health facilities submit. Patients do not file it themselves.
- **Approval is not a payment guarantee.** The same advisory says an approved CPSA does not automatically guarantee that the claim will be approved.
- **Accreditation matters.** In December 2025, PhilHealth said CPSA requests for surgeries scheduled in January 2026 could be resubmitted if they were first disapproved only because the facility's 2026 License to Operate was still being processed.

I could not retrieve the checklist itself, so I cannot tell you its exact contents, visual acuity thresholds or processing time. Ask the facility for the current checklist.

## What Happens After Surgery

PhilHealth Advisory 2025-0034 (signed June 5, 2025) clarifies claim timing:

- A cataract claim is acceptable if the **best corrected visual acuity (BCVA) report** is dated from **day 1 through day 14 after surgery**.
- The facility must show the **type of IOL and its amount** on the Statement of Account.
- IOLs must be checked against the FDA's list of registered lenses.
- The facility and professional fee shares follow PhilHealth's existing case-rate rules (Circular 35, s. 2013).

In practice, this means you should attend the post-operative check-up in the first two weeks. If the BCVA report is not done in that window, the facility's claim may be affected. Follow-up visits are arranged with your surgeon.

## What You May Still Pay

The package is a fixed amount. Your out-of-pocket cost depends on:

- **The lens you choose.** If the lens costs more than the package amount for that lens type, the difference may be charged to you. Ask for the lens type and price in writing.
- **Facility charges and professional fees** that exceed the case rate.
- **Whether the lens was supplied by you or donated.** One news report on the circular says that if the patient supplies the IOL or it is donated, the claim covers only the extraction. I could not confirm this in the official text, so ask PhilHealth or the facility.

PhilHealth also says accredited facilities should keep a minimum stock of IOLs, essential eye medications and supplies, so that patients do not have to buy them outside. It also discourages ophthalmologists from procuring and selling IOLs directly to patients using the package.

**What I could not verify:** Whether the No Balance Billing policy applies to cataract surgery for senior citizens, indigent and sponsored members, and under what conditions. The sources reviewed do not address it. Ask the facility before the operation.

## Step-by-Step: Before You Schedule Surgery

1. **Get your eye doctor's assessment.** Ask whether cataract surgery is recommended for you and which lens options are medically suitable.
2. **Choose a PhilHealth-accredited facility.** Confirm it is a DOH-licensed hospital or ambulatory surgical clinic, and that it is accredited for cataract surgery. Check PhilHealth's provider directory.
3. **Check your PhilHealth membership.** Bring your PhilHealth number and ask the facility what documents it needs for your member category.
4. **Ask for a written cost estimate.** It should show the lens type, the lens price and the PhilHealth amount that will be deducted.
5. **Confirm that the facility will file the CPSA.** Ask when it will be submitted and how you will be told of the result.
6. **Plan your follow-up.** Ask the date of your post-operative check-up and make sure the BCVA report is done within 14 days after surgery.
7. **Keep your records.** Keep your statement of account and medical documents.

### Documents You May Need

| Document | Notes |
|---|---|
| PhilHealth number or Member Data Record | Used for eligibility checks |
| Claim documents the facility prepares | Ask the facility for its list |
| Medical records (pre-operative assessment, biometry, IOL calculation, operative notes, follow-up findings) | Kept in the chart, per PhilHealth's explainer |
| Post-operative BCVA report | Must be dated day 1 to day 14 after surgery for the claim |
| Statement of Account showing IOL type and amount | Prepared by the facility |

The sources reviewed do not give a complete official checklist of what *you* must bring. Ask the facility.

## Common Problems

- **The facility is not accredited.** PhilHealth pays only DOH-licensed, accredited facilities. Check before scheduling.
- **The CPSA was disapproved.** One reason in the advisories was an unprocessed facility license. Ask the facility for the reason and whether it can resubmit.
- **No post-operative BCVA report.** Claims need this report dated within day 1 to day 14 after surgery.
- **The lens was not listed on the bill.** The facility must show the IOL type and amount on the Statement of Account.
- **Unclear extra charges.** Ask for a written breakdown before the operation.
- **Outdated figures online.** Many websites still quote ₱16,000, or print different lens prices. PhilHealth's announcement gives the figures above.

## What to Do If Something Goes Wrong

1. **Ask the facility in writing** why a CPSA or claim was disapproved or delayed.
2. **Keep copies** of your estimates, statement of account and medical records.
3. **Contact PhilHealth.** The advisories list the PhilHealth Action Center hotline as (02) 8662-2588 and the email as actioncenter@philhealth.gov.ph. You can also go to a PhilHealth Regional Office or Local Health Insurance Office. Contact details can change, so confirm on philhealth.gov.ph.

PhilHealth's advisories also warn healthcare professionals against unethical practices to claim cataract reimbursements. If you suspect a facility is billing for services you did not receive, report it to PhilHealth.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How much does PhilHealth pay for cataract surgery?
For adults, ₱20,200 to ₱80,900 per eye depending on the lens. For children, ₱135,000 to ₱187,100 depending on whether an IOL is used and how many eyes are operated.

### Does PhilHealth pay the whole cataract surgery bill?
No. It pays a fixed package amount that is deducted from the bill. You may still pay the difference, depending on the lens, the facility and your membership category.

### Which lens does PhilHealth cover?
PhilHealth has amounts for monofocal, monofocal toric, multifocal and multifocal toric lenses. EDOF lenses are treated as a type of multifocal lens. A higher-priced lens does not mean PhilHealth pays its full price.

### What was the amount before 2025?
₱16,000 per eye.

### When did the new amounts start?
January 30, 2025, under PhilHealth Circular 2025-0001.

### Do I need approval before surgery?
For adult cataract surgery, PhilHealth uses a Cataract Pre-Surgery Authorization (CPSA) filed by the facility. An approved CPSA does not guarantee the claim will be paid.

### Can I have surgery in a clinic instead of a hospital?
PhilHealth's explainer says surgery can be done in a DOH-licensed, PhilHealth-accredited hospital or ambulatory surgical clinic.

### Is there a deadline for the post-operation check?
The BCVA report must be dated day 1 to day 14 after surgery for the claim to be acceptable.

### Can both eyes be operated and claimed?
The sources reviewed give a combined pediatric rate for both eyes but do not clearly state the rules for adults. Ask PhilHealth or the facility.

### Is there a limit on how many cataract surgeries I can claim?
PhilHealth's explainer says that after cataract removal and lens replacement, the cataract does not return, and that blurred vision afterward may have other causes. It does not state an explicit claim limit. Ask PhilHealth.

### Has the package changed in 2026?
I found no official change to the package rates. A December 2025 advisory deals with how facility accreditation affects January 2026 CPSA requests. PhilHealth said in June 2025 that a separate policy on implementing the cataract package would be issued, so check philhealth.gov.ph for new issuances.
