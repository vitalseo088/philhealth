# PhilHealth Cancer Benefits 2026: What the Z Packages and Screening Cover

PhilHealth covers cancer in three main ways: **Z benefit packages** for specific cancers, **screening** through its primary care program (YAKAP), and **case rates** for admissions and procedures that fall outside a Z package. The biggest amount is for breast cancer, at **up to ₱1.4 million** for patients in stages 0 to 4.

This guide is for PhilHealth members, their dependents and families who want to know what the 2026 cancer coverage looks like and how to start. It explains PhilHealth's benefit rules only. It is not medical advice, and PhilHealth does not decide which treatment a patient should have. The treating doctor does. Coverage also depends on the applicable benefit package and eligibility requirements.

## Quick Answer

| Cancer | Z benefit amount listed by PhilHealth |
|---|---|
| Breast cancer (stages 0 to IV) | Up to ₱1.4 million |
| Colon cancer | ₱150,000 (early stages, low risk); ₱300,000 (stage II high risk to III) |
| Rectal cancer | ₱150,000 (stage I); ₱320,000 to ₱400,000 for stage II to III, depending on radiotherapy type |
| Cervical cancer | ₱175,000 |
| Prostate cancer (low to intermediate risk) | ₱100,000 |
| Acute lymphoblastic leukemia in children (standard risk) | ₱500,000 |

A **Z benefit package** is a special package for serious, costly conditions. It pays a set amount to a **contracted health facility**, a hospital that has an agreement with PhilHealth to deliver that package. It is not paid to you directly.

## What Are PhilHealth's Z Benefit Packages for Cancer?

Z benefits cover treatment for specific conditions that normally require long and expensive care. For cancer, PhilHealth's benefits page lists packages for breast, colon, rectal, cervical and prostate cancer, and for standard-risk acute lymphoblastic leukemia in children.

A May 2026 government news report on PhilHealth's cancer and kidney benefits repeated the breast, colon, rectal, cervical and childhood leukemia amounts shown above. It did not mention an effective date or circular number.

Cancers or treatments that do not fall under a Z package may still be paid through PhilHealth's regular **case rates**, which are fixed amounts paid to accredited hospitals for a condition or procedure. The available official sources did not give specific 2026 case-rate amounts for cancer treatment outside the Z packages, so ask the hospital which package applies.

## Breast Cancer: The Largest Package

PhilHealth raised the breast cancer Z benefit from ₱100,000 to up to ₱1.4 million, a 1,300% increase, according to a PhilHealth announcement published in October 2025. It covers patients in stages 0 to 4, including those already in treatment.

A Philippine News Agency report on the increase gave these approximate component ranges. Treat them as a guide and ask the hospital's Z benefits coordinator for the current schedule.

| Component | Range reported |
|---|---|
| Diagnostic tests and prognosis | ₱1,000 to ₱10,000 |
| Surgery (partial to modified radical mastectomy) | ₱30,000 to ₱140,000 |
| Hormonal therapy | ₱2,700 or ₱18,000 |
| Chemotherapy (cytotoxic) | ₱43,000 to ₱185,010 |
| Targeted therapy | Higher amounts, up to over ₱1 million |
| Surveillance for relapse after surgery | ₱5,000 to ₱9,000 |

The same report said members or qualified dependents should contact the Z Benefits Coordinator at a contracted facility, and that no co-payment applies to patients admitted in basic or ward-type accommodation.

## Who Is Eligible?

Z packages are for PhilHealth members and qualified dependents. Each package also has clinical criteria. The criteria listed on PhilHealth's benefits page include:

| Package | Criteria listed |
|---|---|
| Breast cancer | Signed Member Empowerment (ME) form; clinical stages 0 to IV |
| Colon cancer | Signed ME form; stage I to III; low surgical risk; mandatory services per the package circular |
| Rectal cancer | Signed ME form; biopsy-proven stage I to III; no previous pelvic radiation |
| Cervical cancer | Signed ME form; no previous chemotherapy or radiotherapy; treatment plan from a gynecologic oncologist |
| Prostate cancer | Signed ME form; male up to 70 years old; localized, low to intermediate risk |
| Acute lymphoblastic leukemia | Signed ME form; age 1 to under 10; specific lab and diagnosis criteria |

The **Member Empowerment (ME) form** is a consent form the patient signs to avail of the Z package. The criteria above come from a page that mixes older and newer entries, so confirm them with the hospital's Z coordinator before assuming you qualify. The doctor, not PhilHealth, decides the diagnosis and stage.

## Cancer Screening Under YAKAP

PhilHealth's primary care program, **YAKAP** (Yaman ng Kalusugan Program), includes outpatient cancer screening.

- A July 2025 PhilHealth announcement said members could get cancer screening tests free of charge at select hospitals with a valid prescription from their accredited primary care provider.
- A PhilHealth announcement from October 2025 listed selected breast cancer screening rates under YAKAP: ₱2,610 for a mammogram and ₱1,350 for a breast ultrasound.
- A May 2026 announcement lists outpatient cancer screening as a YAKAP benefit but does not say which cancers or tests are covered.

The same May 2026 announcement says YAKAP includes medicines worth up to ₱20,000 a year under **GAMOT**, PhilHealth's outpatient medicines benefit. The sources reviewed do not say whether cancer medicines are included, so ask your YAKAP clinic.

To use YAKAP, register with a YAKAP clinic. PhilHealth says members can select a clinic through the eGovPH app or the PhilHealth Member Portal.

## Step-by-Step: How to Avail of a Cancer Benefit

1. **Get a diagnosis and staging from your doctor.** Package eligibility depends on the cancer type and stage.
2. **Check that the hospital is a contracted facility** for the Z package, or an accredited facility for regular case rates. PhilHealth's benefits page links to a list of Z benefits contracted facilities. Check it before you go.
3. **Contact the hospital's Z Benefits Coordinator.** Ask what documents they need from you.
4. **Sign the Member Empowerment form** if your doctor and the coordinator confirm the Z package applies.
5. **Confirm your PhilHealth membership details** so the hospital can verify your eligibility.
6. **Keep copies** of forms, statements of account and receipts.

For screening, start with your YAKAP clinic and ask for a prescription or referral for the screening test.

## Costs: What You May Still Pay

Z packages pay a set amount to the facility. For breast cancer, the news report above says no co-payment applies to patients in basic or ward-type accommodation. For other cancers and other room types, the available official sources do not clearly specify what extra charges may apply.

**Example (illustration only, not real billing):** A patient is approved for a package and chooses a private room. The hospital may charge for the room upgrade or services outside the package. Ask for a written breakdown before admission.

## Common Problems

- **The hospital is not a contracted facility.** Z packages are delivered through contracted facilities, so confirm before treatment starts.
- **You do not meet the package criteria.** Some packages exclude certain stages, ages or previous treatments. Ask whether another benefit applies.
- **You are already in treatment.** For breast cancer, PhilHealth says patients already in treatment are covered. For other packages, ask the coordinator.
- **A cancer type you need is not listed.** The benefits page version reviewed lists no Z package for lung cancer or other childhood cancers. Ask the hospital whether a case rate or another package applies.
- **Amounts quoted by hospitals differ.** Case rates and Z packages have been adjusted since 2024, so ask which circular and amount the hospital is using.

## What To Do If Something Goes Wrong

1. Ask the hospital's Z coordinator or billing desk to explain in writing how the benefit was applied.
2. Contact your PhilHealth Regional or Local Health Insurance Office. PhilHealth's 2025 and 2026 announcements list its 24/7 line as (02) 866-225-88. Check PhilHealth's official channels for current contact details before calling.
3. Keep your forms, receipts and statements of account.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Does PhilHealth cover cancer treatment?
Yes. It has Z packages for breast, colon, rectal, cervical and prostate cancer and for standard-risk acute lymphoblastic leukemia in children. Other cancer care may be paid through regular case rates.

### How much does PhilHealth pay for breast cancer?
Up to ₱1.4 million under the Z benefit, for stages 0 to 4.

### How much does PhilHealth pay for colon cancer?
₱150,000 for early-stage, low-risk cases and ₱300,000 for stage II high-risk to stage III, as listed on PhilHealth's benefits page.

### Does PhilHealth cover chemotherapy?
Chemotherapy is part of some Z packages, such as the breast cancer package. For other cases, the available official sources do not give a specific chemotherapy amount for 2026. Ask the hospital.

### Does PhilHealth cover cancer screening?
YAKAP includes outpatient cancer screening. For breast cancer, PhilHealth listed ₱2,610 for a mammogram and ₱1,350 for a breast ultrasound.

### Do I get the money directly?
No. Z package amounts are paid to the contracted health facility.

### Can dependents use these benefits?
Z benefits are for members and qualified dependents. Check each package's criteria.

### Can I avail if I am already being treated?
For breast cancer, yes, according to PhilHealth. For other packages, ask the Z coordinator, because some criteria exclude previous treatment.

### Is a cancer package part of YAKAP?
No. Z packages are separate from YAKAP. YAKAP covers primary care, including screening.
