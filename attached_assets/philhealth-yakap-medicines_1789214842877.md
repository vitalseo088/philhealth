# PhilHealth YAKAP Medicines: What's Covered Under GAMOT in 2026

PhilHealth YAKAP medicines are delivered through a specific component called **GAMOT** (PhilHealth Guaranteed and Accessible Medications for Outpatient Treatment). If you're empaneled with a YAKAP Clinic, your clinic doctor can prescribe from a defined list of medicines, and you can have that prescription filled at an accredited GAMOT pharmacy — up to a benefit limit of ₱20,000 per beneficiary, per year.

This guide is for PhilHealth members and dependents registered with a YAKAP Clinic who want to understand which medicines are covered, how prescriptions and dispensing actually work in 2026, and where the rules changed recently. Some figures here — like the full medicine count — come from PhilHealth's own materials but are described slightly differently across documents, and that's flagged explicitly below rather than smoothed over.

## What Is the YAKAP Medicine Benefit (GAMOT)?

GAMOT stands for **PhilHealth Guaranteed and Accessible Medications for Outpatient Treatment**. It's one of the core components of the YAKAP (Yaman ng Kalusugan Program) primary care package, alongside consultations, laboratory tests, and cancer screening.

Under GAMOT, a doctor at your registered YAKAP Clinic can prescribe from a defined formulary of medicines. According to PhilHealth Circular No. 2026-0007 — *Clinical and Pharmaceutical Standards for PhilHealth Yaman Ng Kalusugan Program (YAKAP)* — these medicines must be prescribed based on the patient's clinical needs and current diagnosis, following the approved therapeutic indications listed in the circular's official drug table (Annex A). Standby prescriptions "just in case," or medicines unrelated to the patient's active condition, are explicitly excluded from GAMOT claims under this circular.

Medicines are dispensed only by licensed pharmacists at PhilHealth-accredited GAMOT facilities, and only upon presentation of a valid prescription issued by an authorized YAKAP physician.

## Who Is Eligible for YAKAP Medicine Coverage?

Per PhilHealth's official YAKAP FAQ, this benefit is available to PhilHealth members and their qualified dependents who are registered and empaneled with a YAKAP Clinic. Importantly, the annual ₱20,000 medicine limit is **not shared** across a family — the FAQ confirms the principal member has their own ₱20,000 annual limit, and each qualified dependent has a separate ₱20,000 limit of their own.

## What Medicines Are Covered Under GAMOT?

PhilHealth's medicine formulary under YAKAP spans several therapeutic categories. Based on PhilHealth Circular No. 2026-0007's official Annex A (Table of GAMOT Drugs and Indications), the covered categories include, among others:

- **Anti-infectious medicines** — e.g., Amoxicillin, Co-amoxiclav, Cotrimoxazole, Ciprofloxacin, Clarithromycin, Doxycycline, Azithromycin, Cefixime, Metronidazole, Nitrofurantoin
- **Cardiovascular and antihypertensive medicines** — e.g., Amlodipine, Losartan, Metoprolol, Enalapril, Hydrochlorothiazide, Atenolol, Captopril
- **Diabetes management** — e.g., Metformin, Gliclazide, Dapagliflozin (as add-on therapy)
- **Cholesterol/lipid management** — e.g., Simvastatin, Atorvastatin, Rosuvastatin, Fenofibrate
- **Respiratory medicines** — e.g., Salbutamol, Fluticasone + Salmeterol, Budesonide + Formoterol, Ipratropium, Tiotropium
- **Pain relief and anti-inflammatory medicines** — e.g., Paracetamol, Ibuprofen, Mefenamic Acid, Naproxen, Celecoxib
- **Allergy medicines** — e.g., Cetirizine, Loratadine, Chlorphenamine, Diphenhydramine
- **Other essential medicines** — including Oral Rehydration Salts, Zinc, Omeprazole, Ferrous Salt (iron) preparations, and others listed in the circular

This is a representative summary, not the complete list. The full, current official drug table — including specific approved indications for each medicine — is published as Annex A of PhilHealth Circular No. 2026-0007 on philhealth.gov.ph. PhilHealth's separate YAKAP FAQ describes this formulary as covering **75 molecules**; the exact current count and contents are governed by the circular's published annex, which PhilHealth may update through future issuances.

### The 21 Medicines PhilHealth Has Specifically Guaranteed to Remain in Stock

Separately from the full GAMOT formulary, PhilHealth issued **Advisory No. 2026-0007**, confirming that a specific set of 21 essential medicines is intended to remain continuously available at YAKAP-accredited facilities throughout the year. According to that advisory, these are:

Amoxicillin, Co-amoxiclav, Cotrimoxazole, Nitrofurantoin, Ciprofloxacin, Clarithromycin, Oral Rehydration Salts (ORS), Prednisone, Salbutamol, Fluticasone + Salmeterol, Paracetamol, Gliclazide, Metformin, Simvastatin, Enalapril, Metoprolol, Amlodipine, Hydrochlorothiazide, Losartan, Aspirin, and Chlorphenamine.

PhilHealth's advisory frames this as a stock-availability assurance for members — a subset of the larger GAMOT formulary that facilities are specifically expected to keep on hand — rather than the entirety of what GAMOT covers.

## How Prescriptions and Dispensing Work

### Step-by-Step: Getting Your YAKAP Medicine

1. **Consult your registered YAKAP Clinic doctor.** Only your empaneled clinic's doctor can issue a valid GAMOT prescription for you.
2. **Receive your prescription through the GAMOT App.** Per PhilHealth's FAQ, the doctor issues the prescription electronically through this system.
3. **Go to an accredited GAMOT pharmacy.** You'll need to present the prescription or the UPSC code generated by the app, along with a government-issued ID, so the pharmacist can validate it.
4. **Receive your medicine and sign the GAMOT Availment Slip.** The pharmacist provides this slip after dispensing, which also shows your remaining balance against the ₱20,000 annual limit.
5. **Return for refills as needed, within your prescription's validity and the annual limit.** If a pharmacy doesn't have everything prescribed in stock, PhilHealth's FAQ notes you may be referred to a different accredited GAMOT facility to complete your prescription.

### Dispensing Limits: You Won't Get an Entire Year's Supply at Once

PhilHealth Circular No. 2026-0007 sets specific dispensing limits (Annex B) so that patients stay in regular contact with their prescriber and pharmacist for monitoring. In general terms, based on the circular:

| Medicine Type | Typical Dispensing Limit Per Visit |
|---|---|
| Medicines for acute, short-term conditions (e.g., paracetamol, ibuprofen, antihistamines, ORS) | Up to 7 days' supply |
| Medicines for symptom relief in chronic conditions, and zinc/mineral supplements | Up to 14 days' supply |
| Oral antimicrobials (antibiotics) | Dispensed in full for the prescribed course; prescriptions for oral antimicrobials may not exceed 14 days per prescription, and partial filling is not allowed |
| Maintenance medicines for chronic disease management (e.g., amlodipine, losartan, metformin, atorvastatin, and similar long-term medicines) | Up to 30 days' (one month's) supply per dispensing visit |
| Certain inhalers, creams, and other special preparations | Varies by product — PhilHealth's Annex B sets a specific limit for each (for example, some metered-dose inhalers are limited to one inhaler per 30 days) |

If you need medicine beyond what a single dispensing visit allows, the circular directs you to follow up with your primary care physician so they can determine whether to continue, adjust, or extend your treatment — this follow-up should be based on current clinical practice guidelines, not an automatic renewal.

**Important note on an earlier rule:** PhilHealth's YAKAP FAQ (dated July 31, 2025) described maintenance medicines as prescribable for up to three months at a time, with pharmacies dispensing a one-month supply per visit. PhilHealth Circular No. 2026-0007 — a more recent, Tier 1 issuance — formally sets out the current dispensing-limit table described above and explicitly amends the dispensing-limit provisions of the earlier GAMOT circular (PhilHealth Circular No. 2025-0013). Where the two differ on specifics, the 2026 circular's Annex B is the current authoritative rule.

| Medicines exceeding the ₱20,000 annual limit | The member pays for any medicine dispensed beyond the limit, per the FAQ |

PhilHealth coverage for any specific medicine ultimately depends on the currently effective GAMOT formulary, your doctor's prescription, your remaining annual balance, and your eligibility as an empaneled beneficiary. This is a general summary of the rules, not a guarantee of coverage for a particular prescription.

Because dependents have their own separate ₱20,000 limit, this does not affect Grace's own medicine allowance.

## Common Problems

- **Trying to get a GAMOT prescription filled without going through your registered YAKAP Clinic doctor.** Only your empaneled clinic's doctor can issue a valid prescription under this benefit.
- **Expecting a full year's supply of maintenance medicine in one visit.** Dispensing limits generally cap chronic-disease medicines at 30 days per visit; follow-up visits are part of how the program monitors treatment.
- **A pharmacy not having every medicine in stock.** PhilHealth's FAQ allows for referral to a different accredited GAMOT facility to complete the prescription; ask your pharmacist about this option.
- **Assuming a branded version of a covered generic is automatically free.** Only medicines within PhilHealth's set price for that molecule are covered without extra charge, per the FAQ.
- **Confusing this benefit with hospital or emergency medicine coverage.** GAMOT covers outpatient primary-care prescriptions from your YAKAP Clinic doctor — it is a distinct benefit from inpatient or emergency-department medicine coverage.

## What to Do If Something Goes Wrong

If you believe a pharmacy improperly denied a covered medicine, or you were charged for something that should have been within your GAMOT benefit, PhilHealth Advisory No. 2026-0003 directs members with YAKAP-related concerns to PhilHealth's Corporate Action Center. Confirm the current contact details directly on philhealth.gov.ph, since these can be updated over time.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How many medicines are covered under PhilHealth YAKAP?**
PhilHealth's YAKAP FAQ describes the GAMOT formulary as covering 75 molecules. The specific, currently effective list of medicines and their approved indications is published as Annex A of PhilHealth Circular No. 2026-0007.

**What's the difference between GAMOT and the 21 "essential medicines"?**
GAMOT is the full outpatient medicine benefit under YAKAP. The 21 essential medicines, named in PhilHealth Advisory No. 2026-0007, are a specific subset that PhilHealth has said should remain continuously stocked at YAKAP facilities — not the entire covered formulary.

**Can I choose which pharmacy to get my medicine from?**
Yes, as long as it's a PhilHealth-accredited GAMOT facility with remaining balance available under your ₱20,000 annual limit, per PhilHealth's FAQ.

**Do I need a new prescription every time I refill a maintenance medicine?**
Per PhilHealth's FAQ, a valid prescription from your YAKAP Clinic remains valid for you to return for refills while medicine remains available, though each dispensing visit is still subject to the applicable supply limit (commonly up to 30 days for maintenance medicines).

**Is there a limit on how many days an antibiotic can be prescribed for?**
Yes — PhilHealth Circular No. 2026-0007 states that prescribing limits for oral antimicrobials shall not exceed 14 days per prescription, with extended therapy requiring further clinical evaluation.

**Can someone else pick up my medicine for me?**
PhilHealth's FAQ describes a representative pickup process requiring an authorization letter, a valid ID of the patient/member, and a valid ID of the representative.
