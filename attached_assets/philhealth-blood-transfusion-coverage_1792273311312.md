# PhilHealth Blood Transfusion Coverage 2026: What PhilHealth Pays and What You May Still Owe

Yes, PhilHealth covers blood transfusion, but it pays a fixed amount to the hospital, not your whole bill. If you receive a transfusion without being admitted, PhilHealth's **outpatient blood transfusion** benefit is listed at **₱7,098 per session**. If you are admitted, the transfusion is generally part of the case rate for the condition being treated.

This guide is for PhilHealth members, their dependents and families who want to know what the 2026 coverage looks like before or after a transfusion. It explains PhilHealth's benefit rules only. It is not medical advice, and PhilHealth does not decide whether a transfusion is right for a patient. Your doctor does.

## Quick Answer

| Situation | How PhilHealth pays | Amount |
|---|---|---|
| Transfusion without admission (outpatient) | A fixed "case rate" paid to the accredited facility | ₱7,098 per session (₱5,460 facility fee + ₱1,638 professional fee) |
| Transfusion while admitted to a hospital | Included in the case rate for the condition being treated | Depends on the main condition's case rate |
| Condition with a special "Z benefit" package | Separate package with its own rules | Varies by package; check the specific package |

A **case rate** is a fixed amount PhilHealth will reimburse for a specific illness or procedure. It is meant to cover professional fees and facility charges such as diagnostics, drugs and supplies. It is not a guarantee that the whole bill disappears.

## What Is the Outpatient Blood Transfusion Benefit?

The outpatient blood transfusion benefit covers transfusions that do not require you to be confined (admitted). The benefit began for procedures performed from February 1, 2010, under PhilHealth Circular No. 51, s. 2009, and was originally paid on a fee-for-service basis. PhilHealth has since moved to case-based payment, so the old amounts no longer apply (see "What Changed" below).

According to PhilHealth's benefits page:

- The case rate is **₱7,098** for one or more units of blood or blood products.
- It includes drugs and medicines, X-ray, laboratory and others, and operating room charges.
- It covers outpatient blood transfusion only.
- One day of transfusion of any blood or blood product, **regardless of the number of bags**, counts as **one session**.
- It may be availed of as a **second case rate** at the full case-rate amount.

In PhilHealth's annex listing conditions and procedures allowed as a second case rate (under Circular No. 2024-0037), the procedure is listed under code 36430 with the same ₱7,098 total: ₱5,460 for the health facility and ₱1,638 for professional fees.

## Transfusion During a Hospital Stay

If you are admitted, PhilHealth does not usually pay for the transfusion separately. Under the All Case Rates policy, the case rate is a fixed amount that covers professional fees and facility charges, including room and board, diagnostics and laboratories, drugs, medicines and supplies, and operating room fees. A transfusion given during admission would normally fall within the case rate for the main condition.

PhilHealth's benefits page explains that case-rate benefits are paid to accredited health facilities and that the case rate amount is deducted from the member's total bill. The amount covers both hospital charges and the attending physician's professional fee.

## Who Can Claim?

The outpatient transfusion benefit is for PhilHealth members and their qualified dependents. The facility checks eligibility using your Member Data Record (MDR) or a PhilHealth Benefit Eligibility Form (PBEF).

The sources reviewed for this article do not set out a separate contribution-count rule for this specific benefit. If you are unsure whether your membership is active, check your records with PhilHealth before the procedure.

PhilHealth's benefits page lists the first two items for case-rate benefits in general. It does not give a separate document list for outpatient transfusion alone, so confirm with the facility.

## Step-by-Step: Using PhilHealth for a Transfusion

1. **Check your membership record.** Make sure you can show your MDR or ask the facility to verify eligibility.
2. **Confirm the facility is PhilHealth-accredited.** Benefits are paid to accredited facilities. Check the official PhilHealth provider directory before you go, because provider information can change.
3. **Ask about the process.** Ask the billing or PhilHealth desk how the ₱7,098 case rate will be applied to your bill.
4. **Prepare your documents.** Bring your MDR or PBEF and a completed Claim Form 1.
5. **Request an itemized statement of account.** This shows what was charged and what PhilHealth's amount covered.
6. **Keep copies** of everything you signed and received.

## Costs: What PhilHealth Pays and What You May Still Pay

PhilHealth's payment is capped at the case rate. Whether you pay anything extra depends on what the facility charges and its billing rules, and this guide could not verify the rules for every member category.

**Example (illustration only, not real billing):** Suppose a facility charges ₱12,000 for a transfusion session. PhilHealth's outpatient case rate is ₱7,098, so the part above that, ₱4,902 in this example, could remain the patient's responsibility, depending on the facility's billing rules and the patient's member category.

The available official sources do not clearly specify whether blood processing fees or the cost of the blood units themselves are separately covered. Ask the hospital and blood bank which charges are included in their quote.

## What Changed: Old Figures vs Current Rules

| Topic | Older information | Current position |
|---|---|---|
| Payment method | Fee-for-service under Circular No. 51, s. 2009, from February 1, 2010 | Case rate under the All Case Rates policy |
| Amounts | Some older guides cite ₱9,000, ₱11,200 and ₱14,000 depending on hospital level | Listed as ₱7,098 per session; do not use the older figures |
| 45-day limit | Outpatient sessions used to count against the yearly 45-day benefit allowance | PhilHealth Circular No. 2025-0007 lifted the 45-day benefit limit rule, effective April 4, 2025 |
| Case-rate increases | Rates had been largely unadjusted since 2013 | A 30% adjustment started February 14, 2024; a further 50% increase on about 9,000 case rates covered admissions from January 1, 2025 |

**Note on the 2025 increase:** The two PhilHealth sources found for this benefit, the benefits page and the Circular No. 2024-0037 annex, both show ₱7,098 for outpatient blood transfusion. This article could not confirm whether the 2025 increase changed this particular code. If a hospital quotes a different figure, ask which circular and annex it is using.

## Common Problems

- **The facility is not accredited.** PhilHealth pays accredited facilities, so confirm accreditation first.
- **Your membership record shows a problem.** An MDR issue can delay or block the claim. Resolve it with your PhilHealth office.
- **The bill is higher than the case rate.** This is common because the case rate is a fixed amount, not a reimbursement of actual charges.
- **You need repeated transfusions.** Each transfusion day counts as one session. News coverage of a PhilHealth circular reported that outpatient blood transfusion is exempt from the rule limiting readmissions for the same illness within 90 days to one case rate, but confirm how this applies to your case with PhilHealth.
- **Confusing outpatient and inpatient rules.** The ₱7,098 applies to outpatient sessions. Transfusions during admission fall under the admission's case rate.

## What To Do If Something Goes Wrong

1. Ask the facility's PhilHealth or billing desk to explain, in writing if possible, how the case rate was applied.
2. Request an itemized statement of account.
3. Contact your PhilHealth Regional or Local Health Insurance Office, or the PhilHealth 24/7 line. A PhilHealth circular and a March 2025 PhilHealth announcement print the number as (02) 8662-2588. Check PhilHealth's official channels for current contact details before calling.
4. Keep your receipts, claim forms and statement of account in case you need to follow up.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Does PhilHealth cover blood transfusion?
Yes. Outpatient transfusion has its own case rate, and transfusions during admission are generally covered within the case rate for the main condition.

### How much does PhilHealth pay for an outpatient blood transfusion?
PhilHealth's benefits page lists ₱7,098 for one or more units, split into ₱5,460 for the facility and ₱1,638 for professional fees.

### Does PhilHealth pay per bag of blood?
No. One day of transfusion counts as one session regardless of how many bags are used.

### Does PhilHealth pay for the blood itself?
The available official source does not clearly specify this. The case rate is a fixed amount that includes drugs, medicines, laboratory and other charges, but ask your hospital and blood bank what is included in their charges.

### Is the 45-day limit still in effect?
No. PhilHealth Circular No. 2025-0007 lifted the 45-day benefit limit rule, effective April 4, 2025.

### Do I need to go to a PhilHealth-accredited hospital?
Case-rate benefits are paid to accredited health facilities, so yes. Check the official provider directory first.

### Are there separate packages for serious blood-related conditions?
Some conditions have Z benefit packages with their own rules. For example, the standard-risk acute lymphocytic leukemia package is listed at ₱500,000, and an earlier circular for that package included blood support and blood products among its services. Check the specific package and its criteria on PhilHealth's benefits page.

### Can I claim an outpatient transfusion together with another case rate?
PhilHealth lists the outpatient blood transfusion as allowed as a second case rate, paid at the full case-rate amount. Ask the facility how this applies to your situation.
