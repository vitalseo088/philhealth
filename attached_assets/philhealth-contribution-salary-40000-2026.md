## PhilHealth Contribution for a ₱40,000 Salary in 2026

Here's the direct answer: if your monthly basic salary is **₱40,000**, your total PhilHealth contribution for 2026 is **₱2,000 per month**. How that amount is settled depends on your employment classification:

- If you're an **employed member**, the contribution is shared 50/50: **₱1,000 is deducted from your salary** and **₱1,000 is paid by your employer**.
- If you're a **self-paying member** (freelancer, sole proprietor, professional practitioner, or land-based OFW), you pay the full **₱2,000 monthly premium** yourself.

This guide walks through exactly how that number is calculated, based on PhilHealth's confirmed 2026 contribution rate, and compares the numbers across different employment setups and adjacent salary levels.

## The Formula

Under the Universal Health Care Act (RA 11223) and official PhilHealth directives, the contribution rate for **2026** is **5.0% of monthly basic salary or declared income**, applied within an income floor of ₱10,000 and a ceiling of ₱100,000.

**Formula:**

> Monthly Basic Salary × 5% = Total Monthly Premium

For a ₱40,000 basic salary:

> ₱40,000 × 5% (0.05) = **₱2,000.00**

Since ₱40,000 is well above the ₱10,000 floor and below the ₱100,000 ceiling, the straightforward 5% rate applies directly without adjustment.

## How the ₱2,000 Is Split: Employed Members

For an **employed member** with an employer-employee relationship, the law mandates an equal 50/50 cost sharing:

| Share | Percentage | Monthly Amount | Per Cut-Off (Semi-Monthly) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Employee's share (deducted from your payslip) | 2.5% | ₱1,000.00 | ₱500.00 |
| Employer's share (paid by your company) | 2.5% | ₱1,000.00 | ₱500.00 |
| **Total remitted to PhilHealth** | **5.0%** | **₱2,000.00** | **₱1,000.00** |

If you are paid semi-monthly (e.g. on the 15th and 30th of each month), **₱500.00** will typically be deducted per cut-off. Your employer matches that with another ₱500.00, remitting ₱1,000.00 per pay period (₱2,000.00 total monthly) to PhilHealth via the Electronic Premium Remittance System (EPRS).

## If You're a Self-Paying Member Instead

If you earn ₱40,000 a month but **do not have an employer** — such as freelancers, self-earning consultants, independent contractors, or land-based OFWs — you are classified as a **self-paying member** under PhilHealth Circular No. 2026-0011.

Because there is no employer to split the cost, you shoulder the complete 5%:

| Payment Frequency | Computation | Total Amount Due |
|---|---|---|
| Monthly | ₱40,000 × 5% | ₱2,000.00 |
| Quarterly (every 3 months) | ₱2,000 × 3 | ₱6,000.00 |
| Semi-Annually (every 6 months) | ₱2,000 × 6 | ₱12,000.00 |
| Annually (full year) | ₱2,000 × 12 | ₱24,000.00 |

### Statement of Premium Account (SPA) Requirement

Under PhilHealth's "No SPA, No Payment" policy, self-paying members must present an official SPA reference number when paying through banks, payment centers, or online platforms (GCash, Maya, Link.BizPortal). ## What If Your Salary Changes During the Year?

Your PhilHealth contribution is computed based on your **current monthly basic salary or declared income** at the time of payment:

- **For Employees:** When you get a raise, your employer's HR payroll team updates your salary figure in the EPRS, and future deductions adjust automatically.
- **For Self-Paying Members:** If your monthly revenue fluctuates, you can submit an updated PMRF with supporting income documentation to adjust your declared income on PhilHealth's records.

## Common Problems and What to Do

**Your payslip shows a different deduction than ₱1,000.**
Check whether your HR team computed PhilHealth deductions based on gross taxable earnings rather than pure basic pay. Non-taxable allowances, meal reimbursements, and overtime pay must be excluded from the PhilHealth calculation base.

**You are paying quarterly and want to know your exact due date.**
Self-paying members paying quarterly must settle the quarter's premiums on or before the last day of the calendar quarter (March 31, June 30, September 30, or December 31).

**You suspect your employer deducted ₱1,000 but failed to remit it.**
Log in to your PhilHealth Member Portal to check your Member Data Record (MDR) and payment history. If several months of payments are missing, request a remittance receipt from your HR office.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How much is the PhilHealth contribution for a ₱40,000 monthly salary?**
₱2,000 per month total. If employed, ₱1,000 is deducted from your salary and ₱1,000 is paid by your employer. If self-paying, you pay ₱2,000 in full.

**How much is deducted per cut-off for a ₱40,000 salary?**
For semi-monthly payroll schedules, ₱500.00 is deducted from your payslip each cut-off.

**Can an employer deduct more than ₱1,000 for a ₱40,000 salary?**
No. The law strictly caps the employee share at 2.5% of basic salary. Any deduction beyond ₱1,000 for a ₱40,000 basic salary is unauthorized.

**Do freelancers earning ₱40,000 have to pay monthly?**
No. Freelancers and self-paying members have the option to pay monthly, quarterly, semi-annually, or annually, as long as full annual premiums are settled by December 31.
