## PhilHealth Requirements for Voluntary Members: What You Need in 2026

Here's the direct answer: to register with PhilHealth as someone without an employer, you need a properly filled-out **PhilHealth Member Registration Form (PMRF)**, a valid proof of identity, and an acceptable **proof of income** — submitted at a Local Health Insurance Office (LHIO), PhilHealth Express outlet, or through the PhilHealth Member Portal or eGovPH app. Your monthly contribution is then computed at **5% of your declared monthly income**, subject to a ₱10,000 income floor and ₱100,000 income ceiling.

This guide is for anyone in the Philippines planning to register with PhilHealth on their own — commonly called a "voluntary member" — including self-earning individuals, professional practitioners, and most Overseas Filipinos. It covers the **2026** registration requirements, contribution rules, and how the process actually works, based on official PhilHealth issuances.

> **Important limitation:** PhilHealth's current official term for this group is **"self-paying member,"** not "voluntary member" — the terms are used interchangeably by the public and by many older references, and this guide addresses the same group: people who pay their own premiums without an employer sharing the cost. If you encounter the older term "Individually Paying Member (IPM)" elsewhere, it generally refers to the same or an overlapping group under PhilHealth's evolving terminology.

## Who Counts as a "Voluntary" or Self-Paying Member?

According to **PhilHealth Circular No. 2026-0011** (*Coverage of Self-Paying Members Under the Universal Health Care Act*), self-paying members include, among others:

- **Self-earning individuals** — people who render services or sell goods as a means of livelihood outside of an employer-employee relationship.
- **Professional practitioners** — doctors, lawyers, certified public accountants, and other professionals required to pass a government licensure exam.
- **Overseas Filipinos**, except seafarers and other sea-based workers (who fall under a separate migrant worker category with its own rules).

This is the group most people mean when they say "voluntary member" — anyone paying their own PhilHealth premium without an employer counterpart.

## Registration Requirements

### The Core Form: PMRF

The **PhilHealth Member Registration Form (PMRF)** is the primary document used for new registration and for updating existing member records. It captures your personal details, membership category, declared income, and any qualified dependents.

### Proof of Income

Under PhilHealth Circular No. 2026-0011, acceptable proof of income differs slightly by category:

**For self-earning individuals and professional practitioners**, any of the following:
- A BIR-received Income Tax Return (ITR)
- Income declared, attested, and duly signed on the PMRF (or its electronic form/platform)
- Any other documentary submission as proof of income, subject to examination and validation by the PhilHealth Regional Office

**For Overseas Filipinos** (except seafarers and other sea-based workers), any of the following:
- Employment Contract or Certificate of Employment with Salary
- Payslips or Remittance Receipts
- A BIR-received Income Tax Return (ITR)
- Income declared, attested, and duly signed on the PMRF (or its electronic form/platform)
- Any other documentary submission as proof of income, subject to examination and validation by the PhilHealth Regional Office

### How to Register

Per the circular, self-paying members can register, obtain a PhilHealth Identification Number (PIN), choose a preferred Primary Care Provider, and/or update their records through:

1. **In-person submission** — a properly filled-out PMRF and/or Primary Care Selection Form (PCSF), with required supporting documents, submitted to any Local Health Insurance Office (LHIO), PhilHealth Express outlet, or accredited registration partner.
2. **Online** — through the PhilHealth Member Portal directly.
3. **Mobile** — through the eGovPH App maintained by the Department of Information and Communications Technology (DICT).

**Upon registration into the National Health Insurance Program (NHIP), self-paying members are granted immediate eligibility to Program benefits** — a right established under the Universal Health Care (UHC) Act. This means you don't have to wait or accumulate a set number of contributions before your coverage becomes active, although you remain obligated to keep up with premium payments (see below).

### Keeping Your Records Updated

Self-paying members are required to maintain up-to-date records — specifically their **monthly income, contact information, and declared dependents**. This matters directly for billing: the accuracy of your Statement of Premium Account depends on it, as does your ability to actually use your benefits at a health facility.

## Contribution Rate and Computation

For calendar year 2026, PhilHealth's premium contribution rate remains at **5% of monthly income**, which PhilHealth has described as the final scheduled rate adjustment under the Universal Health Care Act. This applies within an income floor and ceiling:

| Monthly Income | Monthly Premium |
|---|---|
| ₱10,000 or below | ₱500 (fixed, computed off the ₱10,000 floor) |
| ₱10,000.01 to ₱99,999.99 | 5% of declared monthly income |
| ₱100,000 and above | ₱5,000 (fixed, computed off the ₱100,000 ceiling) |

**Formula:** Declared Monthly Income × 5% = Total Monthly Premium

Unlike employed members — where the premium is split equally between employee and employer — **self-paying members are responsible for the full amount themselves**, since there is no employer to share the cost.

## The Statement of Premium Account (SPA) Requirement

Starting in 2026, PhilHealth requires self-paying members to present a **Statement of Premium Account (SPA)** for most payment transactions across all payment channels — payments generally will not be processed without one, with an exception for payments made in accredited collecting agents located in Geographically Isolated and Disadvantaged Areas (GIDAs).

- The SPA for unpaid applicable months from January 2026 onward is automatically generated on the first day of each applicable month.
- It's made available through your PhilHealth Member Portal account, and a copy or notification is also sent to your declared email address and/or contact number.
- It includes the premium due, due date, payment instructions, interest provisions for late payment, and a notice of any unpaid periods.
- **2026 is a transition year** for this requirement: PhilHealth has said it will allow limited, documented flexibility (for example, for first-time SPA users facing system issues, or members with pending record verification) before the SPA becomes strictly mandatory starting January 1, 2027.

If your SPA is missing or shows a discrepancy, you (or a PhilHealth Office on your behalf) can generate a corrected one through the Member Portal or an SPA generator.

## Payment Deadlines and What Happens If You Miss One

- Self-paying members may pay for any number of applicable months at a time, but **all premiums for a calendar year must be settled no later than December 31 of that year**.
- A **one-month statutory grace period** applies: unpaid premiums for the preceding calendar year can be settled without interest until **January 31** of the following year.
- After that grace period, unpaid balances accrue interest, compounded monthly, **not exceeding 1.5%** per Section 9 of the UHC Act.
- **Failure to pay premiums does not prevent you from receiving Program benefits**, since the UHC Act grants immediate eligibility upon enrollment — but you remain obligated to pay all missed contributions, including any applicable interest.
- Continued failure to settle contributions can lead to other consequences described in the circular and the UHC Act's Implementing Rules and Regulations, and intentional violations such as fraud can potentially lead to suspension of benefit entitlement after due process.

## If You Can't Afford to Pay

If you're genuinely unable to pay the prescribed premium, PhilHealth Circular No. 2026-0011 provides a path: you can be referred to a social worker from the DSWD or your local government unit (LGU), or a medical social worker, for an assessment of financial capacity. If found unable to pay, you would submit a case study, certificate of indigency, or similar document to be classified as **Point of Service – Financially Incapable (POS-FI)** or as an **Indirect Contributor – Financially Incapable (FI)**, whichever applies to your situation — shifting you out of the self-paying/direct-contributor category into government-subsidized coverage.

## Step-by-Step: Registering as a Voluntary (Self-Paying) Member

1. **Download and fill out the PMRF** — get the current version from the official PhilHealth website to avoid outdated forms.
2. **Gather your proof of income** based on your category (see above).
3. **Choose your registration channel** — visit an LHIO or PhilHealth Express in person, or register online through the PhilHealth Member Portal or the eGovPH App.
4. **Submit your PMRF and supporting documents** (or complete the equivalent online steps).
5. **Wait for your PhilHealth Identification Number (PIN) and Member Data Record (MDR)** to be issued.
6. **Check for your Statement of Premium Account (SPA)** once generated, and use it to pay your premium through an accepted channel.
7. **Keep your income, contact details, and dependents updated** with PhilHealth going forward, since these affect your future SPAs and benefit availment.

## Common Problems and What to Do

**You don't have a formal payslip or ITR to prove your income.**
For self-earning individuals and professional practitioners, income declared and signed directly on the PMRF is an accepted option, subject to validation by the PhilHealth Regional Office — you're not necessarily limited to an ITR.

**You missed paying your premiums for a previous year.**
You still retain access to Program benefits under the UHC Act's immediate-eligibility rule, but you'll need to settle the missed contributions, potentially with interest if outside the one-month grace period. Guidelines for premiums missed prior to January 2026 are addressed through a separate PhilHealth policy, per the circular.

**You can't find or generate your SPA.**
You can generate a corrected SPA through the PhilHealth Member Portal or an SPA generator, or request an adjustment directly from a PhilHealth Office.

**You genuinely cannot afford the prescribed premium.**
Ask to be referred for a financial capacity assessment by a DSWD or LGU social worker, which can lead to classification as Point of Service – Financially Incapable (POS-FI) or as an Indirect Contributor if you qualify.

**Someone is transacting with PhilHealth on your behalf.**
A representative must present an authorization letter signed by the member, one valid government-issued ID with photo of the member, and one valid government-issued ID with photo of the representative.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is the main requirement to register as a PhilHealth voluntary member?**
A properly filled-out PMRF, along with acceptable proof of income for your category (self-earning individual, professional practitioner, or Overseas Filipino).

**How much do voluntary/self-paying members pay in 2026?**
5% of your declared monthly income, with a minimum of ₱500/month (income at or below ₱10,000) and a maximum of ₱5,000/month (income at or above ₱100,000). Unlike employed members, there's no employer share — you pay the full amount.

**Is PhilHealth's official term "voluntary member" or "self-paying member"?**
PhilHealth's current governing circular (No. 2026-0011) uses "self-paying members." "Voluntary member" is a widely used informal or older term for essentially the same population.

**Can I still get PhilHealth benefits if I haven't paid all my premiums?**
Yes — the UHC Act grants immediate eligibility upon enrollment regardless of payment status, but you remain obligated to settle missed contributions, potentially with interest.

**Do I need a Statement of Premium Account (SPA) to pay?**
For most payment channels, yes, starting in 2026 — with limited flexibility during the 2026 transition period and a documented exception for payments made in Geographically Isolated and Disadvantaged Areas (GIDAs).

**What if I can't afford to pay my PhilHealth premium at all?**
You can request a financial capacity assessment from a DSWD or LGU social worker, which may reclassify you as Point of Service – Financially Incapable (POS-FI) or as an Indirect Contributor.
