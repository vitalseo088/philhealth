# PhilHealth Parent Dependent Requirements: Who Qualifies and What to Prepare (2026)

A PhilHealth member can generally declare a **parent as a dependent** only if the parent is aged **60 or above** and is **not otherwise covered** as a member, and the PhilHealth rules attach an income condition to this. This guide explains the PhilHealth parent dependent requirements in plain language, the documents you may need, and the steps to follow, so you can prepare before visiting PhilHealth or talking to your HR.

This guide is for employed, self-paying, and other contributing members who want to cover a mother or father. It is written for 2026, but forms and procedures can change, so confirm the latest rules with PhilHealth before filing.

## What Is a PhilHealth Dependent?

A **dependent** is a person who can receive PhilHealth benefits through a member's membership without paying a separate premium. The member (and, for employed members, the employer) pays the contribution, and declared dependents are covered under that membership.

Under the Universal Health Care Act (Republic Act No. 11223) and its implementing rules, PhilHealth defines which relatives count as dependents. Being **declared** in your PhilHealth record is what allows PhilHealth and hospitals to confirm that a person is covered under your membership.

## Who Can Be Declared as a Parent Dependent?

Based on PhilHealth's dependent categories, a parent may be declared when:

- The parent is **60 years old or above**
- The parent is **not otherwise covered** as a PhilHealth member
- The parent meets the **income condition** set under the rules **[VERIFY threshold and how it is proven]**

### Senior Citizens May Already Be Covered

Philippine law provides PhilHealth coverage to senior citizens (Republic Act No. 10645). This means your parent aged 60 or above may already have their own coverage as a **senior citizen member**. If so, they would not need to be declared as your dependent. **[VERIFY how senior citizen membership interacts with dependent declarations under current PhilHealth rules.]**

Before filing, ask PhilHealth to check your parent's membership status. This avoids duplicate records and wasted trips.

### When a Parent Cannot Be Declared

A parent generally cannot be declared as your dependent if they are:

- Below 60 years old
- Already a PhilHealth member in their own right (for example, a senior citizen member, a pensioner, or a member in another category)

### Details That Must Match

Delays often come from mismatched records. Check that these agree across your forms and documents:

- Spelling of full names, including middle names and suffixes
- Birthdates
- The parent's name as written on your birth certificate

If you use a different surname (for example, after marriage), bring documents that explain the difference.

## How to Declare a Parent as a PhilHealth Dependent

The steps below describe a general process. Your exact steps may differ by membership category.

1. **Check your parent's status.** Ask PhilHealth whether your parent is already a member, including as a senior citizen.
2. **Get the current PMRF** from PhilHealth. **[VERIFY form version and download options]**
3. **Fill in your member details** and the dependents section, listing your parent's full name, date of birth, and relationship.
4. **Gather supporting documents** such as your birth certificate, proof of the parent's age, and valid IDs.
5. **Submit your documents.**
   - *Employed members:* usually through your employer's HR or PhilHealth officer.
   - *Self-paying and other members:* at a PhilHealth office or Local Health Insurance Office.
   - Ask whether online submission through the Member Portal is available for dependents. **[VERIFY]**
6. **Confirm the update.** After processing, check that your Member Data Record (MDR) lists your parent as a dependent. Keep copies of everything you submitted.

## Costs and Contributions

Declaring a qualified dependent does not require a separate premium. Dependents are covered under the member's contribution, provided the member's membership is active and eligible. **[VERIFY current wording from PhilHealth]**

Coverage for a specific hospital stay or service still depends on the applicable benefit package, eligibility rules, and facility accreditation. Inpatient benefits are generally paid to accredited health facilities through case rates and deducted from the bill under the applicable rules, so coverage does not mean the entire bill is paid.

## Examples

**Example 1: Parent not yet covered.** Rosa is an employed member. Her father is 68 and is not a member in any category. Rosa can ask her HR how to declare him as a dependent, subject to the income condition and PhilHealth's review.

## Common Problems

- **Parent already has a record.** Duplicate membership records can cause rejection or delays. Ask PhilHealth which record applies.
- **Income condition unclear.** The available sources do not clearly specify how income is proven, so ask the PhilHealth office handling your request.
- **Parent below 60.** The parent category does not apply. Other membership options may exist for the parent, so ask PhilHealth.
- **Records not updated.** Processing may still be ongoing, or the form may be incomplete.
- **Hospital cannot find the dependent.** Show your submitted PMRF copy and ask the hospital's PhilHealth desk to verify with PhilHealth.

## What To Do If Something Goes Wrong

1. Keep copies of everything you submitted and any acknowledgment slip.
2. Check your MDR through official PhilHealth channels.
3. Visit the PhilHealth office or Local Health Insurance Office that handles your record and ask about the status.
4. Use the official hotline or email channels listed on the PhilHealth website. Avoid numbers from unofficial pages.
5. If a hospital admission is ongoing, ask the hospital's PhilHealth coordinator to help with verification.
