## PhilHealth Cesarean Coverage 2026: How Much It Pays and Who Qualifies

If you're planning or facing a Cesarean delivery, here's the direct answer: as of the case rate update that took effect on April 30, 2026, **PhilHealth pays ₱58,000 for a primary Cesarean section and ₱62,000 for a Cesarean delivery performed after a failed attempted vaginal birth following a previous Cesarean (TOLAC/VBAC attempt)**. This guide explains what that means in practice, who is covered, what's included in the amount, and what you may still need to pay out of pocket.

This information is based on official PhilHealth Circular No. 2026-0005, *Rationalization of Case Rates for Select Maternal and Gynecologic Services in the Hospital Setting*, published April 30, 2026. Rates and rules for **2026** are covered here — older articles online may still show the previous ₱19,000 or ₱37,050 figures, which no longer apply to admissions on or after the circular's effectivity date.

> **Important limitation:** Several secondary sources online describe the ₱58,000/₱62,000 split as being based on *hospital level* (Level 1 vs. Level 2–3). The official circular does not say this. Based on the actual benefit table in PC No. 2026-0005, the split is based on the **type of procedure**, not the hospital's level — see the table below. If you see a source claiming the split is by hospital tier, treat that as unverified until PhilHealth clarifies further.

### Quick Answer

| Item | Amount | Applies To |
|---|---|---|
| Primary Cesarean Section | ₱58,000 | Standard/elective or emergency C-section (procedure codes 59513, 59514) |
| Cesarean following attempted vaginal delivery after a previous C-section | ₱62,000 | TOLAC/VBAC attempt that ends in a repeat Cesarean (procedure code 59620) |
| Normal Spontaneous Delivery (for comparison) | ₱29,000 | Vaginal delivery, Level 1–3 hospitals |
| Newborn care package | Set separately | Covered under a related PhilHealth issuance, not detailed in PC 2026-0005 |

Coverage applies to **accredited Level 1 to Level 3 hospitals** and is paid directly to the hospital — it is deducted from your bill at discharge, not given to you as cash.

## What Is PhilHealth Cesarean Coverage?

PhilHealth cesarean coverage is a **case rate benefit** — a fixed amount PhilHealth pays an accredited hospital for managing a Cesarean delivery, covering the facility fee, the professional fees of the surgeon and anesthesiologist, standard medicines, and supplies included in the package. It is not a reimbursement you claim yourself in most cases; the hospital deducts it automatically from your total bill.

The current amounts were set by **PhilHealth Circular No. 2026-0005**, which took effect immediately upon publication (April 30, 2026) and applies to all Cesarean admissions on or after that date. It repeals the older PhilHealth Circular No. 025, s. 2015 and relevant portions of PhilHealth Circular No. 2024-0037 covering the same services.

## Who Is Eligible?

Per the circular itself, **"All Filipinos are automatically entitled to avail of this benefits package. PhilHealth beneficiaries shall comply with the existing membership eligibility rules."** In practice, this means:

- You (or the member you're a dependent of) must have an active PhilHealth membership.
- The delivery must take place in a **PhilHealth-accredited Level 1 to Level 3 hospital**.
- A printed Member Data Record (MDR) is **not required** — accredited hospitals are directed to check eligibility and deduct the benefit automatically at discharge.

**On the specific contribution requirement (e.g., a set number of monthly premiums paid within a certain period):** available sources conflict. Some describe a "9 contributions within 12 months" rule, others describe "3 contributions within 6 months," and others state the Universal Health Care Act removed contribution-count conditions entirely for accessing benefits. PhilHealth Circular No. 2026-0005 does not itself restate a specific contribution count for this package — it only points to "existing membership eligibility rules." Because this is a genuinely unclear point across sources, **confirm your specific eligibility status directly with PhilHealth (through the Member Portal, a PhilHealth office, or your hospital's PhilHealth desk) before your delivery**, rather than relying on any single blog's contribution-count claim.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|

**What "no co-payment" on basic/ward accommodation means:** if you are admitted to a basic or ward bed (a shared room, 3 or more occupants) at an accredited public or private hospital, the hospital **cannot balance-bill you** for anything already covered by the case rate — medicines, supplies, professional fees, and salaried labor within the package.

**What happens above ward accommodation:** the circular sets a co-payment cap system for semi-private beds, and treats private/suite accommodation and any services beyond the standard package (e.g., choice of physician, room upgrades) as subject to out-of-pocket payment by the patient.

## What's Included in the Cesarean Case Rate

According to the circular, the reimbursement rate is meant to cover, for the covered episode of care:

- The surgical procedure itself
- Laboratory tests required for the case
- Medications and drugs listed in the current Philippine National Drug Formulary (PNDF)
- Medical supplies
- Professional fees for the attending OB-GYN, anesthesiologist, and other required staff
- Salaried labor involved in the case

**What it does not automatically cover:** private or suite room upgrades, choice-of-physician arrangements beyond the assigned team, and services unrelated to the maternal/gynecologic management of the case. These fall to out-of-pocket payment or a private HMO, if you have one.

## Elective vs. Medically Indicated Cesarean

This is an important detail many summaries skip: PhilHealth Circular No. 2026-0005 specifies that **only "obstetrically and medically indicated" Cesarean procedures, coded with the correct ICD-10 indication in line with recognized Clinical Practice Guidelines, are covered.** Cesarean claims are subject to strict monitoring and utilization review, and claims found non-compliant with this rule can be returned to the hospital (Return to Sender). In practical terms, this means the hospital's documentation of medical necessity matters for whether the claim is honored — it is not something the patient needs to prove personally, but it does mean not every Cesarean automatically qualifies without proper clinical documentation.

## How the Deduction Works: Step by Step

1. **Delivery happens at an accredited facility.** Confirm before admission (ideally during prenatal visits) that the hospital is PhilHealth-accredited for maternal and gynecologic case rates.
2. **The hospital verifies your PhilHealth status.** A printed MDR is not required under this circular; hospitals are expected to verify electronically.
3. **The care team documents medical necessity.** For a Cesarean, this includes the ICD-10 code for the obstetric or medical indication, following recognized clinical guidelines.
4. **The hospital computes your Statement of Account (SOA).** The applicable case rate (₱58,000 or ₱62,000) is deducted from your total bill.
5. **You pay only the remaining balance**, if any — and no balance billing applies if you stayed in basic/ward accommodation, for anything the case rate is meant to cover.
6. **The hospital files the claim with PhilHealth**, generally within 60 calendar days. In the standard scenario, you do not file this yourself.

## Exceptions: When You File the Claim Yourself

Direct filing by the member is not normally allowed under this benefit — the accredited hospital files on your behalf. The circular carries over the general PhilHealth rule that **direct filing is permitted only for confinements abroad or emergency cases handled at non-accredited facilities.** If either applies to you, keep your official receipts and medical records, since you would need to file within the standard filing window.

## Common Problems and What to Do

**The hospital tried to charge you for something the case rate should cover, in a basic/ward bed.**
This is a "no balance billing" violation. The circular directs patients to report non-compliant facilities to PhilHealth's Corporate Action Center — by hotline at (02) 8662-2588 or by email at actioncenter@philhealth.gov.ph.

**You were asked to buy medicines or supplies that should have been part of the package.**
Under the circular, you are entitled to a **full refund** from the hospital for any out-of-pocket cost you paid for items that were supposed to be included in the package. Keep your official receipts as proof.

**Your hospital says the Cesarean wasn't "medically indicated" enough to qualify.**
Ask your OB-GYN to confirm the ICD-10 coding and documentation submitted with the claim. This is a clinical/administrative matter between the hospital and PhilHealth, but incomplete documentation is one reason claims get returned.

**You're unsure whether you meet the contribution requirement.**
Because sources disagree on the exact contribution-count rule (see the Eligibility section above), verify your Member Data Record and contribution status directly through the PhilHealth Member Portal or a PhilHealth office before your due date, rather than relying on an unofficial number.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Does PhilHealth cover the entire cost of a Cesarean delivery?**
Not necessarily. PhilHealth coverage depends on the applicable benefit package and eligibility requirements. The ₱58,000/₱62,000 case rate is deducted from your bill; whether that fully covers your bill depends on the hospital's actual charges, your accommodation type, and whether the case rate exceeds your total cost.

**Is an elective (non-emergency) Cesarean covered?**
Coverage depends on medical indication and correct clinical documentation under recognized Clinical Practice Guidelines, not simply on whether the Cesarean was planned in advance versus performed as an emergency. The circular's own language ties coverage to procedures that are "obstetrically and medically indicated."

**Do I need to submit a claim form myself?**
Generally no. The accredited hospital deducts the benefit and files the claim. You would only file directly in specific exceptions, such as confinement abroad or emergency care at a non-accredited facility.

**Does the case rate differ by hospital level (Level 1 vs. Level 2–3)?**
Based on the official benefit table in PC No. 2026-0005, no — the ₱58,000 and ₱62,000 amounts are tied to the type of procedure (primary Cesarean vs. Cesarean after a failed attempted vaginal delivery/VBAC), not the hospital's accreditation level. Some online sources describe it differently; treat those as unverified pending further PhilHealth clarification.

**What if I already delivered before April 30, 2026?**
The circular states that it applies to admissions/availment upon its effectivity. Deliveries before the effectivity date would fall under the previously applicable rates and rules, not this update.

**Where can I check if my hospital is PhilHealth-accredited for this benefit?**
Check directly through PhilHealth's official accreditation channels or ask the hospital's PhilHealth desk; provider accreditation status can change, so verify close to your admission date.
