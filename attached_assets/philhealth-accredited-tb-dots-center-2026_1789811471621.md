## PhilHealth Accredited TB DOTS Center: Where to Go and What's Covered in 2026

Here's the direct answer: a **PhilHealth accredited TB DOTS center** is a health facility — typically a rural health unit, city or municipal health office, or hospital outpatient department — accredited by PhilHealth to deliver **Directly Observed Treatment, Short-course (DOTS)** for tuberculosis and to receive PhilHealth's **Outpatient TB DOTS Benefit Package**, a fixed case rate of **₱4,000**, paid to the facility in two installments across the treatment course.

This guide is for PhilHealth members, TB patients, and families in **2026** who want to understand where DOTS treatment is delivered, what PhilHealth's package actually pays for, and how it relates to the free TB testing and medicines already available through the government's TB control program.

> **This is not medical advice.** Whether you have TB, and which treatment regimen and registration group applies to your case, is determined by a qualified health professional using national TB program protocols — not something to work out from this article.

> **Provider information can change. Check the official PhilHealth provider directory before visiting.** This guide does not name individual centers; PhilHealth's own accredited facilities listing (TB-DOTS Centers) is the current, verifiable reference.

## What Is the TB DOTS Package?

**DOTS (Directly Observed Treatment, Short-course)** is the World Health Organization–endorsed strategy for TB treatment, in which a patient's medication intake is directly observed by a health worker to ensure completion of the full course. PhilHealth's role is specifically financial: the **Outpatient TB DOTS Benefit Package** reimburses accredited facilities for the cost of managing PhilHealth members and dependents undergoing DOTS treatment.

The package's current governing framework is **PhilHealth Circular No. 014, s. 2014**, which revised the original TB-DOTS benefit (first established in 2002 at ₱4,000) to expand which TB cases are covered, streamline reimbursement, and align the package with national TB control strategy at the time.

**On the current rate:** the ₱4,000 total case rate — split into two payments — comes from PhilHealth Circular No. 014, s. 2014. PhilHealth separately issued **Circular No. 2024-0037**, a general **50% adjustment to select case rates** effective January 1, 2025, which increased many outpatient and inpatient case rates PhilHealth-wide. **Whether the TB DOTS package was included in that 50% adjustment or fell under its list of excluded benefits was not confirmed in the sources reviewed for this article.** Rather than guess, this article states the base ₱4,000 rate established in Circular No. 014, s. 2014 as the confirmed figure, and recommends verifying the current exact amount directly with your accredited facility or PhilHealth, since a broader case rate adjustment took effect afterward.

## An Important Distinction: PhilHealth Coverage vs. Free TB Treatment

This is worth understanding before anything else: **you do not need to be a PhilHealth member to get tested or treated for TB.** TB testing (such as sputum microscopy and GeneXpert/NAAT testing) and the full course of first-line anti-TB medicines are provided **free at government health centers, rural health units, and DOTS-accredited facilities nationwide**, funded through the DOH's National TB Control Program (NTP) — independent of PhilHealth membership.

**PhilHealth's TB DOTS package is a separate, additional benefit** — it reimburses the *accredited facility* (not the patient directly) for managing a PhilHealth member's or dependent's care, on top of the government's free-treatment infrastructure. Practically, this means a PhilHealth member being treated for TB shouldn't need to pay for testing or first-line medicines regardless of the PhilHealth package, since those are already free under the national program; PhilHealth's payment supports the facility's costs of delivering that observed treatment.

## Who Is Eligible for the PhilHealth TB DOTS Package?

Per PhilHealth Circular No. 014, s. 2014, the package covers **both children and adults**, whether **bacteriologically confirmed or clinically diagnosed**, and whether **pulmonary or extra-pulmonary**, across these registration groups:

- **New cases**
- **Retreatment cases:**
  - Relapse
  - Treatment After Failure
  - Treatment After Lost to Follow-up (Return After Default)
  - Previous Treatment Outcome Unknown

### What's Excluded

- **In-patient admissions** (this is an outpatient-only package)
- **Drug-resistant TB**
- **Latent TB infection**

### A Note on HIV Co-Infection

Per the circular, a patient under treatment for first-line, drug-sensitive TB who is also on antiretroviral drugs for HIV **may avail of both** the TB DOTS Package (at an accredited TB DOTS Center) **and** the Outpatient HIV/AIDS Treatment (OHAT) Package (at an accredited HIV treatment hub) **at the same time** — but claims for each are filed separately.

## What Facilities Qualify as TB-DOTS Centers?

PhilHealth's own Accredited Health Facilities page maintains a specific listing titled **"TB-DOTS Centers"** — this is the authoritative, current reference for which facilities are accredited to deliver this package. Per the circular, accreditation of TB-DOTS providers follows PhilHealth's general framework for **Provider Engagement through Accreditation and Contracting of Health Services**, and an applying facility must submit a **Performance Commitment**, signed by both the facility's owner or local chief executive and its head, as part of initial accreditation.

Facilities are also required to maintain a minimum set of patient information — including an **NTP (National TB Control Program) treatment card and TB registry** — readily available to PhilHealth for monitoring and evaluation.

## How Payment Works

| Phase | Payment | Package Code |
|---|---|---|
| After the intensive treatment phase | ₱2,500 | 89221 |
| After the continuation (maintenance) phase | ₱1,500 | 89222 |

The circular also specifies that **payment for referring physicians and other expenses for services done outside the facility** (such as a chest x-ray performed elsewhere) is settled by the accredited facility itself, not billed separately to the patient through PhilHealth.

## How to Find an Accredited TB-DOTS Center

1. **Go to PhilHealth's Accredited Health Facilities page** (philhealth.gov.ph → Partners → Providers → Facilities → Accredited).
2. **Open the "TB-DOTS Centers" listing.**
3. **Check the "updated as of" date** shown on the page before relying on it.
4. **Find your city, municipality, or barangay** in the listing.
5. **Alternatively, start at your local Rural Health Unit or City/Municipal Health Office** — these are common entry points into both the DOH's free TB program and PhilHealth-accredited DOTS treatment, and staff there can direct you appropriately.

## Step-by-Step: What Happens During Treatment

1. **You're diagnosed with TB** (new or retreatment case) through sputum testing, GeneXpert/NAAT, or clinical diagnosis, following DOH protocols.
2. **You register for DOTS treatment** at an accredited TB-DOTS Center, where an NTP treatment card is opened for you.
3. **You undergo the intensive phase** of treatment, with your medication intake directly observed as per DOTS protocol.
4. **The facility claims the first payment (₱2,500)** upon your completion of the intensive phase.
5. **You proceed to the continuation (maintenance) phase.**
6. **The facility claims the second payment (₱1,500)** upon your completion of that phase.
7. **Claims are filed by the facility**, not by the patient directly, as with most outpatient case-rate packages.

## Common Problems and What to Do

**You were asked to pay for TB testing or medicines.**
First-line TB testing and medicines are provided free through the DOH's National TB Control Program regardless of PhilHealth status. If you were charged for these at a government facility, that's worth raising with the facility directly, since this should not depend on your PhilHealth coverage at all.

**You're not sure if your treatment center is PhilHealth-accredited for TB DOTS.**
Check PhilHealth's official "TB-DOTS Centers" listing. Being a facility that provides DOTS treatment (part of the DOH program) is not automatically the same as being PhilHealth-accredited for the reimbursement package — though many DOTS-providing government facilities are also PhilHealth-accredited.

**You have drug-resistant TB and were told this package doesn't apply.**
That's consistent with the circular — drug-resistant TB is explicitly excluded from this particular outpatient package. Ask your facility about applicable alternative programs or benefits for your specific situation.

**You were told you can't access this package because you were previously treated and defaulted.**
"Treatment After Lost to Follow-up" is explicitly one of the covered retreatment registration groups under the circular — this should not, by itself, disqualify you. If a facility tells you otherwise, ask them to clarify against the current circular.

**You have both TB and HIV and aren't sure if you can use both benefit packages.**
Per the circular, you can access both the TB DOTS Package and the OHAT Package simultaneously if you're on first-line, drug-sensitive TB treatment and antiretroviral therapy — but the two are filed as separate claims by their respective accredited facilities.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How much does PhilHealth's TB DOTS package cover?**
A fixed ₱4,000 total, paid in two installments — ₱2,500 after the intensive phase and ₱1,500 after the continuation phase — per PhilHealth Circular No. 014, s. 2014. A broader 2025 case rate adjustment may or may not have affected this specific package; the exact current amount at your facility is worth confirming directly.

**Do I need PhilHealth membership to get treated for TB?**
No. TB testing and first-line medicines are provided free through the DOH's National TB Control Program regardless of PhilHealth status. PhilHealth's TB DOTS package is a separate benefit that reimburses the accredited facility.

**Where can I find an accredited TB-DOTS center?**
On PhilHealth's Accredited Health Facilities page, under the "TB-DOTS Centers" listing. Your local Rural Health Unit or City/Municipal Health Office is also a common starting point.

**Is drug-resistant TB covered under this package?**
No — the circular explicitly excludes drug-resistant TB, along with in-patient admissions and latent TB infection, from this outpatient package.

**Can retreatment cases still get this benefit?**
Yes — relapse, treatment after failure, treatment after lost to follow-up, and cases with unknown previous treatment outcome are all listed as covered retreatment registration groups.

**Can I use this package if I also have HIV?**
If you're on first-line, drug-sensitive TB treatment and antiretroviral therapy for HIV, you may access both the TB DOTS Package and the OHAT Package at the same time, filed as separate claims.

**Do I need to file the claim myself?**
No — the accredited facility files the claim and receives payment directly, as with most outpatient case-rate packages.
