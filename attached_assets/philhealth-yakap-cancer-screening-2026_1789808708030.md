# PhilHealth YAKAP Cancer Screening: What's Covered and How to Avail It (2026 Guide)

If your PhilHealth-accredited primary care doctor thinks you're at risk for breast, lung, liver, or colorectal cancer, YAKAP can cover your screening test at no cost to you — but only if you go through the proper referral process first. This guide explains exactly what's covered, who qualifies, and how the process works, based on PhilHealth's official YAKAP issuances.

This guide is for PhilHealth members and dependents who are already registered — or planning to register — with a YAKAP Clinic. It reflects PhilHealth's YAKAP program as expanded in 2025 and continuing into 2026. Some clinical eligibility details (such as exact age brackets for every cancer type) are set by separate PhilHealth circulars and should be confirmed with your YAKAP Clinic physician, since eligibility is a clinical assessment, not a self-declaration.

## What Is PhilHealth YAKAP?

**YAKAP** stands for **Yaman ng Kalusugan Program**. It's PhilHealth's expanded primary care benefit package, which replaced and broadened the earlier Konsulta program. Instead of only paying for hospital confinement, YAKAP is built around preventive and primary care — the idea being to catch problems early, before they require expensive hospitalization.

Under YAKAP, a member and their registered dependents can access, based on clinical need and a doctor's recommendation:

- Free consultations and check-ups at their registered YAKAP Clinic
- Outpatient laboratory tests
- Selected **cancer screening tests**
- Free essential medicines (up to ₱20,000 worth per beneficiary per year) through accredited GAMOT pharmacies

Cancer screening is one component of this larger package — it is not a stand-alone program you can access on its own without being registered under YAKAP.

## Which Cancer Screening Tests Does YAKAP Cover?

According to PhilHealth's official YAKAP FAQ and its 2025 benefit package circular for selected outpatient cancer screening tests, YAKAP covers six screening tests across four of the most common cancers in the Philippines:

| Screening Test | Cancer Type | PhilHealth Package Rate |
|---|---|---|
| Mammogram | Breast cancer | ₱2,610 |
| Breast ultrasound | Breast cancer | ₱1,350 |
| Low-dose chest CT scan (LDCT) | Lung cancer | ₱7,220 |
| Alpha-fetoprotein (AFP) test | Liver cancer | ₱1,230 |
| Liver ultrasound | Liver cancer | ₱960 |
| Colonoscopy | Colorectal cancer | ₱23,640 |

These figures are the PhilHealth package rates paid to the accredited screening facility — not an amount the patient pays. Once you have a valid referral and go to an accredited cancer-screening facility, PhilHealth's official FAQ states plainly that the facility should not charge the patient anything further for performing the test itself; the package rate already covers supplies, equipment, indirect costs, and the reading physician's professional fee.

## Who Is Eligible for YAKAP Cancer Screening?

Eligibility is **not self-determined** — it's based on a clinical risk assessment done by your YAKAP Clinic doctor, following PhilHealth's quality-standards circulars for each cancer type. In general terms, based on PhilHealth's own guidance:

- **Lung cancer screening (LDCT):** PhilHealth's circular describes the target population as asymptomatic adults aged 50–80 years old with identified risk factors (such as significant smoking history).
- **Breast cancer screening (mammogram/ultrasound):** Targeted at asymptomatic women in defined risk groups, including those with identified risk factors such as a first-degree relative with breast cancer, as set out in PhilHealth's breast cancer screening circular.
- **Liver and colorectal cancer screening:** Also targeted at asymptomatic, high-risk individuals based on risk stratification criteria defined in PhilHealth's respective circulars for each cancer type.

Because the exact age brackets and risk-factor criteria differ by cancer type and are defined in detailed clinical circulars, **the safest way to know if you qualify is to have your YAKAP Clinic doctor assess you directly.** Don't assume you are or aren't eligible based only on this article — the doctor's clinical judgment and PhilHealth's documented criteria determine the final answer.

Importantly, PhilHealth's FAQ also confirms that cancer screening under YAKAP is offered to beneficiaries **based on indication or need as determined by the referring doctor** — it is not an automatic annual benefit available to every member regardless of risk profile.

## How to Avail of Free Cancer Screening Under YAKAP

### Step 1: Register with a YAKAP Clinic

You need an active PhilHealth Identification Number (PIN) first. Registration can be done through:

- The eGovPH app
- The PhilHealth Member Portal
- Any PhilHealth office nationwide
- Directly at a YAKAP Clinic

Register yourself and, separately, your qualified dependents — they can be registered at the same clinic or a different one that's more convenient for them.

### Step 2: Go for Your First Consultation

Visit your registered YAKAP Clinic for your first patient encounter. The doctor will do an initial assessment and risk profiling based on your health history and symptoms (if any).

### Step 3: Get Assessed for Cancer Screening Eligibility

If your risk profile matches the criteria for one of the covered cancer screening tests, your YAKAP Clinic doctor can issue a referral. This referral is required — PhilHealth's official FAQ explicitly states that a member **cannot** go directly to a cancer-screening facility to request one of these tests without this referral.

### Step 4: Visit an Accredited Cancer Screening Facility

Using your referral, go to a PhilHealth-accredited cancer screening facility (these must hold the appropriate DOH license, e.g., for imaging or laboratory services). Present your referral there.

### Step 5: Undergo the Test at No Additional Charge

Per PhilHealth's own FAQ, the facility should not charge you anything further for the screening test itself — this is covered under the PhilHealth package rate paid to the facility.

### Step 6: Follow Up with Your YAKAP Clinic

After you get your results, you're expected to return to your YAKAP Clinic doctor for interpretation, follow-up, and any next steps (such as referral to a higher-level facility if something concerning is found).

## What If You Already Have a Referral from a Private Doctor?

PhilHealth's FAQ addresses this specific situation: if it's your **first time** availing of the cancer screening benefit, you may use a referral you already obtained from a private doctor (not your YAKAP Clinic physician). However, after getting your results, you will still need to be referred back to your registered YAKAP Clinic going forward, since only your YAKAP Clinic doctor can issue prescriptions and recommendations tied to your continuing YAKAP record.

## Costs: Is There Really No Fee?

This is where members sometimes get confused, so it's worth separating two different charges:

1. **The cancer screening test itself** — no additional charge at the accredited screening facility once you have a valid referral, per PhilHealth's FAQ.
2. **The YAKAP Clinic annual fee** — this is a separate matter. PhilHealth's FAQ states that services at **accredited YAKAP Clinics** are free, but a **private** YAKAP Clinic may charge **up to ₱900 per patient per year** if you're registered there. This is a clinic registration/consultation-related fee, not a charge for the cancer screening test itself.

If a facility tries to charge you directly for the screening test after you present a valid referral, that is inconsistent with PhilHealth's published FAQ guidance, and you should raise the concern with PhilHealth directly (see Common Problems below).

## Examples

*The following are illustrative examples only, not official case determinations.*

**Example 1:** A 55-year-old member with a long smoking history is examined at their YAKAP Clinic. Based on the risk assessment, the doctor issues a referral for a low-dose chest CT scan. The member goes to an accredited imaging facility, presents the referral, and undergoes the LDCT scan without being charged for the test itself.

**Example 2:** A 45-year-old woman whose sister had breast cancer discusses this family history with her YAKAP Clinic doctor. Based on the identified risk factor, the doctor may refer her for a mammogram or breast ultrasound as part of YAKAP's covered breast cancer screening tests, subject to the applicable eligibility criteria.

## Common Problems

- **"My YAKAP Clinic doesn't offer the lab test I need."** The clinic should refer you to its partner laboratory or diagnostic facility — this is part of the covered service.
- **"I was asked to pay for the screening test at the facility."** According to PhilHealth's FAQ, this shouldn't happen once you have a valid referral. Confirm the facility is truly PhilHealth-accredited for that specific screening test, and raise the issue with PhilHealth if the charge persists.
- **"I'm not sure if I qualify based on age or risk factors."** Eligibility criteria differ by cancer type and are clinically assessed — don't self-diagnose eligibility. Bring up your concerns and family history directly with your YAKAP Clinic doctor.
- **"My screening facility isn't listed anywhere yet."** PhilHealth has indicated that lists of participating pharmacies and screening facilities are being rolled out and announced progressively — check the official PhilHealth website or ask at your YAKAP Clinic for current accredited facilities in your area.

## What to Do If Something Goes Wrong

If you experience a problem — such as being charged for a covered screening test, being denied a referral without explanation, or being unable to find an accredited facility — PhilHealth's official YAKAP FAQ lists these contact channels:

- Call (02) 8-441-7442 or the PhilHealth Corporate Action Center hotline listed on philhealth.gov.ph
- Text or call the numbers published on PhilHealth's official YAKAP FAQ page (Smart and Globe lines)
- Email actioncenter@philhealth.gov.ph
- Visit any PhilHealth office
- Use the click-to-call feature on the official PhilHealth website

Always verify current contact numbers directly on philhealth.gov.ph, since these can be updated.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is YAKAP the same as PhilHealth Konsulta?**
No. YAKAP replaced and expanded the earlier Konsulta program, adding a broader medicine benefit and cancer screening coverage on top of the primary care consultations Konsulta already offered.

**Do I need to pay anything to register for YAKAP?**
Registration itself is free. However, a private YAKAP Clinic may charge up to ₱900 per patient per year as a clinic fee, per PhilHealth's official FAQ.

**Can I choose which cancer screening test I want?**
No. The specific test you're referred for depends on your doctor's clinical assessment of your risk factors and symptoms, following PhilHealth's eligibility criteria for that cancer type — it isn't a test you can simply request on your own.

**Does YAKAP cover cancer treatment if the screening finds something?**
Cancer screening under YAKAP is a detection benefit, not a treatment benefit. If a screening result leads to a cancer diagnosis, treatment coverage would fall under separate PhilHealth benefit packages (such as the Z Benefit packages), which are governed by their own eligibility rules. Confirm applicable treatment coverage directly with PhilHealth or your treating facility.

**How often can I get screened under YAKAP?**
The available official YAKAP FAQ does not specify a fixed screening frequency for each test; this is generally guided by the clinical standards in PhilHealth's cancer-specific circulars and your doctor's assessment. Ask your YAKAP Clinic doctor about the recommended interval for your situation.

**What if I don't have a PhilHealth PIN yet?**
You'll need to secure one first — through eGovPH, the PhilHealth Member Portal, or any PhilHealth office — before you can register with a YAKAP Clinic.
