## Who This Guide Is For

This guide is for PhilHealth members and their qualified dependents who are pregnant or planning a pregnancy and want to understand how PhilHealth maternity coverage works — what's generally included, who can claim it, and what to do when something goes wrong with a claim. It focuses on PhilHealth's role specifically; it is not medical advice about pregnancy or delivery.

**This guide does not cover:** newborn care benefits in full detail, or high-risk pregnancy complication coverage beyond a general mention — those may warrant separate guides.

## Quick Answer

PhilHealth provides maternity-related coverage mainly through case rate payments made to accredited health facilities for delivery-related care — commonly covering prenatal check-ups, the delivery itself (normal or caesarean), and postpartum care, depending on the applicable package and facility type. Coverage amounts are paid directly to accredited providers and are deducted from the member's total hospital bill according to PhilHealth's rules; they are **not** a cash reimbursement paid straight to the member in most cases, and PhilHealth coverage does not mean the entire hospital bill is paid in full.

## What Is PhilHealth Maternity Coverage?

PhilHealth maternity coverage refers to the benefit packages PhilHealth uses to help pay for pregnancy-related care delivered at accredited facilities. Rather than paying the member directly, PhilHealth generally pays case rates to the accredited hospital, clinic, or maternity provider, and this amount is deducted from what the member ultimately owes.

PhilHealth coverage "depends on the applicable benefit package and eligibility requirements" — meaning the amount and scope of coverage can differ based on factors like delivery type (normal vs. caesarean), whether complications occur, and which facility provided the care.

## Who Is Eligible

Based on how PhilHealth has generally structured maternity coverage:

- The pregnant individual must be either the PhilHealth member themselves or a legal dependent (e.g., spouse) of an active member.
- The member's PhilHealth contributions generally need to be up to date, based on applicable qualifying contribution rules for the member's category (employed, self-paying, voluntary, etc.).
- Delivery generally needs to take place at a PhilHealth-accredited facility for the case rate to apply.

**Note:** Specific qualifying contribution requirements (such as a minimum number of paid months before delivery) and any distinctions between first pregnancy vs. subsequent pregnancies were not verified in detail here and should be confirmed against current PhilHealth maternity benefit materials.

## Maternity Benefits: What's Generally Included

PhilHealth maternity-related coverage has historically been organized around a few general categories, though exact names, bundling, and amounts should be verified as current for 2026:

- **Prenatal care** — check-ups and monitoring during pregnancy.
- **Delivery care** — normal spontaneous delivery or caesarean section, depending on medical necessity.
- **Postpartum care** — care immediately following delivery.
- **Newborn care** — coverage related to the newborn, which may be handled as a related but distinct benefit.

## Step-by-Step: Using Your PhilHealth Maternity Benefit

1. **Confirm your PhilHealth membership is active** and check your Member Data Record (MDR) through the PhilHealth Members' Portal.
2. **Choose a PhilHealth-accredited facility** for prenatal care and delivery. Confirm accreditation directly with the facility or through PhilHealth's official provider directory — do not assume a hospital or clinic is accredited.
3. **Bring your PhilHealth ID/PIN and MDR** to your prenatal visits and at admission for delivery.
4. **Let the facility process the PhilHealth claim.** Accredited facilities typically file the claim directly with PhilHealth and deduct the covered amount from your bill.
5. **Review your final billing statement** to confirm the PhilHealth deduction was applied.
6. **Keep your official receipts and claim documents** in case of a discrepancy or follow-up.

## Common Problems Members Encounter

- **Facility says it is accredited, but the claim is later denied or reduced** — accreditation status and scope of coverage should always be confirmed in advance.
- **Confusion over normal delivery vs. caesarean section coverage** when the delivery type changes due to complications.
- **Newborn coverage handled separately** from the mother's delivery claim, causing confusion about what was or wasn't covered.
- **Contribution record not up to date**, affecting eligibility at the time of delivery.

## What to Do If Something Goes Wrong

- **Claim denied or partially covered:** Ask the facility's billing or PhilHealth desk for the specific reason, and request a copy of the claim documentation.
- **Facility accreditation status unclear:** Confirm directly with PhilHealth before your due date, rather than relying on a facility's own claim of accreditation.
- **Contribution record shows as inactive or incomplete:** Check your MDR and payment history through the Members' Portal, and settle any gaps with PhilHealth as early in the pregnancy as possible.
