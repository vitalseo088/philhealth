## PhilHealth Maternity Benefit 2026: What's Covered, From Prenatal to Postpartum

Here's the direct answer: the **PhilHealth maternity benefit in 2026** is no longer a single lump-sum package. It has been unbundled into separate components — prenatal (antenatal) care, the delivery itself, postpartum follow-up, and emergency referral if complications arise — each with its own case rate. Delivery coverage ranges from **₱14,000** for a normal delivery at a non-hospital birthing facility up to **₱62,000** for a medically indicated Cesarean section in a hospital, depending on where and how you give birth.

This guide is for PhilHealth members and dependents planning a pregnancy or already expecting in **2026**, and pulls together the full maternity benefit picture from two official 2026 issuances: **PhilHealth Circular No. 2026-0005** (hospital-based maternal and gynecologic care) and **PhilHealth Circular No. 2026-0006** (non-hospital and outpatient maternity care). Both circulars replaced the older, single Maternity Care Package (MCP) system.

> **Important limitation:** Because the benefit is now split across two circulars and multiple facility types, a single "PhilHealth maternity benefit amount" doesn't exist anymore — the correct figure depends on where you deliver (hospital vs. birthing facility), whether the delivery is vaginal or Cesarean, and which specific service (prenatal visit, delivery, postpartum visit) you're asking about. This guide breaks down each piece separately rather than quoting one number.

## What Is the PhilHealth Maternity Benefit?

The PhilHealth maternity benefit refers to the set of case rates PhilHealth pays accredited health facilities for maternal care — from prenatal check-ups through delivery to postpartum follow-up. A **case rate** is a pre-set, fixed amount PhilHealth pays the facility for a covered service; it is generally deducted directly from the patient's bill rather than reimbursed as cash.

In 2026, this benefit is structured around what PhilHealth itself calls the **continuum of maternal care**: antenatal care (ANC), intrapartum care (delivery itself), postpartum care (PoPC), and emergency stabilization/referral if something goes wrong during labor. This structure came from **PhilHealth Circular No. 2026-0006** (*Expansion of Maternity Care Benefits for Non-Hospital and Outpatient Facilities*), which explicitly "unbundled" the previous Maternity Care Package into these separate components, while **PhilHealth Circular No. 2026-0005** (*Rationalization of Case Rates for Select Maternal and Gynecologic Services in the Hospital Setting*) set the current rates for hospital-based delivery, including Cesarean sections.

## Who Is Eligible?

Per PhilHealth Circular No. 2026-0006, **"All pregnant persons enrolled in the National Health Insurance Program (NHIP) shall be immediately eligible to avail of PhilHealth benefits, in accordance with the Universal Health Care Act (UHC Act),"** citing PhilHealth Circular No. 2022-0013 (Granting of Immediate Eligibility to Filipino Citizens). The same circular notes that pregnant persons below 21 years old and their infants remain eligible under existing PhilHealth membership and eligibility policies.

PhilHealth Circular No. 2026-0005 states more generally that **"All Filipinos are automatically entitled to avail of this benefits package,"** while beneficiaries must still "comply with the existing membership eligibility rules."

In practice:

- You (or the member you're a dependent of) need an active PhilHealth membership.
- Care must be received at a **PhilHealth-accredited facility** — a hospital, birthing home, lying-in clinic, infirmary, or primary care facility, depending on the service.
- A printed Member Data Record (MDR) is not required for hospital-based delivery claims; facilities are expected to verify eligibility electronically.

**On a specific contribution-count requirement:** neither governing circular restates a fixed number of monthly contributions required within a set period for this benefit. Other online sources cite varying, conflicting figures. Because this point is not confirmed in the circulars themselves, verify your own contribution and eligibility status directly with PhilHealth before your due date rather than relying on an unconfirmed number.

## Prenatal (Antenatal) Care Benefits

PhilHealth Circular No. 2026-0006 requires and covers **at least eight antenatal visits** across pregnancy, distributed as:

| Trimester | Weeks of Gestation | Minimum Visits | Benefit Code | Rate (₱) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1st | 1–13 weeks | 1 | ANV01 | 1,100 |
| 2nd | 14–27 weeks | 2 | ANV02 | 1,500 |
| 3rd | 28–40 weeks | 5 | ANV03 | 3,750 |

Each trimester's visits are claimed as a bundle at the end of that trimester, not per individual visit. Mandatory services at each visit include consultation, physical examination, supplement provision (iron and folic acid, or multiple micronutrient supplementation), and — depending on the trimester — fetal growth monitoring and tetanus vaccination where indicated.

**Tetanus vaccination** is covered separately: ₱150 for the first dose (TDAP1, given 20–36 weeks) and ₱150 for a second dose if warranted (TDAP2, 27–36 weeks).

**Diagnostic antenatal tests** — including blood typing, complete blood count, urinalysis, Hepatitis B screening, HIV screening, syphilis screening, oral glucose tolerance test, and up to two ultrasounds (one transvaginal in the first trimester, one pelvic in the second or third) — are covered as bundled diagnostic packages, filed separately by the diagnostic facility that performs them.

## Delivery Benefits: Normal Delivery vs. Cesarean Section

### Normal (Vaginal) Delivery

| Setting | Benefit Code | Rate (₱) |
|---|---|---|
| Hospital (Level 1–3) | NSD01 | 29,000 |
| Non-hospital birthing facility (birthing home, lying-in clinic, infirmary, primary care facility) | NSDBF | 14,000 |

Only **low-risk** pregnancies are eligible for delivery — and PhilHealth reimbursement — at a non-hospital birthing facility. If complications develop during labor requiring a step-up referral to a hospital, the referring facility can separately claim an Emergency Stabilization and Step-Up Referral benefit (ranging from ₱1,600 to ₱14,700 depending on the stage of labor at referral).

### Cesarean Section

| Procedure | Benefit Code | Rate (₱) |
|---|---|---|
| Primary Cesarean Section | 59513 / 59514 | 58,000 |
| Cesarean delivery following a failed attempted vaginal delivery after a previous Cesarean (TOLAC/VBAC) | 59620 | 62,000 |

Cesarean coverage applies only to procedures that are **"obstetrically and medically indicated,"** properly documented with the correct ICD-10 code in line with recognized Clinical Practice Guidelines. Claims are subject to monitoring and utilization review, and non-compliant documentation can result in a claim being returned to the hospital.

## Co-Payment Rules

For hospital-based delivery under PC No. 2026-0005, PhilHealth enforces a **"no co-payment" policy** for basic/ward accommodation — meaning an accredited hospital cannot bill a patient in excess of the case rate for services already included in the package, if the patient stays in a shared ward-type bed. For semi-private accommodation, a co-payment cap applies (₱23,000 for normal delivery); private/suite accommodation and costs beyond the standard package are treated as out-of-pocket. The same no-co-payment principle for essential services and basic accommodation is echoed in PC No. 2026-0006 for non-hospital and outpatient maternity care, with a note that additional visits beyond the required eight (in private ANC settings) may carry a capped co-payment of up to ₱900 per pregnancy.

## Postpartum Care

After delivery, **three postpartum visits within six weeks** are covered under benefit code PPC01, at a combined rate of **₱1,350**, covering supplement provision, family planning counseling, lactation and nutrition guidance, and mental health screening and support.

## What About Newborn Care?

PhilHealth Circular No. 2026-0006 explicitly states that **newborn care benefits will be issued under a separate PhilHealth Circular** and are not detailed in the two maternity circulars this guide is based on. If you're looking for the specific newborn care package amount, treat any figure you see elsewhere as unconfirmed until that separate issuance is verified directly with PhilHealth.

## Step-by-Step: How the Maternity Benefit Is Applied

1. **Enroll with or visit a PhilHealth-accredited antenatal care provider** early in pregnancy, ideally registering with a YAKAP primary care clinic for smoother referral.
2. **Complete your antenatal visit schedule** across all three trimesters, keeping your Antenatal Care Record (or Mother-Baby Book) updated at every visit.
3. **Plan your delivery setting** with your care team — a hospital or a non-hospital birthing facility — based on whether your pregnancy is assessed as low-risk or high-risk.
4. **Deliver at the accredited facility.** The facility verifies your PhilHealth eligibility and documents the case appropriately (including ICD-10 coding for Cesarean cases).
5. **The applicable case rate is deducted from your Statement of Account (SOA)** at discharge.
6. **Attend your three postpartum visits within six weeks of delivery** to complete the covered continuum of care.
7. **The facility files each claim electronically** — you generally do not file claims yourself except in specific exceptions (confinement abroad, or emergency care at a non-accredited facility).

## Common Problems and What to Do

**A hospital tried to bill you for something in a basic/ward bed that should be covered.**
This may be a no-balance-billing violation. Report it to PhilHealth's Corporate Action Center at (02) 8662-2588 or actioncenter@philhealth.gov.ph.

**You paid out of pocket for medicines or supplies that should have been part of the package.**
You're entitled to a full refund from the facility for items that should have been included. Keep your official receipts as proof.

**Your birthing home says you now need to transfer to a hospital.**
This is expected clinical practice, not a denial of coverage — only low-risk deliveries are reimbursable at non-hospital birthing facilities, and the referring facility separately claims the appropriate step-up referral benefit.

**You're unsure about your contribution eligibility.**
Because the specific contribution-count rule isn't restated in either governing circular and other sources disagree, confirm your status directly with PhilHealth before your due date.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How much is the PhilHealth maternity benefit in 2026?**
There isn't one single figure — it depends on the service and setting. Delivery alone ranges from ₱14,000 (non-hospital normal delivery) to ₱62,000 (Cesarean after a failed VBAC attempt in a hospital), on top of separate antenatal and postpartum benefit amounts.

**Is the old Maternity Care Package (MCP) still used?**
No. The MCP has been unbundled into separate antenatal care, intrapartum (delivery), postpartum care, and emergency referral components under PhilHealth Circular No. 2026-0006.

**Do I need a certain number of PhilHealth contributions to qualify?**
The governing circulars for this benefit do not restate a specific contribution-count requirement; general PhilHealth membership eligibility rules apply. Confirm your specific status with PhilHealth directly.

**Does PhilHealth cover my entire hospital bill for delivery?**
Not necessarily. Coverage depends on the applicable benefit package and eligibility requirements — the case rate is deducted from your bill, and whether that fully offsets your total charges depends on your facility's actual costs and your accommodation type.

**Can I deliver at home and still get PhilHealth coverage?**
Based on the sources reviewed, PhilHealth accreditation applies to specific facility types (hospitals, birthing homes, lying-in clinics, infirmaries, primary care facilities); a purely at-home delivery outside an accredited facility setup was not addressed in these circulars as a covered scenario.

**Is the newborn care benefit included in these amounts?**
No — newborn care is covered under a separate PhilHealth issuance not detailed in the two circulars this guide is based on.
