## PhilHealth GAMOT Pharmacy: How to Find One and What to Bring in 2026

Here's the direct answer: PhilHealth publishes an **official, downloadable list of accredited GAMOT Package Providers** — the pharmacies and drug dispensing facilities where YAKAP members can claim prescribed medicines free of charge. You can find it at PhilHealth's Accredited Health Facilities page, under "GAMOT Package Providers." Bring a **government-issued ID** and a **prescription from a YAKAP or PhilHealth-certified physician**, and covered medicines are dispensed with no out-of-pocket cost, within an annual benefit of up to **₱20,000**.

This guide is for PhilHealth members in **2026** who want to know where to actually go, how to confirm a drugstore is currently accredited, and what happens when a pharmacy turns them away.

> **Provider information can change. Check the official PhilHealth provider directory before visiting.** The accredited list is a snapshot with a stated "updated as of" date, and each facility carries its own accreditation expiry date. Facilities can also temporarily pause GAMOT dispensing or withdraw from the program entirely. This guide deliberately does not name individual pharmacies or reproduce their contact details — those come from the official list, which is the only version current enough to rely on.

## What Counts as a "GAMOT Pharmacy"?

PhilHealth's official term is **GAMOT Facility** — defined in PhilHealth Circular No. 2026-0002 as a **drug dispensing facility engaged by PhilHealth** to provide the contents of the outpatient drug benefit package. A drug dispensing facility, in turn, is any establishment in the retail business of selling drugs and medicines, holding a **License to Operate (LTO) as a drug retailer from the FDA or the DOH**.

In practice, this means the accredited list includes more than just chain drugstores: independent pharmacies, general merchandise stores with pharmacy operations, medical centers, and hospital-affiliated facilities all appear as accredited GAMOT Package Providers, depending on the area.

**PhilHealth GAMOT** (Guaranteed and Accessible Medications for Outpatient Treatment) is the outpatient drug benefit itself — a component of the broader **YAKAP (Yaman ng Kalusugan Program)** — covering 75 medicines for conditions such as infections, asthma, COPD, diabetes, high cholesterol, high blood pressure, heart conditions, and nervous system disorders.

## Where Some Medicines Are Available: Health Center vs. GAMOT Pharmacy

This is a distinction many members miss, and it explains why a pharmacy might not stock what you need.

The GAMOT List of 75 medicines is effectively split in practice. Based on how PhilHealth's Baguio Local Health Insurance Office described its local rollout in May 2026, the **21 basic medicines** carried over from the older Konsulta package are available directly at accredited health centers/YAKAP clinics, while the **additional 45 medicines** — covering more specific treatments for chronic diseases and other listed conditions — are available **only at accredited GAMOT facilities** (the partner pharmacies).

| Medicine Group | Typically Available At |
|---|---|
| The original 21 basic medicines (from Konsulta) | Accredited health centers / YAKAP clinics |
| The additional 45 medicines | Accredited GAMOT facilities (partner pharmacies) |

**A caution on this split:** the above reflects how one PhilHealth Local Health Insurance Office described the arrangement in its own area. Local implementation can vary, so treat this as a useful general pattern rather than a nationwide rule, and confirm with your own YAKAP clinic or PhilHealth office which medicines are dispensed where in your area.

## How to Find an Accredited GAMOT Pharmacy

1. **Go to PhilHealth's Accredited Health Facilities page** (philhealth.gov.ph → Partners → Providers → Facilities → Accredited).
2. **Open the "GAMOT Package Providers" entry** — this is a downloadable PDF directory of accredited facilities.
3. **Check the "updated as of" date** at the top of the list before relying on it.
4. **Find your municipality or city** — the list is organized by region and municipality.
5. **Check the accreditation expiry date** shown for that facility. A facility whose accreditation has lapsed since the list was compiled would no longer be active.
6. **Call ahead** using the contact number in the official listing, to confirm the branch is currently dispensing and has your prescribed medicine in stock.

Separately, PhilHealth has said signages and mini-billboards would be installed at YAKAP and GAMOT facilities so the public can more easily identify accredited locations in person.

## What to Bring and What Happens at the Counter

### Before You Go

- **Register as a YAKAP beneficiary** — through the eGovPH app, the PhilHealth member online portal, at PhilHealth offices, or other available channels. GAMOT sits inside YAKAP, so this registration is the entry point.
- **See a YAKAP or PhilHealth-certified physician** and get a GAMOT prescription. Medicines are released against a valid prescription, not on request.

### At the Pharmacy

- **Present one government-issued ID and your prescription.**
- Covered medicines are dispensed and charged directly to PhilHealth — accredited facilities commit to dispensing them **"with no out-of-pocket expenses on the part of the qualified PhilHealth Member and their dependents,"** per the Performance Commitment every GAMOT facility signs.
- **Expect to sign a GAMOT Availment Slip.** Facilities are required to ensure the true signatures of both the pharmacist and the PhilHealth member appear on the slip for every transaction.

**On prescription security codes:** some GAMOT-participating pharmacies publicly describe GAMOT prescriptions from YAKAP doctors as carrying a **Unique Prescription Security Code (UPSC)**. This detail comes from a participating provider's own materials rather than from the PhilHealth circulars reviewed for this article, so treat it as how some facilities describe the process rather than as a confirmed universal requirement — your prescribing physician or the facility can tell you what applies to your prescription.

PhilHealth coverage depends on the applicable benefit package and eligibility requirements. GAMOT covers medicines on the official GAMOT List within the annual limit — not every medicine a physician may prescribe.

## Why a GAMOT Pharmacy Might Turn You Away

Under PhilHealth Circular No. 2026-0002, an accredited GAMOT facility can **temporarily deactivate** its participation — pausing GAMOT dispensing — but only under narrow conditions: when PhilHealth payment for **valid and compliant claims is delayed beyond 60 calendar days**, and the delay isn't the facility's own fault (not caused by incomplete documentation, claims under investigation, claims returned for correction, or administrative holds from the facility's non-compliance).

The circular frames deactivation as **"a last resort, with due consideration primarily for member welfare,"** and surrounds it with member protections:

| Protection | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Advance notice to PhilHealth | 15 calendar days before deactivation |
| Notices posted at the facility | At least 7 calendar days before the effective date |
| PhilHealth Member Advisory | Issued within 24 hours of the effective date |
| Written referral information | Alternative GAMOT facilities, with name, address, contact, operating hours, and distance/transport options |
| Non-GAMOT prescriptions | The facility must continue accepting and filling prescriptions for medicines **not** on the GAMOT List |
| Resuming service | Within 24 hours of receiving payment, whether partial or full |

PhilHealth is also required to maintain a member support help desk during deactivations, navigate affected members to alternatives, post advisories on its website and social media, and update the list of active GAMOT facilities in its systems.

**Voluntary withdrawal is different.** A facility can also permanently leave the program on 15 days' written notice — and rejoining afterward requires a new application and full compliance with accreditation requirements, not automatic reactivation.

## Common Problems and What to Do

**The pharmacy on the official list says it no longer does GAMOT.**
Check the accreditation expiry date on the list and the "updated as of" date — the entry may have lapsed. If the facility has temporarily deactivated, it should be posting notices and giving you written referral details to alternative GAMOT facilities nearby.

**You were asked to pay for a medicine that should have been free.**
Accredited facilities commit to no out-of-pocket cost for covered GAMOT medicines within program rules and your annual limit. Raise it with the facility first; if unresolved, contact PhilHealth's Corporate Action Center at (02) 8662-2588 or actioncenter@philhealth.gov.ph.

**The pharmacy doesn't stock your prescribed medicine.**
Stock availability at a given branch is a practical matter, not a coverage question. Call ahead using the contact number in the official directory, or ask about another accredited facility in your area.

**Your medicine is on the GAMOT List but the health center doesn't have it.**
This may reflect the practical split described above — the additional medicines beyond the original 21 are generally dispensed at accredited GAMOT pharmacies rather than at health centers. Ask your YAKAP clinic where the specific medicine is dispensed in your area.

**You want to know how much of your ₱20,000 is left.**
GAMOT facilities are required to maintain a registry of member availments and regularly update the amount deducted from each member's benefit, so the facility's record is one reference point; PhilHealth's own member channels are the other.

**Your prescription was refused.**
Confirm two things: that the prescribing physician is YAKAP or PhilHealth-certified, and that the specific medicine is on the official GAMOT List. Medicines outside the list aren't covered by this benefit even at an accredited pharmacy.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Where can I see the official list of PhilHealth GAMOT pharmacies?**
On PhilHealth's Accredited Health Facilities page, under "GAMOT Package Providers" — a downloadable directory that includes each facility's name, contact details, address, municipality, and accreditation expiry date.

**Is the list of accredited pharmacies complete and current?**
It's published with an "updated as of" date and per-facility expiry dates, and PhilHealth updates its systems as facilities deactivate, reactivate, or withdraw. Always check the date on the version you're viewing, and call ahead.

**How many pharmacies are accredited nationwide?**
The count has grown over the program's phased rollout and changes over time, so any specific figure goes stale quickly. The official directory is the reliable reference for whether a given facility is currently listed.

**Do I pay anything at a GAMOT pharmacy?**
Accredited facilities commit to dispensing covered GAMOT medicines with no out-of-pocket expense to qualified members, within the program's rules and the ₱20,000 annual limit.

**Can I just walk into any drugstore and ask for free medicine?**
No. The pharmacy must be an accredited GAMOT facility, you must be a registered YAKAP beneficiary, and you need a valid prescription from a YAKAP or PhilHealth-certified physician.

**Are all 75 medicines available at every accredited pharmacy?**
Not necessarily — stock varies by facility, and in at least some areas the original 21 basic medicines are dispensed at health centers while the additional 45 are dispensed at accredited GAMOT pharmacies. Confirm locally.

**What if there's no accredited GAMOT pharmacy near me?**
Coverage under the program is tied to accredited facilities, and rollout has been phased. Your local PhilHealth office or YAKAP clinic is the right place to ask about the nearest access point in your area.
