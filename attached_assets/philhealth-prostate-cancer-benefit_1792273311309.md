# PhilHealth Prostate Cancer Benefit (2026): The ₱100,000 Z Package, Eligibility, and How to Avail

The PhilHealth prostate cancer benefit is a **Z Benefit package worth ₱100,000** for men with *localized, low-to-intermediate-risk* prostate cancer who are **70 years old or younger**. It is paid by PhilHealth directly to a hospital that PhilHealth has contracted for this package, and it needs an approved **pre-authorization before treatment starts**. As of this review (October 2026), the official PhilHealth benefits page and PhilHealth's June 2026 public statement both still show ₱100,000.

This guide is for patients, spouses and family members in the Philippines who are trying to understand that benefit. It explains the rules in plain language. It does not tell you which treatment is right for you. That decision belongs to you and your doctors.

> **Important limits up front:** The ₱100,000 package covers a narrow group of patients. If the cancer is advanced, if the patient is over 70, or if the hospital is not contracted for this package, the Z Benefit may not apply. Other PhilHealth benefits may still help, and those are covered further below.

## What Is the PhilHealth Prostate Cancer Benefit?

PhilHealth runs two broad ways of paying for hospital care.

**1. Regular inpatient benefits (All Case Rates).** PhilHealth sets a fixed amount for a condition or procedure. The accredited hospital deducts that amount from your total bill, including the attending doctors' professional fees. PhilHealth's benefits page describes this system.

**2. Z Benefits.** These are packages for serious illnesses that need long or expensive treatment. PhilHealth says their purpose is financial risk protection. Prostate cancer (low to intermediate risk) is one of the listed Z Benefit conditions. Z Benefits work differently from a regular case rate in three ways:

- The package is a larger fixed amount for the *whole course of care*, not a single procedure.
- The hospital must be **contracted** by PhilHealth for that package.
- PhilHealth must **approve a pre-authorization** before the benefit is used.

The prostate package was one of the first Z Benefits. The original circular took effect for approved pre-authorizations starting June 21, 2012, and prostate cancer has carried the code **Z003** since then.

PhilHealth's data on cancer is not the only reason this matters. According to Department of Health figures reported by the Philippine News Agency in 2026, prostate cancer is the fifth most common cancer in the country and the third most common among Filipino men.

## Who Is Eligible for the Prostate Cancer Z Benefit?

There are two layers to eligibility: the **medical criteria** and your **PhilHealth membership status**.

### Medical criteria

PhilHealth's benefits page lists these selection criteria for the prostate cancer package:

| Criterion | What PhilHealth lists |
|---|---|
| Signed form | A signed **Member Empowerment (ME) Form** |
| Sex and age | Male patients, age up to 70 years old |
| Cancer extent | Localized prostate cancer (the cancer has not spread beyond the prostate) |
| Other health conditions | No uncontrolled co-morbid conditions |
| Stage, grade and PSA | Clinical stage T1a–T2c, Gleason score 2–7, and PSA as set out in the risk table below |

**Risk groups as PhilHealth defines them:**

| Risk group | Clinical stage | Gleason score | PSA |
|---|---|---|---|
| Low risk | T1–T2a | 2–6 | below 10 ng/ml |
| Intermediate risk | T2b–T2c | 7 | 10–20 ng/ml |

*Plain-language notes:* "Clinical stage" (the T number) describes how far the tumor appears to extend. The "Gleason score" grades how abnormal the cancer cells look under a microscope after a biopsy. "PSA" is a blood-test marker. Your doctors work out these numbers. PhilHealth reviews them through the pre-authorization.

Two further rules come from the original 2012 circular and the pre-authorization checklist:

- **Newly diagnosed cases only.** The 2012 circular covers newly diagnosed prostate cancer.
- **No earlier radiotherapy.** The official pre-authorization checklist lists "no previous radiotherapy for prostate cancer" as a qualification.

> **A discrepancy to note.** The official pre-authorization checklist (Annex A, revised July 2019), in the copy accessed for this review, shows a diagnostics line reading T1a–T3c and Gleason 6–9. That does not match the T1a–T2c and Gleason 2–7 shown on the benefits page and in the 2012 circular. The copy was text-extracted, so it is unclear how that line is used on the form. Treat the benefits page as the headline criteria, and ask the hospital's Z Benefits Coordinator which checklist version they are using.

### Membership status

PhilHealth's 2021 guide says all PhilHealth members and their qualified dependents can use a Z Benefit if they pass the criteria for that package. A man who is a dependent (for example, a member's husband or father who qualifies as a dependent) is also handled on the pre-authorization form.

The hospital checks eligibility with a **PhilHealth Benefit Eligibility Form (PBEF)**. If the PBEF says "Yes," the patient is eligible. If it says "No," the member needs to update their PhilHealth information, and the hospital may ask for the Member Data Record (MDR), proof of contributions or a Claim Form 1.

The member also needs **at least one day left** of the 45-day annual hospital benefit limit before enrolling, according to the 2021 guide.

*What the sources do not settle:* The 2012 circular originally required three months of contributions within the six months before the pre-authorization request. The newer 2021 guide relies on the PBEF instead. The available official sources do not clearly say whether the 2012 three-of-six-months rule still applies in 2026. Ask the hospital or PhilHealth to confirm for your membership category.

## What the ₱100,000 Package Covers

PhilHealth's June 2026 statement, as reported by the Philippine News Agency, says the package covers room and board, drugs, laboratory examinations, use of the operating room and professional fees for the patient's entire course of treatment.

The 2012 circular gives the clinical detail. In summary, it lists:

- **Required services:** PSA test, chest X-ray, bone scan and CT scan of the pelvis and abdomen as indicated, other laboratory tests (creatinine, blood sugar, calcium, liver enzymes, blood count, electrolytes, ECG, cholesterol panel), clearance for surgery, and surgery. Surgery listed is radical prostatectomy with orchiectomy, or laparoscopic prostatectomy with orchiectomy.
- **Other services "when indicated":** radiation therapy, further diagnostics such as abdominal ultrasound and core needle biopsy, and anti-androgen drugs (the circular names goserelin, bicalutamide, flutamide and leuprorelin).
- **Complications:** The 2012 circular says complications arising during the hospital stay for the Z condition are part of the package. Confinements for other causes are paid separately.
- **Payment:** The package is ₱100,000 for the complete course of care, released in full upon discharge, according to the 2012 circular.

*Caution:* That list comes from a 2012 document. PhilHealth may have updated clinical protocols since then. The hospital's Z Benefits Coordinator should tell you what services are included for your treatment plan.

**What the package does not do:**

- It does not pay cash to the patient.
- It is not for advanced or metastatic cancer. The criteria say *localized* and *low to intermediate risk*.
- It does not by itself remove every possible charge. See the costs section below.

## How Much Will You Still Pay?

The amount you pay depends on your PhilHealth category and the agreement you sign in the ME Form.

| Situation | What the official guidance says |
|---|---|
| Senior citizen, lifetime member, indigent, sponsored member or kasambahay (household help) | Z Benefits are covered by the **No Balance Billing** policy. The member should not have to pay (PC 2021-0022 guide). |
| Other members | The PhilHealth benefit is deducted from the hospital bill. A member can be charged extra only for **additional services or upgrades** that were explained and agreed in advance. Extra charges must not exceed the Z Benefit amount set in the hospital's contract (PC 2021-0022 guide). |

Signing the ME Form is how you confirm that you understood the treatment plan and any agreed extra charges.

**What I could not verify:** PhilHealth's newer breast cancer Z circular says no co-payment is charged to patients in basic or ward accommodation, and PhilHealth publicly urged hospitals in January 2025 to follow the no co-payment policy. The sources reviewed do not state clearly how that applies to the prostate package specifically. Ask the hospital for the co-payment terms in writing before treatment.

## Step-by-Step: How to Avail of the Benefit

1. **Get your diagnosis and risk classification from your doctors.** Ask directly whether your case fits the PhilHealth Z package criteria (localized, low to intermediate risk, age 70 or below).
2. **Check your PhilHealth record.** Have your PhilHealth Identification Number (PIN) ready. If your records or contributions are out of date, update them early so the PBEF does not come back "No."
3. **Pick a hospital contracted for the prostate cancer package.** Check the official list on the PhilHealth website (see the provider note below). Being on the Z list for some *other* package is not enough. Confirm the hospital is contracted for prostate cancer.
4. **Meet the hospital's Z Benefits Coordinator.** Every contracted hospital has at least one. The coordinator guides you, gives you information and talks to PhilHealth about your pre-authorization.
5. **Complete the Member Empowerment (ME) Form.** You and your attending doctor sign it. It documents your treatment plan, schedule and any agreed extra charges.
6. **The hospital submits the pre-authorization.** The hospital sends the original pre-authorization checklist and request, plus a photocopy of the ME Form, to the PhilHealth Regional Office. PhilHealth reviews the request; ask the coordinator for the expected turnaround.
7. **Wait for approval before treatment.** PhilHealth's 2026 statement stresses that pre-authorization must be approved *before surgery or operation*.
8. **Start treatment within the validity period.** An approved pre-authorization has a validity window, so ask the coordinator when yours lapses.
9. **The hospital files the claim.** Members do not file Z Benefit claims themselves. PhilHealth pays the contracted hospital directly.

### Documents You May Need

| Document | Who prepares it | Required or situational |
|---|---|---|
| Pre-authorization checklist and request (prostate cancer) | Hospital | Required (hospital submits the original) |
| Member Empowerment (ME) Form | Hospital and patient | Required (photocopy is submitted) |
| PhilHealth Benefit Eligibility Form (PBEF) | Hospital | Proof of eligibility. A "Yes" means the patient may use the benefit |
| PhilHealth number / MDR | Patient | Keep ready |
| Proof of contributions, MDR or Claim Form 1 | Patient | **May be required only if the PBEF says "No"** |
| Biopsy, PSA and imaging results | Hospital chart | The official form says results need not be attached but must be in the chart, as PhilHealth may check them during monitoring |

Other hospital-specific paperwork may apply. Ask the Z Benefits Coordinator for the full checklist.

## If You Do Not Qualify for the Z Package

PhilHealth's own guidance says patients who do not qualify at pre-authorization can still use other PhilHealth benefits under a different payment scheme, such as case rates. The benefits page explains that inpatient benefits are paid to accredited hospitals through All Case Rates and deducted from the bill, including the doctors' professional fees. The documents listed there are the Member Data Record or PBEF, plus a duly accomplished PhilHealth Claim Form 1.

Things to know:

- **I did not find a verified, prostate-specific case rate** for prostate surgery or radiotherapy outside the Z package, so this article does not quote one. Ask the hospital which case-rate code applies to the planned procedure. PhilHealth has a Case Rates Search portal for this purpose.
- **Chemotherapy administration.** An annex to a 2024 PhilHealth circular lists chemotherapy administration (code 96408) at ₱7,280 per case rate, with a note referring to chemotherapy sessions for liver, lung, ovarian and prostate cancer. PhilHealth then raised most case rates by 50% for admissions from January 1, 2025. I could not confirm the current 2026 figure for this code, so check with the hospital.
- **Case rates usually do not cover the whole bill.** That is how a fixed rate works, so ask for an estimate of what remains.

## Does PhilHealth Cover Prostate Cancer Screening?

Based on the official circular, **not through the outpatient cancer screening package.**

PhilHealth Circular 2025-0014 lists these covered outpatient screening tests: mammogram, breast ultrasound, low-dose chest CT scan, alpha fetoprotein, liver ultrasound, and colonoscopy used as a screening test. Prostate screening is not on that list.

The sources reviewed do not state whether a PSA blood test is available under the YAKAP primary care laboratory benefit. If you want to know, ask a PhilHealth-accredited YAKAP clinic directly. Whether and when to be screened is a decision for you and your doctor.

## Common Problems

- **The PBEF says "No."** This usually points to a membership record or contribution issue. Update your information and bring the documents the hospital asks for.
- **The hospital is not contracted for this package.** Only contracted hospitals can file Z claims. Ask for a referral to one that is.
- **Treatment started before approval.** PhilHealth requires pre-authorization before Z services. Do not agree to start Z-covered treatment until the hospital confirms approval.
- **The pre-authorization expired.** Approvals have a validity window. Ask the coordinator when yours was approved and when it lapses.
- **Your numbers fall outside the criteria.** If stage, Gleason score or PSA fall outside the listed ranges, or you have uncontrolled co-morbid conditions, the pre-authorization may be disapproved.
- **Unexpected extra charges.** Ask for the agreed amounts in the ME Form and for a written statement of account.
- **Old or conflicting numbers online.** Some websites list different amounts or ranges for this package. The official PhilHealth benefits page lists ₱100,000. Do not rely on a third-party figure without checking.

## What to Do If Something Goes Wrong

1. **Ask the Z Benefits Coordinator for the reason.** The official pre-authorization form has a space for PhilHealth to state the reasons if a request is disapproved.
2. **Ask whether another benefit applies.** PhilHealth guidance says non-qualifying patients can still use case rates.
3. **Ask for copies** of your signed ME Form and the approved pre-authorization.
4. **Contact PhilHealth.** Recent PhilHealth circulars list the main line as (02) 8662-2588. The PhilHealth Action Center email on older PhilHealth letterheads is actioncenter@philhealth.gov.ph. Contact details can change, so confirm on philhealth.gov.ph or at your nearest PhilHealth office or Local Health Insurance Office.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How much does PhilHealth pay for prostate cancer?
For eligible patients, the Z package pays ₱100,000 for the entire course of care. The official benefits page lists this amount.

### Is the amount the same as the breast cancer package?
No. PhilHealth raised the breast cancer Z package to ₱1.4 million (the breast cancer circular took effect March 30, 2024). The official benefits page still lists prostate cancer at ₱100,000.

### Does PhilHealth give me the ₱100,000 in cash?
No. PhilHealth pays the contracted hospital directly, and the benefit is deducted from the patient's bill.

### Can I use my PhilHealth at any hospital for this?
For the Z package, no. Only hospitals contracted for it can file the claim. For regular case rates, any PhilHealth-accredited hospital can be used.

### What if I am older than 70?
The Z package criteria say age up to 70. Regular PhilHealth case rates may still apply, but this article could not verify a specific prostate rate for you.

### What if my cancer is advanced?
The Z package is for localized, low-to-intermediate-risk cancer. The sources reviewed did not identify a separate PhilHealth package for advanced or metastatic prostate cancer. Ask the hospital which benefits apply.

### Do I need approval before surgery?
Yes. The hospital must get the Z Benefit pre-authorization approved before the services are given. PhilHealth's 2026 statement says this applies before surgery.

### How long does pre-authorization take?
This article could not verify a current official processing time or validity period. Ask the hospital's Z Benefits Coordinator for both.

### Will I have to pay anything?
It depends on your category. Senior citizens, lifetime members, indigent, sponsored members and kasambahay are covered by No Balance Billing under the 2021 guide. Other members may pay for extra services or upgrades that were agreed in advance.

### Does PhilHealth cover prostate cancer screening?
The outpatient cancer screening circular (PC 2025-0014) does not list prostate screening. Ask a YAKAP clinic whether PSA testing is available under the primary care package.

### Has the benefit changed for 2026?
No official change was found. The benefits page and PhilHealth's June 2026 statement both show ₱100,000. PhilHealth circulars note the Cancer Control Act directs PhilHealth to expand cancer coverage over time, so watch PhilHealth's website for new issuances.
