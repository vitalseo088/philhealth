## PhilHealth Employee and Employer Contribution 2026: The Complete Guide

If you're an employed member in the Philippines, your PhilHealth contribution is a shared legal obligation between you and your employer. For **2026**, the total premium contribution rate is **5.0% of your monthly basic salary**, split exactly **50/50**:

- **Employee Share:** **2.5%** deducted directly from your monthly basic salary via payroll
- **Employer Share:** **2.5%** paid by your company as its mandatory counterpart contribution

This guide breaks down how the employee and employer shares are computed, how the income floor and ceiling apply, the special rules for Kasambahays, employer remittance deadlines through the Electronic Premium Remittance System (EPRS), and what steps to take if your company fails to remit your contributions.

| Remittance Deadline | 10th to 15th of the following month | Depends on the employer's PhilHealth Employer Number (PEN) |

## The Contribution Formula for Employed Members

**Formula:**

> Total Monthly Premium = Monthly Basic Salary × 5%
> Employee Share = Monthly Basic Salary × 2.5%
> Employer Share = Monthly Basic Salary × 2.5%

### What Counts as "Monthly Basic Salary"?

For PhilHealth computation purposes, the basis is your **monthly basic salary** — your fixed contractual pay before statutory deductions:

- **Included:** Basic pay for regular work hours.
- **Excluded:** Overtime pay, night shift differential, holiday pay, discretionary allowances (meal, transportation, housing), performance bonuses, commissions, and the statutory 13th month pay.

If an employee receives an hourly or daily wage, the monthly equivalent is computed based on standard Department of Labor and Employment (DOLE) payroll factors (e.g., 261 days for office workers or 313 days for 6-day operations).

## Understanding the Floor and Ceiling

1. **The ₱10,000 Income Floor:** Even if an employee works part-time or earns less than ₱10,000 basic salary per month, PhilHealth pegs the computation base at ₱10,000. The employee pays ₱250 and the employer pays ₱250.
2. **The ₱100,000 Income Ceiling:** For executives, managers, and high earners making ₱100,000, ₱150,000, or ₱300,000 a month, the contribution is capped at the ₱100,000 ceiling. The employee deduction is capped at ₱2,500.00 per month, and the employer counterpart is capped at ₱2,500.00 per month.

## Special Rules for Kasambahays (Domestic Workers)

Under Republic Act No. 10361 (Batas Kasambahay) and PhilHealth guidelines:

- If the Kasambahay's monthly wage is **below ₱5,000**, the household employer must shoulder the **100% full premium** (₱500.00 minimum). No amount is deducted from the Kasambahay.
- If the Kasambahay's monthly wage is **₱5,000 or higher**, the standard 50/50 split applies between the household employer and the domestic worker.

## Employer Remittance Responsibilities: The EPRS Workflow

Employers are legally designated as collecting and remitting agents under the National Health Insurance Act:

1. **Deduction:** The employer deducts the employee's 2.5% share from their salary (usually split across semi-monthly payrolls, e.g. 15th and 30th).
2. **Billing Statement:** By the end of each applicable month, employers generate the preliminary billing statement through the web-based **Electronic Premium Remittance System (EPRS)**.
3. **Payment:** The employer remits both the employee and employer shares to PhilHealth via accredited electronic payment gateways (Link.BizPortal, BancNet, accredited banks).
4. **Posting:** The payment automatically reconciles and posts to each employee's PhilHealth record.

### Remittance Deadlines

Employers must remit PhilHealth contributions according to the last digit of their PhilHealth Employer Number (PEN):

- Private employers typically have until the **10th or 15th day** of the month following the applicable payroll month.
- Government employers remit on or before the **10th day** of the following month.

Late remittances incur a monthly compounded penalty of **1.5% per month** under Section 9 of the UHC Act.

## What to Do If Your Employer Deducted but Did Not Remit

Under Philippine law, employers who deduct PhilHealth contributions from employee wages but fail to remit them commit an offense punishable by fines and imprisonment under RA 11223.

If your contributions are not updated on your PhilHealth Member Portal:

1. **Verify Your Payslips:** Check your payslips to confirm that PhilHealth deductions were taken.
2. **Check Member Portal:** Check your Member Data Record (MDR) and Premium Contribution History online.
3. **Inquire with HR/Payroll:** Ask your company's payroll department for the EPRS transaction confirmation and remittance receipt.
4. **File an Inquiry with PhilHealth:** If the employer refuses to remit or cannot produce receipts, file a formal complaint at any PhilHealth Local Health Insurance Office (LHIO) or email `actioncenter@philhealth.gov.ph`. Under the UHC Act, failure of the employer to remit does not diminish the employee's entitlement to healthcare benefits.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is the employer required to pay 50% of my PhilHealth contribution?**
Yes. For all formally employed workers in private corporations and government agencies, employers are legally required to shoulder half (2.5%) of the total 5.0% contribution.

**Can an employer deduct the full 5% from my salary?**
No. Deducting the employer's share from the employee's salary is illegal. The maximum deduction from your basic salary is 2.5%.

**What if I have two employers?**
Both employers must deduct and remit PhilHealth contributions based on your respective salaries with each employer, up to the individual statutory caps.

**Are overtime and allowances included in PhilHealth computation?**
No. PhilHealth contribution is strictly computed on basic monthly salary. Overtime, night differentials, and allowances are excluded.
