# PhilHealth YAKAP Beneficiaries: Who Qualifies and How to Register (2026 Guide)

A PhilHealth YAKAP beneficiary is either a PhilHealth member or that member's qualified dependent — and both are entitled to the exact same free primary care benefits once they're registered and empaneled with a YAKAP Clinic. This guide explains who counts, who doesn't, and how to get yourself and your family properly enrolled.

This guide is for PhilHealth members trying to understand who in their family can use YAKAP, and for anyone unsure whether they personally qualify. It reflects PhilHealth's current YAKAP framework as governed mainly by PhilHealth Circular No. 2025-0017 (selection and empanelment) and PhilHealth's official qualified-dependents rules. Where PhilHealth's published member-category lists differ slightly across its own pages (for example, between formal-economy, informal-economy, and sponsored-member dependent pages), this guide notes the common elements rather than assuming they're identical for every member type.

## What Does "YAKAP Beneficiary" Mean?

Under PhilHealth Circular No. 2025-0017, which governs selection and empanelment for PhilHealth's Primary Care Benefit Package (YAKAP), a **beneficiary** is defined as any individual — **member or qualified dependent** — who is eligible to avail of PhilHealth benefits under the National Health Insurance Program (NHIP), subject to the applicable registration, coverage, and entitlement rules.

In plain terms, this means two groups of people can be YAKAP beneficiaries:

1. **PhilHealth members** — the principal person enrolled with PhilHealth, under any membership category (employed, self-earning, voluntary, indigent, senior citizen, and so on).
2. **Qualified dependents** — specific family members of the member who are covered under the member's PhilHealth registration without needing to be members themselves.

Both groups access the same core YAKAP services — consultations, laboratory tests, cancer screening referrals, and the medicine benefit — through registration and empanelment with a YAKAP Clinic.

## Who Is Eligible? Members and Qualified Dependents Explained

### PhilHealth Members

Under the Universal Health Care Act (Republic Act No. 11223) and PhilHealth Circular No. 2022-0013, Filipino citizens — whether direct contributors (who pay premiums themselves or through an employer) or indirect contributors (whose premiums are subsidized, such as indigent or sponsored members) — are granted immediate eligibility to PhilHealth benefits. This is the legal basis for why PhilHealth describes YAKAP as being available to every Filipino, not only those who are current on their contributions.

### Qualified Dependents

PhilHealth's official qualified-dependents pages (published separately for formal-economy, informal-economy, and sponsored members, but consistent on the core categories) list the following as qualified dependents of a member:

- **Legitimate spouse**, as long as they are not themselves a PhilHealth member
- **Children** — legitimate, legitimated, acknowledged, illegitimate (as appearing on the birth certificate), adopted, or stepchildren — who are **below 21 years old, unmarried, and unemployed**
- **Children 21 years old or above** who have a congenital or acquired disability, physical or mental, that renders them totally dependent on the member for support, as determined by PhilHealth
- **Foster children**, as defined under the Foster Care Act of 2012 (Republic Act No. 10165)
- **Parents 60 years old or above**, who are not themselves enrolled members, and whose monthly income is below the threshold PhilHealth has set
- **Parents with a permanent disability**, regardless of age, that renders them totally dependent on the member for subsistence

Important: PhilHealth's official pages state that qualified dependents must be **declared by the principal member** and listed under the member's **Member Data Record (MDR)** for hassle-free benefit availment. A relative who is not declared on the MDR is not automatically treated as a qualified dependent, even if they meet the relationship criteria described above.

### Registration Transaction Rules by Age

PhilHealth Circular No. 2025-0017 also sets out who can handle YAKAP transactions directly:

- **Beneficiaries 18 years old and above** may register, select their YAKAP Clinic, execute agreements (such as the Mutual Care Agreement), and deal with PhilHealth directly, without needing parental or guardian consent.
- **Minor beneficiaries** cannot transact on their own since they don't have legal personality under this circular — a parent or legal guardian must handle all official YAKAP transactions on their behalf, including registration, clinic selection, and signing agreements.

## How to Register a Member and Their Dependents as YAKAP Beneficiaries

### Step 1: Confirm an Active PhilHealth PIN for the Member

The principal member needs an active PhilHealth Identification Number (PIN) before anyone — including their dependents — can register with a YAKAP Clinic.

### Step 2: Declare Dependents on the Member Data Record

Before a family member can be treated as a YAKAP beneficiary, they need to be properly declared as a qualified dependent and reflected in the member's Member Data Record, per PhilHealth's official qualified-dependents guidance.

### Step 3: Select a YAKAP Clinic for Each Beneficiary

According to PhilHealth's official YAKAP FAQ, registration channels include:

- The **eGovPH** app
- The **PhilHealth Member Portal**
- Any **PhilHealth office** nationwide
- **Directly at a YAKAP Clinic**

Each beneficiary — the member and every dependent — selects a clinic. They don't have to pick the same one; a dependent can be registered wherever is most convenient for them.

### Step 4: Complete the First Patient Encounter (FPE) for Each Beneficiary

Every beneficiary must individually complete a First Patient Encounter at their chosen clinic. This is where the clinic gathers health history and formally begins the empanelment process.

### Step 5: Complete Empanelment

Per PhilHealth Circular No. 2025-0017, empanelment is what establishes the ongoing care relationship between a beneficiary and their chosen YAKAP Clinic for the year. Until this is completed for a given beneficiary, that person has only expressed a clinic preference — they are not yet entitled to the clinic's ongoing free YAKAP services.

## What Benefits Do YAKAP Beneficiaries Receive?

Once registered and empaneled, each beneficiary — member or dependent — can access, based on clinical need and their doctor's recommendation:

- Consultations at their registered YAKAP Clinic
- Outpatient laboratory tests
- Selected cancer screening tests (for breast, lung, liver, and colorectal cancer, subject to eligibility criteria)
- Free essential medicines, up to a limit per beneficiary

On the medicine benefit specifically, PhilHealth's official FAQ confirms this important detail: the **₱20,000 annual medicine limit applies separately to each beneficiary**. The member and each dependent each have their own ₱20,000 limit — they are not required to share one combined limit as a family.

## Examples

*The following are illustrative examples only, not official case determinations.*

**Example 1:** A member registers themselves and their two unmarried, unemployed children (ages 12 and 19) as YAKAP beneficiaries. All three are declared on the member's Member Data Record and each completes their own First Patient Encounter at the same YAKAP Clinic. Each child now has their own ₱20,000 annual medicine limit, separate from the member's.

**Example 2:** A member's 62-year-old mother, who is not a PhilHealth member herself and has no income above the PhilHealth threshold, is declared as a qualified dependent. She registers at a YAKAP Clinic closer to her own home rather than the member's, since dependents aren't required to use the same clinic.

## Common Problems

- **"My relative isn't recognized as my dependent."** Check whether they were formally declared on your Member Data Record — PhilHealth's official guidance is clear that dependents must be listed there to avail of benefits smoothly.
- **"My spouse is also a PhilHealth member — can I still declare them as my dependent?"** Based on PhilHealth's official dependents criteria, a legitimate spouse qualifies as a dependent only if they are **not themselves a PhilHealth member**. If both spouses are separately registered members, each would typically be their own YAKAP beneficiary rather than one being the other's dependent — confirm your specific situation with PhilHealth.
- **"My adult child with a disability wasn't accepted as a dependent."** PhilHealth requires the disability to be one that PhilHealth determines renders the child totally dependent on the member for support — this determination isn't automatic and may require documentation. Ask your Local Health Insurance Office (LHIO) what's needed.
- **"I registered online but my dependent still can't get free consultations."** Selection alone isn't empanelment — each beneficiary, including dependents, must complete their own in-person First Patient Encounter before ongoing free services apply.

## What to Do If Something Goes Wrong

If you have trouble registering a dependent, verifying eligibility, or accessing benefits as a beneficiary, PhilHealth's official YAKAP FAQ lists these contact channels:

- Call the PhilHealth hotline listed on the official YAKAP FAQ page (open 24/7)
- Text or call the Smart/Globe numbers published on PhilHealth's official YAKAP FAQ
- Email actioncenter@philhealth.gov.ph
- Visit any PhilHealth office or Local Health Insurance Office
- Use the click-to-call feature on the official PhilHealth website

Always verify current contact numbers directly on philhealth.gov.ph, since these can be updated.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is there a difference in YAKAP benefits between a member and a dependent?**
No — the available official sources describe the same core benefits (consultations, lab tests, cancer screening referrals, medicines) applying to both, with each beneficiary having their own separate ₱20,000 annual medicine limit.

**Can grandparents or siblings be registered as my dependents?**
PhilHealth's official qualified-dependents categories cover spouse, children (including those with qualifying disabilities), foster children, and parents meeting specific age or disability conditions. Other relatives, such as grandchildren or siblings, are not listed among these categories in PhilHealth's official guidance reviewed for this article.

**Do all my dependents need to register at the same YAKAP Clinic as me?**
No. Each beneficiary selects their own clinic, and dependents can be registered wherever is most convenient for them.

**What happens if my dependent turns 21?**
PhilHealth's dependent criteria generally require children to be below 21, unmarried, and unemployed to qualify (unless they meet the disability exception). The sources reviewed for this article don't detail the exact transition process once a dependent ages out — confirm with PhilHealth or your LHIO how this affects an existing YAKAP registration.

**Do I need to re-register my dependents every year?**
The official sources reviewed for this article describe empanelment as establishing a care relationship for continuity of care, but don't specify an annual re-registration requirement distinct from the general MDR declaration and empanelment process. Confirm directly with PhilHealth if you're unsure about renewal requirements for a specific beneficiary.
