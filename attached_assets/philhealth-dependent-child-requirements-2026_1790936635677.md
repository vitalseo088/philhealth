## PhilHealth Dependent Child Requirements: The Exact Documents You Need

Here's the direct answer: for most children, declaring them as a PhilHealth dependent needs just **one core document** — a **Birth Certificate** — attached to a PhilHealth Member Registration Form (PMRF). But the exact requirement shifts depending on whether the child is legitimate or illegitimate, adopted, a stepchild, or 21 or older with a disability, and PhilHealth also accepts specific alternative documents when a PSA-issued birth certificate isn't available yet — which matters most for a newborn.

This guide breaks down the documentary requirements by child type, based on **PhilHealth Circular No. 50, s. 2012** ("Updated Documentary Requirements for Member Registration, Amendment and Benefit Availment"), which remains the governing framework for this specific documentation.

> **Important limitation:** this circular dates from 2012. No subsequent PhilHealth issuance revising these specific documentary requirements for child dependents was identified in the research for this article, so it is presented as the current operative framework — but if you're in doubt, confirming with a PhilHealth office that nothing more recent has superseded a specific requirement is a reasonable precaution.

### Quick Answer

| Child Type | Core Documents |
|---|---|
| Legitimate or illegitimate, below 21 | Birth Certificate (or two valid alternate IDs/documents, per specific combination rules) |
| Adopted, below 21 | Court Decree/Resolution of Adoption, AND the child's Birth Certificate (or alternate IDs) |
| Stepchild, below 21 | Marriage Certificate (between the natural parent and stepparent), AND the child's Birth Certificate (or alternate IDs) |
| Mentally or physically disabled, 21 years old and above | Medical Certificate from the attending physician describing the disability, AND the child's Birth Certificate (or alternate IDs) |
| Newborn (before a PSA copy is available) | Certificate of Live Birth (hospital-issued) accepted as proof of identity, age, and relationship |

## The Baseline Requirement: Who Needs What

Under the current eligibility rules (covered in more general detail in PhilHealth's own Qualified Dependents guidance), a child qualifies as a dependent if legitimate, legitimated, acknowledged illegitimate, adopted, or a stepchild — **below 21 years old, unmarried, and unemployed** — or, regardless of age, if a congenital or acquired disability renders them totally dependent on the member. The documentary requirements below are specifically about **proving** that status to PhilHealth, per Circular No. 50, s. 2012.

## 1. Legitimate or Illegitimate Child, Below 21

**Required:** either of the following:

- A **Birth Certificate**; **or**
- **Two valid IDs/documents** — one from the "proof of identity and age" list for minors, and one from the "proof of relationship" list (both detailed in the circular's Annex A).

In practice, a straightforward **PSA-issued Birth Certificate** is the simplest path, since it independently establishes both identity and the parent-child relationship in a single document.

## 2. Adopted Child, Below 21

**Required:** both of the following:

- A **Court Decree or Resolution of Adoption**; **and**
- Either the child's **Birth Certificate**, **or** two valid alternate IDs/documents (one proving identity/age, one proving relationship).

The adoption decree is the non-negotiable document here — it's what formally establishes the adoptive relationship that a standard birth certificate alone wouldn't necessarily reflect.

## 3. Stepchild, Below 21

**Required:** both of the following:

- A **Marriage Certificate between the member and the child's natural parent** (establishing the member as the stepparent); **and**
- Either the child's **Birth Certificate**, **or** two valid alternate IDs/documents.

This combination matters because a stepchild's own birth certificate alone won't show the member as a parent — the marriage certificate is what legally connects the member to the child through the natural parent.

## 4. Mentally or Physically Disabled Child, 21 Years Old and Above

**Required:** both of the following:

- A **Medical Certificate from the attending physician**, stating and describing the extent of the disability; **and**
- Either the child's **Birth Certificate**, **or** two valid alternate IDs/documents.

Since this category exists specifically because the usual age-21 cutoff doesn't apply, the medical certificate is the document doing the real work — it's what PhilHealth uses to assess whether the disability renders the child totally dependent on the member for support.

## Registering a Newborn: What to Do Before the PSA Copy Arrives

This is one of the most practically important scenarios, since PSA-issued birth certificates typically take time to process after a birth. Per the circular's list of acceptable identification documents, a **Certificate of Live Birth** — the hospital or local civil registry copy issued at or shortly after birth — is explicitly recognized as valid proof of a newborn's identity, age, **and** relationship to the parent, even before the official PSA copy is available.

This means you generally don't need to wait for the PSA copy to begin the process of declaring a newborn as a dependent — many accredited hospitals have PhilHealth desks that can help tag a newborn using the hospital-issued Certificate of Live Birth, particularly relevant for claiming benefits connected to the delivery itself.

## What Counts as a Valid Alternate ID

When a Birth Certificate alone isn't used, the circular requires **one document proving identity/age** and **one document proving relationship**, drawn from two separate lists:

**For proof of identity/age (minors below 18):** Certificate of Live Birth, Baptismal Certificate, School ID, Passport, Police Clearance, Alien Certification of Registration/Immigrant Certification of Registration, a certification from the National Council for the Welfare of Disabled Persons, a DSWD or Local Social Welfare Development Office certification, an Integrated Bar of the Philippines ID, a Barangay Certification (subject to validation), or an affidavit from two disinterested persons (subject to validation).

**For proof of relationship:** Certificate of Live Birth (for newborns), Baptismal Certificate, a DSWD or Local Social Welfare Development Office certification, a Barangay Certification (subject to validation), or an affidavit from two disinterested persons (subject to validation).

**On affidavits specifically:** the circular specifies that affidavits must be administered by officials legally authorized to administer oaths — including public notaries, among others listed in the circular — to be accepted as valid supporting documents.

## Step-by-Step: Declaring a Child as a Dependent

1. **Identify your child's category** — legitimate/illegitimate under 21, adopted, stepchild, or 21+ with a disability — since the required documents differ.
2. **Gather the core document(s)** for that category, as listed above.
3. **If a PSA birth certificate isn't yet available** (common for a newborn), use the Certificate of Live Birth or an accepted alternate combination instead.
4. **Fill out the PMRF**, or the Member Data Record (MDR) if you're amending existing information, noting the child's details.
5. **Submit the form and documents** to the nearest PhilHealth Office, or through the hospital's PhilHealth desk if this is tied to a delivery/newborn claim.
6. **Confirm the child now appears on your updated Member Data Record** before relying on their coverage for a future visit.

## Common Problems and What to Do

**Your baby was just born and you don't have the PSA birth certificate yet.**
Use the hospital-issued Certificate of Live Birth — it's explicitly accepted as proof of identity, age, and relationship for a newborn under PhilHealth's documentary requirements, so you don't need to wait for the PSA copy to start the process.

**Your child is a stepchild and you're not sure what to submit.**
You'll need both the marriage certificate (between you and the child's natural parent) and the child's birth certificate (or equivalent alternate documents) — the marriage certificate is what establishes your relationship to the child in the first place.

**You adopted your child and the original birth certificate doesn't show you as the parent.**
The Court Decree or Resolution of Adoption is the required document precisely for this reason — it's what formally establishes the adoptive relationship for PhilHealth's purposes, alongside the child's birth certificate.

**Your child turns 21 with a disability and you're not sure if they still qualify.**
They can still qualify, but you'll need a **current medical certificate from the attending physician** describing the disability and establishing that the child remains totally dependent on you — this is a different, additional requirement compared to a standard under-21 child dependent.

**You only have a Barangay Certification or an affidavit instead of a birth certificate.**
These are accepted as alternate documents in specific combinations under the circular, but a Barangay Certification and certain affidavits are explicitly noted as **"subject to validation"** — meaning PhilHealth may still assess their sufficiency on a case-by-case basis.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What's the single most important document for declaring a child as a dependent?**
For most children, a Birth Certificate — it independently proves both identity/age and the parent-child relationship.

**Can I declare my newborn before I get the PSA birth certificate?**
Yes — the hospital-issued Certificate of Live Birth is explicitly accepted as valid proof for a newborn, ahead of the PSA copy becoming available.

**What extra document does an adopted child need compared to a biological child?**
A Court Decree or Resolution of Adoption, in addition to the child's birth certificate or equivalent alternate documents.

**What extra document does a stepchild need?**
A Marriage Certificate between you and the child's natural parent, in addition to the child's birth certificate or equivalent alternate documents.

**Does a disabled child need anything beyond a birth certificate once they turn 21?**
Yes — a current Medical Certificate from the attending physician, describing the extent of the disability, is required in addition to the birth certificate or equivalent documents.

**Are affidavits accepted as proof for a child dependent?**
In specific combinations, yes, but affidavits must be administered by an official legally authorized to administer oaths, and some alternate documents (including certain affidavits and Barangay Certifications) are explicitly subject to further validation by PhilHealth.

**Is this documentary framework still current in 2026?**
It's based on PhilHealth Circular No. 50, s. 2012, which was the most recent dedicated documentary-requirements circular identified for this article. No subsequent circular revising these specific child-dependent document rules was found, but confirming with a PhilHealth office is a reasonable step if you want certainty for your specific case.
