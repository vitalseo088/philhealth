# PhilHealth Medicine Benefits 2026: What GAMOT Covers and How to Claim It

In 2026, PhilHealth's outpatient medicine benefit is called **GAMOT** (Guaranteed and Accessible Medications for Outpatient Treatment). It covers a fixed list of 75 medicines, up to an **annual benefit limit of ₱20,000**. Under the rules, you pay nothing out of pocket until that limit is used up, provided the medicine is on the list, prescribed through PhilHealth's system and dispensed by an accredited facility.

This guide is for PhilHealth members and their dependents who have, or expect to get, a prescription. It explains PhilHealth's rules, not medical advice. Which medicine you need, and how much, is your doctor's decision.

> **Two things to know up front.** First, 21 of the 75 medicines can be obtained directly at your YAKAP Clinic, and the other 54 through accredited partner pharmacies, according to PhilHealth. Second, the GAMOT circular says the list, the fixed prices and the limit can be revised, and PhilHealth has issued a 2026 circular that adds dispensing limits. See [Limits on how much you can get](#limits-on-how-much-you-can-get).

| When does the limit reset? | Every January 1. Unused balance is not carried over |

## What Is PhilHealth GAMOT?

A few terms help here:

- **GAMOT** is PhilHealth's outpatient drug benefit package. It works alongside YAKAP, the primary care benefit that replaced Konsulta, and PhilHealth's circular says it functions as the pharmaceutical arm of its outpatient benefits.
- **YAKAP Clinic** is the PhilHealth-accredited primary care provider you register with. See our guide to YAKAP clinic selection for how to choose one.
- **GAMOT facility** is a licensed drug retailer, hospital pharmacy, primary care facility with dispensing capacity or other dispensing facility that PhilHealth has accredited to provide the benefit.
- **GAMOT List** is the list of covered medicines, referenced from the Philippine National Formulary. Each preparation (for example, a specific tablet strength) has a **fixed fee** that PhilHealth uses to pay the facility and to count against your limit.
- **Annual Benefit Limit (ABL)** is the ₱20,000 yearly cap.
- **Unique Prescription Security Code (UPSC)** is a computer-generated code that identifies your prescription, issued through the **GAMOT Application**, the software doctors use to prescribe and pharmacies use to validate and record dispensing.

**How it grew:** the earlier Konsulta package covered 21 medicines. PhilHealth's launch announcements said GAMOT added 54 more for a total of 75, and the nationwide circular took effect on August 21, 2025. PhilHealth's own news release in January 2026 confirmed that the 21 medicines remain available at YAKAP-accredited clinics for the year.

## Who Is Eligible?

PhilHealth's GAMOT circular says all Filipinos under the Universal Health Care Act, qualified foreign-national members with qualifying contributions, and their duly qualified dependents are eligible. Your **PhilHealth Identification Number (PIN)** is used to verify your membership and to track how much of the benefit you have used. If you are not yet registered, the circular says you can register at any Local Health Insurance Office (LHIO) or PhilHealth Express outlet.

PhilHealth's launch announcements also told members to register with a YAKAP clinic and have a medical assessment first. The circular itself allows an accredited physician who has a GAMOT Application account to prescribe, and it requires the prescription to come through that application. Ask your doctor whether they can issue GAMOT prescriptions before you plan around the benefit.

### Prescriptions follow approved indications

PhilHealth's clinical and pharmaceutical standards (Circular 2026-0007) tie each GAMOT medicine to approved therapeutic indications and tell doctors to align prescriptions with the patient's needs and clinical condition. The same circular states that medicines prescribed **on request**, or as **standby medicines for non-active conditions**, and medicines unrelated to the current diagnosis, are not eligible for GAMOT claims. So you cannot stock up on listed medicines "just in case".

### What GAMOT does not cover

Under the circular, GAMOT does not cover:

- Medicines used during a hospital confinement or outpatient emergency care
- Branded medicines priced above the fixed fee, unless the facility dispenses them at the agreed fixed fee
- Medicines covered by other outpatient benefits
- Medicines in the Philippine National Formulary that are not on the GAMOT List, unless PhilHealth grants an explicit exception

## Step-by-Step: How to Get Your Medicines Under GAMOT

1. **Make sure you have a PIN.** Register or update your record first (LHIO, PhilHealth Express, or online).
2. **Choose a YAKAP Clinic.** PhilHealth's launch announcements pointed members to registration through the eGovPH app, the PhilHealth Member Portal or PhilHealth offices. See our YAKAP clinic guide for the full process.
3. **Have your consultation.** The doctor confirms your identity (your PIN or a valid government ID) and prescribes only what is clinically indicated.
4. **Get a GAMOT prescription.** The authorized physician issues it through the GAMOT Application. It carries the date, the UPSC, your details, generic names, strength, form, quantity and directions, and the doctor's name, signature and license number. You get a printed, handwritten or digital copy.
5. **Go to a GAMOT facility.** Bring a government-issued ID and the prescription. If the consultation was remote and you have no printed copy, the UPSC plus your ID is enough. If someone claims for you, they need an authorization letter, the UPSC, and a valid ID of both you and the representative.
6. **Claim within the time window.** Anti-infectious medicines must be dispensed within **2 days** of the prescription date, and other medicines within **2 weeks**. After that, the pharmacy can still dispense them but you pay out of pocket, and the doctor should update the prescription if you still need the medicine.
7. **Check and sign the GAMOT Availment Slip.** The slip lists what you received, what PhilHealth covered and your **remaining balance**. You sign it, and it asks whether you paid anything out of pocket.
8. **Follow up with your doctor.** PhilHealth wants regular follow-ups so that treatment is reviewed.

## Limits on How Much You Can Get

**The ₱20,000 limit.** The circular sets the ABL at ₱20,000, and the Presidential Communications Office described it as per patient per year. The circular text we reviewed does not explicitly say whether each dependent has a separate limit. Check your remaining balance on each GAMOT Availment Slip.

**What counts toward it.** PhilHealth reimburses the facility at the fixed fee for each preparation, and that fee is what is deducted from your limit. Examples from the fixed fee schedule in Annex A.2 of the circular (per unit, as issued in 2025 and subject to revision):

| Preparation | Fixed fee per unit |
|---|---|
| Amlodipine 5 mg tablet | ₱4.50 |
| Metformin 500 mg tablet | ₱4.75 |
| Losartan 50 mg tablet | ₱9.00 |
| Amoxicillin 500 mg capsule | ₱5.25 |
| Paracetamol 500 mg tablet | ₱2.25 |

*Arithmetic illustration only, not medical advice:* if a prescription called for 30 tablets of a ₱4.50 item, PhilHealth would count ₱135 against the limit.

**Quantity limits.** PhilHealth's original circular allowed doctors to prescribe maintenance medicines for up to three months and NSAIDs for up to one week, and allowed pharmacies to dispense at most one monthly supply of a maintenance medicine at a time (the next month's supply can be dispensed two weeks after the previous dispensing). A later circular, 2026-0007, amends those rules with **dispensing limits** by type of medicine. From its Annex B, as best we can read our copy:

- Short-course medicines for acute, self-limiting conditions, including NSAIDs, antihistamines and paracetamol: **7 days**
- Oral medicines for chronic disease management (for example, amlodipine, losartan, metformin, atorvastatin): **30 days**
- Oral antimicrobials: dispensed in full within the claiming period, with prescriptions not exceeding **14 days**
- A few items such as omeprazole, prednisone and zinc appear with **14-day** limits
- Inhalers, creams, eye preparations and similar forms have their own per-product limits in the Annex

The table layout in our copy was hard to read, so confirm the exact limit for your medicine with the pharmacy or doctor. If a medicine runs past its dispensing limit, PhilHealth says the patient should follow up with the primary care physician, who decides whether to stop or extend treatment.

**Reset.** The ABL returns to the full amount every January 1. Any unused balance from the previous year is not added.

## What Will It Cost You?

- **Within the limit:** no out-of-pocket payment, per the circular.
- **After the limit is used up:** you pay the fixed fee for listed medicines, unless you prefer a branded product priced above it. Pharmacies must apply mandatory discounts (such as senior citizen and PWD) and VAT exemption once the limit is consumed and on any amount you pay yourself.
- **Outside the list or the rules:** full price at the pharmacy. This includes medicines claimed after the time window.

## Medicines During a Hospital Stay

GAMOT is for outpatient use. If you are admitted, PhilHealth pays an accredited hospital a fixed **case rate**, and PhilHealth's case rate policy says that rate covers professional fees and facility charges including drugs, medicines and supplies. PhilHealth's 2026 circular on maternal and gynecologic case rates also states that its package includes medicines listed in the Philippine National Drug Formulary and that patients in basic or ward accommodation have no co-payment. A case rate is a fixed amount per case, not a promise that PhilHealth pays an entire bill, so what you pay depends on your room type and the rules for your case.

## Examples

*These are illustrations, not real cases or medical advice.*

**Example 1: a clinic-stocked medicine.** A member empaneled at a YAKAP Clinic is prescribed a medicine from the clinic's 21-item set. The clinic dispenses it from its stock, the member signs the availment slip, and the amount counts against the ₱20,000 limit.

**Example 2: a partner pharmacy.** A doctor issues a GAMOT prescription for a medicine that is not among the 21. The member takes the printed prescription and a valid ID to an accredited partner pharmacy within two weeks. The pharmacist checks the prescription in the GAMOT Application, dispenses it, and the slip shows the remaining balance.

**Example 3: the representative.** A member cannot go in person. The member's relative brings an authorization letter, the UPSC, and a valid ID of both the member and the relative.

## Common Problems

- **The clinic is out of stock.** PhilHealth told clinics to keep sufficient stock of the 21 medicines and acknowledged complaints of stockouts in news coverage. GAMOT facilities must also keep all committed medicines available and post a list of what they stock. Ask the pharmacy for its list before you go.
- **The pharmacy refuses to serve you.** The circular says refusing a beneficiary is grounds for investigation. Notify PhilHealth through its official channels.
- **The prescription is rejected.** A pharmacist may check with the prescribing doctor if something looks inconsistent, and may withhold a medicine that looks inappropriate (for example, because of a drug interaction or allergy) and send you back to the doctor.
- **The prescription expired.** GAMOT prescriptions are automatically cancelled in the system 90 days after the prescription date, and the claiming windows (2 days and 2 weeks) apply first.
- **You are charged inside your limit.** Check the availment slip. If you paid out of pocket within your limit, report it (see below).
- **You are not sure the pharmacy is accredited.** Do not assume. Check PhilHealth's official provider information before you go.

## What To Do If Something Goes Wrong

1. Keep the prescription (or UPSC), the GAMOT Availment Slip and any receipt.
2. Ask the facility to explain in writing why you are being charged.
3. Tick the out-of-pocket question on the availment slip truthfully, and use the comments section for complaints. PhilHealth uses the slip for monitoring.
4. Report to your LHIO or the **PhilHealth Corporate Action Center**: the circulars list the hotline as (02) 8662-2588 on the letterhead (written (02) 866-2588 in the text of one 2026 circular) and the email as actioncenter@philhealth.gov.ph. Confirm current contact details on philhealth.gov.ph.

## Where to Find a GAMOT Pharmacy or Clinic

PhilHealth said there were around 4,300 partner clinics at the August 2025 launch and 304 accredited pharmacies as of September 30, 2025 (56 in Metro Manila). In July 2026, the President said members can get medicines from more than 2,000 accredited providers. These figures are not directly comparable and change often. Use PhilHealth's official provider information before visiting. *Provider information can change. Check the official PhilHealth provider directory before visiting.*

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Are PhilHealth medicines really free?**
Under the GAMOT circular, you pay nothing out of pocket until the ₱20,000 annual limit is used up, if the medicine is on the list and prescribed and dispensed under the rules.

**How many medicines are covered?**
75 on the GAMOT List. 21 are available at YAKAP Clinics and the other 54 at partner pharmacies, according to PhilHealth.

**Do I need a prescription?**
Yes. Only medicines prescribed by authorized physicians through the GAMOT Application are covered.

**Can I use any pharmacy?**
Any PhilHealth-accredited GAMOT facility. The circular says beneficiaries can avail of the benefit at any GAMOT facility.

**Does the ₱20,000 carry over?**
No. It resets on January 1 and unused balance is not added.

**Are branded medicines covered?**
Only if the facility dispenses them at the GAMOT fixed fee. If you prefer a branded product priced above the fixed fee, you pay the difference.

**Can I ask for medicines to keep at home?**
Medicines prescribed on request or as standby for a non-active condition are not eligible under PhilHealth's 2026 standards.

**Does my dependent have the same benefit?**
Dependents are eligible. Whether each dependent has a separate ₱20,000 limit is not spelled out in the circular text we read; the Presidential Communications Office described the limit as per patient.

**What if my medicine is not on the list?**
It is not covered by GAMOT unless PhilHealth grants an explicit exception. Ask your doctor whether another PhilHealth benefit applies.

**Is the list going to change?**
The circular says the list, fixed fees and limit may be revised through a supplemental circular. The President said in 2026 that GAMOT would expand in 2027. We did not find a published change to the 75-medicine list or the ₱20,000 limit as of this review.
