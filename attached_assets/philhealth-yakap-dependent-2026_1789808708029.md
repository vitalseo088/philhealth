# PhilHealth YAKAP Dependent: How to Add and Register a Family Member (2026 Guide)

To get a family member covered as a PhilHealth YAKAP dependent, you first need to formally declare them as your qualified dependent with PhilHealth using the PhilHealth Member Registration Form (PMRF) and supporting documents — and then separately register them with a YAKAP Clinic. This guide walks through both parts of that process.

This guide is for PhilHealth members who want to add a spouse, child, parent, or other qualified family member as a dependent, and then get that dependent enrolled in YAKAP so they can use free consultations, lab tests, cancer screening referrals, and medicines. It reflects PhilHealth's PMRF-based dependent declaration process alongside the YAKAP-specific registration and empanelment rules under PhilHealth Circular No. 2025-0017.

## What Is a PhilHealth YAKAP Dependent?

A PhilHealth **dependent** is a specific family member of a PhilHealth member who is covered under that member's PhilHealth registration, without needing to be a member themselves. Under PhilHealth Circular No. 2025-0017, which governs YAKAP's selection and empanelment rules, a **beneficiary** — the term used for anyone entitled to YAKAP benefits — is defined as "any individual, member or qualified dependent, who is eligible to avail of PhilHealth benefits" under the National Health Insurance Program.

So a "YAKAP dependent" is simply a qualified dependent who has also been registered and empaneled with a YAKAP Clinic — because being a PhilHealth dependent on paper and being able to actually use YAKAP's free services are two different milestones.

## Who Qualifies as a Dependent?

PhilHealth's official qualified-dependents pages list the following categories:

- **Legitimate spouse**, provided they are not themselves a PhilHealth member
- **Children** — legitimate, legitimated, acknowledged, illegitimate (as shown on the birth certificate), adopted, or stepchildren — who are **below 21 years old, unmarried, and unemployed**
- **Children 21 years old or above** with a congenital or acquired disability, physical or mental, that renders them totally dependent on the member for support, as determined by PhilHealth
- **Foster children**, as defined under the Foster Care Act of 2012 (Republic Act No. 10165)
- **Parents 60 years old or above**, not themselves PhilHealth members, whose monthly income is below the threshold PhilHealth has set
- **Parents with a permanent disability**, regardless of age, that renders them totally dependent on the member for subsistence

PhilHealth's pages are explicit that a dependent must be **declared by the principal member** and reflected in the member's **Member Data Record (MDR)** — this declaration is what makes benefit availment smooth for that dependent.

## Step-by-Step: How to Add a Dependent to Your PhilHealth Record

### Step 1: Download and Fill Out the PMRF

Get the PhilHealth Member Registration Form (PMRF) from the PhilHealth website's Downloads section, or from any PhilHealth office. Tick the **"For Updating/Amendment"** box in the purpose field, and complete the dependent declaration section with the dependent's name, relationship, and other required details.

### Step 2: Gather Supporting Documents

According to PhilHealth's official process (as described on PhilHealth's data-amendment page for informal-economy members and PhilHealth's Member Management Group service handbook), a properly accomplished PMRF must be accompanied by documents that establish the relationship between the member and the dependent. Based on PhilHealth's own published checklist for this transaction, this includes at minimum:

- The duly accomplished PMRF (original copy for walk-in submission)
- A valid photo-bearing government ID of the member (and of the representative, if someone else is submitting on the member's behalf)
- For a spouse: a scanned or photocopy of the Marriage Contract/Certificate
- A selfie of the member holding a valid ID, if submitting by email

PhilHealth's checklist for this specific transaction did not, in the official document reviewed for this article, spell out a separate document list for every dependent category (such as children or parents) beyond what's outlined above — for those categories, it's best to confirm the exact document PhilHealth will require (such as a birth certificate for a child) directly with your Local Health Insurance Office (LHIO) before your visit, since requirements can vary by case.

### Step 3: Submit the PMRF and Documents

PhilHealth's official process allows submission through:

- **Walk-in** at any PhilHealth office or Local Health Insurance Office (LHIO)
- **By email**, with scanned copies of the PMRF and supporting documents sent to the applicable PhilHealth regional office email address

### Step 4: Wait for Your Updated Member Data Record

Once processed, PhilHealth issues an updated MDR reflecting the newly declared dependent. This MDR is the document PhilHealth uses to verify who is attached to your membership.

## Step-by-Step: Registering That Dependent for YAKAP

Having a dependent declared on your MDR does not automatically enroll them in YAKAP. A separate registration step is needed.

### Step 1: Select a YAKAP Clinic for the Dependent

Per PhilHealth's official YAKAP FAQ, this can be done through the eGovPH app, the PhilHealth Member Portal, any PhilHealth office, or directly at a YAKAP Clinic. The dependent doesn't need to be registered at the same clinic as the member — whichever is more convenient for them is acceptable.

### Step 2: Complete the Dependent's First Patient Encounter (FPE)

The dependent must personally visit the chosen YAKAP Clinic for their own consultation, where the doctor reviews their health history and risk factors.

### Step 3: Complete Empanelment

Per PhilHealth Circular No. 2025-0017, this formally establishes the ongoing care relationship between the dependent and the clinic for the year. Until this step is completed, the dependent has only expressed a clinic preference — they are not yet entitled to that clinic's ongoing free services.

### Who Handles These Steps for a Minor Dependent

PhilHealth Circular No. 2025-0017 specifies that for beneficiaries who are minors, all official YAKAP transactions — including registration, clinic selection, and signing agreements such as the Mutual Care Agreement — must be undertaken by a parent or legal guardian, since minors don't have legal personality to transact on their own. Beneficiaries 18 and above can handle these steps independently.

## What Benefits Does a YAKAP Dependent Get?

Once declared as a dependent and separately registered and empaneled with a YAKAP Clinic, a dependent can access, based on clinical need and the doctor's recommendation:

- Consultations at their registered YAKAP Clinic
- Outpatient laboratory tests
- Selected cancer screening tests (for breast, lung, liver, and colorectal cancer, subject to eligibility criteria)
- Free essential medicines, up to a separate limit

PhilHealth's official FAQ confirms that the ₱20,000 annual medicine limit applies **separately to each beneficiary** — a dependent has their own ₱20,000 limit, distinct from the member's.

## Examples

*The following are illustrative examples only, not official case determinations.*

**Example 1:** A member marries and wants to declare their spouse as a dependent. They fill out the PMRF, tick "For Updating," and submit it at their LHIO along with their Marriage Certificate and valid ID. Once the updated MDR reflects the spouse, the member separately registers the spouse at a YAKAP Clinic and the spouse completes their own First Patient Encounter.

**Example 2:** A member's newborn child needs to be declared as a dependent and later registered for YAKAP. The member submits the PMRF update declaring the child, and once the child is old enough for a clinic visit, the parent (as legal guardian) handles the YAKAP Clinic selection and First Patient Encounter on the child's behalf.

## Common Problems

- **"I declared my dependent, but they still can't get free YAKAP consultations."** Declaration on the MDR and YAKAP registration are two different steps — the dependent must still be registered with a YAKAP Clinic and complete their First Patient Encounter and empanelment.
- **"My spouse is also a PhilHealth member — can I add them as a dependent?"** Based on PhilHealth's official dependent criteria, a legitimate spouse only qualifies as a dependent if they are **not** themselves a PhilHealth member. Confirm your specific situation with PhilHealth if both spouses are separately registered.
- **"I'm not sure what documents to bring for a parent or child dependent."** PhilHealth's official transaction checklist for this article confirmed the general requirement (PMRF plus documents establishing the relationship) but did not fully detail every document per dependent category in the source reviewed. Confirm the exact required document (such as a birth certificate) with your LHIO before submitting.
- **"My dependent's registration or MDR update is taking a while."** The available official sources reviewed for this article don't state a fixed processing time for PMRF updates. If it's been an unusually long wait, contact PhilHealth directly to check status.

## What to Do If Something Goes Wrong

If you run into a problem declaring or registering a dependent, PhilHealth's official YAKAP FAQ lists these contact channels:

- Call the PhilHealth hotline listed on the official YAKAP FAQ page (open 24/7)
- Text or call the Smart/Globe numbers published on PhilHealth's official YAKAP FAQ
- Email actioncenter@philhealth.gov.ph
- Visit any PhilHealth office or Local Health Insurance Office
- Use the click-to-call feature on the official PhilHealth website

Always verify current contact numbers directly on philhealth.gov.ph, since these can be updated.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Do I need to add my dependent before I can register them for YAKAP?**
Yes. Your dependent needs to be formally declared and reflected in your Member Data Record before they can be meaningfully registered and empaneled as a YAKAP beneficiary under your membership.

**Can I add more than one dependent at a time?**
The PMRF has a dependent declaration section that can list multiple dependents; PhilHealth's own form notes that an additional form may be used if necessary for more entries.

**Does my dependent need their own PhilHealth PIN?**
The PMRF asks for the dependent's PhilHealth Identification Number "if previously issued," which indicates a dependent may already have or be assigned a PIN as part of this process. The sources reviewed for this article did not fully detail when or how a new PIN is issued to a first-time dependent, so this is best confirmed with PhilHealth.

**Can my dependent choose a different YAKAP Clinic than mine?**
Yes. Each beneficiary, including dependents, selects their own clinic — it doesn't have to match the member's.

**What happens if I don't update my MDR before my dependent needs care?**
The sources reviewed for this article don't describe an alternative process for using YAKAP benefits without a prior MDR declaration. To avoid delays, it's advisable to complete the dependent declaration process before your family member needs primary care.
