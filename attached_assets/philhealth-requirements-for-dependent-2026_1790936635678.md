## PhilHealth Requirements for Dependent: Who Qualifies and What You Need in 2026

Here's the direct answer: PhilHealth lets a member add certain family members as **dependents** — spouse, children, and in some cases parents — who can access the member's PhilHealth coverage without paying a separate premium. Each category has its own specific eligibility criteria, and adding someone requires a **PhilHealth Member Registration Form (PMRF)** marked "For Updating," along with a document proving the relationship.

This guide is for PhilHealth members in **2026** who want to know exactly who qualifies as a dependent, what proof is needed for each type, and how the declaration process works — based on PhilHealth's own published dependent eligibility rules.

> **Important limitation:** PhilHealth's official dependent criteria, as published on its own website, list qualified categories with their own independent conditions (age, marital status, employment, income, or disability). Some secondary sources online describe an additional rule — that parents can only be declared if the member has no spouse and no children — but this specific restriction was **not found stated in PhilHealth's own official dependent criteria** reviewed for this article. If you're declaring parents alongside a spouse or children, confirm directly with PhilHealth whether any such hierarchy applies in practice, rather than assuming either way.

## Who Qualifies as a PhilHealth Dependent?

Per PhilHealth's own published eligibility criteria, the following are qualified dependents:

- **Legitimate spouse who is not a member** — your legal husband or wife, provided they are not themselves an active PhilHealth member under their own category.
- **Children** — legitimate, legitimated, acknowledged illegitimate (as shown on the birth certificate), adopted, or stepchildren, **below 21 years of age, unmarried, and unemployed**.
- **Children 21 years old or above** who have a **congenital disability** (physical or mental) or an **acquired disability** that renders them **totally dependent on the member for support**, as determined by PhilHealth.
- **Foster children**, as defined under **Republic Act No. 10165** (the Foster Care Act of 2012).
- **Parents who are 60 years old or above**, not themselves an enrolled PhilHealth member, **whose monthly income is below an amount to be determined by PhilHealth** in accordance with the guiding principles of the National Health Insurance Act of 2013.
- **Parents with a permanent disability, regardless of age**, as determined by PhilHealth, that renders them **totally dependent on the member for subsistence**.

**On the specific income threshold for parent-dependents:** PhilHealth's own criteria describe this as "an amount to be determined by PhilHealth," without stating the specific peso figure in the source reviewed for this article. If you're trying to determine whether a parent's income qualifies, confirm the current threshold directly with PhilHealth rather than relying on a figure from an unofficial source.

## Dependent-by-Dependent: What's Required

### 1. Legal Spouse

- Must be **legally married** to the member — civil or church marriage recognized under Philippine law.
- The spouse must **not already be an active PhilHealth member** in their own right (for example, through their own employment).
- **Document needed:** PSA-issued Marriage Certificate, along with valid ID.

### 2. Children Under 21

- Covers legitimate, legitimated, acknowledged illegitimate (as it appears on the birth certificate), adopted children, and stepchildren.
- Must be **below 21 years of age, unmarried, and unemployed** — all three conditions apply together.
- **Document needed:** PSA-issued Birth Certificate establishing the parent-child relationship.

### 3. Children 21 and Older, With a Disability

- Applies when a congenital or acquired disability (physical or mental) renders the child **totally dependent on the member for support**, as assessed by PhilHealth.
- **Documents needed:** Birth Certificate, plus a **current medical certificate** documenting the disability — this isn't a one-time requirement, since PhilHealth's assessment depends on the disability's current status.

### 4. Foster Children

- Covered under **Republic Act No. 10165**, the Foster Care Act of 2012.
- The specific documentation required to establish foster care status for PhilHealth purposes was not detailed in the sources reviewed for this article — confirm the exact requirements with a PhilHealth office if this applies to you.

### 5. Parents Aged 60 and Above

- The parent must **not already be an enrolled PhilHealth member** in their own right.
- The parent's **monthly income must fall below the threshold PhilHealth has set.**
- **Documents needed:** The member's own Birth Certificate (to establish parentage), along with proof of the parent's income status as PhilHealth requires.

**Worth noting:** many Filipino senior citizens are separately covered as PhilHealth members in their own right under the government-funded Senior Citizen membership category (per Republic Act No. 10645) once they turn 60 — in that scenario, declaring them as your dependent may be unnecessary, since they already have independent coverage.

### 6. Parents With a Permanent Disability, Regardless of Age

- Qualifies when the disability renders the parent **totally dependent on the member for subsistence**, as determined by PhilHealth.
- **Documents needed:** The member's own Birth Certificate, along with medical documentation of the disability.

## How to Add a Dependent: Step by Step

1. **Confirm the person meets the specific criteria** for their dependent category (spouse, child, parent, etc.) listed above.
2. **Download the current PhilHealth Member Registration Form (PMRF)** from the official PhilHealth website.
3. **Mark the form "For Updating"** and fill in the dependent's details.
4. **Gather the required supporting document** for that dependent type — marriage certificate, birth certificate, medical certificate, or proof of income, as applicable.
5. **Submit the PMRF and documents** to any PhilHealth Local Health Insurance Office (LHIO), or through an applicable online updating channel if available.
6. **Confirm the dependent now appears on your updated Member Data Record (MDR)** before relying on their coverage for a planned medical visit.

## Common Problems and What to Do

**Your spouse already has their own PhilHealth coverage through work.**
If your spouse is an active PhilHealth member in their own right, they generally shouldn't be declared as your dependent — they're already covered under their own membership.

**Your child just turned 21 and you're not sure if they still qualify.**
Unless your child has a qualifying disability, turning 21 removes their dependent eligibility regardless of whether they're still studying, unemployed by choice, or otherwise. They would need to register under their own PhilHealth membership going forward.

**You want to add your parents, but you also have a spouse or children on record.**
As noted above, PhilHealth's own published criteria don't state a rule restricting parent-dependents to members with no spouse or children — but this restriction is commonly described in secondary sources. Confirm directly with PhilHealth how this is actually applied before assuming either outcome.

**You're not sure what counts as "below the income threshold" for a parent.**
PhilHealth's own criteria describe this threshold as something PhilHealth itself determines, without stating the figure in the source reviewed here. Ask a PhilHealth office directly for the current applicable amount.

**You added a dependent but they weren't recognized during a hospital visit.**
Confirm the dependent actually appears on your current Member Data Record (MDR) — an update that was submitted but not yet processed, or documents that didn't fully establish eligibility, can result in a dependent not being recognized at the point of care.

**Your dependent is a live-in partner, not a legally married spouse.**
Based on PhilHealth's criteria, only a **legal spouse** qualifies as a spousal dependent — a live-in or common-law partner who isn't legally married to the member does not meet this requirement.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Who can be declared as a PhilHealth dependent?**
A legal spouse who isn't themselves a PhilHealth member, children under 21 who are unmarried and unemployed, children 21 or older with a qualifying disability, foster children under RA 10165, parents 60 and above below the PhilHealth income threshold, and parents of any age with a qualifying permanent disability.

**Do I need to pay extra to add a dependent?**
No — a dependent shares the member's existing PhilHealth coverage without requiring a separate premium.

**Can I declare my live-in partner as a dependent?**
No — PhilHealth recognizes only a legally married spouse for this category.

**What documents do I need to add my spouse?**
A PSA-issued Marriage Certificate, along with a valid ID, submitted with a PMRF marked "For Updating."

**Can both of my parents be declared as dependents?**
PhilHealth's criteria list parents as a qualified category based on age/income or disability, without an explicit cap on declaring both — but confirm with PhilHealth directly regarding your specific household situation, especially if you also have a spouse or children on record.

**What happens to my child's dependent status when they turn 21?**
Unless they have a qualifying disability, they stop meeting the dependent criteria at 21 and would need to register under their own PhilHealth membership.

**Is there a specific income limit for declaring a parent as a dependent?**
Yes, in principle — PhilHealth sets this threshold itself, but the specific peso amount was not confirmed in the sources reviewed for this article. Confirm the current figure directly with PhilHealth.
